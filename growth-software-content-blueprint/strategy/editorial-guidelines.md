# Editorial Guidelines

These rules govern every page in `site/`. They come from the source strategy DOCX (Sections 2, 8, 9 and 12) and the build brief. They apply equally to human editors and AI-assisted drafting.

## 1. Who we write for

- Small SEO and digital marketing agencies managing several client accounts.
- Founders and operators buying their first sales, marketing and analytics stack.
- In-house B2B marketing teams that have to justify software costs.
- Ecommerce growth teams choosing email, social and conversion software.

Assume the reader is busy and capable. They want a decision made easier, not a tour of the category.

## 2. Editorial signature

1. **"What it really costs."** Where pricing is verified, model a realistic buyer scenario: seat minimums, credit allowances, usage limits, add-ons and renewal pricing. Where it is not verified, say so and link to the official pricing page.
2. **"Best for / not for."** Every recommendation names the buyer it suits and the buyer who should skip it. There are no universal winners.
3. **"What we checked."** Each article discloses its evidence: official pricing pages, product documentation and vendor materials. **No hands-on testing has been performed for this draft corpus**, so no page may claim it.
4. **"Pricing checked on."** Every material pricing claim carries a checked-on date and a link to the official source.
5. **"Correct this page."** Readers and vendors can submit evidence-based corrections. Vendors never get editorial control.

## 3. Voice

- Practical, specific and candid about trade-offs. Write like an experienced operator advising a peer.
- Separate product facts ("The vendor's pricing page lists…") from editorial interpretation ("In our assessment, this suits…").
- Approved phrasing: "Our editorial assessment", "Based on the product information reviewed", "A good fit for teams that…", "Consider another option if…", "Pricing checked on [date]".
- Banned phrasing: "revolutionary", "game-changing", "best-in-class", "ultimate solution", "in today's digital landscape", "we tested", "our testing found", "users rate", "verified customer rating", "number one", and "best overall" unless the selection criteria are explained right next to it.
- Do not make every product sound equally good. Name real limitations.
- Vary openings. Do not start articles with a definition of the category or a rhetorical question used everywhere else.

## 4. Factual accuracy

- Treat every price, plan name, limit, integration, trial, discount and feature as a claim that needs checking.
- Product facts come first from official sources: pricing pages, documentation and release notes. Third-party material gives context only and is labelled as such.
- If a fact could not be verified during drafting, either write around it ("check the vendor's current plan limits for…") or mark it `Needs verification` in the research log. Never guess a number and present it as fact.
- Never fabricate testing, screenshots, customer quotes, case studies, benchmarks, conversion lifts, market share, customer counts, review counts or ratings.
- Never invent affiliate programs, commission rates, coupon codes or vendor relationships.
- Log every verified or flagged claim in `strategy/research-source-log.csv`.

## 5. Originality

- Never copy text, tables, page structures or distinctive phrasing from DeployHyre, SaasHyre, MakeToolz, G2, Capterra or any other publisher. Only high-level patterns are borrowed (TL;DR, quick table, methodology, FAQs).
- Never imply affiliation with G2, Capterra, TrustRadius or any vendor.
- Reuse facts across pages, not prose. A product that appears on many pages gets a consistent factual description, with analysis adapted to each page's question.

## 6. Ordering and rankings

- Listicles present products in an editorial order that follows the article's disclosed selection criteria. State this, and state that payment does not influence the order.
- Do not call a list "ranked" unless the article explains the ranking method.

## 7. Commercial content

See `monetization-and-disclosure-policy.md`. In short: no page is sponsored, no affiliate link is confirmed, and every outbound vendor link in this draft is a plain official link.

## 8. Review cadence

- Pricing guides and comparisons: re-check every 60–90 days.
- Evergreen guides: re-check every 4–6 months, or straight after a known product change.
