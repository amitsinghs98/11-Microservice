## How to use each brief

- **Primary query** is a search-intent hypothesis, not a verified search-volume claim. Check the live SERP and query overlap before drafting.
- **Distinct angle** states why this page should exist instead of another generic software roundup.
- **In-depth writing brief** identifies the comparison dimensions, scenarios, caveats and original analysis the article needs.
- **Internal links** lists useful neighboring subjects. Convert them to exact URLs from the final 300-page manifest; do not create dead links to pages that are not drafted.
- Verify product names, pricing, plan limits, features, integrations and affiliate terms from primary sources. Record source URL and checked date.
- Do not fabricate testing, user reviews, star ratings, savings, performance lifts, customer numbers, commissions or vendor partnerships.

## Cannibalization and editorial controls

### Do not turn adjacency into random breadth

Before publishing, compare each new query and intended reader outcome against the original A001–A100 map and every other new page. Merge or reposition pages that answer the same question. Examples that need an explicit boundary: content workflow vs AI writing; PPC reporting vs general marketing analytics; B2B vs ecommerce CDPs; help desk vs shared inbox; feature flags vs A/B testing; agency project management vs general task management.

### Recommended publishing priority

**First expansion wave:** Content Operations, Paid Media, Agency Operations, Customer Retention, and Data/RevOps. These are natural extensions of the existing agency/growth-software positioning.

**Second expansion wave:** Affiliate/Partnerships, Webinars/Demand Generation, and Website Experience. Publish when there is enough editorial research and relevant software profiles.

**Optional specialized waves:** Ecommerce Growth and Product-Led Growth. They can be valuable, but activate them only if the brand can support these audiences with depth. Do not add them merely to increase page count.

### Page-depth guidance

- Best-of guides: approximately 1,400–2,000 words when the category warrants depth.
- Comparisons and alternatives: approximately 1,400–2,100 words where trade-offs require it.
- Pricing pages: model real billing units and scenarios; never pad or guess.
- Practical guides: approximately 1,200–1,800 words with steps, templates, ownership and QA.
- Cluster hubs: approximately 800–1,400 words plus useful navigation and buyer education.

### Internal-link rules

1. Each cluster hub should link to every supporting article in that cluster.
2. Each supporting article should link to its hub and 2–5 closely related pages.
3. `/growth-stack/` should link to all 15 hubs, grouped by acquisition, conversion, retention, measurement and operations.
4. Comparisons and alternatives should link to canonical product profiles when those profiles exist.
5. Use varied, descriptive anchors. Do not repeat exact-match anchors mechanically.
6. Keep an internal-link CSV and orphan-page report. Mark unpublished targets as planned; never leave accidental broken links.
