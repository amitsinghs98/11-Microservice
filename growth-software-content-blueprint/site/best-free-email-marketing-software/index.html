Best Free Email Marketing Software: What the Free Plans Actually Allow

The best free email marketing software for you is the plan whose first limit sits furthest from your list: Kit, Sender and EmailOctopus allow thousands of subscribers for free, while MailerLite, Mailchimp and Klaviyo stop at 250. Each plan caps something different, so check contacts, sends, automation and branding before you build.

Which free plan should you choose?

Choose by the limit you'll hit first: subscriber count for most growing lists, automation for anyone who needs a welcome sequence, and daily sending for big lists you rarely email. No free plan is unlimited, and the "best" one changes with your list size.

  • A broadcast newsletter heading toward thousands of readers: Kit's free plan, if you can live without automated sequences.
  • A small business that wants automation and a roomy cap: Sender or EmailOctopus, both of which include basic automations on their free tiers.
  • A large contact file emailed occasionally: Brevo, which stores a large number of contacts but limits sending per day.
  • A list under 250 that needs a full feature set to learn on: MailerLite Free.
  • A new online store testing flows: Klaviyo's free plan, as a short stepping stone.
  • A sandbox only: Mailchimp Free, whose send allowance is the tightest of the plans we could confirm.

Is it free forever, or a trial?

Every plan on this page is a free-forever tier that costs nothing for as long as you stay inside its limits. A trial is different: it unlocks paid features for a fixed period, then charges you or drops you to a reduced plan.

The two often sit side by side on the same signup page, which is where buyers get caught. If you build an automation while a trial is active and the free tier doesn't include that feature, it stops when the trial ends. Before you build anything, write down which features belong to the free plan and which belong to the trial. EmailOctopus, for example, states plainly that its free plan is not a trial (checked 4 November 2026).

What does each free plan include?

Each block gives confirmed limits, who the plan suits and when to skip it. A first paid price appears only where the vendor confirmed it.

Kit Free: best for broadcast-only newsletters

Kit's pricing page describes a free tier for up to 10,000 subscribers with no sending limits (checked 4 November 2026). Its help center adds that the free plan has one user seat and does not include visual automations, email sequences or paid apps (checked 4 November 2026). Paid plans are priced by subscriber count; the same article lists Creator at $39 a month for up to 1,000 subscribers.

Strength: the highest free cap here by a wide margin. Limitation: without sequences, every new subscriber gets only your next broadcast. Skip it if a welcome series or lead magnet delivery is central to your plan, because that's the feature you'll pay for first.

Sender Free: best for small businesses that want automation without paying

Sender's own site describes a Free Forever plan with up to 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month, including automation, landing pages and signup forms, with a "Powered by Sender" footer on free-plan emails (checked 4 November 2026).

Strength: the most generous combination of cap and automation we could confirm. Limitation: a smaller integration ecosystem than the largest platforms, so confirm your store, form or CRM connector exists. Skip it if branded footers are unacceptable for your brand from day one.

EmailOctopus Free: best for simple newsletters with light automation

EmailOctopus lists a free plan with 2,500 subscribers and 10,000 emails a month, 3 automations, 1 landing page, 1 form, 1 user and 30 days of reports, with EmailOctopus branding that only a paid plan removes (checked 4 November 2026).

Strength: clear, published limits. Limitation: 30-day reporting makes it hard to see how engagement changes over a quarter. Skip it if you need multi-branch journeys or more than one teammate.

Brevo Free: best for large lists you email occasionally

Brevo's help center states the free plan allows 300 emails a day and storage for up to 100,000 contacts, with up to 2,000 unique contacts able to enter active automations and the Brevo logo on emails (checked 4 November 2026). The Starter plan starts at $9 a month for 5,000 emails, and logo removal on Starter is a paid add-on (checked 4 November 2026).

Strength: the contact allowance won't push you to upgrade. Limitation: a list of more than 300 people can't receive one campaign on the same day. Skip it if your sends are time-sensitive, such as launches, webinars or sales.

MailerLite Free: best for a small list that needs a full toolkit

MailerLite's free plan covers 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with 3 automations, 3 forms, 1 landing page and 1 website; once you go over 250 active subscribers, you must upgrade (checked 4 November 2026). MailerLite introduced its Comfort and Power plans in June 2026, and the Comfort tier for 500 subscribers lists $12 a month with 5,000 emails (checked 4 November 2026).

Strength: a polished editor and real automation on a free account. Limitation: the cap is low, and MailerLite bills on active subscribers, so check how it counts them. Skip it if you're importing an existing list of more than 250 people.

Klaviyo Free: best for a new store testing ecommerce flows

Klaviyo's free plan covers 250 active profiles and 500 email sends a month, with email support limited to the first 60 days (checked 4 November 2026). Klaviyo's paid email plans start at $20 a month and rise with profile count (checked 4 November 2026). SMS allowances on the free plan were described inconsistently across Klaviyo's pages, so check the live page.

Strength: store data and flow templates from the first day. Limitation: 500 sends disappear quickly once cart and post-purchase flows run. Our shortlist of email platforms for ecommerce brands compares the paid options. Skip it if you don't sell online.

Mailchimp Free: best as a sandbox

Mailchimp's Free plan includes 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with a daily limit of 250, and sending is paused if you exceed the contact or send limit (checked 4 November 2026).

Strength: a familiar interface and a very large integration catalog. Limitation: 500 sends covers two campaigns to a full 250-contact list. If paid Mailchimp tiers are your sticking point, our guide to Mailchimp alternatives, grouped by reason for switching, lays out the options. Skip it if you plan to send weekly.

HubSpot free tools: best if you already live in HubSpot's CRM

HubSpot's free tools include marketing email alongside the free CRM, with HubSpot branding. We couldn't confirm the current free send allowance from an official HubSpot page, so check HubSpot's Marketing Hub pricing before relying on it. The HubSpot Marketing Hub and ActiveCampaign comparison covers what changes once you pay. Skip it if email is your main channel and you don't need the CRM.

Which plans survive a 500-subscriber newsletter?

At 500 subscribers, Sender and EmailOctopus can carry a weekly newsletter and a short welcome sequence for free. Kit can carry the newsletter but not the sequence, Brevo can carry both only by splitting each send across two days, and MailerLite, Klaviyo and Mailchimp are already past their caps.

The assumptions behind this hypothetical are 500 active subscribers, one newsletter a week (about 4.3 a month) and a three-email welcome sequence for about 40 new signups a month. That comes to roughly 2,150 newsletter emails plus 120 welcome emails, or about 2,270 sends a month.

  • Sender and EmailOctopus: well inside their 2,500-subscriber and monthly send limits, and both include automations. The trigger to pay is branding, not volume.
  • Kit: 500 subscribers is nowhere near the 10,000 cap, but the welcome sequence needs a paid plan. If you can skip it, Kit stays free for the longest.
  • Brevo: about 2,270 monthly sends is fine, but at 300 emails a day each weekly newsletter has to go out over two days.
  • MailerLite: the 250-subscriber cap is exceeded, so the realistic option is Comfort at the 500-subscriber tier, listed at $12 a month.
  • Klaviyo and Mailchimp: both the contact and send caps rule them out.

What do free plans cost you that isn't on the price tag?

The usual hidden costs are vendor branding in every email, short reporting history, limited support and a single seat. None of them shows on the price, but each can push you to upgrade sooner than the contact cap would.

The skeptical question is whether those trade-offs matter more in practice than the limits suggest. The documentation can tell you where the caps sit, but not how a branded footer or a two-day send window lands with your particular audience.

Will your automations move with you?

Mostly no: contacts export as a file, but automations, segments built on platform-specific events, templates and forms generally have to be rebuilt by hand. That makes the free plan you start on a bigger commitment than its price suggests.

Keep a plain document for every automation, listing its trigger, delays, conditions and copy. Export contacts with signup source, signup date, consent wording and tags at least once a quarter. What else do buyers ask about free email plans?

Most questions come down to deliverability, cold outreach and account ownership. The short answers are below.

Is there a free email marketing plan with no limits?

No. Every free plan reviewed here limits contacts, sends, automation, branding or reporting, so the useful question is which limit you'll reach first.

Do free plans hurt deliverability?

Not by themselves. List quality, consent and domain authentication matter more than the plan tier, though some free plans restrict authentication or support options, so check before you send at volume.

Can I use a free email marketing plan for cold outreach?

Generally no. Marketing platforms expect opted-in lists, and their acceptable-use policies typically prohibit purchased or scraped lists. Outbound needs different tools and processes.

Should an agency put a client on a free plan?

Only if the client owns the account and understands what happens at the cap. Disputes over who owns an account are harder to fix than a small monthly subscription.

What's the verdict?

For a broadcast-only newsletter, Kit's free plan lets you grow the furthest before paying, while Sender and EmailOctopus are the stronger free choices if you also need simple automation. MailerLite, Klaviyo and Mailchimp suit lists under 250, and Brevo suits large lists that are rarely emailed.

Whichever you pick, note the upgrade trigger now and document your automations from the start.
