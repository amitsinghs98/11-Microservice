Which customer knowledge base software should you choose?

Use the help center built into your support platform unless you need versioning, complex permissions or many languages, because native help centers connect articles to tickets, bots and reporting with no extra integration. Choose a dedicated knowledge tool such as Document360 or Helpjuice when knowledge has its own owner and the structure outgrows what your help desk offers.

  • A portal in the same suite as your tickets: Zendesk help center.
  • Answers inside a SaaS messenger: Intercom's help center.
  • Small email-first support teams: Help Scout Docs.
  • Freshworks or HubSpot customers: the knowledge base in Freshdesk or HubSpot Service Hub.
  • Dedicated knowledge teams with versions and workflows: Document360.
  • Heavily branded, customized portals: Helpjuice.
  • Developer and API documentation: GitBook.

What separates a customer self-service portal from an internal wiki?

A customer portal is written for people who don't know your product's vocabulary, must be findable through search engines and in-product search, and is judged by whether it prevents tickets. An internal wiki is written for colleagues, sits behind login and is judged by whether staff find process answers quickly.

The same software can sometimes do both, but the structure shouldn't be shared. Customer articles follow tasks ("How do I export invoices?"), while internal pages follow processes and policies. If you're building the internal side, our guides to knowledge base software for marketing operations and SOP documentation tools cover that job.

A customer portal also overlaps with training. Structured courses and certifications belong in a customer education platform, while the knowledge base handles the quick, searchable answer.

What does each knowledge base tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Native help centers are strongest at connecting articles to tickets, bots and agent workflows. Dedicated tools are strongest at structure, review workflows and versions, but need integration work to feed your support channels.

Zendesk help center: best for portals tied to tickets

Zendesk's help center is its customer-facing portal and article management tool, part of the same suite as its tickets. That shared suite is the main reason to choose it, because articles and tickets live in one system; check which portal, community and multi-brand options your plan includes. Skip it if you don't use Zendesk for support; you'd be buying a suite for its portal.

Intercom help center: best for answers inside the messenger

Intercom's knowledge hub is its tool for publishing help content for customers and support teams. It fits SaaS teams that want self-service where customers already talk to them; confirm how articles surface in the messenger and to Fin on Intercom's official pages. Skip it if you need complex versioned documentation; it's built for support content first.

Help Scout Docs: best for small email-first teams

Help Scout offers Docs as its knowledge base for teams that support customers mainly by email. Check on its pricing page whether Docs is in your plan and how articles reach customers before they write in. Skip it if you need granular permissions, approval workflows or article versioning; confirm these before buying.

Freshdesk knowledge base: best for Freshworks users

Freshdesk's portal and knowledge base sit in the same product as its tickets, which keeps articles and support conversations in one vendor. Check multilingual options, multiple portals and AI features by plan on the Freshdesk pricing page. Skip it if your support doesn't run on Freshworks.

HubSpot knowledge base: best when service runs on HubSpot

HubSpot's knowledge base is part of its Service Hub, alongside tickets and contact records in the HubSpot CRM. It suits teams that want one CRM view of support. Confirm which Service Hub tier includes the knowledge base before planning around it. Skip it if you'd be upgrading HubSpot only for documentation.

Document360: best for dedicated knowledge teams

Document360 is a standalone knowledge base platform aimed at teams that run documentation as its own product. It's a candidate when a documentation owner needs structure and review workflows; confirm versioning, workflow and public or private knowledge base options, and current tiers, on its pricing page. Skip it if you don't want to maintain an integration with your help desk.

Helpjuice: best for customized portals

Helpjuice focuses on a customizable knowledge base, aimed at teams that want the portal to look and behave exactly as designed. That flexibility needs someone to build and maintain templates. See its official pricing for current plans. Skip it if you'd rather have a portal that works with default settings.

GitBook: best for developer documentation

GitBook is built for product and API documentation, and it suits technical audiences more than a general support portal does. Check current plans and its Git and editing features on GitBook’s pricing page. Skip it if most of your customers are non-technical users asking account and billing questions.

How should you structure a customer help center?

Structure a help center around the jobs customers are trying to do, in the order they meet them, and keep it to two levels deep. Good search can't rescue articles that are titled with internal feature names nobody types.

  1. Start from tickets: group the last few months of tickets by intent and write the top intents first.
  2. Use task titles: "How do I add a user?" rather than "User management".
  3. Limit categories: getting started, account and billing, core workflows, integrations, troubleshooting.
  4. Assign owners and review dates: every article has one person responsible and a date to recheck it.
  5. Close the loop: add article feedback and review searches that return nothing.

Does a knowledge base really reduce support tickets?

Vendors describe ticket deflection as a core benefit, but the effect depends on whether articles match real questions and whether customers can find them at the moment of need. Treat any deflection figure, including your own, with caution until you've checked it against ticket volume per customer.

How do you measure self-service deflection without fooling yourself?

Measure deflection as a change in tickets per active customer, alongside searches that end in a ticket, rather than counting article views. Views tell you about interest; they don't show anyone stopped needing help.

Three signals together give an honest view: the share of searches with no result, the share of article views followed by a contact within the same session, and article ratings with comments. Your help desk or knowledge tool should expose at least the first two; ask the vendor to show you where.

What else do buyers ask about customer knowledge base software?

The recurring questions cover deflection, internal wikis, AI search and migration. Check every plan-specific detail on the vendor's own pricing page.

Can one tool serve both our public help center and our internal wiki?

Some can, using permissions to hide internal articles, but the audiences need different structures and tone. Many teams keep the public help center in their support tool and run a separate internal wiki.

How do we measure ticket deflection honestly?

Compare ticket volume per active customer before and after changes, and track searches that end in a ticket. Article views alone don't prove anyone avoided contacting you.

Do we need a custom domain for our help center?

It's worth having for trust and for search visibility, because a help center on your own subdomain reads as part of your product. Check whether the plan you're considering includes custom domains and SSL.

Should AI answers replace knowledge base articles?

No. AI answers draw on your articles, so weak or outdated articles produce weak AI answers. Treat the knowledge base as the source of truth and the AI layer as a way of delivering it.

How hard is it to migrate a knowledge base?

Article text usually exports cleanly, but categories, redirects, images, translations and internal links take real effort. Ask the new vendor what its import supports and plan redirects for every old URL.

What's the verdict on customer knowledge base software?

For most small and mid-sized teams, the help center already in your support platform is the right starting point, as long as someone owns the content. Move to a dedicated tool when versioning, permissions or localization become the bottleneck, not because the editor looks nicer.
