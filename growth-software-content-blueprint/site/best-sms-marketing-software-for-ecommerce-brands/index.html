Best SMS Marketing Software for Ecommerce Brands

The right SMS marketing software for ecommerce is the one whose consent capture, message pricing and store integration still make sense when your list doubles. Postscript and Attentive are SMS-first, Klaviyo and Omnisend combine SMS with email, and Brevo and Twilio cover budget and build-your-own needs.

Which SMS platform should you choose?

Choose Klaviyo or Omnisend if you want SMS and email on one customer profile; choose Postscript if you're a Shopify brand that wants an SMS specialist; choose Attentive if SMS is a primary revenue channel and you want strategy support at scale. Brevo suits price-sensitive stores sending occasional campaigns, and Twilio suits teams building transactional texts into their own systems.

  • One profile for email and SMS: Klaviyo.
  • Email plus SMS on a smaller budget: Omnisend.
  • Shopify brands wanting an SMS specialist: Postscript.
  • Large brands where SMS leads: Attentive.
  • Occasional campaigns with prepaid credits: Brevo.
  • Custom transactional messaging: Twilio.

Who is this list for, and how is SMS different from email?

It's for ecommerce teams adding text messaging to an existing email programme, or replacing an SMS app that no longer fits. SMS differs from email in three ways that matter for buying: every message has a direct cost, consent rules are stricter and vary by country, and recipients tolerate far fewer sends.

Because of that, the cheapest platform per message isn't always the cheapest programme. Flows that suppress customers who just bought, quiet-hour controls and a single unsubscribe status across channels usually save more than a lower rate.

What are the strengths and limits of each SMS platform?

Combined platforms win on shared consent and suppression; specialists win on SMS-specific features and support. Developer platforms win on control and lose on everything a marketer needs out of the box.

Klaviyo: best for one profile across email and SMS

Klaviyo runs SMS from the same profiles, segments and flows as email, so a purchase can stop both channels at once. SMS is billed separately from email; check the meter and regional availability on its pricing page. Limitations: two usage meters make cost forecasting harder. Skip it if you only need SMS.

Omnisend: best for both channels on a smaller budget

Omnisend runs email and SMS from one platform aimed at small and mid-sized stores, with prebuilt ecommerce automations. Limitations: whether SMS credits are included or sold as an add-on depends on your plan, so confirm it before you commit. Skip it if you run complex, data-heavy flows and haven't checked its segmentation against them. Plans are on its pricing page.

Postscript: best for Shopify brands wanting an SMS specialist

Postscript is SMS-first and Shopify-focused. Its pricing page describes plans built on per-message rates, with a platform fee on most tiers, and its help center explains that carrier fees are billed separately from the message rate, that international sends are priced differently, and that usage is charged when you reach a billing threshold or month end.

Strengths: SMS-specific tools and itemised usage billing. Limitations: email stays in another tool, so sync unsubscribes carefully. Skip it if you're not on Shopify.

Attentive: best for large brands where SMS leads

Attentive offers SMS and email for brands where text messaging is a primary channel. Ask for pricing and service terms through its official site. Limitations: contract terms and minimums need careful review. Skip it if SMS is still an experiment.

Brevo: best for occasional campaigns

Brevo sells SMS as prepaid credits alongside its email plans, which suits stores sending a handful of promotions rather than many automated flows. Limitations: ecommerce triggers are less developed than the specialists'. Skip it if abandoned-cart texts are your main use case. See Brevo pricing.

Twilio: best for custom transactional messages

Twilio is a messaging API: you pay per message and per phone number, and your developers build the sending logic. It suits order and delivery notifications sent from your own systems. Limitations: no campaign builder, consent capture or segmentation. Skip it if marketers need to run campaigns themselves. Rates are on Twilio's SMS pricing page.

What does ecommerce SMS really cost?

Total SMS cost is subscribers times sends per month times the per-message rate, plus carrier fees, number fees and any platform fee, and MMS or international texts raise it further. Model it before you sign, because frequency drives cost directly.

A worked illustration (hypothetical, no vendor rates): a brand with 10,000 SMS subscribers sending four campaigns a month sends 40,000 campaign messages, before flows. If abandoned-cart and shipping texts add a quarter on top, you're at 50,000 messages. Multiply by each vendor's current per-message and carrier rates, then add the platform fee. Halving campaign frequency halves the biggest line.

Also ask whether long messages are billed as several segments, whether inbound replies are charged and what a dedicated short code or toll-free number costs.

Do SMS subscribers tolerate frequent sends?

Tolerance varies by brand, product and audience, and vendors publish benchmarks that suit their pricing model. The only reliable guide is your own unsubscribe and complaint rate by send frequency.

What compliance questions should you ask vendors?

Ask vendors how their tools support consent capture, record keeping and opt-out handling in each country you send to, then confirm the legal requirements with your own counsel. This page doesn't give legal advice, and rules differ by country and state.

  • How is consent captured, timestamped and stored per subscriber, and can you export that record?
  • Are opt-out keywords handled automatically, and does an opt-out sync to every connected tool?
  • Can you set quiet hours by recipient time zone?
  • Who handles sender registration (for example, US 10DLC or toll-free verification), and how long does it take?
  • Which countries can you send to, and do rules or rates change by country?
  • How are transactional and marketing messages separated?

What mistakes do ecommerce teams make when buying SMS software?

The most common mistake is comparing per-message rates and ignoring frequency, carrier fees and contract minimums. The second is running SMS and email on separate tools without syncing suppression, so a customer gets both messages after buying.

  • Launching without a frequency cap.
  • Texting abandoned carts without suppressing completed orders; our guide to abandoned cart recovery platforms covers trigger logic.
  • Trusting platform-attributed revenue without a holdout group.
  • Collecting phone numbers without a clear consent statement at the point of capture.
  • Leaving SMS profiles out of your customer data platform, so audiences differ by channel.

What else do ecommerce teams ask about SMS marketing software?

These are the follow-up questions buyers raise once pricing is clear. Short answers follow.

Is MMS worth the extra cost?

Sometimes, for product launches where an image sells. Test it on a segment, because MMS usually costs more per message than plain SMS.

Can I move my SMS subscribers to a new platform?

Usually, if you can export consent records with timestamps and sources. Confirm the new vendor accepts them and how sender registration transfers.

Should transactional texts go through my marketing SMS tool?

They can, but keep them clearly separated from promotions. Some brands send order updates through a developer platform and marketing through their campaign tool.

Does SMS work outside the US?

Yes, but rates, sender types and consent rules change by country. Check each vendor's supported countries before you collect numbers abroad.

What's the verdict?

Most small and mid-sized stores are best served by adding SMS to their email platform, with Klaviyo or Omnisend, so consent and suppression live on one profile. Shopify brands that want SMS specialism should look at Postscript, larger SMS-led brands at Attentive, and teams building notifications at Twilio.
