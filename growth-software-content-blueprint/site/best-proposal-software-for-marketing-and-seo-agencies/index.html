Best Proposal Software for Marketing and SEO Agencies

The right proposal software for marketing agencies turns a scope, a set of pricing options and a signature into one document the client can approve without a call. PandaDoc and Proposify suit agencies that send many proposals and want controls; Qwilr and Better Proposals suit agencies that sell on presentation; Ignition and Bonsai suit owners who want the signed proposal to start billing.

Which proposal tool should your agency choose?

Choose by what happens after the client says yes: if a signed proposal should flow into a CRM deal, pick PandaDoc or Proposify; if it should start invoicing, pick Ignition or Bonsai. If your proposals win on design and storytelling, Qwilr or Better Proposals will feel more natural.

  • Agencies sending high volumes with CRM sync and approval rules: PandaDoc.
  • Sales-led agencies that want template governance and pipeline views: Proposify.
  • Web-page proposals with interactive pricing: Qwilr.
  • Small agencies that want quick, attractive proposals with less setup: Better Proposals.
  • Retainer agencies that want proposal, engagement terms and billing in one flow: Ignition.
  • Freelancers and two-person studios that want proposals, contracts and invoices together: Bonsai.
  • Agencies already living in HubSpot that only need quotes: HubSpot quotes in Sales Hub.

None of these is a universal winner. A ten-person SEO shop and a fifty-person full-service agency need different things from the same document.

Is proposal software the same as CPQ or a contract suite?

No: proposal software is built to persuade and get a signature, while CPQ (configure, price, quote) tools enforce complex product catalogs and discount rules, and contract suites manage negotiation, redlines and obligations after signing. Most marketing and SEO agencies need proposal software plus a simple e-signature step, not CPQ.

CPQ earns its keep when you sell hundreds of SKUs with dependent pricing, which is rare in services. A contract lifecycle suite becomes useful when legal teams redline master service agreements every week. If you're there, read our guide to RFP and proposal management software for B2B agencies, which covers formal bids and content libraries.

There's also a newer middle ground. Digital sales rooms bundle a proposal, case studies, stakeholder chat and a mutual action plan into one shared space, which helps when several people on the client side must agree. Our comparison of digital sales room software for complex deals explains when that's worth the extra setup.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

PandaDoc and Proposify lead on controls and integrations, Qwilr and Better Proposals lead on presentation, and Ignition and Bonsai lead on what happens after signature. The trade-off is usually depth in one of those areas at the cost of another.

PandaDoc: best for agencies sending many proposals through a CRM

PandaDoc positions itself as a document platform for proposals, quotes and contracts, with a content library, pricing tables, built-in e-signature and CRM integrations. For an agency, the appeal is consistency: account managers pull approved service descriptions and case studies from a library instead of copying last month's proposal. It's a good fit for teams that want approval steps before anything with a discount goes out. The limitation is breadth; it does many document jobs, so setup takes deliberate work. Skip it if you send three proposals a month. Check which features sit on which tier on the PandaDoc pricing page.

Proposify: best for sales-led agencies that want template governance

Proposify focuses on proposals specifically, with locked template sections, reusable content, interactive pricing, e-signature and a pipeline view of proposal status. That focus suits agencies with a dedicated new-business lead who wants to stop junior staff from rewriting scope language. It's less of a general document tool than PandaDoc. Consider another option if you mainly need contracts and forms rather than sales proposals. Plans are on the Proposify pricing page.

Qwilr: best for proposals that read like a web page

Qwilr builds proposals as interactive web pages rather than paginated documents, with embedded video, configurable pricing and acceptance built in. For an SEO agency, that format makes it easy to embed a short audit walkthrough or a sample report next to the price. The limitation is that some procurement teams still want a PDF to file, so check export options. Skip it if most of your clients are formal enterprises with fixed procurement steps. See the Qwilr pricing page.

Better Proposals: best for small agencies that want speed

Better Proposals offers designed templates, e-signature, payment collection through connected gateways and notifications when a client opens a document. It's a good fit for a small agency that wants a professional-looking proposal out the door the same day. In our assessment, its governance and approval controls are lighter than PandaDoc's or Proposify's. Skip it if several managers need sign-off before a proposal is sent. Plans are on the Better Proposals pricing page.

Ignition: best for retainer agencies that want proposals to start billing

Ignition combines proposals, engagement terms and automated payment collection, so a signed retainer can trigger recurring invoices without re-keying. That joined-up flow is its main argument, and it matters most for agencies whose revenue is mostly monthly retainers. Its proposals are more structured and less design-led than Qwilr's. Consider another option if you mostly sell one-off projects with bespoke creative pitches. Read the Ignition pricing page for plan and payment-processing terms, and our guide to retainer billing software for what happens after signature.

Bonsai: best for freelancers and very small studios

Bonsai bundles proposals, contracts, invoicing and light client management for independent consultants and small teams. The benefit is having one tool instead of four when you're the person who sells, delivers and bills. The limitation is depth: larger agencies will outgrow its approval and reporting features. Skip it if you have a separate sales function. See the Bonsai pricing page.

HubSpot quotes: best if your agency already runs on HubSpot

HubSpot's Sales Hub includes quotes that pull line items and contacts from your CRM deals. That avoids another subscription if your proposals are short and price-led. Quotes aren't persuasive documents, though, so many agencies pair them with a separate proposal tool. Skip this route if your proposals depend on narrative, case studies and options. Plan details are on the HubSpot Sales Hub pricing page, and our review of CRM software for marketing agencies covers the CRM decision itself.

What should a monthly SEO retainer proposal contain?

A strong SEO retainer proposal states the business problem, the monthly deliverables, what's excluded, how success is reported and what the client must provide. Price options and terms come after the client agrees with the diagnosis, not before.

A workable outline, which most of the tools above can hold as a reusable template:

  1. Situation: two or three findings from your initial audit, in the client's language, such as lost rankings on revenue pages.
  2. Objectives: outcomes for the first 90 days and the first year, framed as leading indicators you control and lagging ones you influence.
  3. Monthly scope: technical fixes, content briefs or articles, link acquisition approach and reporting cadence, each with a quantity or time allowance.
  4. Exclusions: development work, paid content placement, translation or anything else the client might assume is included.
  5. Pricing options: two or three tiers as optional line items, so the client chooses scope rather than haggling over one number.
  6. Client responsibilities: CMS access, approvals within a set number of days and a named contact.
  7. Reporting: what the monthly report covers and how changes to scope are agreed.
  8. Terms and signature: minimum term, notice period, payment method and the signature block.

Do proposal analytics actually help you close?

Open and time-on-page alerts tell you when a client is reading, which helps you time a follow-up, but they don't explain why a deal stalls. Treat them as a prompt to call, not as a measure of interest.

What mistakes do agencies make when buying proposal software?

The most common mistake is buying for design and ignoring what happens after signature, which leaves someone re-typing the scope into the CRM and the invoicing tool. The second is letting every account manager build their own templates, which undoes the consistency you paid for.

  • Pricing the entry tier: check which tier includes e-signature, CRM sync or payment collection, and price the plan you'll need.
  • Skipping a content library: without approved scope blocks, proposals drift and promises multiply.
  • No owner: someone must retire outdated case studies and pricing, or the library becomes a liability.
  • Ignoring the billing hand-off: check that line items reach your invoicing software without manual work.
  • Treating every deal the same: a five-figure retainer deserves a tailored proposal; a small add-on may only need a quote.

What else do agencies ask about proposal software?

Most questions are about signatures, payments and templates. The answers below are general; confirm specifics on each vendor's official pages.

Do proposal tools include legally binding e-signatures?

Most proposal tools include an e-signature step with an audit trail of who signed and when. Whether a signature is enforceable for a particular contract depends on your jurisdiction, so take legal advice for anything unusual.

Can clients pay a deposit when they accept a proposal?

Several tools can collect a payment at acceptance through a connected payment gateway. Check which gateways and currencies each vendor supports and what processing fees apply on the official pricing page.

Should proposals and contracts be separate documents?

Many agencies sign a master services agreement once and then use proposals or statements of work for each engagement. That keeps legal terms stable while scope changes, and it shortens every later proposal.

How many proposal templates does an agency need?

Start with one template per core service, such as SEO retainers, paid media management and website projects. Add variants only when the scope or buyer genuinely differs.

Is a CRM's built-in quote tool enough?

A CRM quote is enough for short, price-led renewals and add-ons. For new business, where the narrative and options do the selling, a dedicated proposal tool usually does a better job.

What's the verdict?

Pick PandaDoc or Proposify if you need volume, approvals and CRM sync, Qwilr or Better Proposals if presentation wins your deals, and Ignition or Bonsai if you want the signed proposal to start billing. Confirm the tier that includes e-signature, integrations and payments before you commit.
