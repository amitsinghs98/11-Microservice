Which sales compensation management software should a B2B team choose?

Teams with a small number of payees and simple plans are usually best served by QuotaPath, which publishes its pricing; Salesforce-centered teams should look at Salesforce Spiff first. CaptivateIQ and Everstage suit growing teams with several plan types, and Xactly and Varicent are built for enterprises that also manage territories and quotas centrally.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • Small teams that want published pricing: QuotaPath.
  • Salesforce-native organizations: Salesforce Spiff.
  • Growing teams with many plan variations: CaptivateIQ.
  • Per-payee pricing and guided onboarding: Everstage.
  • Enterprise incentive programs: Xactly Incent.
  • Enterprises joining comp with territory and quota planning: Varicent.

What does compensation software do, and where does payroll take over?

Sales compensation software calculates what each rep has earned under their plan, using CRM bookings, quotas, accelerators, draws and SPIFFs, then routes the result for approval and shows reps their statements. Payroll pays the approved amount, handles tax and deductions, and records it; the comp tool doesn't replace it.

Plan design sits before both. Deciding on-target earnings, pay mix, quota and accelerator thresholds is a leadership and finance decision; the software models and enforces the result. Treat any tool that promises to "design" your plan as a modeling aid, not a substitute for that conversation. And if a customer data platform or enrichment tool can rewrite account ownership in your CRM, lock that field down before commissions depend on it.

The data flows matter more than the interface. Bookings come from the CRM, quotas from planning, and payouts go to payroll or the ERP, so read our guides to CRM for B2B SaaS and designing a RevOps data model before you choose.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The order follows our criteria for B2B teams: speed to a correct first payout, then how well each tool handles plan complexity and controls, then contract weight. Payment doesn't affect inclusion or order.

QuotaPath: small teams that want published pricing

QuotaPath publishes per-user plans alongside a monthly platform fee that covers an initial block of users, implementation and support, and offers a free trial. It handles commission tracking, rep-facing earnings views and payout workflows.

Pricing model: published per-user plans plus a platform fee; free trial.

Strengths: Transparent cost and quick setup for teams with a few plan types.

Limitations: Very complex enterprise rules, multi-entity payouts and heavy territory logic suit the enterprise tools better.

Skip it if: you run dozens of plan variants across regions and currencies.

Salesforce Spiff: Salesforce-centered teams

Salesforce Spiff calculates commissions from Salesforce data, with commission estimates on opportunities, a plan designer and audit trails. Its pricing page lists per-user pricing, as a standalone add-on or within Salesforce suites, with connectors to non-Salesforce systems priced separately.

Pricing model: per user per month billed annually; connectors to non-Salesforce systems cost extra.

Strengths: Native to Salesforce, so reps see expected earnings where they work deals.

Limitations: Teams on other CRMs pay for connectors and lose the native advantage.

Skip it if: your CRM isn't Salesforce.

CaptivateIQ: growing teams with many plan variations

CaptivateIQ prices per seat, covering admins and every payee, plus a one-time setup fee, with ASC 606 reporting sold as a separate add-on.

Pricing model: per seat plus one-time setup fee; quote-based.

Strengths: Positioned for flexible plan logic, which matters as plan variations multiply.

Limitations: No public price, and flexibility means someone must own and document the logic.

Skip it if: your plans are simple enough for a published-price tool.

Everstage: per-payee pricing with guided onboarding

Everstage quotes per payee, with the quote split into platform license, support and onboarding. It says there is no enforced seat minimum but positions the platform for teams with a sizeable payee count. Its pages describe ASC 606 support inconsistently (a scoped add-on on the pricing page, built in elsewhere), so confirm it in the quote.

Pricing model: per-payee annual quote including onboarding.

Strengths: Clear cost driver (payees), with onboarding itemized in the quote.

Limitations: Quote-only, and small teams sit below its stated sweet spot.

Skip it if: you pay commissions to only a handful of people.

Xactly Incent: enterprise incentive programs

Xactly Incent automates complex incentive compensation for large sales organizations, with plan administration, payout accuracy controls and reporting.

Pricing model: quote-based through a demo request.

Strengths: Built for large, complex incentive programs with enterprise controls.

Limitations: Implementation is a project, and pricing isn't public.

Skip it if: you need a first payout within weeks with a lean RevOps team.

Varicent: compensation plus territory and quota planning

Varicent's ICM software schedule sets charges on a per-entitlement basis, with add-ons priced separately. It pairs incentive compensation with sales planning for territories, quotas and capacity.

Pricing model: per-entitlement enterprise contract; quote-based.

Strengths: One vendor for planning territories and quotas and paying on them.

Limitations: Enterprise scope and cost; more than most growing teams need.

Skip it if: your territories and quotas are set in a spreadsheet once a year.

How much effort does it really take to get the first payout right?

Vendors describe implementation timelines from their own experience, but the effort depends mostly on how clean your CRM data is and how many plan exceptions you have. Run at least one parallel payout cycle against your current spreadsheet before you switch.

What does an example sales compensation plan look like in software?

A typical account executive plan has an on-target earnings split between base and variable pay, a quota, a commission rate on bookings up to quota, accelerators above it, and rules for draws, clawbacks and SPIFFs. The software needs every one of those rules written down before it can calculate them.

The plan below is hypothetical and for illustration only; it isn't a recommendation for your team.

  • Role: mid-market account executive, annual quota set by finance.
  • Pay mix: 50/50 base to variable at target.
  • Base rate: variable target divided by quota, paid on new annual contract value up to 100% of quota.
  • Accelerator: 1.5 times the base rate on bookings above quota.
  • Draw: recoverable draw for the first two months of a new hire's ramp.
  • Clawback: commission recovered if a customer cancels within a stated period after signing.
  • SPIFF: fixed bonus for multi-year deals during one quarter, approved in advance by sales and finance.
  • Payout timing: monthly, after the booking is marked closed-won and the contract is countersigned.

Each line maps to a field or rule: closed-won date, contract value, cancellation date, deal term. Missing fields are a frequent reason calculations fail, which is why RevOps analytics tools and clean CRM data come first.

What controls should cover corrections and disputes?

Every change to a calculated payout should have a requester, a reason, an approver and a timestamp, and reps should be able to raise a dispute against a specific line on their statement. Without that audit trail, commission errors become trust problems.

  1. Lock periods: close each pay period after approval; corrections go into the next period as adjustments.
  2. Two-person approval: manager plus finance approve adjustments above a threshold you set.
  3. Dispute workflow: reps raise disputes in the tool, with a target response time.
  4. Plan change log: version every plan and record when each version took effect.
  5. Payroll reconciliation: match approved totals to payroll each period.
  6. Accrual forecast: share projected payouts with finance, using pipeline from your sales forecasting software.

What mistakes do teams make when buying compensation software?

The commonest mistake is automating an undocumented plan. The second is underestimating implementation, which often costs more time than the license costs money.

  • Ambiguous crediting rules: split deals, renewals and expansion need explicit rules.
  • No parallel run: switching without a side-by-side cycle hides calculation errors.
  • Forgetting accounting: ask whether commission capitalization support (ASC 606) is included or an add-on.
  • Seat counting surprises: confirm whether admins, managers and payees are all billed.
  • Treating it as a payroll replacement: payroll still pays, taxes and records the money.

What else do buyers ask about sales compensation management software?

Most questions are about when to leave spreadsheets and how pricing works. Short answers follow.

When should a team move commissions out of spreadsheets?

Usually when plan variations multiply, disputes rise or finance can't trust accruals. A team with a handful of reps on one plan may not need software yet.

Does sales compensation software pay reps?

No. It calculates and approves earnings and produces statements; payroll or your payment system pays the money and handles tax.

What is a SPIFF?

A short-term incentive for a specific behavior, such as selling a product or closing multi-year deals in a quarter. It should be approved and documented like any other plan rule.

How are compensation tools usually priced?

Per user or per payee, often with a platform or setup fee and annual contracts. Some vendors publish prices; enterprise vendors usually quote.

What should we ask in a demo?

Ask the vendor to calculate a real month of your commissions, show the audit trail for a correction and walk through a rep dispute end to end.
