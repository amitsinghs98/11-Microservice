Which ecommerce platform should a growing DTC brand choose?

Pick Shopify if speed to market and app choice matter most, BigCommerce if you want more built-in catalog depth and fewer apps, and WooCommerce if you need to own the hosting and code. Look at Shopify Plus, Adobe Commerce, Salesforce Commerce Cloud or commercetools only when you have the budget, the team and a requirement the mid-market platforms can't meet.

  • Fastest launch, largest app and theme ecosystem: Shopify.
  • High-volume brands that want to stay on Shopify: Shopify Plus.
  • Larger or more complex catalogs with fewer add-ons: BigCommerce.
  • Brands that want full code and hosting control on WordPress: WooCommerce.
  • Complex catalogs and custom logic with an in-house or agency dev team: Adobe Commerce.
  • Multi-brand, multi-region retailers already in the Salesforce ecosystem: Salesforce Commerce Cloud.
  • Engineering-led brands building a headless, API-first stack: commercetools.
  • Small catalogs where the brand site matters more than commerce depth: Squarespace.

What problem does a platform decision actually solve for a DTC brand?

A platform decides what you can change without a developer: the storefront, the checkout, how products and variants are modelled, and how you sell across currencies. It also sets a cost floor (subscription, payment and transaction fees, apps and hosting) that grows with every order you take.

For a DTC brand the platform is also where most of your growth tools plug in. Email, reviews, search, subscriptions and loyalty all read the same product, order and customer data, so a platform with a thin API or awkward data model makes every later tool harder to run. That's why this page weighs APIs and data access as heavily as themes.

What is each platform good at, and when should you skip it?

Each block covers the best-fit buyer, the main strength and the reason to look elsewhere. Confirm plan names, fees and feature availability on the official pages, because they change more often than platform positioning does.

Shopify: best for brands that want to launch and iterate quickly

Shopify is the default shortlist entry for DTC brands because the hosted checkout, theme editor and app store remove most of the build. Your payment choice affects the fee stack, so check the vendor's pricing page for whether transaction fees change when you use a third-party payment provider instead of Shopify's own payments, and model both before choosing a gateway. The limitation is app dependence. Subscriptions, advanced bundles, loyalty and many merchandising jobs are app-based, and each app adds a monthly fee, a script and a data copy. Skip it if you need deep control of checkout logic or back-end data structures from day one.

Shopify Plus: best for high-volume brands that want to stay on Shopify

Shopify Plus is Shopify's enterprise tier, aimed at higher-volume brands that have outgrown the standard plans but want to keep the Shopify ecosystem. Confirm on the official page which checkout, multi-store and support features the tier adds. It's sales-led, so get the contract terms in writing and ask whether any part of the fee scales with your sales. Consider another option if your blocker is the product data model rather than scale, because Plus doesn't change that.

BigCommerce: best for larger catalogs that would otherwise need several apps

BigCommerce positions itself around open APIs and headless options, and is often shortlisted by brands that would rather have catalog features built in than added through apps. Confirm which catalog, customer-group and B2B features each plan includes if you have many variants or plan to sell wholesale alongside DTC. The ecosystem of themes, apps and agencies is smaller than Shopify's, which can mean fewer ready-made integrations for niche tools. Check on the official pricing page whether plan placement depends on your online sales, because that decides when you're moved up a tier.

WooCommerce: best for brands that want to own the code

WooCommerce is an open-source commerce plugin for WordPress, so you choose the host, the payment gateway and every extension. That gives the most control of any option here and keeps content and commerce in one CMS. The cost is responsibility: hosting performance, security patches, plugin conflicts and upgrades are yours or your agency's. Skip it if nobody on the team can maintain WordPress safely.

Adobe Commerce: best for complex catalogs with dev capacity

Adobe Commerce, built on Magento, handles complicated catalogs, multiple storefronts and custom business logic. It's a strong fit when your requirements are genuinely unusual and you have a long-term development partner. It isn't a fit for a lean DTC team, because upgrades, extensions and performance tuning need ongoing engineering time.

Salesforce Commerce Cloud: best for multi-brand retailers on Salesforce

Salesforce Commerce Cloud targets larger retailers running several brands and regions, often alongside Salesforce service and marketing products. Its value rises with how much of that ecosystem you already use. Most growing DTC brands are better served by a mid-market platform until they have the volume and the internal team to run an implementation partner.

commercetools: best for engineering-led headless builds

commercetools is an API-first commerce back end: you pick the front end, CMS, search and checkout pieces and connect them. That's powerful for brands with a product and engineering team, and painful for anyone else, because you build much of what Shopify gives you out of the box. If you go this way, pair it with one of the headless CMS platforms built for omnichannel content.

Squarespace: best for small catalogs and brand-led sites

Squarespace suits a founder selling a small range where design and content matter more than merchandising depth. It's quick to run and keeps the site in one tool. Skip it if you expect a large catalog, several markets or a growth stack of specialist apps, because the integration options are narrower.

Which features and fees matter most as a DTC brand grows?

Fees compound with order volume, so the payment, transaction and app costs matter more than the headline plan price. On the feature side, checkout control, subscriptions, multi-currency and staff permissions are the four that most often force a migration.

  • Payment and transaction fees: separate the payment processor's rate from any platform transaction fee, and check whether using your own gateway changes the platform fee.
  • Themes and storefront control: check how much of the product page, cart and checkout you can change without code, and whether the theme language is one your agency knows.
  • APIs: confirm rate limits, webhooks and access to orders, customers and inventory, because every growth tool depends on them.
  • Subscriptions: native support or an app; either way, check who owns the payment tokens if you later switch. Our subscription commerce software guide covers the app side.
  • Multi-currency and markets: local pricing, duties, translated content and domain structure per market.
  • Roles: granular staff permissions matter once freelancers, agencies and support staff share the admin.
  • Search and feeds: native search is often basic, and shopping channels need clean product data, so budget for onsite search software and product feed management early.

What does each platform really cost for a small store and a multi-market store?

For a small catalog, payment processing and apps can easily cost more than the plan fee itself, so model all three together. For a multi-market store, implementation, localization and the extra apps or licences for each market usually outweigh the platform subscription.

The two models below are hypothetical and list cost lines, not prices, because no pricing page could be confirmed for this draft. Fill in each vendor's current figures from the official pages.

Scenario A: small catalog, one market

Assume around 150 products, one currency, a single storefront and a team of three. Your cost lines are the monthly plan, payment processing on each order, any platform transaction fee for a non-native gateway, a premium theme, and roughly four to six apps (reviews, email, search, subscriptions or bundles). On Shopify and Squarespace, apps are the swing factor. On WooCommerce, managed hosting and developer hours replace most subscription lines.

Scenario B: multi-market store

Assume around 2,000 SKUs, three currencies, two languages and an agency partner. Add lines for market-specific pricing and duties, translation, expansion stores or market settings, feed management for each country's shopping channels, a higher plan or enterprise contract, and agency retainers. This is the point where Shopify Plus, BigCommerce's upper tiers and Adobe Commerce become a fair comparison, because the platform fee is no longer the main cost.

How painful is migrating a live DTC store to a new platform?

Expect platform import tools, where they exist, to move products and core records but not everything. URLs, customer passwords, subscription payment tokens, app data and theme customizations are the usual gaps to plan for.

What mistakes do DTC brands make when choosing a platform?

The commonest mistake is comparing plan prices instead of the full fee stack at next year's order volume. The second is choosing an enterprise or headless platform before the team can run it.

  • Ignoring the gateway decision, then discovering a transaction fee on every order.
  • Installing an app for every gap without checking what the platform already does.
  • Assuming checkout can be customized freely on any plan.
  • Forgetting that subscription tokens may not move with you.
  • Treating multi-currency display as the same thing as true local pricing and duties.
  • Skipping a staff-roles audit until an agency needs admin access.

What else do buyers ask about ecommerce platforms for DTC brands?

Most questions are about fees, headless builds and when to upgrade. The short answers are below.

Is Shopify or BigCommerce better for a growing DTC brand?

Shopify usually wins on speed and app choice, and BigCommerce on built-in catalog features and fewer add-ons. Compare them on your own catalog, gateway and app list rather than on plan prices alone.

When does a DTC brand need an enterprise platform?

When volume, multi-brand operations or custom logic create requirements the mid-market tiers can't meet, and you have the team to run a longer implementation. Revenue alone isn't a reason to move.

Is headless commerce worth it for a small team?

Rarely. Headless gives front-end freedom but makes you responsible for the storefront, integrations and performance, which a small team usually can't sustain.

Do platform transaction fees still matter if I use the built-in payments?

They matter less, but you still pay payment processing on every order. Check the official pricing page for how fees change with your gateway and plan.

Can I sell in several currencies without a separate store?

Most platforms here offer some form of multi-currency or market settings. Check whether that covers local prices, duties and translated content, or only currency display.

What's the verdict on ecommerce platforms for DTC brands?

For most growing DTC brands, Shopify or BigCommerce is the right starting point, chosen on fee stack, catalog shape and how many apps you'd otherwise need. Move to Shopify Plus, Adobe Commerce, Salesforce Commerce Cloud or commercetools when a concrete requirement and a capable team justify it, not before.
