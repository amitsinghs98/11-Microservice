Which event integration setup should you choose?

Choose the event platform whose native connector writes registration, attendance and engagement status into the system your marketing operations team already trusts, whether that's HubSpot, Marketo Engage or Salesforce campaigns. If no native connector covers a status you need, add a general automation tool for that gap rather than replacing the platform.

  • Enterprise webinar programmes on Marketo or Eloqua: ON24 or Goldcast, both of which position themselves for enterprise B2B event marketing; confirm a native connector exists for your platform.
  • HubSpot-centred teams running regular webinars: Livestorm or Zoom Webinars, after confirming the current HubSpot connector and which plan includes it.
  • Conference and field event programmes: Cvent, which handles registration on the event management side; confirm which CRM and automation connectors your package includes.
  • Gaps, edge cases and smaller tools: Zapier, to move a status the native connector misses.

What should an event-to-automation integration actually sync?

At minimum it should sync four status changes for every person: registered, cancelled, attended (live) and watched on demand. B2B teams also need engagement data, such as questions asked, polls answered and minutes watched, so they can score and route attendees rather than sending everyone the same email.

Each status should land on a campaign or program member record, not just on the contact. That is what lets you report how many registrants became pipeline, and it is the difference between event reporting and event attribution. What does each integration do well, and where does it fall short?

In our editorial assessment ON24 and Goldcast are the first to check when engagement data drives scoring, Cvent when in-person registration data matters most, and Zoom Webinars and Livestorm for simpler, HubSpot-first stacks; Zapier is a patch, not a foundation. The details below show where each one needs checking.

ON24: best for engagement-led scoring

What it does: ON24 markets an enterprise webinar and digital events platform. ON24 publishes material on its engagement score and on a HubSpot integration; read both to confirm which inputs the score uses and what activity the connector writes to your contacts.

Limitations: confirm which engagement fields reach your automation platform and whether the score arrives as a field you can use in lead scoring. Prices were not confirmed for this draft; check ON24's site.

Skip it if you run a handful of webinars a year and only need attended/no-show status.

Goldcast: best for B2B event teams that report on accounts

What it does: Goldcast publishes an integrations page; check that your CRM and automation platform are listed before you shortlist it. It positions itself for B2B event marketing teams.

Limitations: ask how quickly attendance and engagement sync after a large event and how sync failures are surfaced. Prices were not confirmed for this draft; check Goldcast's site.

Skip it if you need in-person conference registration as well; pair it with a registration tool or look at Cvent.

Cvent: best for conferences and field events

What it does: Cvent sells event management and registration software; ask which CRM and automation connectors carry registration and attendance data from it.

Limitations: complex registration types need careful mapping to campaign member statuses, or every attendee looks the same in your CRM. Plan the mapping before the first event, not after.

Skip it if your programme is webinar-only.

Zoom Webinars: best for teams already on Zoom

What it does: Zoom Webinars is Zoom's webinar product; check Zoom's own pages for how it is licensed and which CRM and automation connectors are currently available.

Limitations: check how much engagement detail, beyond registration and attendance, each connector passes through.

Skip it if engagement-based scoring is central to your follow-up.

Livestorm: best for HubSpot-centred webinar teams

What it does: Livestorm's HubSpot integration page describes how Livestorm connects to HubSpot; confirm there which registrant, attendee and replay data sync before you build workflows on it.

Limitations: confirm which plan includes the connector and whether Marketo or Salesforce campaign sync is native or needs a third-party tool.

Skip it if you run large multi-track events.

Zapier: best for filling gaps

What it does: Zapier connects apps through triggers and actions, so it can move a status that a native connector misses, such as a cancellation, into your automation platform.

Limitations: each extra hop is another place for silent failures. Usage-based pricing means high-volume events can consume more tasks than expected. Compare options in our Zapier vs Make vs n8n comparison.

Skip it if a native connector already covers every status you need.

How should you test an event integration before launch?

Run four test registrations through the full lifecycle, registration, cancellation, live attendance and replay view, and confirm each status lands on the right campaign member record with the right timestamp. Then break something deliberately and check that someone is alerted.

  1. Registration: new contact and existing contact; check deduplication and source fields.
  2. Cancellation: confirm reminders stop and the status changes.
  3. Attendance: join live; confirm attended status and engagement fields after the event ends.
  4. Replay: watch on demand; confirm a separate on-demand status rather than overwriting "no-show" silently.
  5. Error handling: submit a record with a missing required field and confirm where the error appears.

Segmentation and attribution depend on this test. If you need a wider data pipeline, our guide to marketing data integration platforms covers warehouse-based options.

Do event integrations really sync cleanly in practice?

Vendor integration pages describe what a connector can do, not how it behaves when a large registration wave arrives at once or a field mapping changes mid-campaign. Treat every connector as untested until your own end-to-end test passes.

What mistakes do teams make with event automation integrations?

The usual mistakes are syncing contacts without campaign membership, treating replay viewers as no-shows, and sending the same follow-up to everyone. Each one weakens both the attendee experience and your reporting.

  • No status model: agree registered, attended, on-demand and no-show statuses across every event tool.
  • Scoring on registration alone: registration signals interest; attendance and engagement signal intent.
  • Unowned errors: name the person who monitors sync failures.

What else do buyers ask about event automation integrations?

Buyers mostly ask about native connectors, campaign objects and replay tracking. Short answers follow.

Is a native connector always better than Zapier?

Usually, because native connectors are maintained by the vendor and map statuses directly. A general automation tool is useful for gaps but adds another point of failure.

Should event data go to the CRM or the marketing automation platform first?

Follow your existing data flow. Many teams sync to the automation platform, which then updates CRM campaigns, but avoid two tools writing the same field.

How do I track replay views separately from live attendance?

Create a distinct on-demand status and confirm your event platform sends replay activity, so replay viewers are not treated as no-shows.

What engagement data is worth syncing?

Attendance duration, questions asked, poll responses and resource clicks are the most useful for scoring and routing.

How often should we audit event integrations?

Check after every major event and at least quarterly, and whenever a vendor changes its connector or your field mapping changes.

What's the verdict on event marketing automation integrations?

For engagement-led scoring, check ON24 or Goldcast first; for conferences, Cvent; for HubSpot-first webinar programmes, Livestorm or Zoom Webinars; and Zapier only to close gaps. Whatever you pick, define campaign statuses first and test the full lifecycle before launch.
