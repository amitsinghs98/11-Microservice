Best GEO and AI Search Visibility Tools for Brand Monitoring

AI search visibility tools run a fixed set of prompts through assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI features, then report how often your brand is mentioned or cited. Dedicated trackers (Profound, Peec AI, Otterly.AI, Scrunch) give the most control; Semrush, Ahrefs and Writesonic suit teams that want this inside an existing tool. Which AI search visibility tool should you choose?

If AI answers are a board-level concern and you need citation analysis across many engines, shortlist Profound and Scrunch; if you want a self-serve tracker priced by prompt, start with Peec AI or Otterly.AI. If you already pay for Semrush, Ahrefs or Writesonic, test their visibility features first, because a second subscription only makes sense if theirs can't track your prompts.

  • Enterprise brand and comms teams: Profound or Scrunch.
  • Marketing teams wanting self-serve prompt tracking: Peec AI.
  • Small teams and agencies testing the category: Otterly.AI.
  • Existing Semrush customers: the AI Visibility Toolkit.
  • Ahrefs users wanting broad, modelled coverage: Brand Radar.
  • Content teams that also want drafting: Writesonic.

What are GEO and AEO, and what can't they promise?

GEO (generative engine optimization) and AEO (answer engine optimization) describe work aimed at getting a brand mentioned, recommended or cited in AI-generated answers. A visibility score summarizes a sample of those answers; it is not a ranking signal, and improving it doesn't guarantee traffic or revenue.

Assistant answers vary by wording, model version, location and session, and many answers send no click at all. That's why this category is monitoring, closer to brand tracking than to rank tracking. How did we choose these tools?

We compared prompt tracking, citation and source discovery, sentiment, competitor share of voice, geography and language controls, refresh cadence and repeatability. We favored tools that show the raw answers behind a score, because a number you can't audit is hard to defend to a client or a CFO.

How do these tools collect their data, and why does sampling matter?

There are two approaches: tracking a prompt set you write, which is precise but small, and querying a large modelled prompt database, which is broad but less tailored to your buyers. Either way the result is a sample, so its size, wording and refresh cadence decide how much a change in the score means.

  • Prompt-set design: mix unbranded category questions, comparisons, problem-led questions and branded checks. Branded prompts alone flatter you.
  • Repeatability: the same prompt can return different answers on consecutive runs. Ask how many times each prompt runs per cycle.
  • Geography and language: answers differ by country; confirm the tool can set location per prompt.
  • Engines: coverage of ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Google's AI results varies by tool and plan, and some engines are add-ons.
  • Citations versus mentions: a citation links to a source URL; a mention is your name in the text. Track both separately.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Each entry names the buyer it suits and a reason to skip it. Features come from product information reviewed; check every plan limit on the vendor's own page, because this category reprices often.

Profound: best for enterprise brand and comms teams

Profound tracks how answer engines mention and cite a brand, with sentiment, competitor benchmarking and source analysis, and adds tools for spotting prompts where competitors are cited instead of you (see Profound). Skip it if you're a small team that needs 30 prompts tracked weekly; a self-serve tool will do.

Scrunch: best for managing how AI describes a brand

Scrunch focuses on monitoring brand presence and the sources AI engines rely on, aimed at marketing and brand teams (see Scrunch). Skip it if you can't get written confirmation of engines, refresh cadence and prompt allowance for your plan.

Peec AI: best for self-serve prompt tracking

Peec AI prices by the number of prompts and AI models tracked, with competitor and source views (see Peec AI pricing). Skip it if you need every engine on day one; check which models are included and which cost extra.

Otterly.AI: best for testing the category cheaply

Otterly.AI monitors brand mentions and cited links for a set of search prompts across AI search engines, with plans scaled by prompt count (see Otterly.AI). Skip it if you need enterprise roles or multi-brand governance.

Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit: best for existing Semrush customers

Semrush sells AI visibility as a separate toolkit that combines prompt tracking with its keyword and competitor data (see Semrush pricing). Skip it if you track many client domains, since per-domain pricing adds up quickly; confirm the current basis before buying.

Ahrefs Brand Radar: best for a broad market view

Brand Radar searches a large database of AI responses to prompts that Ahrefs models from its search data, so you can compare brand mentions and citations across a market without writing prompts first. Skip it if you need precise tracking of the exact questions your buyers ask; check whether custom prompts are available on your plan.

Writesonic: best for content teams that want tracking and drafting

Writesonic positions itself as an AI search visibility platform with SEO content tools, and its pricing page lists 50, 100 and 200 tracked prompts on the Starter, Basic and Growth plans, with sentiment from Growth and the widest set of AI platforms on Enterprise (checked 4 November 2026). Skip it if you only want monitoring; you'd pay for writing features too. Our Writesonic alternatives guide compares other routes.

Can you trust a visibility score from one week to the next?

Vendors describe their sampling, but none of the documentation reviewed shows how much a score moves when nothing about the brand has changed. That baseline noise is what separates a real gain from a lucky sample.

How do you set up a practical 30-prompt monitoring program?

Write 30 prompts in four groups that mirror how buyers actually ask, fix the engines, country and cadence, and run two baseline cycles before acting on anything. Thirty is enough to show patterns without making review a full-time job.

  1. 10 unbranded category prompts: "best [category] for [audience]", "what [category] tools do agencies use".
  2. 8 comparison prompts: "[you] vs [competitor]", "alternatives to [competitor]".
  3. 6 problem-led prompts: the pain your product solves, phrased without naming any tool.
  4. 6 branded checks: pricing, reviews, limitations and "is [brand] good for [use case]", to catch outdated or wrong descriptions.
  5. Fix settings: the same engines, country, language and weekday for every run, and log any model update the vendor reports.
  6. Record per prompt: mentioned (yes/no), position in answer, cited URLs, sentiment and named competitors.
  7. Review monthly: act on prompts where a competitor is consistently cited from a source you could also earn.

How should you interpret AI visibility results?

Treat changes as signals to investigate, not wins to report, until they persist across several cycles and multiple engines. The most useful output is usually the list of cited sources, because it tells you which pages and publications shape the answers.

  • Don't compare scores across vendors; each samples differently.
  • Separate branded from unbranded prompts in every report.
  • Pair visibility with outcomes in your marketing analytics software, such as referral sessions from AI assistants and branded search trends.
  • Don't credit AI visibility with pipeline it can't prove; our guide to attribution for small teams without false certainty explains why.

What else do buyers ask about AI search visibility tools?

The recurring questions concern accuracy, overlap with SEO tools and how often to check. Short answers follow.

Is an AI visibility score a ranking factor?

No. It is a vendor's summary of sampled AI answers, useful for spotting trends and sources, but it does not feed into how any search engine or assistant ranks content.

How often should AI visibility be checked?

Weekly runs with a monthly review suit most brands, because daily changes are often sampling noise rather than real movement.

Can I monitor AI visibility without a paid tool?

You can run prompts by hand and log answers in a spreadsheet, but it is slow, hard to repeat consistently and limited to the engines you check yourself.

Do these tools work for agencies with many clients?

Some do, but check how pricing scales per domain, project or prompt, and whether reports can be shared or white-labeled before committing.

What's the verdict?

Use AI search visibility tools as monitoring, not as a scoreboard: Profound or Scrunch for enterprise programs, Peec AI or Otterly.AI for self-serve tracking, and the Semrush, Ahrefs or Writesonic options if you already pay for those platforms. Whatever you buy, design the prompt set yourself and judge vendors on how openly they show the raw answers.
