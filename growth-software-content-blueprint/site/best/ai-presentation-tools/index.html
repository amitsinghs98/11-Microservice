Which AI presentation tool should your team choose?

If your company already runs on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, start with the AI built into PowerPoint or Slides, because files stay native and IT has already approved them. Choose Beautiful.ai or Pitch when brand control across many reps matters most, and Gamma when fast, web-first drafts matter more than PowerPoint fidelity.

  • Native PowerPoint files with corporate templates: Microsoft Copilot in PowerPoint.
  • Google Workspace teams: Gemini in Google Slides, or Plus AI as an add-on for more structured generation.
  • Brand-locked decks for a large sales team: Beautiful.ai.
  • Collaborative decks with link sharing: Pitch.
  • Fast first drafts from a prompt or document: Gamma.
  • Design-led campaign decks: Canva.
  • Non-linear, conversational presenting: Prezi.

Do you need a slide generator, a design suite or the AI in your office suite?

Slide generators (Gamma, Plus AI) are fastest from prompt to draft, design suites (Canva, Beautiful.ai, Pitch) give the most layout and brand control, and office-suite AI (Copilot, Gemini) keeps files where your company already works. The right group depends on where the finished deck has to live.

If buyers expect a PowerPoint attachment, or legal needs a file in your document system, a web-first generator adds a conversion step every time. If decks are mostly shared as links and presented live, web-first tools give you view tracking and easier updates. General-purpose assistants can also draft decks: Anthropic's Claude pricing page lists Claude Slides on its Free and paid plans (checked 4 November 2026), and our ChatGPT vs Claude comparison for marketing workflows looks at that route.

What does each presentation tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The office-suite options are the safest default, while the specialist tools earn their seat only when they solve a problem your suite can't. Check the "skip it if" lines against how your decks are actually delivered.

Microsoft Copilot in PowerPoint: best for Microsoft 365 companies

Copilot can draft a presentation from a prompt or an existing Word document, add slides and summarize a deck, working in your organization's templates. It requires a Copilot license alongside Microsoft 365; see Microsoft's PowerPoint page for current availability.

Strengths: native files, existing permissions and corporate templates. Limitations: results are only as good as your master template, and design polish trails the design suites. Skip it if your company isn't on Microsoft 365.

Gemini in Google Slides: best for Google Workspace teams

Gemini in Slides helps generate slides, images and speaker-ready text inside the deck you're editing, within Workspace sharing and admin controls. Availability depends on your Workspace plan; see Google Slides.

Strengths: no new vendor to approve. Limitations: whole-deck generation is less structured than in dedicated generators. Skip it if you need a full deck from a long source document.

Plus AI: best for structured decks without leaving Slides or PowerPoint

Plus AI works as an add-in that generates and rewrites slides inside Google Slides and PowerPoint, so files stay native. Plans are per user; see Plus AI's site.

Strengths: generator speed with office-suite output. Limitations: another vendor with document access to review. Skip it if the built-in assistant already meets your needs.

Beautiful.ai: best for keeping a large sales team on brand

Beautiful.ai uses smart templates that adjust layout as content changes, with team themes and locked brand assets, plus AI generation. Plans are per user, with team tiers; see Beautiful.ai's site.

Strengths: reps can't easily break the design. Limitations: constrained layouts frustrate designers who want freedom. Skip it if a design team builds every deck by hand.

Pitch: best for collaborative, link-shared decks

Pitch combines real-time collaboration, AI deck generation, templates and shareable links with engagement analytics. See Pitch's site for plans and export options.

Strengths: clean shared workspace and link-based delivery. Limitations: confirm how decks look after PowerPoint export before you rely on it. Skip it if buyers insist on editable PowerPoint files.

Gamma: best for fast drafts from a prompt or document

Gamma turns a prompt, notes or an imported document into a card-based presentation, web page or document, with AI editing and themes, and it can export to common formats. It's priced per user with AI credits; see Gamma's site.

Strengths: fastest route from a messy brief to a readable draft. Limitations: its card layout doesn't always map cleanly to fixed slides. Skip it if pixel-perfect PowerPoint output is mandatory.

Canva: best for design-led campaign decks

Canva's presentation editor uses Magic Studio for drafting and design, with brand kits and templates shared across a team. Paid plans are priced per person; see Canva's site.

Strengths: visual quality and an asset library marketers already know. Limitations: brand control needs an admin to set up kits and template locks. Skip it if decks are data-heavy and edited mostly by sales.

Prezi: best for conversational, non-linear presenting

Prezi offers zooming, non-linear presentations with AI-assisted creation and a video mode that places the presenter beside the content. See Prezi's site for plans.

Strengths: memorable live presenting. Limitations: the format doesn't suit every buyer or every procurement process. Skip it if decks are mainly sent as attachments.

Will an AI-built deck survive export to PowerPoint or Google Slides?

Native office tools avoid the problem; for web-first tools, assume fonts, spacing, animations and charts may shift until you've checked an export yourself. Vendor pages describe export options, not how faithful the result is.

How should you test a sales deck and a campaign strategy deck?

Run two briefs through every shortlisted tool: a ten-slide sales deck built from CRM-style account notes, and a campaign strategy deck built from a planning document. We haven't run these; they're protocols for your trial.

  1. Sales deck: give each tool the same account notes, value proposition, two proof points and pricing structure. Check that it uses your template and keeps claims to what you supplied.
  2. Strategy deck: import the same planning document, ask for objectives, audiences, channels, budget split and timeline, and check whether imported data and charts stay editable.
  3. Export each deck to PPTX and PDF, then open the PPTX in PowerPoint and Google Slides.
  4. Have a rep, not a designer, edit three slides and see whether the layout holds.
  5. Present in each tool's presenter view and note speaker notes, timers and remote controls.

Account context usually lives in your CRM; our list of CRMs for B2B SaaS sales teams covers which ones make that data easy to pull.

What should a manual QA check cover before a deck goes out?

Check every number, name, logo and claim against its source, then check layout after export. AI drafting is fast precisely because it skips this step, so a person has to do it.

  • Facts: prices, dates, metrics and customer names match an approved source.
  • Claims: no invented case studies, logos or results.
  • Brand: correct template, fonts, colors and logo versions.
  • Images: rights cleared for every generated or stock image.
  • Export: fonts, charts and animations intact in the format the buyer receives.
  • Notes: speaker notes are accurate and contain nothing you'd mind a buyer reading.

Approved claims and proof points belong in a shared library; sales enablement software handles that, and digital sales rooms handle delivery for complex deals.

How do seats and AI credits affect the cost?

Most of these tools charge per seat, and some add AI credits that cap how many decks you can generate each month. The cheapest option is often the AI already included in, or added to, your office suite.

Count who creates decks and who only edits or views them, then ask whether viewers and occasional editors need paid seats. Ask how AI credits are counted per deck or per action, whether they reset monthly, and whether export or brand locking sits on a higher tier. What else do sales and marketing teams ask about AI presentation tools?

Questions tend to cover editing, confidentiality and whether designers are still needed. Brief answers follow.

Can AI presentation tools use our existing brand template?

Office-suite assistants work inside your existing templates, while design suites and generators usually need your theme rebuilt as a brand kit, so budget setup time.

Is it safe to upload confidential sales material?

Check each vendor's data processing terms, whether your content is used for model training, and whether admins can restrict sharing before uploading pricing or customer data.

Do we still need a designer if we use an AI deck tool?

Yes, for master templates, flagship decks and visual QA; the AI mainly saves time on first drafts and routine updates.

Can reps edit AI-generated decks without breaking the design?

Tools with locked templates handle this best, so ask a rep to edit slides during the trial rather than relying on a vendor demo.
