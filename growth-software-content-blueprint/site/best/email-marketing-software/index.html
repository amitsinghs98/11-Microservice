Best Email Marketing Software for Small Businesses

The best email marketing software for a small business is the one whose billing unit matches how you send: subscriber-priced tools suit small lists mailed often, while send-priced tools suit large lists mailed rarely. Below, eight platforms are matched to creators, local businesses, online stores and B2B teams.

Which email platform should you choose?

Pick MailerLite or Brevo if cost control matters most, Kit if you're a creator, Klaviyo if you run an online store, and ActiveCampaign or HubSpot Marketing Hub if email has to feed a sales pipeline. Mailchimp and Constant Contact remain sensible for local businesses that value familiarity, as long as you price them at next year's list size rather than today's.

  • Lean budget, broad features: MailerLite, which prices mainly on subscribers and bundles forms, landing pages and automation.
  • Large list, few campaigns a month: Brevo, whose paid marketing plans are organized around email volume rather than stored contacts.
  • Creators and paid newsletters: Kit, built around tags, sequences and creator commerce.
  • Shopify and WooCommerce stores: Klaviyo, for store-event depth, with the caveat that cost rises with profile count.
  • B2B teams with a pipeline: ActiveCampaign for automation depth, or HubSpot Marketing Hub if you already run HubSpot CRM.
  • Local businesses that want guidance: Mailchimp or Constant Contact.

What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

Each platform below is strong for one buyer type and a poor fit for at least one other. Read the "skip it if" line first; it rules tools out faster than the feature lists rule them in.

MailerLite: best for lean teams that want everything in one place

What it does: campaigns, a visual automation builder, signup forms, pop-ups, landing pages, a simple website builder and digital product sales.

Pricing model: subscriber-based tiers with a free plan; check the current caps and send allowances on the MailerLite pricing page.

Strengths: an approachable editor and a wide bundled toolset, which keeps the stack small for a one-person marketing function.

Limitations: MailerLite reviews new accounts, so businesses with imported lists of uncertain origin should expect questions. Ecommerce and predictive features are thinner than store specialists.

Skip it if: you need lead scoring and sales handoff, or your list came from anywhere other than your own opt-in forms.

Mailchimp: best for local businesses that want a familiar tool

What it does: campaigns, automation flows, forms, landing pages, segmentation and a large integration library.

Pricing model: contact-based tiers with monthly send limits linked to your contact count, plus a free plan; confirm the tiers and overage terms on the Mailchimp marketing pricing page.

Strengths: breadth of integrations and templates, and a short learning curve.

Limitations: contact-tier pricing gets expensive as lists grow, and introductory promotions can mask the full monthly price. Check how unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts are counted before importing.

Skip it if: your list will pass 10,000 contacts within a year and you mail it infrequently. Our roundup of Mailchimp alternatives for growing businesses covers where teams usually go next.

Brevo: best for big lists mailed a few times a month

What it does: email campaigns, automation, SMS and WhatsApp messaging, a lightweight CRM and transactional email.

Pricing model: paid marketing plans are organized mainly by monthly email volume, and the free plan carries a daily send cap; see the Brevo pricing page for current tiers.

Strengths: a volume model can cost far less than contact pricing for a large, lightly mailed list.

Limitations: the advantage reverses if you send daily, and some automation and reporting features sit on higher tiers.

Skip it if: you mail a mid-sized list several times a week.

Kit: best for creators, coaches and consultants

What it does: tag-based subscriber management, sequences, visual automations, landing pages, paid newsletter subscriptions and creator recommendation features.

Pricing model: subscriber-based tiers with a free plan; see the Kit pricing page.

Strengths: a simple tagging model suits audiences that span several lead magnets or products.

Limitations: design controls and store-event depth are lighter than ecommerce tools.

Skip it if: you need CRM-style scoring. B2B consultants should compare our guide to newsletter platforms for B2B creators.

Klaviyo: best for stores where order data should drive email

What it does: segmentation on store events, pre-built ecommerce flows, predictive analytics, SMS and revenue reporting.

Pricing model: billed on active profiles, with SMS priced separately; see the Klaviyo pricing page.

Strengths: store integrations and attribution reporting that general tools struggle to match.

Limitations: without a store, most of the value goes unused while the profile-based bill still grows.

Skip it if: you're a service business. Stores watching cost can compare our Klaviyo alternatives for email and SMS.

ActiveCampaign: best for B2B firms that need branching automation

What it does: email, a visual automation builder with conditional logic, site tracking, lead scoring and CRM features on higher tiers.

Pricing model: contact-based tiers with a trial rather than a permanent free plan; see the ActiveCampaign pricing page.

Strengths: automation depth for the money, which our HubSpot Marketing Hub vs ActiveCampaign comparison weighs against CRM alignment.

Limitations: the builder rewards teams with time to design journeys, and features such as lead scoring depend on tier.

Skip it if: you send one newsletter a month and nothing else.

Constant Contact: best for owners who want guided tools

What it does: email campaigns, event registration, social posting and basic automation.

Pricing model: contact-based tiers with a trial; see the Constant Contact pricing page.

Strengths: a workflow aimed at non-marketers, plus event features that suit nonprofits and local organizers.

Limitations: segmentation and behavioral automation are lighter than ActiveCampaign or Klaviyo.

Skip it if: you plan multi-step journeys triggered by website behavior.

HubSpot Marketing Hub: best for teams already on HubSpot CRM

What it does: email, forms, landing pages, workflows on paid tiers and reporting on the same record your sales team uses.

Pricing model: free tools plus paid tiers billed by seats and marketing contacts; confirm current terms, including any onboarding fees, on the HubSpot Marketing Hub pricing page.

Strengths: one contact record for marketing and sales simplifies handoff and attribution.

Limitations: the gap between entry and professional tiers is wide, and the workflows most teams want sit higher up.

Skip it if: you have no CRM plans and simply need a newsletter.

What does email software really cost at 2k, 10k and 50k contacts?

At 2,000 contacts the difference between vendors is usually small in absolute terms; by 50,000 contacts, the billing unit decides whether you pay a modest or a painful monthly bill. Model your own numbers with the method below, then run the same inputs through each vendor's official pricing calculator.

These scenarios are hypothetical. They show which inputs move the bill, not what any vendor charges today.

Scenario 1: 2,000 contacts, four campaigns a month

That's about 8,000 sends a month. Most free plans will be too small for a list this size, so expect a paid entry tier. Here, usability and setup time matter more than price, because the gap between subscriber-priced tools is modest at this count.

Scenario 2: 10,000 contacts, weekly sends plus a welcome flow

Weekly campaigns plus automation come to roughly 45,000 to 50,000 sends a month. This is where contact-priced tiers start to diverge, and where send caps on lower plans begin to bite. Compare a mid-tier subscriber plan, a send-volume plan and an automation-led plan at the same count, and check which features you lose on the cheapest tier that fits.

Scenario 3: 50,000 contacts, twice-monthly sends

That's about 100,000 sends a month from a big but lightly mailed list, the profile where a send-volume model like Brevo's deserves a serious quote. On contact-priced tools, pruning unengaged contacts may cut the bill more than switching vendors would. Our explainer on marketing automation pricing units shows how to normalize contact, send and seat pricing into one monthly figure.

At every size, ask whether unsubscribed, bounced or archived contacts count toward billing, and whether a paid account is moved up a tier automatically when you exceed its limit.

Which platform fits your type of business?

Match the platform to the data that should trigger your emails: subscriber tags for creators, simple segments for local firms, store events for ecommerce, and CRM fields for B2B. Once you know which data drives your program, the shortlist usually narrows to two or three tools.

  • Creator with a growing newsletter: Kit or MailerLite. Check export options before building paid subscriptions on top.
  • Local business with occasional promotions: Mailchimp, Constant Contact or MailerLite, priced at the list size you expect next year.
  • Ecommerce store: Klaviyo first, with Brevo or MailerLite as lower-cost options for stores that don't need predictive features.
  • B2B service firm or SaaS: ActiveCampaign or HubSpot. If journeys are the core of your program, compare our list of lifecycle email automation software, and check how each connects to your small-business CRM.

What should you ask before migrating?

Ask whether contacts export with tags, custom fields, consent records and unsubscribe status intact, and whether automations must be rebuilt by hand. Migration is mostly a data and sender-reputation project, so those two answers decide how long it really takes.

  • Does the new platform review or approve accounts and imported lists, and how long does that take?
  • What sending ramp does the vendor recommend for a new domain or IP?
  • Can both platforms run in parallel for one billing cycle without double-counting contacts?
  • Which reports and historical engagement data will you lose on export?

Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your sending domain before the first campaign. Our guide to deliverability monitoring tools for marketing teams explains how to watch the results after you switch.

What mistakes do small businesses make when buying?

The most common mistake is pricing today's list instead of the list you'll have in 12 months. The second is buying automation nobody has time to build.

  • Ignoring send caps. Contact allowances get the attention, but monthly send limits cause the surprise upgrades.
  • Trusting open rates. Privacy features distort opens; measure clicks, conversions and revenue, as our guide to measuring email ROI alongside deliverability explains.
  • Paying twice for AI writing. Many platforms bundle writing assistants; compare them with dedicated AI email copywriting tools before upgrading for that feature alone.

What else do buyers ask about email marketing software?

Most remaining questions come down to free plans, billing units and whether you need a CRM as well. The answers below are general; confirm any limit on the vendor's own pricing page.

Is a free email marketing plan enough for a small business?

Usually only while the list is very small. Free plans in this category are sized for trying the product, so most established businesses with a real customer list will need a paid tier.

Should I choose a platform priced by contacts or by sends?

Choose the unit that matches your behavior. Large lists mailed rarely favor send-based pricing, while smaller lists mailed often favor contact-based pricing with generous send allowances.

Do I need a separate CRM as well as an email platform?

B2B teams with a sales pipeline usually do. Creators and most local businesses can manage with tags and segments inside the email platform.
