Quick answer

Most small businesses overpay for email in one of two ways: they buy an automation platform they never configure, or they stay on a cheap tool long after its contact-based pricing has overtaken a better-fitting alternative. Based on the product information reviewed, these are our starting picks by buyer type:

  • Lean budget, broad feature set: MailerLite, because the published paid tiers include most features and price mainly on subscriber count.
  • Large list, modest send frequency: Brevo, whose paid plans are built around send volume rather than stored contacts.
  • Creators and paid newsletters: Kit, which is designed around subscriber tagging, creator commerce and newsletter growth.
  • Shopify and WooCommerce stores: Klaviyo, for its depth of ecommerce event data, with the caveat that cost rises quickly with profile count.
  • B2B teams that need branching automation: ActiveCampaign, or HubSpot Marketing Hub if the business already runs on HubSpot CRM.
  • Local businesses that value familiarity and support: Mailchimp or Constant Contact.

None of these is a universal winner. The right choice depends on list size, how often you send and whether email needs to talk to a store or a CRM.

How we chose

We assessed each platform against eight criteria from the client brief: everyday usability, list-size economics, automation depth, landing pages, forms, segmentation, deliverability support and price at realistic contact counts.

Evidence came from official pricing pages, product documentation and vendor materials. No hands-on testing was performed for this draft, and we did not run sends or measure inbox placement. The platforms

MailerLite

Best for: small businesses and solo operators who want a clean editor, landing pages and basic automation without paying for an enterprise suite.

What it does: campaign email, a visual automation builder, signup forms, pop-ups, landing pages, a website builder and digital product sales.

Pricing: the official MailerLite pricing page lists a Free plan with 250 subscribers, 2,500 monthly emails and two seats; a Comfort plan from $12 per month (monthly billing, 500 subscribers, 5,000 monthly emails); and a Power plan from $25 per month with unlimited monthly emails under a fair-use policy (checked 4 November 2026). Comfort's send allowance is ten times the ceiling of your subscriber tier.

Strengths: the vendor states that paid plans share the full feature set and differ mainly by usage limits, which makes forecasting easier. Unsubscribed and bounced addresses do not count toward billing, according to the same page.

Limitations: the Free plan's 250-subscriber cap is small, and the pricing page says free-plan sending is locked once you exceed it. MailerLite reviews new accounts, so businesses with purchased lists or borderline content should expect friction. Paid accounts are moved up a tier automatically when they exceed their subscriber limit.

Before you choose: confirm which features in your workflow are Power-only, such as multiple automation triggers.

Mailchimp

Best for: local businesses and generalists who want a familiar tool with a wide integration library.

What it does: email campaigns, automation flows, forms, landing pages, segmentation, and SMS in some markets.

Pricing: the Mailchimp marketing pricing page lists a Free plan with one seat, one audience, up to 250 contacts and 500 sends per month (250 per day); Essentials from $13 per month; Standard from $20 per month for 500 contacts; and Premium from $350 per month including 10,000 contacts (checked 4 November 2026). Standard's monthly send limit is 12 times your maximum contact count and Premium's is 15 times. The page states that overages apply if contact or send limits are exceeded.

Strengths: broad integrations, a large template library and multivariate testing on Premium.

Limitations: contact-tier pricing becomes expensive as lists grow, and the free plan is now too small for most real businesses. Check how Mailchimp counts unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts before you import a large list.

Before you choose: introductory discounts were advertised on the pricing page when we checked; budget for the full price after the promotional period. Our roundup of Mailchimp alternatives for growing businesses covers where teams typically go next.

Brevo

Best for: businesses with large lists that send a few campaigns a month, and teams that also want SMS or transactional email under one vendor.

What it does: email campaigns, automation, SMS and WhatsApp messaging, a lightweight CRM and transactional email.

Pricing: Brevo's paid marketing plans are priced mainly by monthly email volume rather than stored contacts, and its free plan carries a daily send limit. We could not fetch the page during drafting, so check current tiers on the Brevo pricing page.

Strengths: a volume-based model can be far cheaper than contact-based pricing for a 50,000-contact list mailed twice a month.

Limitations: the economics reverse if you send daily. Some automation and reporting features sit on higher tiers, so read the plan matrix before assuming parity.

Before you choose: estimate your monthly sends, not just your contact count.

Kit

Best for: creators, coaches and consultants growing a newsletter or selling digital products.

What it does: tag-based subscriber management, visual automations, landing pages, forms, paid newsletter subscriptions and creator recommendation features.

Pricing: subscriber-based tiers with a free plan; see the Kit pricing page for current limits.

Strengths: a simple tagging model suits audiences that cross several products or lead magnets.

Limitations: design controls and ecommerce event depth are lighter than store-focused tools. B2B teams needing CRM-style lead scoring will outgrow it. B2B consultants should also compare our guide to newsletter platforms for B2B creators.

Klaviyo

Best for: Shopify, WooCommerce and other online stores where order, browse and cart data should drive email.

What it does: ecommerce-event segmentation, pre-built flows, predictive analytics, SMS and revenue reporting.

Pricing: billed on active profiles, with SMS priced separately; see the Klaviyo pricing page.

Strengths: deep store integrations and attribution reporting that general tools struggle to match.

Limitations: for a service business without a store, much of the value goes unused and the profile-based bill still grows. If cost is the issue, our guide to Klaviyo alternatives compares cheaper ecommerce options.

ActiveCampaign

Best for: B2B companies and service firms that need multi-branch automation, lead scoring and sales handoff.

What it does: email marketing, a visual automation builder with conditional logic, lead scoring, site tracking and CRM features on higher tiers.

Pricing: contact-based tiers with a free trial rather than a free plan; see the ActiveCampaign pricing page.

Strengths: automation depth for the price. Our HubSpot Marketing Hub vs ActiveCampaign comparison covers the CRM-alignment trade-off in detail.

Limitations: the builder rewards teams with time to design journeys; a business that sends one newsletter a month is paying for unused capability. Key features such as lead scoring are tier-dependent.

Constant Contact

Best for: local businesses, nonprofits and event-led organisations that prefer guided tools and phone support.

What it does: email campaigns, event registration, social posting, SMS in some markets and basic automation.

Pricing: contact-based tiers with a trial; see the Constant Contact pricing page.

Strengths: an approachable workflow for owners who are not marketers.

Limitations: automation and segmentation are lighter than ActiveCampaign or Klaviyo. Consider another option if you plan multi-step behavioural journeys.

HubSpot Marketing Hub

Best for: B2B teams already using HubSpot CRM who want email, forms and reporting on the same contact record.

What it does: email, forms, landing pages, workflows on paid tiers, attribution reporting and native CRM data.

Pricing: free tools plus paid tiers billed by seats and marketing contacts; higher tiers have historically carried onboarding fees, so confirm current terms on the HubSpot Marketing Hub pricing page.

Strengths: one record for marketing and sales, which simplifies attribution and handoff.

Limitations: the gap between entry and professional tiers is large, and the workflow features many teams want sit on the higher tier. Skip it if you have no CRM plans.

Cost at 2k, 10k and 50k contacts

Headline prices describe the smallest list. What matters is how the bill behaves as you grow. The scenarios below are hypothetical and use only the rules and figures we verified; for other vendors, run the same contact count through the official pricing calculator.

2,000 contacts, four campaigns a month

That is about 8,000 sends a month. Both MailerLite's and Mailchimp's free plans are out of reach at 250 contacts, so this list needs a paid tier on either. Mailchimp states that Standard's monthly send limit is 12 times your maximum contact count, so four campaigns a month to 2,000 people stays well inside it. Brevo's volume model and Kit's subscriber model are both worth pricing here, because at this size the difference between vendors is usually small in absolute terms.

10,000 contacts, weekly sends plus a welcome flow

Expect 40,000 to 50,000 sends a month. This is where contact-based pricing starts to diverge sharply and where Mailchimp's Premium tier begins (it includes 10,000 contacts from $350 per month, checked 4 November 2026). Most small teams do not need Premium; the real comparison is mid-tier MailerLite, Mailchimp Standard, Brevo and ActiveCampaign at the same count.

50,000 contacts, twice-monthly sends

About 100,000 sends a month. MailerLite's pricing page lists Comfort at $319 per month on monthly billing for up to 50,000 subscribers, or $287.10 per month billed yearly (checked 4 November 2026); Comfort's allowance at that tier would be ten times the tier ceiling. A send-volume model like Brevo's deserves a quote at this size because the list is large but the cadence is low. Our explainer on marketing automation pricing units shows how to normalise contact, send and seat pricing into one monthly figure.

At every size, check whether unsubscribed, bounced or archived contacts count toward billing. Pruning inactive contacts is often the cheapest upgrade available.

Migration questions

Switching platforms is mostly a data and reputation project, not a design one. Ask these before you sign:

  • Can we export contacts with tags, custom fields, consent timestamps and unsubscribe status intact?
  • Will automations need rebuilding by hand, or is there an import path?
  • Does the new platform require list review or approval, and how long does it take?
  • How should we warm up a new sending domain or IP, and what volume ramp does the vendor recommend?
  • Can both platforms run in parallel for one billing cycle without double-counting contacts?

Authentication matters here too. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your sending domain before the first campaign; our guide to email deliverability tools for marketing teams explains how to monitor the results.

Buying mistakes to avoid

  • Pricing only today's list. Model the list you expect in 12 months.
  • Ignoring send caps. Contact allowances get attention; monthly send limits cause the surprise invoices.
  • Buying automation you won't build. Be honest about who will maintain journeys.
  • Treating open rates as proof of performance. Privacy features distort opens; measure clicks, conversions and revenue, as our guide to measuring email ROI alongside deliverability explains.
  • Skipping the AI-writing question. Many platforms now bundle writing assistants; compare them against dedicated AI email copywriting tools before paying for an upgrade.

FAQs

Is a free email marketing plan enough for a small business?

Only for a very small list. MailerLite's and Mailchimp's free plans were both capped at 250 contacts when we checked on 4 November 2026, so most established businesses will need a paid tier.

Should I choose by contact price or send price?

Choose by the one that matches your behaviour. Large lists that send rarely favour volume pricing; smaller lists that send often favour contact pricing with generous send multipliers.

Do I need a separate CRM?

B2B teams with a sales pipeline usually do. Creators and most local businesses can manage with tags and segments inside the email platform.

Which platform has the best deliverability?

We did not measure inbox placement, and vendor claims are not comparable. Deliverability depends more on your authentication, list quality and sending habits than on the platform.

Verdict

Our editorial assessment: start with MailerLite or Brevo if cost control matters most, Kit if you are a creator, Klaviyo if you run a store, and ActiveCampaign or HubSpot if email must feed a sales pipeline. Mailchimp and Constant Contact remain reasonable for local businesses that value familiarity, provided you price them at next year's list size. Whatever you choose, verify current limits on the vendor's pricing page and recheck at every tier change.
