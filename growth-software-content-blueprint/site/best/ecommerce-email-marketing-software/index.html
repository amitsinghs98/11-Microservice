Best Email Marketing Platforms for Ecommerce Brands

For an online store, the email platform is really a data integration: it is only as good as the order, cart and browse events it receives. This guide compares seven platforms on that basis, then separates the platform fee from SMS and usage charges.

Quick answer

  • Deepest store data and segmentation: Klaviyo, for brands willing to pay more as profiles grow.
  • Email plus SMS at a lower entry cost: Omnisend, a reasonable middle ground for Shopify, WooCommerce and BigCommerce stores.
  • Simplest start on Shopify: Shopify Email, because it lives inside the Shopify admin.
  • Budget stores with simple needs: MailerLite, which lists ecommerce triggers and Shopify and WooCommerce integrations on its paid plans.
  • SMS-led brands with large budgets: Attentive, which is quote-based and aimed at larger retailers.
  • Brands already using Mailchimp or Drip: both can work, but check event depth before you rebuild flows there.

Who this guide is for

This page is for direct-to-consumer founders and ecommerce growth teams. If your business does not sell through an online store, start with our main guide to email marketing software for small businesses, which covers creators, local firms and B2B teams. We assume you run Shopify or WooCommerce, want automated revenue flows rather than only newsletters, and are deciding whether to add SMS.

How we chose

We looked at seven things from the client brief: ecommerce event coverage, revenue attribution, segmentation on purchase behaviour, product feeds and dynamic product blocks, SMS, abandoned-cart flows, and Shopify or WooCommerce integration.

Evidence came from official pricing pages, integration listings and product documentation. No hands-on testing was performed, and we have not compared attribution numbers between tools, because each vendor defines attributed revenue differently. The order reflects editorial fit for a typical growing store; payment did not influence it.

The platforms

Klaviyo

Best for: Shopify and WooCommerce brands with meaningful order volume that want to segment on purchase history, predicted lifetime value and product interest.

What it does: email and SMS from one customer profile, pre-built ecommerce flows, product feeds for dynamic recommendations, predictive analytics and revenue attribution reporting.

Pricing model: billed by active profiles, with SMS and MMS priced separately. Confirm the current tiers on the Klaviyo pricing page.

Strengths: the breadth of store events it can act on, and a large ecosystem of review, loyalty and subscription app integrations.

Limitations: cost grows with every active profile, so stores with large, low-value lists can find it expensive. Its attribution window is a vendor definition, not incrementality.

Skip it if: you send a weekly newsletter and one cart reminder. Our comparison of Klaviyo alternatives for ecommerce email and SMS covers cheaper routes.

Omnisend

Best for: small and mid-sized stores that want email and SMS with ready-made ecommerce automations, without Klaviyo-level spend.

What it does: campaigns, pre-built cart, browse and post-purchase workflows, product recommendation blocks, forms, SMS and push notifications.

Pricing model: contact-based tiers, with messaging usage such as SMS charged on top; see the Omnisend pricing page.

Strengths: a shorter path from install to working flows, and native support for several store platforms.

Limitations: predictive analytics and segmentation depth are lighter than Klaviyo's. Check how SMS credits are allocated on each tier.

Shopify Email

Best for: new Shopify stores that want branded campaigns and basic automations without another vendor.

What it does: email campaigns and automations built in the Shopify admin, using store products and customer data directly.

Pricing model: usage-based, billed through Shopify, with an included allowance. Shopify has been expanding its messaging tools, so check the current name, allowance and rates in the Shopify Email help documentation.

Strengths: no integration to break, and the lowest setup effort on this list.

Limitations: Shopify-only, with less segmentation and reporting depth than dedicated platforms. Plan to graduate once flows become your main revenue lever.

MailerLite

Best for: small stores watching cost that need a good editor, pop-ups and simple cart or purchase automations.

What it does: the official MailerLite pricing page lists ecommerce triggers, ecommerce sales tracking and Shopify and WooCommerce integrations on paid plans, with the Comfort plan from $12 per month on monthly billing (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: predictable subscriber-based billing and a strong set of forms and landing pages.

Limitations: SMS is not listed on its marketing plans (the vendor sells SMS through its separate transactional product, MailerSend), and product recommendation and predictive features are thinner than ecommerce specialists.

Mailchimp

Best for: stores already on Mailchimp that want to add cart and purchase flows without migrating.

What it does: store integrations, product blocks, abandoned-cart and purchase-follow-up flows, and predictive segmentation on Premium according to the Mailchimp pricing page.

Pricing model: contact tiers with monthly send limits; Essentials starts at $13 per month and the page states overages apply above limits (checked 4 November 2026).

Limitations: integration depth varies by store platform, so test which events actually sync before building flows.

Drip

Best for: independent brands that want ecommerce-focused automation with a visual workflow builder.

What it does: store-event segmentation, on-site pop-ups, cart and post-purchase workflows and revenue reporting.

Pricing model: contact-based; see the Drip pricing page.

Limitations: a smaller app ecosystem than Klaviyo; confirm SMS availability and integration coverage for your stack.

Attentive

Best for: larger retailers for whom SMS is a primary channel and who want managed strategy support.

What it does: SMS-first messaging with email, sign-up units and segmentation.

Pricing model: quote-based; see the Attentive website.

Limitations: aimed at brands with budget and volume. Consider another option if you are pre-scale or want self-serve pricing.

Flows to build first

Whichever platform you choose, build in this order. Each flow depends on a specific store event, so confirm the event syncs before you design anything.

  1. Welcome series (trigger: form signup). Sets expectations and captures preferences.
  2. Abandoned checkout (trigger: checkout started, no order). Usually the first flow worth measuring.
  3. Browse abandonment (trigger: product viewed by a known visitor). Needs on-site tracking to identify the visitor.
  4. Post-purchase (trigger: order placed or fulfilled). Delivery updates, care tips and review requests.
  5. Winback (trigger: no order within your typical repurchase window).
  6. Back-in-stock (trigger: inventory change). Requires product feed or catalogue sync.

Store-size cost scenarios

These hypothetical scenarios illustrate cost drivers. Apart from the figures marked as checked, run each scenario through the vendor's calculator.

Launch store: 3,000 email contacts, no SMS

The platform fee is the whole bill. Shopify Email or MailerLite keeps cost low; at this size, the time you spend building flows matters more than the price gap between vendors.

Growing brand: 25,000 profiles and 6,000 SMS subscribers

Now the bill has two parts. The email platform fee scales with profiles or contacts, while SMS is charged per message or via credit bundles, and costs differ by country and by message length. A campaign to 6,000 SMS subscribers can cost more than a month of email, so price them separately. Klaviyo, Omnisend and Attentive should all be quoted here.

Established WooCommerce store: 80,000 profiles, weekly sends

At this scale, pruning inactive profiles can cut cost meaningfully on profile-based plans. Ask whether suppressed profiles still count toward billing, and whether a dedicated sending IP is included or extra.

To connect email revenue to the rest of your reporting, pair your platform with one of the marketing analytics tools for performance teams rather than relying on each vendor's own attribution.

Mistakes and questions for sales

  • Comparing attributed revenue across vendors. Attribution windows differ; holdout tests tell you more, and our guide to tying email revenue to deliverability health shows how to read both together.
  • Forgetting SMS consent rules. SMS marketing generally needs separate, explicit consent. Confirm requirements for each market you sell into.
  • Treating email as the CRM. Wholesale or high-touch B2B buyers may need a proper CRM for small businesses alongside the email tool.

Questions to ask: Which store events sync in real time? Are SMS credits included or extra? Do suppressed profiles count toward billing? What happens to flows and history if we leave?

FAQs

Is Klaviyo worth it for a small Shopify store?

It can be if flows drive a large share of revenue. For a store sending a few campaigns a month, a lower-cost platform is often enough until flows justify the upgrade.

Should I buy email and SMS from the same vendor?

One profile across channels makes suppression and frequency control easier. Separate vendors can be cheaper, but you then need to sync consent and unsubscribes reliably.

Does WooCommerce change the shortlist?

Shopify Email is out, and integration quality varies more. Check that the WooCommerce plugin sends cart and browse events, not just orders.

Verdict

Our editorial assessment: Klaviyo is the default for stores where automated flows are a primary revenue channel and budget allows. Omnisend is the pragmatic choice for email plus SMS at a lower entry point, Shopify Email is the easiest place to start on Shopify, and MailerLite suits cost-conscious stores without SMS plans. Price the platform fee and messaging usage separately, and confirm event sync before you commit.
