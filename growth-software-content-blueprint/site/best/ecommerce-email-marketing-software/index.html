Best Email Marketing Platforms for Ecommerce Brands

Which ecommerce email platform should you choose?

Choose Klaviyo if automated flows already drive a large share of revenue and you can absorb profile-based pricing; choose Omnisend for email and SMS at a lower entry point; choose Shopify Email if you're new to Shopify and want no extra vendor. Drip, Mailchimp and Brevo are reasonable second-tier picks, and Attentive is for larger brands where SMS leads.

  • Deepest store data: Klaviyo.
  • Email plus SMS on a mid-market budget: Omnisend.
  • Simplest start on Shopify: Shopify Email.
  • Independent brands that want visual workflows: Drip.
  • Stores already on Mailchimp: stay and add flows, if the events you need sync.
  • Big lists with modest send frequency: Brevo.
  • SMS-first retailers with budget: Attentive.

Who is this list for, and what problem does it solve?

It's for direct-to-consumer founders and ecommerce growth teams on Shopify or WooCommerce who want automated revenue flows, not only newsletters. The problem it solves is choosing a platform whose event data, SMS model and pricing still work at two or three times your current order volume.

What are the strengths and limits of each platform?

Klaviyo and Omnisend are purpose-built for stores; the others are general or channel-specific tools with ecommerce features added. That difference shows up most in browse-abandonment tracking, product feeds and how attribution is reported.

Klaviyo: best for brands that segment on purchase history

Klaviyo runs email and SMS from one customer profile, with pre-built flows, product feeds for dynamic recommendations, predictive analytics and revenue reporting. It's billed on active profiles, with SMS priced separately; confirm tiers on the Klaviyo pricing page.

Limitations: stores with large, low-value lists can find it expensive, and its attribution window is a vendor definition. Skip it if you send a weekly newsletter and one cart reminder; our comparison of Klaviyo alternatives covers cheaper routes.

Omnisend: best for email and SMS without enterprise spend

Omnisend offers campaigns, pre-built cart, browse and post-purchase workflows, product recommendation blocks, forms, SMS and push. Pricing is contact-tiered with messaging usage on top; see the Omnisend pricing page.

Limitations: predictive analytics and segmentation depth are lighter than Klaviyo's. Skip it if you need custom predictive models or a large partner app ecosystem.

Shopify Email: best for the first year on Shopify

Shopify Email lives in the Shopify admin and uses store products and customer data directly, with usage-based billing through Shopify. Check the current allowance and rates on Shopify's email marketing page.

Limitations: it's Shopify-only, with less segmentation and reporting depth than dedicated platforms. Skip it if flows are already your main revenue lever.

Drip: best for independent brands that like visual workflows

Drip provides store-event segmentation, on-site pop-ups, cart and post-purchase workflows and revenue reporting, on contact-based pricing listed on the Drip pricing page.

Limitations: a smaller integration ecosystem than Klaviyo. Skip it if you need SMS from the same vendor; confirm current channel support first.

Mailchimp: best for stores that don't want to migrate yet

Mailchimp connects to major store platforms and supports product blocks plus cart and purchase-follow-up flows, on contact tiers with send limits; see the Mailchimp pricing page.

Limitations: integration depth varies by store platform. Skip it if browse-abandonment and catalog-driven recommendations are central to your plan.

Brevo: best for big lists mailed infrequently

Brevo combines email, SMS, WhatsApp and transactional messages, with email plans organized around send volume and SMS bought separately; see the Brevo pricing page.

Limitations: ecommerce-specific segmentation and reporting are less developed than store-first tools. Skip it if you mail daily.

Attentive: best for SMS-led retailers with budget

Attentive is SMS-first, with email, sign-up units and segmentation, and is sold through sales conversations via the Attentive website rather than self-serve tiers.

Limitations: it's aimed at brands with scale. Skip it if you're pre-scale or want published pricing; our guide to SMS marketing software for ecommerce brands covers self-serve options.

Which flows should you build first?

Build the welcome series and abandoned checkout first, then browse abandonment, post-purchase, winback and back-in-stock. Each depends on a specific store event, so confirm that event syncs in real time before you design any email.

  1. Welcome series (trigger: form signup). Sets expectations and captures preferences.
  2. Abandoned checkout (trigger: checkout started, no order). Usually the first flow worth measuring.
  3. Browse abandonment (trigger: product viewed by a known visitor). Needs on-site tracking that identifies the visitor.
  4. Post-purchase (trigger: order placed or fulfilled). Delivery updates, care tips and review requests.
  5. Winback (trigger: no order within your typical repurchase window).
  6. Back-in-stock (trigger: inventory change). Requires a product feed or catalog sync.

How do platform fees differ from SMS and usage charges?

The platform fee scales with profiles, contacts or email volume, while SMS is usually charged per message or through credit bundles that vary by country and message length. Price the two separately, because a single SMS campaign to a mid-sized list can rival a month of email.

These store-size scenarios are hypothetical and show which inputs drive cost; run each through the vendor's own calculator.

Launch store: 3,000 email contacts, no SMS

The platform fee is the whole bill. Shopify Email or an entry tier elsewhere keeps cost low; at this size, the time you spend building flows matters more than the price gap.

Growing brand: 25,000 profiles and 6,000 SMS subscribers

Now the bill has two parts. Ask each vendor whether SMS credits are included, how unused credits roll over, and what international messages cost. Klaviyo, Omnisend and Attentive should all be quoted here.

Established WooCommerce store: 80,000 profiles, weekly sends

On profile-based plans, suppressing long-inactive profiles can cut cost meaningfully. Ask whether suppressed profiles still count, and whether a dedicated sending IP is included or extra. Check that the WooCommerce plugin sends cart and browse events, not only orders.

Can you trust each platform's attributed revenue?

Treat attributed revenue as a directional signal, not proof of incremental sales, because each vendor sets its own attribution window and rules. Two platforms reporting on the same store can credit email with very different totals.

What mistakes do ecommerce teams make when buying?

The costliest mistake is choosing on a feature list without confirming which store events actually sync. The second is treating SMS as a cheap add-on rather than a separate cost line with its own consent rules.

  • Skipping SMS consent. SMS marketing generally needs separate, explicit consent; confirm requirements for each market you sell into.
  • Using email as the customer database. Brands with wholesale, subscriptions or several storefronts may need a proper customer data platform for retention teams alongside the email tool.
  • Forgetting exit costs. Ask what happens to flows, templates and engagement history if you leave.

What else do ecommerce buyers ask?

Store owners most often ask whether Klaviyo is worth the cost, whether to buy SMS from the same vendor, and how WooCommerce changes the shortlist. Short answers follow.

Is Klaviyo worth it for a small Shopify store?

It can be once automated flows drive a meaningful share of revenue. A store sending a few campaigns a month is often fine on a lower-cost platform until flows justify the upgrade.

Should email and SMS come from the same vendor?

One profile across both channels makes suppression and frequency control easier. Separate vendors can be cheaper, but you then have to sync consent and unsubscribes reliably.

Does WooCommerce change the shortlist?

Yes. Shopify Email drops out, and integration quality varies more, so confirm the plugin sends cart and browse events as well as orders.

What's the verdict?

Our editorial assessment: Klaviyo is the default when flows are a primary revenue channel and budget allows; Omnisend is the pragmatic choice for email plus SMS; Shopify Email is the easiest place to start on Shopify. Before you commit, price the platform fee and messaging separately, confirm event sync, then start a trial from the vendor's official site.
