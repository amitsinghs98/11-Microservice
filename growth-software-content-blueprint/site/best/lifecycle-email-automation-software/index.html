Quick answer

Lifecycle programs rarely fail because the email builder is weak. They fail because the platform cannot see what users did in the product, so every journey falls back to time delays. Choose for data first, messaging second.

  • SaaS teams sending on product events: Customer.io.
  • Early-stage SaaS wanting simplicity: Loops.
  • Large consumer apps across email, push and in-app: Braze or Iterable.
  • B2B teams whose journeys depend on CRM stages: HubSpot Marketing Hub or ActiveCampaign.
  • Ecommerce brands: Klaviyo.
  • Mid-sized teams wanting data and journeys in one tool: Ortto.

A sample lifecycle map

The map below is a hypothetical starting point for a B2B SaaS product with a free trial. Adapt the events to your product before comparing tools, because the events you need are the real requirements list.

Hypothetical lifecycle map for a SaaS free trial
Stage Entry trigger Goal Exit condition Data needed
Welcome Account created First login and setup Setup completed Sign-up event, role, plan
Onboarding Setup completed Reach the key first action Key action performed Product events, feature flags
Activation Key action performed once Repeat usage, invite teammates Usage threshold met Event counts, seat count
Expansion Approaching plan limit Upgrade or add seats Plan changed Billing data, usage against limits
Reactivation No activity for a set period Return to the product Activity resumes or user suppressed Last-seen date, engagement history

Every stage needs an exit condition. Without one, a user who upgraded keeps receiving upgrade prompts, which is the most common lifecycle complaint. Receipts and password resets sit outside these journeys and are usually better sent through a dedicated service; our guide to transactional email services for SaaS explains that split.

The platforms

Customer.io

Best for: SaaS and app teams with developers who can send product events.

Customer.io triggers journeys from user attributes and custom events, supports branching, and offers email, push, SMS and in-app messages along with a transactional API and a data pipelines product. Plans scale by profiles; see the Customer.io pricing page.

  • Strengths: event-based logic is central rather than bolted on.
  • Limitations: value depends on clean event tracking, so it is a poor fit without engineering time.
  • Skip it if: your journeys are driven by sales stages rather than product use.

Loops

Best for: early-stage SaaS teams that want event-triggered email without a heavy platform.

Loops is email-focused software for SaaS, with event-triggered sequences, newsletters and transactional email in one product, according to its official site. Pricing is tiered by contacts; see the Loops pricing page.

  • Strengths: a short path from sign-up event to a working onboarding sequence.
  • Limitations: narrower channel coverage and less complex branching than enterprise tools.
  • Before you choose: list the conditions your onboarding branches need and confirm they are supported.

Braze

Best for: large consumer brands running lifecycle across many channels and markets.

Braze is an enterprise customer engagement platform whose journey builder, Canvas, orchestrates email, mobile push, in-app messages and other channels. Pricing is quote-based; start at Braze's official site.

  • Strengths: mobile and cross-channel depth that email-first tools do not match.
  • Limitations: implementation effort and cost suit teams with dedicated lifecycle and engineering staff.
  • Skip it if: you are a small B2B team sending mostly email.

Iterable

Best for: mid-market and enterprise teams wanting cross-channel journeys with strong segmentation.

Iterable offers a visual journey builder, user and event data handling, and email, SMS, push and in-app channels. Pricing is quote-based; see Iterable's official site.

  • Strengths: flexible segmentation on event data for teams that run many experiments.
  • Limitations: overkill below a meaningful user base, and no public price list to budget from.
  • Before you choose: ask how data volume, channels and seats affect the quote at renewal.

HubSpot Marketing Hub

Best for: B2B teams whose lifecycle is tied to CRM stages and sales handoff.

HubSpot builds workflows on its CRM, so journeys can branch on deal stage, lifecycle stage, owner or form history. Workflow depth and support for custom behavioural events depend on edition; see HubSpot's Marketing Hub pricing.

  • Strengths: sales and marketing share one record, which simplifies handoff and attribution.
  • Limitations: product-event lifecycle often requires higher editions or extra integration work.
  • Skip it if: you need product-led journeys on a small budget.

ActiveCampaign

Best for: SMBs that want detailed automation and a light CRM in one subscription.

ActiveCampaign offers a visual automation builder with conditions, goals, site and event tracking, and deal pipelines. Pricing is tiered by contacts and plan; see the ActiveCampaign pricing page. The trade-offs against HubSpot are covered in our HubSpot Marketing Hub and ActiveCampaign comparison.

  • Strengths: deep automation for the price, with goals that act as exit conditions.
  • Limitations: product-event data usually needs an integration layer, and large automations can become hard to audit.
  • Before you choose: check how custom event tracking is supported on your plan.

Klaviyo

Best for: ecommerce brands whose lifecycle runs on browse, cart and order events.

Klaviyo integrates closely with ecommerce platforms and builds flows from order, product and browsing events, with email and SMS. Pricing is based on profiles, with SMS billed separately; see the Klaviyo pricing page. Our ecommerce email platform guide and the roundup of Klaviyo alternatives go deeper.

  • Strengths: ecommerce events work with little setup.
  • Limitations: less natural for SaaS, where events relate to product use rather than orders.
  • Skip it if: you do not sell through an online store.

Ortto

Best for: mid-sized teams that want customer data, journeys and analytics without a separate data platform.

Ortto combines a customer data platform with a journey builder, email, SMS, push and in-app messages, and built-in reporting. Pricing is tiered by contacts; see the Ortto pricing page.

  • Strengths: data and messaging together reduce integration work.
  • Limitations: a smaller ecosystem than the large incumbents.
  • Before you choose: confirm which data sources connect natively.

How contact and event pricing works

Lifecycle tools price on different units, which makes like-for-like comparison hard. Contact- or profile-based pricing charges for every stored person, including inactive ones. Some enterprise tools also factor in data volume, channels or message counts. Seats, SMS credits and premium support often sit on top. Because no prices are quoted here, request a written quote at your current and expected profile counts. In practice:

  • Prune or archive inactive contacts if you pay per profile.
  • Ask whether every tracked event counts toward a limit, or only events that trigger journeys.
  • Budget SMS and push separately from email.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

  • Buying before mapping events, then discovering the product does not emit them.
  • Building journeys without exit conditions or frequency caps.
  • Mixing receipts with promotional sends on the same stream.
  • Judging journeys by open rates instead of activation or retention.

Ask vendors: how are custom events ingested and billed? Can journeys exit on any event? How are transactional and marketing messages separated for unsubscribe handling? What data can we export if we leave?

FAQs

What separates lifecycle automation from regular email automation?

Lifecycle automation reacts to what users do over time, with entry and exit rules for each stage. Regular email automation often means time-based sequences after a form fill.

Do we need a customer data platform first?

Not always. Customer.io, Ortto and others ingest events directly. A separate data platform helps when many tools need the same events.

Should onboarding emails use the transactional or marketing stream?

Messages users need to use the product can be treated as transactional; promotional nudges should respect marketing unsubscribes. Check your legal obligations.

How many journeys should we launch first?

Start with welcome and onboarding, measure activation, then add expansion and reactivation once the first two are stable.

Verdict

Our editorial assessment: product-led SaaS teams should start with Customer.io or Loops, sales-led B2B teams with HubSpot or ActiveCampaign, ecommerce brands with Klaviyo, and large multi-channel apps with Braze or Iterable. Ortto suits teams that want data and journeys in one place. Whichever you shortlist, bring your lifecycle map to the demo and ask the vendor to show each trigger and exit working with your events.
