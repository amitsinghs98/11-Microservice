Which loyalty platform should you choose?

Start with Smile.io or BON Loyalty if you want a points programme live quickly on Shopify, move to LoyaltyLion or Rivo when you need deeper rules and on-site customisation, and choose Yotpo if reviews and loyalty should share one vendor. Pick Open Loyalty or Talon.One only when developers will build the experience across several channels.

  • Quick, low-risk Shopify launch: Smile.io.
  • Budget Shopify programmes: BON Loyalty.
  • Brands wanting deeper rules and analytics: LoyaltyLion.
  • Shopify brands wanting a customisable on-site experience: Rivo.
  • Loyalty alongside reviews and UGC: Yotpo.
  • Headless or omnichannel programmes: Open Loyalty.
  • Promotions and loyalty from one rules engine: Talon.One.

Who is this list for, and when does loyalty make sense?

It's for ecommerce teams deciding whether to launch, replace or redesign a loyalty programme. Loyalty makes sense when customers naturally buy more than once, such as consumables, apparel or beauty, and much less sense for one-off purchases like furniture.

The question isn't which app has the most features. It's whether points, tiers and rewards change behaviour enough to cover their cost. Feature counts barely matter if the programme only rewards people who would have bought anyway.

Will a loyalty programme pay for itself?

A programme pays for itself when the margin on extra repeat orders exceeds the cost of rewards redeemed plus the software fee. You can estimate that with four inputs before you talk to any vendor.

The inputs are your monthly repeat orders, your average gross margin per order, the share of redemptions you expect, and the reward value per redemption. A hypothetical example: a store with 1,000 repeat orders a month at a gross margin of $25 per order considers a programme. If it lifts repeat orders by 5%, that's 50 extra orders, or $1,250 in extra margin. If 300 customers a month redeem a $5 reward, rewards cost $1,500, so this design loses money before the software fee. Lower the reward, raise the earning threshold or target tiers at higher spenders, then rerun the numbers.

Also track points liability: unredeemed points are a promise to give future discounts. Ask whether the platform reports outstanding points and supports expiry rules.

What are the strengths and limits of each loyalty platform?

The Shopify apps are quick and cheap but differ in how far you can customise rules and design. The engines can model almost any programme but need developers and a clear spec.

Smile.io: best for a quick Shopify launch

Smile.io offers points, referrals and VIP programmes and lists a free plan alongside paid tiers. Smile's own comparison pages place VIP tiers on higher plans, so check which plan your design needs. Limitations: more advanced features sit on paid tiers, so map your programme design to a plan before launch. Skip it if you need a fully custom experience.

BON Loyalty: best for budget programmes

BON is a Shopify loyalty and referral app aimed at smaller stores that want points and rewards without a large monthly fee. Limitations: check its reporting and integrations against your email and finance needs before you commit. Skip it if you need liability reporting for finance. See its Shopify App Store listing.

LoyaltyLion: best for rules depth and analytics

LoyaltyLion says it prices by average monthly orders, and its help center describes a free plan for Shopify stores. It suits brands that want more control over earning rules and programme reporting than a basic points app offers. Limitations: costs scale with orders, so confirm how sustained overage is handled. Skip it if you want the cheapest possible start.

Rivo: best for a customisable on-site experience

Rivo is a Shopify-native loyalty platform focused on customising the on-site loyalty experience. Limitations: Shopify only. Skip it if you run another storefront. See rivo.io.

Yotpo: best for loyalty alongside reviews

Yotpo sells loyalty and referrals beside reviews and UGC, so one vendor can show points balances on review requests and reward reviews with points. Limitations: the value depends on using more than one Yotpo product. Skip it if you're happy with your current reviews tool. See yotpo.com.

Open Loyalty: best for headless and omnichannel programmes

Open Loyalty is an API-first loyalty engine for brands that want to design their own front end across app, web and stores. Limitations: you build and maintain the customer experience. Skip it if you lack developer capacity. See openloyalty.io.

Talon.One: best for promotions and loyalty in one engine

Talon.One is a rules engine for coupons, promotions, loyalty and referrals, used where discount logic must be consistent across channels. Limitations: it's an engineering project. Skip it if a standard points app covers your needs. See talon.one.

Do loyalty members buy more, or were they going to anyway?

Members usually look more valuable than non-members, but much of that gap is self-selection, because your best customers join first. Only a holdout or staged launch separates the programme's effect from the customer's.

How do you stop loyalty abuse?

Most abuse comes from duplicate accounts farming sign-up points and from points earned on orders that are later refunded. Check that a platform can reverse points on refunds and cancellations and cap earning per customer.

  • Reverse points automatically on refunds, returns and cancelled orders.
  • Award sign-up points only after a first order, not on account creation.
  • Set expiry rules so liability doesn't build indefinitely.
  • Review unusual earners each month.

What mistakes do brands make when buying loyalty software?

The biggest mistake is designing rewards before running the economics, so the programme gives margin away to people who'd have bought anyway. The second is launching without connecting loyalty data to email, where most reminders happen.

  • Copying a competitor's earn rate without checking your own margins.
  • Ignoring points liability in finance reporting.
  • Not sending points balances to your email platform; see our list of email platforms for ecommerce.
  • Measuring success by member count instead of incremental margin.

What else do buyers ask about ecommerce loyalty software?

These are the questions that come up once a programme moves from idea to budget. Short answers follow.

Points or tiers: which should a small brand start with?

Points are simpler to explain and launch. Tiers work better once you have enough high-spending repeat customers to make status meaningful.

Should loyalty and referrals be in the same tool?

It's convenient when one app covers both, because rewards share a balance. Dedicated referral tools offer more fraud and attribution control.

What is points liability?

It's the value of points customers have earned but not yet redeemed. It represents future discounts, so finance teams often want it reported and capped with expiry rules.

Can I switch loyalty platforms without losing balances?

Usually, if you export each customer's points balance and tier and import them before launch. Tell members about the change in advance.

What's the verdict?

Run the viability model first; if the numbers work, Smile.io or LoyaltyLion will suit most Shopify brands, Yotpo makes sense when you'll also use its reviews, and Open Loyalty or Talon.One fit brands with developers and several channels. If the numbers don't work, a referral programme or better post-purchase email may be the cheaper retention lever.
