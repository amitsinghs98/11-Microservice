Quick answer

  • Large keyword sets with daily updates and unlimited users: AccuRanker.
  • Lowest cost per client for daily tracking inside a suite: SE Ranking.
  • Agencies already paying for Semrush: Semrush Position Tracking, if the keyword allowance fits.
  • Teams on Ahrefs that can live with weekly updates: Ahrefs Rank Tracker.
  • White-label reporting from a standalone tracker: Nightwatch.
  • Freelancers and very small agencies: Wincher.
  • Local pack and map grids: BrightLocal or Local Falcon, alongside an organic tracker.

What agency fit means for a rank tracker

A single in-house site can use almost any tracker. Once you report on many clients, six things start to decide the bill and the workload:

  • Keyword quota: and whether a second device, location or search engine counts as another keyword.
  • Update frequency: daily, weekly or on demand.
  • Location granularity: country, city or postcode level.
  • Competitors per project: clients ask "who is beating us?" every month.
  • Client workspaces and seats: separating clients and giving them view-only access.
  • White-label reports: your logo, your domain and scheduled delivery.

The trackers

AccuRanker

Best for: agencies tracking thousands of keywords that want every team member and client logged in. What it does: daily rank tracking with tagging, advanced filtering, search intent, Search Console and Analytics connections, and reporting exports; higher tiers add dynamic tagging, a read API and Looker Studio connection.

Pricing: the AccuRanker pricing page lists Professional from $224 a month at 2,000 keywords (with 3,000 and 5,000 options) and Expert from $764 a month at 10,000 keywords. Unlimited users are included, and annual payment carries a 10% discount (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: no seat arithmetic, and a clear keyword-based price ladder. Limitations: it only tracks rankings, so research, audits and links need other tools. Skip it if your whole portfolio is under a few hundred keywords.

SE Ranking

Best for: small and mid-sized agencies that want daily tracking plus research and audits in one bill. What it does: rank tracking across major search engines, competitor tracking, share of voice, a report builder and guest links for clients.

Pricing: the SE Ranking pricing page lists Core at $129 a month (10 projects, 2,000 daily keywords, 10 reports) and Growth at $279 (30 projects, 5,000 daily keywords, 30 reports). Extra keywords start at $22.40 on Core. The Agency Pack adds white-labelling and 30 client seats for $69 a month on annual billing only (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: generous projects and keywords per dollar. Limitations: white-labelling requires the annual add-on. Skip it if you need more than 30 projects without moving to a custom plan.

Semrush Position Tracking

Best for: agencies already using Semrush for research. What it does: daily tracking within the suite, with multi-location and device tracking listed from the Pro+ plan.

Pricing: the Semrush pricing page shows 500 daily keywords and 5 websites on the $139 SEO and $199 Starter plans, and 1,500 keywords and 15 websites on Pro+ at $299. Advanced is quote-based with 5,000+ keywords (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: rankings sit next to research and audit data. Limitations: keyword allowances are small for a multi-client portfolio unless you move to Advanced. Skip it if tracking is your main reason to buy. Our SE Ranking and Semrush agency comparison sets the two side by side.

Ahrefs Rank Tracker

Best for: Ahrefs customers who report monthly and do not need daily data. What it does: tracks keyword positions and competitors per project.

Pricing: the Ahrefs plan comparison lists 750, 2,000 and 5,000 tracked keywords on Lite ($129), Standard ($249) and Advanced ($449), with weekly updates on all plans and 20 competitors per project (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: no separate subscription if you already pay for Ahrefs. Limitations: weekly updates hide short-term volatility. Skip it if clients expect daily movement reports.

Nightwatch

Best for: agencies wanting a standalone tracker with white-label reporting. What it does: rank tracking with local and device segmentation and client reporting. Pricing is tiered by keyword volume; see the Nightwatch website. Details were not verified for this draft. Skip it if you need research tools in the same subscription.

Wincher

Best for: freelancers and small agencies who want simple daily tracking. What it does: a lightweight rank tracker with competitor monitoring. Pricing is tiered by keywords and websites; see the Wincher website. Details were not verified. Skip it if you need extensive white-labelling or very large keyword sets.

BrightLocal

Best for: local agencies tracking many locations. What it does: local rank tracking and grid tracking alongside citations, reviews and Google Business Profile reporting. See the BrightLocal pricing page; figures not verified. Skip it if your clients are national ecommerce or SaaS brands.

Local Falcon

Best for: visualising map-pack visibility across a grid of points around a business. What it does: runs geo-grid scans and reports how a listing ranks at each point. Pricing is usage-based; see Local Falcon. Details not verified. Skip it if you only need standard organic rankings.

Local and map-pack tracking is a separate job

Standard organic tracking reports one position for a keyword in one location. Local pack results change block by block, so a dentist may rank first at the practice and fifth two miles away. Grid tools like Local Falcon and BrightLocal sample many points; organic trackers do not.

Some suites now include both. SE Ranking, for example, lists a Local and Map Rank Tracker with 1,500 keywords and three locations on its Core and Growth plans (checked 4 November 2026). Before buying, decide whether clients need organic rank, local pack rank, grid visibility or location-level reporting. Alerts, exports and client reports

Alerting is easy to overlook in a demo. Check whether you can set per-client thresholds, such as a drop of more than five positions on a priority keyword, and where alerts go. If the tracker only emails, a workflow tool can route alerts into Slack or a task board; our workflow automation tools for agencies cover that.

For reporting, confirm scheduled PDF or link-based reports, branding options, CSV exports and Looker Studio or API access. Many agencies push rankings into a dedicated reporting layer instead; see our agency SEO reporting software comparison.

Mistakes and questions to ask

  • Does a mobile result, extra city or second search engine count as a separate keyword?
  • Is every keyword updated daily, or only some?
  • How many competitors can each project track?
  • Can clients get view-only access without using a paid seat?
  • Is white-labelling included or tied to annual billing?
  • Can you import historical rankings from your current tool?

A common mistake is tracking every keyword a client ever mentioned. Track the terms tied to revenue pages, and review the list quarterly. Another is buying a standalone tracker on top of a suite that already tracks enough; our agency SEO stack guide helps spot that overlap. Finally, connect ranking reports to the pipeline they influence; a CRM built for agency client accounts makes that link easier to show.

FAQs

Is daily rank tracking necessary?

For monthly client reporting, weekly data is often enough. Daily data helps during migrations, algorithm updates and competitive launches, when you need to see changes quickly.

Why do tracked positions differ from what I see in Google?

Personalisation, location and device all change results. Trackers check from defined locations without personal history, so treat them as a consistent benchmark.

Should we track rankings in AI answers too?

That is a different measurement with its own tools and caveats; several suites now sell AI prompt tracking separately from keyword tracking.

Verdict

Based on the product information reviewed, SE Ranking delivers the most daily tracking capacity per dollar among suites, while AccuRanker is the cleanest choice when keyword volumes are high and many people need access. Semrush and Ahrefs trackers make sense mainly for agencies already paying for those suites, with Ahrefs suited to weekly reporting. Add a grid tool if local pack visibility matters, and size every option against your real keyword, location and device counts rather than the plan name.
