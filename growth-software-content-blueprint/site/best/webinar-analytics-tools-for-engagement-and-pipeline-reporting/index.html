Which webinar analytics software should you choose?

Choose ON24 or Goldcast if webinar data has to arrive in your CRM as account-level engagement that sales will act on, and choose Livestorm, Demio or BigMarker if you mainly need clean per-attendee reports and a dependable CRM sync at a lower commitment. Zoom Webinars suits teams that already run Zoom and want serviceable attendance reporting without adding another vendor.

The deciding factor for most B2B teams isn't the dashboard inside the webinar tool. It's whether attendance duration, poll answers and questions reach the contact record in your CRM in a form that someone can filter, score and report on. Webinar analytics software that stops at a registration count tells you how good your promotion was, not whether the event moved pipeline.

  • Enterprise programme reporting: ON24, for teams running a large calendar of webinars and wanting engagement benchmarks across them.
  • Account-level pipeline focus: Goldcast, for B2B teams that judge events by account engagement in Salesforce or HubSpot.
  • Mid-market, browser-based: Livestorm, for teams that want per-attendee engagement data without an enterprise contract.
  • Already on Zoom: Zoom Webinars, for attendance and registration exports with minimal change.
  • Interactive and series events: BigMarker, for teams mixing live, automated and series webinars.
  • Lean demand-gen teams: Demio, for marketers who want simple engagement reporting and marketing-automation hand-offs.

Who needs webinar analytics beyond a registration count?

You need deeper webinar analytics as soon as someone asks what an event contributed to pipeline, because registration and attendance totals can't answer that. In-house B2B marketers who defend budget to a revenue leader, and agencies reporting webinar results to clients, feel this first.

A registration count measures the promotion. Attendance rate measures the reminder sequence and the topic. Neither tells you which attendees stayed for the pricing segment, asked about integrations or answered "evaluating this quarter" in a poll. Those signals are what sales can use, and they only exist if the platform records them per person and passes them on.

Do webinar engagement scores really predict pipeline?

Engagement scores are a useful sorting signal, but the vendor documentation we reviewed doesn't show they predict pipeline on their own, and each platform defines its score differently. Treat a score as a prompt for sales follow-up, then check it against opportunities created in your CRM over the following quarter.

What does each webinar analytics tool do well, and where does it fall short?

ON24 and Goldcast lead on turning engagement into sales-ready account signals, while Livestorm, BigMarker and Demio give lean teams per-attendee reporting for less effort. Zoom Webinars is the pragmatic choice when the priority is avoiding another vendor, not deeper analysis.

ON24: best for enterprise programme reporting

What it does: ON24 positions itself as a digital engagement platform for live, simulive and on-demand webinars, with engagement data collected across a whole programme rather than one event at a time. Its product information emphasises engagement scoring, attendee-level activity and integrations with major CRM and marketing-automation systems.

Pricing model: Quote-based, typically annual. No public price list was confirmed for this draft.

Strengths: Cross-event comparison is the reason to buy it. If you run many webinars a year, being able to compare engagement across topics and formats matters more than any single dashboard.

Limitations: It's a platform commitment, and the reporting pays off only when your team uses it consistently across events.

Skip it if you run a handful of webinars a year. You'll pay for a programme you don't have.

Goldcast: best for account-level pipeline focus

What it does: Goldcast is an event platform built for B2B marketers, and its positioning centres on connecting event engagement to accounts and pipeline in the CRM. It also promotes content repurposing of recordings.

Pricing model: Check the vendor's current plans; higher tiers are sold by quote.

Strengths: The emphasis on account-level reporting fits teams running account-based programmes, where one engaged buyer at a target account matters more than fifty anonymous attendees.

Limitations: Account-level reporting is only as good as your CRM's account matching. Messy account data produces confident-looking but misleading reports.

Skip it if your CRM hygiene is weak. Fix deduplication and account matching first.

Livestorm: best for mid-market per-attendee data

What it does: Livestorm runs webinars in the browser without a download for attendees and records registration, attendance and interaction data per person. It offers integrations with common CRMs and marketing-automation tools.

Pricing model: Tiered subscription with a free entry plan; see Livestorm's pricing page for current tiers and which integrations sit where.

Strengths: A reasonable balance of reporting depth and set-up effort for teams without a dedicated webinar operations person.

Limitations: Cross-event programme benchmarking is lighter than at the enterprise end, so you may still build comparisons in a spreadsheet or BI tool.

Skip it if you need account-level pipeline modelling inside the event tool itself.

Zoom Webinars: best if you already run Zoom

What it does: Zoom Webinars provides registration, attendance, poll and Q&A reports that you can export, plus integrations with several marketing-automation platforms.

Pricing model: Licensed by attendee capacity as part of Zoom's plan structure; check Zoom's current pricing for the capacity you need.

Strengths: Familiar to attendees and to your own presenters, with little procurement friction.

Limitations: It reports what happened; turning that into pipeline insight is work you'll do in your CRM or analytics stack.

Skip it if webinars are a core demand channel and you need engagement scoring rather than raw exports.

BigMarker: best for mixed live, automated and series webinars

What it does: BigMarker covers live, automated and recurring webinars and virtual events, with attendee reporting and CRM integrations.

Pricing model: Tiered plans, with larger audiences and enterprise features by quote.

Strengths: Useful when you report on several formats and want the data in one place.

Limitations: The breadth means more configuration, and reports are only consistent if every event is set up the same way.

Skip it if you only run occasional live sessions.

Demio: best for lean, steady-cadence teams

What it does: Demio is a browser-based webinar platform aimed at marketers, with engagement reporting and hand-offs to marketing-automation tools.

Pricing model: Tiered subscription by audience size; see Demio's pricing page.

Strengths: Simple enough that a single marketer can run and report on a monthly series.

Limitations: Less depth for multi-track or account-based reporting.

Skip it if you need enterprise governance or cross-event benchmarks.

How should you score a webinar beyond registrations?

Score each webinar on a short ladder: registrations, attendance rate, average viewing duration, the share of attendees who interacted, and opportunities influenced within a set window. Agree the window and the attribution rule before the event, not after you see the numbers.

A workable scorecard uses five fields per event: registrants, live and replay attendees, median minutes watched, attendees who asked a question or answered a poll, and opportunities created or progressed at attending accounts within, say, 90 days. That window is an editorial suggestion, not a benchmark; choose one that matches your sales cycle.

Replays deserve their own line. If most viewing happens on demand, a live-only report undercounts the event, so confirm that replay viewers are captured as contacts with duration data. Our guide to building a webinar funnel through to sales follow-up covers routing those signals to reps.

Attribution should stay humble. Multi-touch models can show webinars influenced a deal; they rarely prove the webinar caused it. Our guide to attribution without false certainty explains how to present that honestly.

What mistakes do buyers make with webinar analytics?

The biggest mistake is buying on the dashboard demo and never checking what lands in the CRM. The second is treating engagement scores from different platforms as comparable when each vendor calculates them differently.

  • Not testing the sync field by field. Ask for a sample record and check which fields map to contacts, campaigns and activities.
  • Ignoring data retention. Confirm how long attendee-level data stays available in the platform, and export it to your own systems either way.
  • Losing questions. Q&A text is often the richest buying signal; make sure it exports per attendee, not as an event-level dump.
  • Counting duplicates. Repeat registrants and colleagues sharing links inflate counts unless deduplication is handled, a problem our CRM data quality shortlist addresses.

What else do buyers ask about webinar analytics?

Most follow-up questions concern how far a CRM can stand in for webinar reporting and how to treat replay viewers. The short answers below assume you sync per-attendee data to a CRM.

Can my CRM replace dedicated webinar analytics software?

Partly. A CRM can report on campaign membership and influenced opportunities if the webinar platform syncs attendance and engagement fields, but it usually can't capture viewing duration or poll answers unless the platform sends them.

Are engagement scores comparable across webinar platforms?

No. Each vendor defines its own score from different inputs and weightings, so compare platforms on the underlying fields they export, such as minutes watched and interactions, rather than on the score.

Should replay viewers count as attendees?

Report them separately from live attendees but include both in the event total. Many B2B buyers watch on demand, so excluding replays understates an event's reach and its influence on pipeline.

How long should we keep webinar attendee data?

Keep it as long as your sales cycle and attribution window require, subject to your privacy policy and consent terms. Export it to your CRM or warehouse rather than relying on the webinar platform's own retention.

What's the verdict on webinar analytics tools?

For pipeline reporting, ON24 and Goldcast offer the most direct path from engagement to sales action, provided your CRM data is clean enough to support account-level reporting. Livestorm, Demio and BigMarker are sensible, lower-commitment choices for per-attendee reporting, and Zoom Webinars is fine if you'll do the analysis elsewhere.
