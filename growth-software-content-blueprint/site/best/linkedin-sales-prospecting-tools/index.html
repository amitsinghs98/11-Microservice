Which LinkedIn prospecting tool should you choose?

Buy LinkedIn Sales Navigator first if LinkedIn is where you research buyers, because it's LinkedIn's own product and the only one built on full access to its search and relationship data. Add one browser extension for the job Sales Navigator doesn't do well, such as CRM logging (Surfe) or finding a verified email or phone (Apollo.io, Lusha, Kaspr).

  • Account and lead research, alerts and warm paths: LinkedIn Sales Navigator.
  • Saving LinkedIn profiles to the CRM without retyping: Surfe.
  • Contact data plus sequences for small teams: Apollo.io's extension.
  • Quick email and phone lookups: Lusha.
  • European contact data from the Cognism group: Kaspr.
  • Seeing LinkedIn context inside HubSpot records: HubSpot's Sales Navigator integration.

What's the difference between research tools and automation tools?

Research tools help a person find, understand and record prospects; automation tools act on LinkedIn for you by visiting profiles, sending invitations or messaging at scale. LinkedIn's User Agreement restricts bots and unauthorised automated access, so automation puts your account, and your colleagues' accounts, at risk.

The line isn't always obvious from marketing copy. A useful test is to ask what happens when you close the laptop. If the tool keeps visiting profiles, sending connection requests or scraping search results, it's automation. If it only reads what you're already looking at, enriches it from another source and saves it to your CRM when you click, it's a research and productivity tool. This page covers the second group only. Read LinkedIn's User Agreement yourself, because extensions that read profile pages can still sit in a grey area depending on how they collect data.

Multichannel sequencers that include manual LinkedIn steps (a reminder to view a profile or send a note yourself) are a different case again; our sales engagement platform guide covers them.

Which tools are worth shortlisting?

Shortlist Sales Navigator plus the single extension that fixes your biggest manual step. Here's who each one suits, and who should skip it.

LinkedIn Sales Navigator: best for research and warm introductions

Sales Navigator adds advanced lead and account filters, saved lead and account lists, alerts on job changes and company news, and visibility of shared connections that can turn a cold approach into an introduction. Higher tiers add team features and CRM integration; compare them on LinkedIn's Sales Navigator plan page. Limitations: it doesn't export contact lists or provide email addresses, and seats are tied to individual LinkedIn accounts. Skip it if your buyers aren't active on LinkedIn.

Surfe: best for getting LinkedIn data into the CRM

Surfe is a browser extension that lets reps save a LinkedIn profile to HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive and other CRMs, see whether a contact already exists, and enrich it with an email or phone. Plans are on the Surfe pricing page. Limitations: it's only as tidy as your CRM's duplicate rules. Skip it if you're on Sales Navigator's CRM-sync tier and that covers your needs.

Apollo.io: best for small teams that want contacts and sequences together

Apollo's Chrome extension shows contact details from Apollo's database alongside the profile you're viewing and can add the person to a sequence or CRM. Check credit allowances on the Apollo pricing page. Limitations: it's a full prospecting platform, so you may pay for features you don't use. Skip it if you only need CRM logging.

Lusha: best for occasional lookups

Lusha's extension returns emails and phone numbers for the profile you're on, which suits reps who need a number before a call. See Lusha pricing. Limitations: limited controls for teams managing shared lists. Skip it if you need account-level research.

Kaspr: best for European contact details

Kaspr, part of the Cognism group, offers a LinkedIn extension for emails and phone numbers with a European focus. Plans are on the Kaspr pricing page. Limitations: a lookup tool rather than a research platform. Skip it if most of your buyers are in North America and you already have a data provider.

HubSpot's Sales Navigator integration: best for HubSpot teams

HubSpot's integration with Sales Navigator shows LinkedIn information and InMail options inside HubSpot contact and company records, so reps don't switch tabs. Requirements are on its listing in the HubSpot App Marketplace. Limitations: it surfaces LinkedIn data rather than syncing lists both ways, and HubSpot tier requirements apply. Skip it if HubSpot isn't your CRM.

How should account lists and CRM sync work together?

Keep the CRM as the master list of target accounts and use LinkedIn lists as a working view of it, not a separate database. Sync or log only the people you've decided to pursue, with an owner and a source, so the CRM doesn't fill up with profiles nobody contacts.

  1. Define target accounts in the CRM with a tier and owner, then mirror them as saved account lists in Sales Navigator.
  2. Research inside LinkedIn using alerts and shared connections to decide who to approach and how.
  3. Log chosen contacts to the CRM through Sales Navigator's integration or an extension, matching on existing records first.
  4. Record activity (InMails, calls, meetings) against the CRM record so reporting reflects reality.
  5. Review quarterly: remove accounts that are closed or disqualified from both places.

What are the safe-use limits?

LinkedIn limits activity such as invitations and doesn't publish every threshold, so the safe approach is manual, personalised activity at a human pace and no tool that acts while you're away. Treat any vendor promise of "safe" automated volumes with suspicion, because only LinkedIn decides what triggers a restriction.

What else do B2B teams ask about LinkedIn prospecting?

The common questions concern whether Sales Navigator is necessary, how to handle shared seats and how LinkedIn prospecting relates to LinkedIn ads. Short answers follow.

Do we need Sales Navigator, or is a free LinkedIn account enough?

For occasional research, a free or Premium account can be enough. Sales Navigator earns its cost when reps work defined account lists daily and need its filters, alerts and saved lists.

Can several reps share one Sales Navigator seat?

No. Seats are tied to an individual LinkedIn account, and sharing logins puts the account at risk under LinkedIn's terms.

Are LinkedIn automation tools ever safe to use?

Tools that act on LinkedIn without the user, such as auto-inviting or auto-messaging, conflict with LinkedIn's rules on automated access. We don't recommend them, whatever limits the vendor advertises.

How does LinkedIn prospecting relate to LinkedIn ads?

They target the same accounts from different angles. Many teams run ads to their target account list so that a rep's outreach lands with a buyer who has already seen the brand.

What's the verdict?

In our editorial assessment, Sales Navigator is the foundation for any team that prospects on LinkedIn seriously, and the best companion is whichever extension removes your biggest manual step: Surfe for CRM logging, Apollo.io for contacts plus sequences, or Lusha and Kaspr for lookups. Leave automation tools out of the stack.
