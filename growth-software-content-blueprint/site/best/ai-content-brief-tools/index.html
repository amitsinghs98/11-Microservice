Three kinds of brief tool

SERP-driven brief builders (Frase, NeuronWriter) pull the current top results for a query, extract headings and questions, and help you assemble an outline quickly. They are fast and concrete but anchor you to what already ranks.

Content optimisation suites (Surfer, Clearscope, MarketMuse, Semrush) add term or topic recommendations and a score that writers can track while drafting. Our content optimisation tools comparison covers the scoring side in depth.

General AI research assistants (Perplexity, ChatGPT) do not see search volumes or ranking data, but they are useful for gathering background sources, explaining a topic to a writer and drafting questions. They need the most fact-checking.

A brief workflow from query to source list

Here is a hypothetical example using the query "how to choose a CRM for a marketing agency". The steps apply in any of the tools above.

  1. Query and intent. Review the top results yourself. If most are guides and checklists rather than product pages, the intent is informational with commercial undertones, so the brief should promise a decision method, not a sales pitch.
  2. Outline. Use the tool's heading extraction to see common sections, then add what is missing, such as client-permission needs specific to agencies.
  3. Entities and terms. Accept suggested terms (pipelines, client portals, seat pricing) only where they serve the reader; drop irrelevant ones.
  4. Questions. Collect questions from search features and forums, and keep the five that real buyers ask.
  5. Source list. List primary sources the writer must use, such as vendor documentation and pricing pages, and mark any AI-suggested sources "unverified" until an editor opens them.
  6. Brief sign-off. Add audience, angle, word range, internal links and the claims that need evidence.

The finished brief then feeds the drafting stage, covered in our shortlist of long-form AI blog writing tools.

The tools

Frase

Best for: small content teams and freelancers who want research and outline in minutes.

Frase analyses top results for a query, surfaces headings and questions, and lets you build an outline before drafting with AI assistance and an optimisation score. Strengths are speed and research aggregation. The limitation is that briefs can mirror competitors too closely unless an editor adds an original angle. Skip it if you need enterprise permissions. Pricing is a tiered subscription; see Frase's pricing page and the official Frase site.

Surfer

Best for: teams that want the brief and the writer's scorecard in the same tool.

Surfer generates guidelines for structure, length and terms, and writers draft against a live content score. Strengths are workflow continuity and editor extensions. Limitations: scores are directional rather than ranking guarantees, and chasing term counts can hurt readability. Pricing is tiered with limits on content editor usage; see Surfer pricing and Surfer's website. If you are already weighing a switch, our roundup of Surfer SEO alternatives compares options by publishing volume.

Clearscope

Best for: agencies and editorial teams that value simple, shareable reports.

Clearscope produces term recommendations and a content grade, with integrations into Google Docs and WordPress so writers work where they already draft. Its strength is clarity for non-specialist writers. Its limitation is price relative to budget tools, and it offers less site-wide topic planning than MarketMuse. Skip it if you produce only a handful of articles a month. See Clearscope pricing and Clearscope's site.

MarketMuse

Best for: content strategists planning topic coverage across a large site.

MarketMuse models topics, inventories existing content and suggests what to create or update, alongside detailed briefs. Its strength is the site-level view. Its limitation is a steeper learning curve and more value at higher tiers. Skip it if you need a quick per-article brief only. See MarketMuse pricing and the MarketMuse website.

Semrush content tools

Best for: teams already using Semrush for keyword research.

Semrush includes topic research and writing-assistant features that draw on its own keyword data, with Google Docs and WordPress add-ons. The strength is keeping keyword data and briefing in one subscription. The limitation is that packaging changes, and content features may sit in a separate toolkit, so confirm what your plan includes on Semrush's pricing page or the Semrush website.

NeuronWriter

Best for: cost-conscious freelancers and small agencies.

NeuronWriter offers SERP-based term suggestions, competitor outline views and AI drafting help at a lower price point than premium suites, based on its product pages. Limitations: a less polished collaboration layer and fewer enterprise controls. Pricing is tiered by analyses and projects; check the official NeuronWriter site.

Perplexity

Best for: gathering cited background sources before a brief is written.

Perplexity answers questions with linked citations and offers deeper research modes. That makes it useful for the source-list step. It has no ranking or volume data, and citations still need opening and checking. Free and paid plans are listed on the Perplexity website.

ChatGPT

Best for: turning messy research into a structured brief draft.

ChatGPT can summarise sources, propose outlines and draft question lists, and its research features compile longer reports. Without SERP data it cannot tell you what ranks, so pair it with a SERP tool. See OpenAI's plan comparison. Editor QA and fact-check rules

  • Every source in the brief must be opened by a person before it reaches a writer. AI-suggested URLs can be wrong or misattributed.
  • Statistics need a primary source and a date. If neither exists, cut the statistic.
  • Suggested terms are prompts, not requirements. Remove any that do not fit the reader's intent.
  • Competitor headings show coverage gaps, not a template. The brief must state what this article adds.
  • Record which claims need evidence so the drafting stage cannot skip them.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

The common mistakes are paying for two optimisation suites, treating a content score as the goal, and letting writers receive briefs with unchecked sources. Ask vendors: How many briefs or analyses does each plan include? Can we share briefs with freelancers who have no seat? Which export formats are supported? Which search engines and locations does the SERP data cover?

FAQs

Can a chatbot replace a SERP-driven brief tool?

Not fully. A chatbot can organise research and draft an outline, but it does not show you what currently ranks, so you would be guessing at search intent.

What content score should writers aim for?

Use the score as a coverage check rather than a target. A readable article that answers the query beats one padded to hit a number.

How long should an SEO brief be?

Long enough to state intent, angle, outline, required sources and claims needing evidence. For most articles, that fits on one or two pages.

Verdict

Choose the tool type first. SERP-driven builders like Frase and NeuronWriter are fast and affordable; optimisation suites like Surfer, Clearscope, MarketMuse and Semrush suit teams that want a scorecard or site-level planning; research assistants like Perplexity and ChatGPT support the source list but cannot replace search data. Whatever you pick, an editor should sign off every brief before drafting begins.
