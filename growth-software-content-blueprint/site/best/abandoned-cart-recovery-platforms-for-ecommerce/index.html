Which cart recovery platform should you choose?

Choose a tool that shares one customer profile across email and SMS, so recovery messages are coordinated and stop as soon as someone buys. Klaviyo or Omnisend covers most brands; add a specialist SMS tool only if SMS is a major revenue channel.

  • Email and SMS on one profile: Klaviyo.
  • Email, SMS and push in one workflow: Omnisend.
  • Enterprise SMS-first programmes: Attentive.
  • Shopify SMS specialist: Postscript.
  • Capturing emails before recovery: Privy.
  • Managed Shopify SMS: Recart.
  • Recovery run as a service: Rejoiner.
  • Native baseline: Shopify's abandoned checkout emails.

When and through which channel should recovery messages go out?

A common pattern is a first reminder within a few hours, a second the next day, and a final message a few days later, stopping immediately on purchase. Use email as the default and SMS only for subscribers who consented to texts, because texts cost more per message and feel more intrusive.

  • Email: low cost per message, room for product images and reviews.
  • SMS: fast and visible, but needs separate consent and costs more per send.
  • Onsite reminders: a returning visitor sees their cart on the next visit; no consent message is needed, but it only works if they come back.
  • Retargeting ads: reach anonymous visitors, but attribution is murky and ad platforms count their own conversions.

Coordinate them: one shopper shouldn't get an email, a text and an ad for the same cart within the same hour. Checkout friction causes many abandonments in the first place, so read our list of checkout optimisation software before you add more messages.

What consent do you need for cart recovery texts and emails?

For marketing texts in the US, you generally need prior express written consent; the FCC's rules define it as a signed written agreement that authorises the seller to send advertising or telemarketing messages using an autodialer to a specified number (checked 4 November 2026). Email rules are different and vary by country, with stricter opt-in requirements in places such as the EU and UK.

These rules have been revised and litigated, so have counsel review your consent wording. Store consent per channel with a timestamp, honour opt-outs immediately across every tool, and set frequency caps. Our list of consent and preference management platforms covers tools that record consent.

How much revenue do recovery flows really recover?

Less than most dashboards suggest, because some shoppers would have come back and bought anyway. Platform "recovered revenue" counts every purchase after a message within the attribution window, which isn't the same as the revenue the message caused.

What does cart recovery really cost?

The real costs are the platform fee, per-message SMS costs, and the margin you give away in discounts to people who would have bought anyway. The discount can be the largest of the three, so test recovery without one first.

Hypothetical example: 2,000 carts are abandoned a month at an average value of $80. If a three-message flow is linked to 200 purchases, that's $16,000 in attributed revenue. If a holdout group shows 120 of those shoppers would have bought anyway, only 80 orders ($6,400) were caused by the flow. A 10% discount given to all 200 buyers costs $1,600, a quarter of the incremental revenue, mostly to people who needed no incentive. Replace these numbers with your own before you judge any tool.

What are the strengths and limits of each platform?

Recovery flows look alike; the differences are identity capture, cross-channel suppression and how each tool counts recovered revenue. Each block names who should choose it and who should skip it.

Klaviyo: best for brands that want email and SMS recovery on one customer profile

Klaviyo runs abandoned cart, abandoned checkout and browse abandonment flows from the same customer profile as the rest of your email and SMS, with branching by cart value, product and past behaviour. Its free plan covers 250 active profiles and 500 email sends a month, and the pricing page lists paid email plans from $20 a month for 251–500 active profiles (both checked 4 November 2026). Paid costs scale with active profiles, so price it at your list size, not your starting size.

For reporting, Klaviyo's default attribution window is five days for email opens and clicks, using last touch, and the window can be changed (checked 4 November 2026). That matters when you compare its recovered-revenue figure with another tool's.

Limitations: it's a broad marketing platform, so you're buying far more than cart recovery; costs grow with your active profile count.

Skip it if you only want a single recovery email and don't plan to build lifecycle marketing.

Omnisend: best for smaller brands wanting email, SMS and push in one workflow

Omnisend offers prebuilt abandoned cart and abandoned checkout automations that can combine email, SMS and push steps in one sequence (checked 5 November 2026). It suits small and mid-sized stores that want multichannel recovery without a large setup project. Check the Omnisend pricing page for current tiers and SMS credit terms.

Limitations: less depth in segmentation and data tooling than Klaviyo for larger, data-heavy teams.

Skip it if you need advanced customer data modelling across many sources.

Attentive: best for larger brands where SMS is the primary recovery channel

Attentive is an SMS and email platform aimed at mid-market and enterprise brands, with sign-up units for capturing phone numbers, triggered cart and browse abandonment messages, and managed strategy services. Pricing isn't quoted here; see the official Attentive site.

Limitations: enterprise contracts and message costs need careful modelling, and running email elsewhere means coordinating suppression across two tools.

Skip it if your SMS list is small and you're not ready to commit to a contract.

Postscript: best for Shopify brands focused on SMS

Postscript is a Shopify-focused SMS marketing platform with abandoned cart and checkout automations, list growth tools and compliance features; ask which sending-time controls it offers. It suits Shopify brands that want a specialist SMS tool next to a separate email platform. See the official Postscript site for current pricing.

Limitations: SMS only, so email recovery and suppression must be coordinated with your email tool.

Skip it if you aren't on Shopify or want one platform for every channel.

Privy: best for small stores that need to capture emails before they can recover carts

Privy combines on-site pop-ups and forms with email and SMS, including cart reminder flows. Its main value is identity capture: you can't send a cart email to an anonymous visitor. See the official Privy site for current plans.

Limitations: lighter automation and reporting than dedicated email platforms.

Skip it if you already capture most visitors' emails and need deeper flows.

Recart: best for Shopify brands wanting a managed SMS programme

Recart is a Shopify SMS tool with capture pop-ups and cart and browse recovery, and it positions itself around done-for-you programme management. It suits lean teams that want SMS run with little internal effort. See the official Recart site.

Limitations: it's built for Shopify; check how much control you keep over message frequency and discounts in a managed setup.

Skip it if you want to run SMS strategy fully in-house.

Rejoiner: best for brands that want recovery managed as a service

Rejoiner combines email software with a managed service that builds and runs lifecycle and cart recovery programmes. It suits brands with no in-house email specialist. See the official Rejoiner site for engagement terms.

Limitations: you depend on the service team's capacity and approach; ask who owns the flows if you leave.

Skip it if you have an email marketer on staff.

Shopify abandoned checkout emails: best for stores starting from zero

Shopify can send automated abandoned checkout emails to customers who entered an email address at checkout (checked 5 November 2026). It's a sensible baseline before you add a dedicated platform; check the Help Center for setup and sending costs.

Limitations: expect less branching, reporting and SMS coordination than a dedicated email and SMS platform; check the Help Center for which abandonment automations are available.

Skip it if you already run a dedicated email platform; use its flows instead and turn the native email off to avoid duplicates.

What mistakes do brands make with cart recovery?

The most expensive mistake is putting a discount in the first message, which trains shoppers to abandon carts on purpose. The second is running recovery from two tools that don't share suppression, so buyers keep getting reminders.

  • Comparing tools on their own recovered-revenue dashboards with different attribution windows.
  • Texting people who only consented to email.
  • Ignoring identity resolution across devices; a customer data platform can help, as covered in our list of ecommerce CDPs for retention teams.
  • Never running a holdout, so nobody knows the true incremental value.

What else do buyers ask about abandoned cart recovery software?

Most questions are about discounts, timing and whether a separate tool is needed. Short answers follow; confirm plan specifics on each vendor's site.

Should every cart recovery message include a discount?

No. Start without one and add an incentive only in a later message, if at all, because early discounts reward shoppers who would have bought anyway and teach others to wait.

How many cart recovery messages should I send?

Two or three messages over a few days is a common pattern. Stop immediately when the shopper buys, and test fewer messages before adding more.

Can I recover carts from anonymous visitors?

Not by email or SMS, because you don't know who they are. Capture emails earlier with sign-up forms, or use retargeting ads and onsite reminders for anonymous visitors.

Do I need a separate cart recovery tool if I already use an email platform?

Usually not. Most ecommerce email platforms include cart and checkout abandonment flows, and a separate tool adds a second set of suppression rules to manage.

What's the verdict?

Most brands should run cart recovery from the email and SMS platform they already use, with Klaviyo or Omnisend as the main shortlist, and add Attentive or Postscript only if SMS is central. Start with Shopify's native emails if you have nothing yet.
