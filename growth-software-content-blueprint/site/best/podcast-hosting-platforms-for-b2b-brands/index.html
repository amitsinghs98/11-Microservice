Which podcast hosting platform should a B2B brand choose?

Transistor and Captivate are the strongest starting points for most B2B shows because both are built around multiple shows, team access and private feeds, while Casted suits marketing teams that want the podcast inside a wider B2B content programme. Buzzsprout is the simplest choice for a single show, and Libsyn, Simplecast and Podbean cover longer-established, monetisation-minded or private-podcast needs.

Among podcast hosting platforms for B2B, the questions that matter differ from those of consumer creators. You care about owning the RSS feed, sharing access with an agency, running private feeds for customers or employees, and getting download numbers you can explain to finance, more than about ad marketplaces.

  • Several shows, one team: Transistor
  • Growth and marketing tools built in: Captivate
  • Podcast as part of a B2B content engine: Casted
  • One show, minimal admin: Buzzsprout
  • Established hosting with broad distribution: Libsyn
  • Network-style analytics and teams: Simplecast
  • Private and internal podcasts: Podbean

What does a podcast host do, and what does it not do?

A podcast host stores your audio files, generates the RSS feed that apps such as Apple Podcasts and Spotify read, and reports downloads; it doesn't record or edit episodes or find you an audience. Treat recording, editing and guest booking as separate purchases even when a host bundles some of them.

That separation matters when you compare prices, because a host bundling editing tools may look expensive next to a pure host, or cheap next to a host plus an editor. Are podcast download numbers trustworthy enough to report?

Downloads are an imperfect proxy for listening, and hosts can count them differently, so they're defensible only if you know the counting method behind them. Ask each host whether its download counting follows the IAB Tech Lab podcast measurement guidelines, and report trends over time rather than single-episode totals.

What does each podcast host do well, and where does it fall short?

Transistor and Captivate balance team features with simplicity, Casted adds B2B content workflows as a broader platform, and Buzzsprout trades flexibility for simplicity. Libsyn, Simplecast and Podbean each fit narrower situations well.

Transistor: best for several shows and one team

Transistor lets one account run multiple podcasts and invite team members, and it supports private podcasts for internal or customer audiences. Strengths: suits a brand running a main show plus a customer or employee feed. Limitations: it's a host, not a production suite. Skip it if you want editing tools bundled in.

Captivate: best for marketing-led shows

Captivate positions itself around podcast growth, with multiple shows, team access, private podcasting, website tools and dynamic insertion for promotional segments. Strengths: dynamic insertion lets you update calls to action across your back catalogue. Limitations: check which growth features sit on which tier. Skip it if you only need a feed and basic stats.

Casted: best for podcasts inside a B2B content programme

Casted describes itself as a B2B marketing platform built around podcasts and video, combining hosting with content hubs, clips and integrations with marketing systems. Strengths: designed for reusing each episode across channels. Limitations: it's a platform purchase rather than a basic host. Skip it if the podcast is a side project.

Buzzsprout: best for one show with minimal admin

Buzzsprout is aimed at making a single show simple to publish; check its pricing page for how plans are metered and what any free plan keeps hosted. Strengths: approachable for a first show. Limitations: confirm how it handles several shows and team access before committing. Skip it if you run several feeds.

Libsyn: best for established shows wanting broad distribution

Libsyn is a long-established podcast host with distribution and monetisation options. Strengths: an established platform. Limitations: check how its plans are metered if you publish long episodes. Skip it if you want a modern team workflow above all.

Simplecast: best for teams wanting deeper analytics

Simplecast emphasises analytics, team roles and embeddable players, with enterprise options for larger publishers. Strengths: analytics presentation that is easier to share internally. Limitations: check how seats and shows scale with plan. Skip it if you only need a basic feed and simple stats.

Podbean: best for private and internal podcasts

Podbean offers public hosting alongside business plans for private and internal podcasts. Strengths: a path for internal communications as well as marketing. Limitations: many features target consumer creators. Skip it if you want a lean, B2B-focused tool.

How do you keep ownership and switch hosts safely?

Keep the show's RSS feed under your company's account, publish on your own domain where the host allows it, and confirm the host supports a permanent 301 redirect of the feed before you sign. Those three steps let you change hosts without losing subscribers.

Before switching, ask the new host to import your back catalogue and the old host to redirect the feed to the new URL; podcast apps follow that redirect. Then check episode artwork, transcripts and show notes, and keep your old analytics exports, because download history doesn't move between hosts.

Questions to ask on migration: who owns the feed and email associated with it, how long the old host keeps the redirect active, whether dynamic-insertion segments carry over, and whether private-feed subscribers must re-subscribe.

Which host fits your B2B podcast scenario?

Match the host to the shape of your programme: number of shows, whether any feed is private, and whether an agency needs access. The right answer for a founder's interview show differs from a company running customer, partner and employee feeds.

  • Multi-show brand programme: Transistor, Captivate or Simplecast, with agency access on team seats.
  • Podcast as a content source: Casted, or any host paired with a tool from our content repurposing workflow shortlist.
  • Internal or customer-only shows: Transistor, Captivate or Podbean private feeds.

What else do B2B teams ask about podcast hosting?

Most questions concern switching hosts, private feeds and free plans. The answers below assume you want to keep long-term ownership of the show.

Can we move our podcast to another host later?

Usually yes, if the old host redirects your RSS feed to the new one so podcast apps follow it. Check that redirect support, and export your analytics first, because download history doesn't transfer.

Do B2B podcasts need private feeds?

Only if you have an audience you don't want public, such as customers, partners or employees. Private feeds are a strong reason to choose a host that supports them well.

What is dynamic insertion useful for in B2B?

It lets you swap promotional segments across old episodes, so a back catalogue promotes your current event or offer rather than an expired one. Check whether the host restricts it to certain plans.

Should we use a free podcast host?

A free plan is fine for a pilot, but check what happens to episodes, analytics and the feed if you stop paying or move. For a branded show, own the feed on a paid plan from the start.

What's the verdict on podcast hosting for B2B brands?

Transistor and Captivate are the most balanced shortlist for B2B teams, Casted is worth it only if you'll use its content workflows, and Buzzsprout is a fine low-admin choice for a single show. Whatever you pick, secure feed ownership and redirect support before you sign.
