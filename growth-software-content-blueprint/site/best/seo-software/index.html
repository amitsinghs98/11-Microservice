Quick answer

Most SEO software decisions come down to one question: which job should a single subscription own, and which jobs deserve a specialist? The picks below follow the selection criteria explained in the methodology section, not a universal ranking.

  • Broadest all-in-one suite, including PPC and local: Semrush.
  • Backlink-led research and content discovery: Ahrefs.
  • Small agencies watching per-client cost: SE Ranking.
  • In-house teams wanting a gentler learning curve: Moz Pro.
  • Hands-on technical audits on a desktop: Screaming Frog SEO Spider.
  • High-volume daily rank tracking: AccuRanker.
  • Multi-location and local service businesses: BrightLocal.
  • Free first-party performance data for any site you own: Google Search Console.

If you only need one function, start with the specialist guide for it: keyword research tools for agencies, rank trackers built for multi-client work, technical audit tools for large sites or backlink analysis tools for link builders.

How we chose and what we checked

We scored each platform against the jobs an SEO team actually performs, then weighed the limits that tend to force an upgrade. The rubric:

  • Coverage of core jobs: keyword research, backlink analysis, site audits, rank tracking and reporting.
  • Limits that bite: projects, tracked keywords, users, crawl allowances, export rows and report counts.
  • Total team cost: the price once extra seats, reporting add-ons and white-labelling are included, not the headline figure.
  • Fit for the buyer: agency, in-house, local or ecommerce workflows, plus the skill level each tool assumes.

Evidence came from official pricing pages, product documentation and vendor materials. No hands-on testing was performed for this draft. Prices and limits marked "checked 4 November 2026" were read from the vendor's own pricing page on that date; anything else is described qualitatively with a link to the official source. The platforms, one by one

Semrush

Best for: agencies and in-house teams that want keyword research, competitor analysis, audits, rank tracking, local and paid search data in one account.

What it does: Semrush is a broad marketing suite. Its feature menu covers keyword research, backlink analysis, site audits, rank tracking, local SEO, content tools, marketing reports and, more recently, AI search visibility tracking.

Pricing: the Semrush pricing page lists an SEO plan at $139 a month (5 websites, 500 keywords tracked daily), Starter at $199, and Pro+ at $299 with 15 websites and 1,500 daily keywords. Advanced is quote-based. Annual billing saves up to 17%, extra users start at $45 a month, and white-label reporting sits in a $20-a-month Pro Report add-on (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: the breadth of the toolset, including PPC and local data that rival suites handle less fully, makes it a credible single platform for a generalist agency.

Limitations: the bill grows with add-ons. Seats, branded reports and higher tracking limits are each priced separately, so the headline plan rarely matches what a team pays.

Who should not buy: teams that only need rank tracking or only need backlink data, and solo operators who will never touch the PPC or content modules. A specialist will usually cost less.

Before you choose: compare its agency economics against a lower-cost suite in our SE Ranking versus Semrush breakdown, and see Semrush alternatives grouped by switching reason.

Ahrefs

Best for: link builders, content strategists and consultants who lean heavily on backlink and competitor content data.

What it does: Ahrefs combines Site Explorer for backlink and organic research, Keywords Explorer, Site Audit, Rank Tracker and Content Explorer, with newer AI visibility features under Brand Radar.

Pricing: the Ahrefs pricing page lists Lite at $129 a month, Standard at $249, Advanced at $449 and Enterprise at $1,499 a month with an annual commitment. Each self-serve plan includes one user; extra users cost $40, $60 or $80 a month depending on plan, and Lite carries 1,000 credits per user per month (checked 4 November 2026). A $29 Starter plan and a free tier for verified site owners also exist.

Strengths: link research is the product's centre of gravity, and Standard and above add Content Explorer, Batch Analysis and broken-backlink reports.

Limitations: the plan comparison lists weekly rank-tracking updates on every plan, which matters for agencies that report daily movements. Report Builder is a separate $99-a-month add-on.

Who should not buy: agencies whose client reporting depends on daily rankings, and budget-limited teams that need several people logged in at once.

Before you choose: if Ahrefs is close but not right, our guide to Ahrefs alternatives by job maps each function to a narrower tool.

SE Ranking

Best for: small and mid-sized agencies that need many projects, daily rank tracking and client-ready reporting without enterprise pricing.

What it does: rank tracking, keyword and competitor research, backlink research and monitoring, website audits, local marketing tools and a report builder.

Pricing: the SE Ranking subscription page lists Core at $129 a month (10 projects, 1 manager seat, 2,000 daily keywords) and Growth at $279 (30 projects, 3 seats, 5,000 daily keywords). Annual billing saves 20%. White-label reporting and client seats come in an Agency Pack add-on at $69 a month, annual billing only (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: project and keyword allowances are generous for the price, and the Growth plan includes three seats rather than charging for each one.

Limitations: in our assessment it is a strong generalist rather than the deepest source for any single dataset. Teams that live inside backlink or PPC data may find a specialist more thorough.

Who should not buy: enterprise teams that need governance features such as SSO and audit logs on a self-serve plan, and link-building shops that need the largest possible link index.

Moz Pro

Best for: in-house marketers and small teams who want a clear interface and well-known authority metrics.

What it does: Moz Pro bundles Keyword Explorer, Link Explorer, site crawls, rank tracking and on-page grading. Its Domain Authority score is widely referenced, though it is Moz's own metric, not a Google signal.

Pricing: tiered monthly plans with limits on tracked keywords, keyword queries and seats. Figures were not verified for this draft; see the Moz Pro pricing page.

Strengths: approachable reporting and a large library of educational material make it easier for non-specialists.

Limitations: agencies with dozens of clients should check campaign and query caps carefully before committing.

Who should not buy: PPC-heavy teams and agencies that want white-label client portals as a core feature.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider

Best for: technical SEOs who want full control of crawl configuration.

What it does: a desktop crawler that finds broken links, redirects, duplicate content, metadata issues, hreflang problems and structured-data errors, with JavaScript rendering and integrations for Google Analytics, Search Console and PageSpeed Insights.

Pricing: the Screaming Frog pricing page shows a free version capped at 500 URLs and a paid licence at €245 per user per year, with lower per-licence rates from five licences upward (checked 4 November 2026; USD and GBP prices are also listed).

Strengths: low annual cost and very deep configuration. Crawl size is limited mainly by your machine's memory and storage.

Limitations: it is not a keyword or backlink tool, and desktop crawling makes team collaboration harder than a cloud crawler.

Who should not buy: non-technical marketers who want prioritised to-do lists rather than raw crawl data.

AccuRanker

Best for: agencies and in-house teams tracking thousands of keywords daily.

What it does: dedicated rank tracking with tagging, filtering, search intent labels, Search Console and Analytics connections, and reporting exports.

Pricing: the AccuRanker pricing page lists the Professional plan from $224 a month for 2,000 keywords and Expert from $764 a month for 10,000 keywords, with daily updates, unlimited users and a 10% discount for annual payment (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: unlimited users and daily updates on every plan simplify agency budgeting.

Limitations: you still need separate tools for research, links and audits.

Who should not buy: small sites tracking a few hundred keywords, where a suite's built-in tracker is cheaper.

BrightLocal

Best for: local agencies and multi-location brands.

What it does: local rank and grid tracking, citation building and auditing, review monitoring and Google Business Profile reporting.

Pricing: tiered plans scaled by locations and features; see the BrightLocal pricing page. Figures were not verified for this draft.

Strengths: it is built around location-level work that general suites often treat as an add-on.

Limitations: thin for national keyword research, backlink analysis or large technical audits.

Who should not buy: ecommerce or SaaS teams without physical locations. Our local SEO software guide for multi-location businesses compares it with other local options.

Google Search Console

Best for: every team, as the free baseline.

What it does: reports the queries, clicks, impressions and pages Google associates with a verified property, plus indexing, links and experience reports.

Pricing: free from Google Search Console.

Strengths: first-party data that no third-party estimate replaces.

Limitations: it shows only sites you own, with no competitor research and limited history.

Who should not buy: nobody needs to skip it; just don't treat it as a complete stack. Our free SEO tools roundup covers what else costs nothing.

What a three-person team really pays

Hypothetical scenario: a small agency with three people who all need logins, about 12 client sites, and branded monthly reports. Figures use the monthly prices checked on 4 November 2026 and exclude tax.

  • Semrush Pro+: $299 plus two extra users from $45 each, plus the $20 Pro Report add-on, comes to at least $409 a month. The 15-website allowance covers 12 clients.
  • Ahrefs Standard: $249 plus two users at $60 each is $369; adding Report Builder at $99 brings it to $468 a month, with weekly rank updates.
  • SE Ranking Growth: three seats are included. On annual billing it is $223.20 a month plus the $69 Agency Pack, about $292 a month equivalent, paid upfront.

The cheapest headline price does not always produce the cheapest team bill, which is why our SEO software pricing guide for 5, 10 and 20 clients models these scenarios in more detail. For agencies, the reporting layer can also be bought separately; see client reporting software for SEO agencies.

Recommendations by scenario

Solo consultant or two-person agency

One suite plus Search Console is usually enough. SE Ranking Core or an Ahrefs entry plan keeps costs predictable; add Screaming Frog when an audit outgrows the suite's crawler.

Agency with 10 to 30 clients

Seat and project limits now drive cost. Price the full team on SE Ranking Growth and Semrush Pro+ side by side, and consider a dedicated tracker if keyword counts climb past the suite's allowance. Agencies at this size also need a client pipeline; our CRM options for marketing agencies cover that side.

In-house marketing team

Pick the suite your team will open weekly. Moz Pro or Semrush suit generalists; pair either with an analytics layer from our marketing analytics software guide so organic results are reported alongside other channels.

Local business or multi-location brand

Lead with BrightLocal or a suite with location-level tracking, and keep Google Business Profile work in the same workflow.

Ecommerce store

Large catalogues with faceted navigation need a crawler that handles scale, so budget for Screaming Frog or a cloud crawler alongside Semrush or Ahrefs for category-keyword research. Content teams may also want a content optimization tool for product and category copy.

Specialist workflows

Link builders should start from Ahrefs or a dedicated link index; teams tracking brand mentions in AI answers should read our AI search visibility tools comparison before paying for a suite's add-on.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

The most common error is buying overlapping suites. Two all-in-one platforms duplicate most of each other's core jobs, so a second suite rarely earns its cost. Map jobs to tools first using our guide to building an SEO stack without duplicate features.

  • Does the plan count a client site as a project, and do verified and unverified sites count differently?
  • Are tracked keywords updated daily or weekly, and do mobile results or extra locations count separately?
  • How much does each extra user cost, and is there a cap?
  • Is white-label reporting included or an add-on, and is it tied to annual billing?
  • What happens to historical data if you downgrade or cancel?

FAQs

Do I need an all-in-one suite or separate tools?

Most small teams get more value from one suite plus free Google tools. Separate tools make sense once a single job, such as rank tracking for thousands of keywords, outgrows what the suite includes.

Is Semrush or Ahrefs better for an agency?

It depends on the workflow. Semrush covers more channels; Ahrefs centres on link and content research. The detailed answer is in the comparison linked above.

Are third-party traffic and authority scores accurate?

They are estimates built from each vendor's own data. Use them to compare sites within one tool, and use Search Console for your own sites' real performance.

How often should we review our SEO subscriptions?

Re-check plan limits and prices every quarter, and before every annual renewal, because vendors change tiers and add-ons frequently.

Verdict

Based on the product information reviewed, Semrush is the most complete single suite, Ahrefs is the natural home for link-led research, and SE Ranking offers the most favourable agency economics among the three. Specialists such as Screaming Frog, AccuRanker and BrightLocal earn their place when one job outgrows a suite. Whatever you choose, keep Search Console connected, price the whole team rather than the headline plan, and revisit the decision as your client list changes.
