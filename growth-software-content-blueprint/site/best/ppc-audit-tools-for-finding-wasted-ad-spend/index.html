Best PPC Audit Tools for Finding Wasted Ad Spend

PPC audit tools are good at one thing: scanning an account faster than a person can and flagging where spend is leaking. They're bad at knowing your margins, your lead quality and why a campaign was built the way it was, so treat every recommendation as a hypothesis to check.

Which PPC audit tool should you choose?

Agencies auditing many Google Ads accounts should compare Optmyzr and Adalysis, which pair audits with ongoing monitoring and multi-account views. Smaller in-house teams can get far with the free native recommendations, Tag Assistant for tracking checks, and Opteo if they want a guided list of fixes.

  • Free first pass inside the platform: Google Ads Recommendations and optimization score, and Microsoft Advertising's recommendations.
  • Tracking gaps and tag problems: Google Tag Assistant.
  • Agencies wanting deep, scriptable audits across accounts: Optmyzr.
  • Structured audit checks with ad testing and alerts: Adalysis.
  • Lean teams that want prioritised, one-click improvements: Opteo.
  • A quick prospect-level snapshot: WordStream's Google Ads Grader.

What counts as wasted ad spend in a PPC audit?

Wasted spend is money that couldn't reasonably have produced a valuable outcome: irrelevant search queries, traffic you can't measure, budget spent outside the schedule or geography you intended, and clicks landing on pages that don't match the ad. Spend that produced low-quality leads counts too, but only your CRM can show it.

A useful audit covers six areas, and the tools below differ in how many they reach:

  1. Query waste: search terms with spend and no conversions, broad match drift and missing negatives.
  2. Tracking gaps: missing or duplicate conversion actions, primary goals counting micro-conversions, and tags not firing.
  3. Budget anomalies: sudden cost spikes, campaigns limited by budget while others underspend, and pacing off plan.
  4. Account structure: overlapping campaigns competing for the same queries, and ad groups mixing intents.
  5. Policy issues: disapproved or limited ads and assets quietly losing delivery.
  6. Landing-page mismatch: broken URLs, slow pages and offers that don't match the ad.

Invalid clicks are a separate problem with separate tools; our guide to ad fraud detection software covers that.

What does each audit tool catch, and when should you skip it?

Each block covers best fit, which waste areas the tool reaches and its main blind spot. Confirm current features and supported platforms on the official pages.

Google Ads Recommendations: best for a free first pass

Google Ads surfaces recommendations and an optimization score inside the account, covering items such as keywords, bidding, ads and assets, and some measurement setup. It's the fastest way to spot disapprovals and missing assets. Read it critically: the score measures adoption of Google's suggestions, not profitability, and suggestions like broader match types or higher budgets can increase spend. Skip auto-apply unless you've reviewed each recommendation type.

Microsoft Advertising recommendations: best for Microsoft Ads accounts

Microsoft Advertising offers its own recommendations inside the platform. If you import campaigns from Google, audit the Microsoft side separately, because imported settings, negatives and budgets often drift. It's limited to Microsoft's network.

Google Tag Assistant: best for tracking gaps

Tag Assistant lets you debug whether Google tags and conversion events fire on your site. Tracking gaps make every other audit unreliable, because a campaign that looks wasteful may simply be under-measured. Use it before trusting any waste report. It doesn't analyse spend.

Optmyzr: best for agencies auditing many accounts

Optmyzr combines account audits and diagnostics with rule-based optimization, scripts, budget monitoring and reporting across Google, Microsoft and other platforms. For agencies, the value is running the same checks across every client and catching problems as they return. Its breadth takes time to learn. Skip it if you manage one small account. Our PPC management software for agencies guide compares it on day-to-day management.

Adalysis: best for structured audit checks and ad testing

Adalysis runs recurring audit checks on search accounts and pairs them with ad testing and performance alerts. That suits teams who want a standing list of issues by account and a disciplined way to retire weak ads. It's centred on search, so look elsewhere for paid social audits.

Opteo: best for lean teams wanting a guided fix list

Opteo presents improvements as a prioritised list you can review and push to the account, alongside monitoring alerts. It's approachable for in-house marketers who manage Google Ads part-time. The trade-off is less control than a custom rule engine, and you still need to judge each suggestion against business context.

WordStream Google Ads Grader: best for a quick snapshot

WordStream's Google Ads Grader produces a one-off report on a connected account. It's handy for a prospect conversation or a first look at an inherited account. It has no ongoing monitoring or history, so it can't tell you whether waste is new or chronic.

Which manual checks should you run before applying audit recommendations?

Confirm tracking, business context and history before you change anything, because an audit tool sees the account but not the business. Ten minutes of manual checking stops you pausing the campaign that drives phone sales or adding a negative that blocks your best query.

  1. Verify conversions: check primary conversion actions, values and recent tag health before calling anything wasteful.
  2. Check offline outcomes: look at CRM lead quality and phone calls; call tracking often reveals conversions the account never saw.
  3. Read the change history: find out whether a "problem" was a deliberate test.
  4. Review search terms yourself: scan the flagged queries before bulk-adding negatives, and check for conflicts with active keywords.
  5. Visit the landing pages: load them on mobile, check the offer matches the ad, and compare against the patterns in our guide to landing page builders for paid campaigns.
  6. Size the fix: estimate spend affected over the last 30 days and fix the biggest items first.
  7. Log every change: record what changed, why and when you'll check it.

How much should you trust an audit tool's severity scores?

Treat them as triage, not a verdict. Based on the product information reviewed, these tools rank issues by rules and account data, but none can see your margins, sales feedback or the strategy behind a structure.

What mistakes do teams make when auditing PPC accounts?

The classic mistake is chasing the optimization score instead of profit. The second is applying a batch of fixes at once, so nobody can tell which change helped or hurt.

  • Auditing before confirming conversion tracking works.
  • Turning on auto-applied recommendations account-wide.
  • Adding broad negatives that block converting queries.
  • Ignoring post-click waste, where the click was fine but the page wasn't.
  • Running audits once a year instead of monitoring monthly.

What else do buyers ask about PPC audit tools?

Questions tend to focus on free options, auto-apply and how often to audit. Short answers follow.

Is Google's optimization score a good audit?

It's a useful checklist of Google's suggestions, but it isn't a measure of profitability or waste. Review each recommendation against your goals before applying it.

Can a free PPC grader replace a paid audit tool?

For a one-off snapshot, yes. Graders don't monitor the account over time or across clients, so they won't tell you when waste comes back.

How often should you audit a PPC account?

Run a full audit when you inherit an account or change strategy, and lighter monthly checks on search terms, tracking, budgets and policy status in between.

Should audit fixes be applied automatically?

Only for rule types you've reviewed and trust, such as clear policy or broken-URL fixes. Changes to match types, bids and budgets deserve a person's judgement and a change-log entry.

What's the verdict on PPC audit tools?

Start free with native recommendations and Tag Assistant, then add Optmyzr or Adalysis if you audit many accounts, or Opteo if one part-time marketer runs the account. Whichever you pick, run the manual checks before applying anything.
