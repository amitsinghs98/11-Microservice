Which CRM data quality tool should you choose?

Start with your CRM's native duplicate rules, then add one bulk tool matched to your platform: Insycle or Dedupely for HubSpot, Cloudingo, DemandTools or Plauti for Salesforce. Openprise only earns its cost when data flows through several systems that all need the same rules.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • HubSpot, small team: HubSpot's own tools, then Dedupely if duplicates are the main pain.
  • HubSpot or Salesforce, broad cleanup: Insycle.
  • Salesforce with an experienced admin: Validity DemandTools.
  • Salesforce without a full-time admin: Cloudingo.
  • Salesforce with strict data-residency review: Plauti Duplicate Check.
  • Many systems, complex hierarchies: Openprise.

What data quality problems do these tools actually fix?

They fix five recurring problems: duplicate records, inconsistent field values, missing values, invalid contacts and broken account hierarchies. Most tools are strong on the first two and weaker on hierarchies, which often need manual review.

Duplicates split activity history and double-count pipeline. Inconsistent fields ("US", "USA", "United States") break segmentation and routing. Missing values can sometimes be filled from other fields, but often need lead enrichment software. Invalid emails are better caught by email verification and list cleaning tools than by a dedupe tool. Broken hierarchies, such as subsidiaries not linked to parents, distort account-level reporting and ABM targeting.

What are each tool's strengths and limitations?

The order follows our criteria for safe cleanup: control over match and merge logic first, then audit and rollback, then breadth of fixes beyond duplicates. Payment plays no part in inclusion or order.

Salesforce duplicate management: the free first line of defence in Salesforce

Salesforce includes matching rules and duplicate rules that can alert or block users when they create a record that looks like an existing one, plus duplicate record sets you can review and merge. It's the place to stop new duplicates before you buy anything.

Pricing model: included with Salesforce editions; no separate tool to license.

Strengths: Prevents duplicates at entry, which is cheaper than cleaning them later.

Limitations: Native merging is record-by-record or small batches, cross-object matching (lead to contact) is limited, and there's no bulk normalisation of messy fields.

Skip it if: you have thousands of existing duplicates to clear; pair it with a bulk tool.

HubSpot data quality tools: HubSpot portals that want cleanup without another vendor

HubSpot offers a duplicate management view for contacts and companies, and its operations-focused hub (HubSpot has been repackaging this area; confirm the current product name on its pricing page) adds formatting fixes, property-issue detection and automated data-quality actions.

Pricing model: native duplicate review sits in the CRM; the deeper automation is tied to paid hub tiers. Check which tier unlocks which data-quality feature.

Strengths: No sync to maintain, and fixes apply where your team already works.

Limitations: Merge rules are less configurable than dedicated tools, and a HubSpot merge is hard to undo, so export before large batches.

Skip it if: you need custom match logic across several objects or a CRM other than HubSpot.

Insycle: HubSpot and Salesforce teams doing bulk cleanup with rules

Insycle connects to HubSpot, Salesforce and some other CRMs to find and merge duplicates with configurable match fields and master-record rules, standardise formats, fill missing values from other fields, and run those fixes on a schedule.

Pricing model: tiered subscription; see Insycle’s pricing page for which features and record volumes sit on each plan.

Strengths: Broad set of fixes beyond dedupe, with preview and history of operations, which makes it a good fit for a RevOps generalist.

Limitations: Rule configuration takes thought; a careless master-record rule can overwrite good data at scale.

Skip it if: you only need occasional one-off merges.

Dedupely: small teams that just need duplicates found and merged

Dedupely focuses on finding and merging duplicate contacts, companies and deals in HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive and similar CRMs, with fuzzy matching and an auto-merge option once you trust the rules.

Pricing model: subscription tiers by record volume; see Dedupely pricing.

Strengths: Narrow and quick to understand, which suits a team without a dedicated ops person.

Limitations: Little beyond dedupe: no deep normalisation, enrichment or hierarchy repair.

Skip it if: your bigger problem is inconsistent fields rather than duplicates.

Validity DemandTools: Salesforce admins running heavy-duty data work

DemandTools is a long-standing Salesforce data-quality suite covering dedupe, mass updates, imports, standardisation and lead-to-account matching, aimed at admins.

Pricing model: quote-based or seat-based licensing; confirm with Validity.

Strengths: Deep Salesforce control, including cross-object and lead-to-account matching.

Limitations: Salesforce only, and the interface assumes an experienced admin.

Skip it if: you run HubSpot or a smaller CRM.

Cloudingo: Salesforce teams wanting scheduled dedupe without admin-level tooling

Cloudingo finds and merges Salesforce duplicates using filters you configure, runs them on a schedule, and can check imports for duplicates before they land.

Pricing model: tiered by record volume; check Cloudingo’s pricing page.

Strengths: Approachable for Salesforce teams without a full-time admin.

Limitations: Salesforce only, and less broad than DemandTools for non-duplicate fixes.

Skip it if: you need multi-CRM support.

Plauti Duplicate Check: Salesforce orgs that want dedupe native inside the platform

Plauti Duplicate Check is a Salesforce-native app, so data doesn't leave Salesforce, covering prevention at entry, batch dedupe and merge rules.

Pricing model: per-user or edition-based licensing via the vendor; check the official page.

Strengths: Native processing helps where security review blocks external tools.

Limitations: Salesforce only; a free edition, if offered, will be limited.

Skip it if: you are not on Salesforce.

Openprise: RevOps teams orchestrating data quality across many systems

Openprise is a no-code data orchestration platform for cleansing, normalising, deduplicating, enriching and routing records across CRM and marketing automation.

Pricing model: quote-based; expect an annual contract and implementation work.

Strengths: Handles complex rules across systems, including account hierarchy and lead-to-account logic.

Limitations: More platform than a small team needs, and setup takes real time.

Skip it if: you have one CRM and fewer than tens of thousands of records.

Can an automated merge destroy good CRM data?

Yes: a merge keeps one master record and discards or overwrites values from the others, and in many CRMs that can't be cleanly reversed. The risk sits in the master-record and field-survival rules, not in the matching, so those rules deserve the most review.

What does a safe, rollback-based cleanup workflow look like?

Back up, test on a small segment, review, then run in batches with a log you can restore from. Never run a first bulk merge across the whole database.

  1. Export a full backup of the affected objects, including record IDs, owners and associations, and store it with a date.
  2. Agree match rules in writing: which fields define a duplicate (email, domain, normalised company name) and how fuzzy matching should be.
  3. Agree survival rules: which record wins (oldest, most active, owned by sales) and which field values survive (most recent, non-empty, from a trusted source).
  4. Dry-run on one segment, such as a single country or a few hundred records, and have the record owners review the preview.
  5. Run in batches with the tool's operation log on, and keep a mapping of merged IDs to surviving IDs.
  6. Check downstream systems: marketing automation, billing and integrations that store CRM IDs may need updating.
  7. Turn on prevention (native duplicate rules or the tool's entry checks) so the problem doesn't return.

If a batch goes wrong, the backup and the merged-ID mapping are your rollback; a tool's undo feature, where it exists, is a bonus, not a plan. Our guide to designing a RevOps data model helps you decide which fields should be authoritative in the first place.

Which tool fits your scenario?

Match the tool to your CRM, the size of the backlog and who will own it. A small HubSpot team and a Salesforce org with an admin need different tools.

  • Startup moving off spreadsheets: native rules plus a one-off Dedupely cleanup.
  • HubSpot team after a messy import or migration: Insycle for normalisation and merges, with a backup first.
  • Salesforce org with lead and contact duplicates: DemandTools or Cloudingo for lead-to-contact matching.
  • Multiple CRMs, marketing automation and enrichment feeds: Openprise, or fix the integration upstream; see data integration platforms for lean teams.

What mistakes do teams make when cleaning CRM data?

The biggest mistake is cleaning without fixing the source: if a form, import or integration keeps creating duplicates, the backlog returns within weeks. The second is merging on company name alone, which collapses separate subsidiaries into one account.

Also watch for enrichment overwriting values reps entered by hand, and for cleanup scheduled without telling sales, who then lose track of records mid-deal.

What else do buyers ask about CRM data quality tools?

Questions tend to focus on safety and on whether native features are enough. Here are short answers.

Are native CRM duplicate rules enough?

For preventing new duplicates in a well-run CRM, often yes. For clearing an existing backlog, normalising fields or matching leads to accounts in bulk, most teams need a dedicated tool.

Can a merged CRM record be restored?

It depends on the CRM and the tool, and often only partially. Treat a full export and a log of merged-to-surviving record IDs as your real rollback.

How often should CRM data be deduplicated?

Run prevention continuously and a scheduled dedupe pass at a cadence that matches your inflow, such as weekly for high-volume inbound and monthly or quarterly for smaller databases.

Should data quality tools also enrich records?

Some do, but enrichment overwriting trusted values is a common source of new errors. Decide which source wins for each field before connecting enrichment to a cleanup tool.

Who should own CRM data quality?

One named owner, usually in RevOps or marketing ops, should own rules and schedules, with sales leaders signing off on merge and survival rules that affect their records.

What's the verdict?

Turn on native duplicate prevention first, then buy the narrowest tool that clears your backlog: Dedupely for simple dedupe, Insycle for broad HubSpot or Salesforce cleanup, and a Salesforce-specific tool when you need deep admin control. Whatever you choose, the backup-and-batch workflow above matters more than the vendor.
