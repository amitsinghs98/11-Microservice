Quick answer

Most marketing teams end up with two layers: a general AI assistant for thinking, research and drafting, and, if volume or brand risk justifies it, a specialist platform that stores brand rules or plugs into an SEO workflow. Our editorial picks by job:

  • Long-form drafting from source material: Claude, for working through long briefs, transcripts and reference documents in one project.
  • A flexible all-round team assistant: ChatGPT, for breadth of features, file handling and custom assistants.
  • Brand governance across a large organisation: Writer, for style-guide enforcement and admin controls.
  • A marketing team that wants brand voice built in: Jasper, for stored brand voice and campaign-oriented workflows.
  • SEO-led content programmes: Surfer, for drafting inside a search-data optimisation editor.
  • Repeatable go-to-market workflows: Copy.ai, for chaining sales and marketing tasks into workflows.
  • A final editing and consistency layer: Grammarly, for tone and style checks wherever people write.

None of these removes the need for an editor. The order below follows our selection criteria, not payment.

General assistants versus writing platforms

General assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude are horizontal tools. They are strong at reasoning over material you give them, and they get new capabilities quickly, but brand rules live in whatever instructions and files each user remembers to attach. Governance is mostly an admin and data-policy question, not a writing feature.

Purpose-built platforms such as Jasper, Writer and Copy.ai wrap language models in marketing structure: saved brand voices, style rules, templates, shared knowledge and approval-friendly workspaces. You pay for that structure, usually per seat. SEO tools such as Surfer and Writesonic add search data to the drafting surface. The practical test is simple: if your problem is "people write inconsistently", a platform may pay off; if your problem is "people need help thinking and drafting", a general assistant may be enough. How we chose and the shared test prompt

We compared tools on six criteria: output control (how precisely you can steer length, structure and tone), brand voice handling, collaboration and permissions, integrations with the places marketers publish, usage limits, and cost model. Evidence came from official product pages, pricing pages and documentation. No hands-on testing was performed for this draft, so we make no claims about output quality, and vendors had no say in the order.

Because output quality is what you are buying, run the same prompt through every shortlisted tool during its trial. A common test pack we suggest:

  1. Short copy: "Write five LinkedIn ad headlines under 70 characters for [product] aimed at [role]. Avoid superlatives."
  2. Long-form: paste a 300-word brief with three supplied sources and ask for a 1,200-word draft that cites only those sources.
  3. Brand voice: supply your style guide and two approved samples, then ask for a product update email in that voice.
  4. Constraint check: ask for a claim the sources do not support and see whether the tool flags it or invents it.

Score each result for accuracy, edits needed, voice fit and time to a publishable version. The AI content quality scorecard gives a fuller rubric.

The tools

Claude (Anthropic)

Best for: writers and strategists who draft long pieces from substantial source material.

Claude is a general AI assistant. For marketers, its relevant features are Projects, which hold reference files and standing instructions, and a long context window that copes with full transcripts, research reports or previous articles in a single conversation.

  • Key capabilities: long-document analysis, drafting and rewriting, saved project knowledge, side-by-side drafts, and team workspaces with admin controls on business plans.
  • Pricing model: a free tier and paid individual and team plans priced per user, with usage limits that vary by plan. See Anthropic's pricing page.
  • Strengths: handles nuanced instructions and long inputs well, which suits briefs with many constraints.
  • Limitations: no built-in SEO data, CMS publishing or marketing templates; brand rules depend on each project being set up properly.
  • Skip it if: you need enforced brand rules across dozens of writers or search data inside the editor.

Official site: Claude by Anthropic.

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

Best for: teams that want one assistant for research, drafting, data questions and file work.

ChatGPT covers the widest spread of everyday tasks: drafting, summarising uploads, web-connected research and analysing spreadsheets. Custom GPTs and projects let a team package instructions and reference files for repeat jobs.

  • Key capabilities: chat drafting, file and data analysis, web search, custom GPTs, and workspace plans with admin and data controls.
  • Pricing model: free tier plus per-user paid plans for individuals and businesses, with model access and limits differing by tier. See OpenAI's ChatGPT pricing.
  • Strengths: breadth, frequent feature releases and a large integration ecosystem.
  • Limitations: breadth also means no opinionated marketing workflow; outputs drift toward generic phrasing unless you supply strong examples.
  • Skip it if: your main problem is inconsistent brand voice across a large team.

Official site: ChatGPT.

Writer

Best for: larger organisations where brand, legal and terminology rules must be applied consistently.

Writer is positioned as an enterprise generative AI platform. Based on the product information reviewed, its distinguishing features are style-guide and terminology enforcement, a company knowledge layer for grounding outputs, its own model family and tools for building internal AI apps.

  • Key capabilities: centrally managed style rules, approved terms, knowledge retrieval, integrations into common writing surfaces, and admin and security controls.
  • Pricing model: a lower per-user tier for small teams and enterprise pricing by quote. Check Writer's plans page for current tiers.
  • Strengths: governance is the product, not an add-on.
  • Limitations: the value depends on investing time in configuring rules and knowledge; heavy for a five-person team.
  • Skip it if: you are a small agency or founder who mainly needs drafting help.

Official site: Writer.

Jasper

Best for: in-house marketing teams producing campaign content at volume in a consistent voice.

Jasper is built for marketers rather than general users. It stores brand voices and company knowledge so that every user starts from the same context, and organises work around campaigns and marketing content types.

  • Key capabilities: brand voice and style settings, shared knowledge assets, marketing templates and workflows, a browser extension and team collaboration.
  • Pricing model: per-seat plans with a free trial and a custom-priced business tier. Check Jasper's pricing page.
  • Strengths: brand context is shared by default rather than rebuilt by each user.
  • Limitations: per-seat cost is typically higher than a general assistant, and the underlying drafting quality still depends on the models it uses.
  • Skip it if: only one or two people write and they can maintain a good prompt library themselves.

Official site: Jasper. We compare it head to head in Jasper vs Copy.ai vs Writesonic.

Surfer

Best for: content teams whose articles are judged mainly on organic search performance.

Surfer's core is a content editor that compares a draft with top-ranking pages and suggests terms, headings and length. It also offers AI article drafting inside that optimisation environment.

  • Key capabilities: SERP-based content scoring, term suggestions, AI drafting, content audits and Google Docs and WordPress extensions.
  • Pricing model: tiered subscription with limits on content editor usage and seats. See Surfer's pricing page.
  • Strengths: keeps search data and drafting in the same window.
  • Limitations: scores are directional, not ranking guarantees; it is not designed for ads, email or brand storytelling.
  • Skip it if: most of your writing is not search-driven.

Official site: Surfer. For the briefing stage, see our guide to AI tools for building SEO content briefs.

Writesonic

Best for: SEO-focused teams that also want to monitor how their brand appears in AI search answers.

Writesonic began as an AI writer and has broadened toward SEO content and AI search visibility features. Its article writing tools combine research and drafting, with brand voice settings.

  • Key capabilities: SEO article generation, brand voice, a chat assistant and AI visibility tracking features.
  • Pricing model: tiered subscriptions with usage limits. See Writesonic's pricing page.
  • Strengths: combines content production with emerging AI search monitoring in one vendor.
  • Limitations: the product scope has shifted over time, so confirm which modules your plan actually includes.
  • Skip it if: you need deep brand governance or you already pay for an SEO suite with similar features.

Official site: Writesonic.

Copy.ai

Best for: revenue teams that want to automate repeatable sales and marketing tasks, not just write copy.

Copy.ai now positions itself as a go-to-market AI platform. Its workflows chain steps, such as researching an account and drafting outreach, and it keeps shared brand and company information for those workflows.

  • Key capabilities: chat, workflow builder, brand voice, shared knowledge and integrations with sales tools.
  • Pricing model: tiered plans, with workflow usage on higher tiers. See Copy.ai's pricing page.
  • Strengths: good fit when the same writing task repeats hundreds of times.
  • Limitations: workflow design takes setup time, and long-form editorial work is not its focus.
  • Skip it if: you only need occasional ad or social copy.

Official site: Copy.ai.

Grammarly

Best for: teams that want a consistent editing layer across email, docs and browser tools.

Grammarly works where people already write. Beyond grammar and clarity, business plans add shared style guides and brand tones, and it now includes generative writing assistance.

  • Key capabilities: grammar, clarity and tone suggestions, style guides, brand tones and generative rewrites across many apps.
  • Pricing model: free tier plus per-user paid plans. See Grammarly's plans page.
  • Strengths: low adoption friction and useful as a final check on any tool's output.
  • Limitations: it is an editing assistant first; it will not plan or research an article.
  • Skip it if: you need a drafting engine rather than a polish layer.

Official site: Grammarly.

What human editors still have to do

Every tool here can produce fluent text that is wrong. Editors remain responsible for four things: verifying every factual claim against a primary source, adding the experience and examples that AI cannot supply, cutting generic filler, and checking that the piece answers the reader's actual question. Rights checks matter too, particularly for quoted material and images.

In practice, budget editing time per piece rather than assuming drafting time disappears. A clear process makes that time predictable; our guide to building an AI content workflow with editorial checks sets out roles, handoffs and approval gates.

Recommendations by scenario

Founder or solo marketer

One general assistant, plus a free editing layer, is usually enough. Add a specialist tool only once a specific bottleneck appears.

Small agency with several clients

Use project or workspace features to keep each client's voice and sources separate. Consider Jasper if account managers keep rebuilding brand context, and an SEO tool if most retainers are search-led. The AI SEO tools comparison helps if search work dominates.

In-house B2B team of 10 or more

Governance starts to matter. Writer or Jasper earns its seat cost when inconsistent messaging is a real, recurring problem; otherwise a business plan of a general assistant with shared projects may cover it.

Ecommerce growth team

Your volume is short copy: product pages, ads and email. Start with the short-form copywriting shortlist and the AI email copy tools.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

  • Buying seats for everyone on day one. Pilot with the two or three heaviest writers first.
  • Paying twice for the same model. Many platforms use the same underlying models as the general assistants you may already pay for.
  • Ignoring usage limits. Message caps, credits and word allowances shape real cost more than the headline price.
  • Assuming brand voice is automatic. Every tool needs good examples and maintenance.

Questions to ask vendors: Is our content used to train models, and can we opt out? What happens when we hit a usage limit? Can we export brand settings and templates if we leave? Which features are limited to higher tiers? For cost modelling, see the AI content tool pricing breakdown.

FAQs

Can one AI writing tool cover a whole marketing team?

Sometimes. A business plan of a general assistant covers many small teams. Specialist platforms become worthwhile when brand consistency, SEO data or repeatable workflows are the bottleneck.

Will AI-written content hurt search performance?

Search engines have said they assess content on quality and usefulness rather than how it was produced. Thin, unedited or inaccurate content is the risk, not the tool itself.

Is it safe to paste client material into these tools?

Check each vendor's data-use terms and your client contracts first. Business tiers commonly offer stronger data controls than free or individual plans, but confirm the specifics in writing.

How long should a trial run?

Long enough to put real briefs through the shared test pack above, usually one to two weeks with at least two writers involved.

Verdict

Start with the job, not the tool. For most teams, a strong general assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT handles drafting, and a specialist earns a place only when it fixes a named problem: Writer or Jasper for brand consistency, Surfer or Writesonic for search-led content, Copy.ai for repeatable go-to-market workflows, Grammarly for final polish. Whatever you choose, run the same test brief through each shortlisted option, measure editing time, and keep a human accountable for every published claim.
