Quick answer

  • High-volume agencies with many mailboxes: Instantly or Smartlead. Both are built around connecting many sending accounts to one platform; Smartlead puts more emphasis on client-facing agency features. Our Instantly vs Smartlead breakdown compares their operating costs.
  • Personalised multichannel campaigns: lemlist, when LinkedIn steps and custom visuals matter more than raw volume.
  • Agencies wanting client separation on a budget: Saleshandy or Woodpecker.
  • Small, careful programs: QuickMail or Mailshake.

If your team needs calling, task queues and manager reporting across SDRs, you are shopping for a sales engagement platform instead; see our guide to sales engagement platforms for SDR teams.

Platform vs mailbox infrastructure

A cold email platform schedules sequences, rotates senders, tracks replies and stops follow-ups when someone responds. It usually does not supply the mailboxes. Those come from Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or a third-party mailbox provider, each billed per user and each with its own acceptable-use policy and sending limits.

Most agencies send from secondary domains so that a client's primary domain is insulated from outbound reputation risk. That is a reasonable safeguard, but it is not a licence to send more: every domain still needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC, an honest sender identity and a working opt-out. Our wider explainer on building a compliant B2B outreach stack maps each layer from data to CRM.

The platform fee is therefore only part of the cost. Domains, mailbox licences, verification credits and monitoring all sit outside it, which our cold email pricing guide models per mailbox.

The platforms

Instantly

Best for: agencies running email-heavy campaigns across many connected mailboxes.

What it does: sequences, sender rotation, a warm-up network, a unified reply inbox and optional lead data and CRM modules.

Pricing model: sending plans tiered by contact and email volume, with lead data sold separately; see the Instantly pricing page.

Strengths: mailbox scale and a single inbox for replies across clients.

Limitations: warm-up networks do not substitute for relevance; reputation still depends on what you send and to whom. Add-ons for data and CRM can change the real price.

Skip it if: you need native calling or LinkedIn tasks.

Smartlead

Best for: agencies that want to give clients their own view of campaigns and replies.

What it does: unlimited-mailbox positioning, warm-up, sender rotation, a master inbox, an API and client-access features.

Pricing model: tiered by active leads and email volume; see the Smartlead pricing page.

Strengths: agency reporting and client separation.

Limitations: the interface rewards technical operators; check which white-label features sit on which tier.

lemlist

Best for: agencies whose campaigns rely on personalisation and LinkedIn touches.

What it does: multichannel sequences across email, LinkedIn and calls, personalised images, a warm-up tool and a lead database.

Pricing model: per seat, with mailboxes and credits varying by plan; see the lemlist pricing page.

Limitations: per-seat pricing grows with every operator, and LinkedIn automation carries platform-policy risk you should assess. Our lemlist and Reply.io comparison covers multichannel trade-offs.

Saleshandy

Best for: cost-conscious agencies that want many sender accounts and client management in one place.

What it does: sequences, sender rotation, a unified inbox, a lead finder and agency client features.

Pricing model: tiered by prospects and emails; see the Saleshandy pricing page.

Limitations: fewer native CRM integrations than larger platforms; confirm your CRM is supported.

Woodpecker

Best for: European-facing agencies wanting a steady, email-first tool with an agency panel.

What it does: sequences with conditions, deliverability safeguards, bounce protection and multi-client management.

Pricing model: based on contacted prospects, with add-ons; see the Woodpecker pricing page.

Limitations: less suited to very high-volume operations than Instantly or Smartlead.

QuickMail

Best for: agencies prioritising deliverability discipline over volume.

What it does: sequences, inbox rotation, deliverability monitoring and team permissions.

Pricing model: tiered; see the QuickMail pricing page.

Limitations: a smaller ecosystem and fewer built-in data features.

Mailshake

Best for: small in-house teams that want simple sequences with optional phone and social steps.

What it does: email sequences, a lead catcher for replies, and a sales engagement tier with calling.

Pricing model: per seat; see the Mailshake pricing page.

Limitations: per-seat billing and lighter multi-client separation make it a weaker agency fit.

Opt-out, suppression and reputation duties

Cold outreach is lawful in many B2B contexts, but the rules differ by country and the sender carries the obligations. This is general information, not legal advice.

  • Identify the sender honestly. Accurate from-names, subject lines and a real business identity.
  • Offer a working opt-out in every message and honour it promptly. US CAN-SPAM rules also require a valid postal address.
  • Keep a global suppression list per client that every campaign checks, across every mailbox and platform.
  • Assess a lawful basis in GDPR and UK markets, typically legitimate interest documented in an assessment, and respect stricter rules for sole traders and individuals.
  • Watch complaint signals. Google and Yahoo publish sender requirements, including authentication and spam-rate thresholds, that apply to bulk senders.

Safe launch checklist

  1. Written client sign-off on targeting, messaging and the opt-out wording.
  2. SPF, DKIM and DMARC published and passing on every sending domain.
  3. Mailboxes warmed gradually, with conservative daily caps set in the platform.
  4. List verified; risky and catch-all addresses reviewed or removed.
  5. Suppression list imported, including existing customers and prior opt-outs.
  6. Unsubscribe link or reply-to-opt-out tested end to end.
  7. Bounce and reply thresholds that pause a campaign automatically.
  8. Reply handling assigned, with replies and opt-outs synced to the CRM.
  9. A weekly review of bounce, complaint and reply rates per mailbox.

FAQs

Can I run cold email from our main email marketing tool?

Usually not. Permission-based platforms such as those in our small-business email marketing roundup generally prohibit sending to non-opted-in lists in their terms.

Do I need one platform account per client?

Not always. Several platforms offer workspaces or client views; separate accounts give cleaner billing and data separation.

Is warm-up enough to protect deliverability?

No. Warm-up can help a new mailbox, but sustained reputation depends on list quality, relevance and low complaint rates.
