Which online course platform should a B2B marketing team choose?

For a first lead-generation course, Thinkific or LearnWorlds give you the most control over gating, quizzes and certificates without enterprise pricing; Kajabi suits founders who want the course, email and checkout in one tool. If the course is really customer onboarding at scale, a customer education platform such as Skilljar is the better fit.

Courses produce leads only when the topic sits close to a buying problem and the learner data reaches sales. The platform matters less than those two decisions.

  • Flexible course builder with certificates: Thinkific
  • Interactive lessons and branded academies: LearnWorlds
  • All-in-one course, email and checkout: Kajabi
  • Simple paid courses and checkout: Teachable
  • Lightweight courses and digital products: Podia
  • Courses inside a community: Circle
  • Customer and partner academies: Skilljar

What does each course platform do well, and where does it fall short?

The creator platforms overlap heavily, so decide on the two or three features you can't do without, usually gating, CRM sync and certificates. Then check which plan includes them.

Thinkific: best for a free lead-magnet course with certificates

Thinkific offers a course builder with quizzes, certificates, free and paid enrolment, and a library of integrations. It suits a marketing team that wants a free course with an email gate and a certificate at the end. Pricing is tiered with a free entry plan; confirm which tier includes the CRM or automation connections you need on its pricing page. Its limitation is that deeper lead routing usually depends on an integration or automation tool. Skip it if the course must sit behind your product's SSO for many customer accounts.

LearnWorlds: best for interactive, branded academies

LearnWorlds focuses on interactive video, assessments and a customizable school site, which helps a course feel like part of your brand rather than a template. It's a good fit for teams planning certifications. Check transaction fees, SSO and white-label options by tier. Skip it if you want the simplest possible setup.

Kajabi: best for course, email and checkout in one tool

Kajabi bundles courses with email marketing, landing pages and checkout, which suits a founder-led brand without a separate marketing automation stack. For a B2B team that already runs HubSpot or another automation platform, that bundle can duplicate tools you pay for. Skip it if your CRM is the system of record and you don't want a second email tool.

Teachable: best for straightforward paid courses

Teachable is a course and checkout platform aimed at selling training simply. It suits paid workshops or certifications where payment is the main gate. Check transaction fees on lower plans and how learner data reaches your CRM. Skip it if you need heavy interactivity or multi-audience academies.

Podia: best for a small catalog and digital downloads

Podia sells courses, downloads and webinars from one storefront with email built in. It's quick to launch a short course alongside templates. Assessment and certificate depth is lighter than course-first platforms. Skip it if certification is the point of the program.

Circle: best when the course should live inside a community

Circle is primarily a community platform with courses, events and spaces. It suits cohort-style programs where discussion matters as much as lessons. Course tooling is thinner than dedicated course platforms. See our comparison of online community platforms for B2B brands if community is the bigger goal.

Skilljar: best for customer and partner academies

Skilljar is a customer education platform with learning paths, certificates, multiple audiences and CRM connections, sold on a quote. It's overbuilt for a single marketing lead magnet but right when education supports onboarding and retention. When should learning content be gated, and how should data reach the CRM?

Gate the course at enrolment when the content is substantial and the audience is narrow, and leave introductory lessons open when you need reach or search visibility. Whatever you gate, sync enrolment, lesson completion and certificate status to the CRM as named fields that sales has agreed to use.

  • Open preview, gated course: the first lesson is public, enrolment requires an email address.
  • Gated certificate: lessons are open, the exam and certificate require registration.
  • Paid tier: free fundamentals, paid advanced training or certification.

Map the fields before launch: enrolled date, course name, last lesson completed, completion status and certificate. Push them through a native integration or your automation tool, and use our guide to marketing automation software for small teams for nurture after enrolment. Check exports too, so you can move learner records if you change platforms.

What should you measure beyond enrolments?

Measure completion of decision-relevant lessons, the share of learners who match your ideal customer profile, and pipeline influenced by learners within a set window. Enrolments and video views show reach, not demand.

Most creator platforms report enrolments, progress and quiz results; attribution to opportunities happens in your CRM. Agree an attribution window with sales, and report learners as a segment rather than claiming every deal they touch. What mistakes do teams make with course-led lead generation?

The biggest mistake is assuming every enrolment is a lead; the second is building a long course nobody finishes. Short, practical courses with one clear next step tend to be easier to maintain and measure.

  • Choosing a platform before deciding who owns course updates.
  • Gating everything, then wondering why search traffic never finds the course.
  • Sending all learners to sales instead of nurturing based on lessons completed.
  • Ignoring certificate and progress exports until a migration forces the issue.

What else do B2B marketers ask about course-led lead generation?

These answers cover gating, pricing and measurement. They assume a marketing team running a free or low-cost course, not a full customer academy.

Should a B2B course be free or paid?

Free courses collect more sign-ups and suit lead generation; paid courses filter for commitment and can fund the program. Many teams run a free introductory course and charge for certification or advanced training.

Is an email course enough instead of a course platform?

Yes, for a short sequence of lessons with no quizzes or certificates, your email platform can deliver it. Move to a course platform when you need progress tracking, assessments, certificates or video hosting in one place.

Which course activity signals sales readiness?

Completion of a lesson tied to a buying decision, such as implementation or pricing, is a stronger signal than enrolment. Agree the signal with sales before launch and pass it to the CRM as a field, not a vague score.

Can we move courses off a platform later?

Usually the videos and documents can be re-uploaded elsewhere, but learner progress, quiz results and certificates are harder to carry over. Check what the export includes before you build a large catalog.

What's the verdict on online course platforms for B2B lead generation?

Start with Thinkific or LearnWorlds for a gated course with certificates, pick Kajabi only if you want it to replace your email tool, and move to a customer education platform when the program serves customers more than prospects. Whichever you choose, the CRM fields and the course topic decide whether leads are qualified.
