Which Meta Ads reporting tool should you choose?

Ecommerce brands that care most about creative should look at Motion, and those that want Meta set against Shopify revenue should compare Triple Whale and Northbeam. Agencies reporting to many clients usually do better with a connector (Supermetrics or Funnel) or a client reporting platform such as AgencyAnalytics than with a Meta-only analytics tool.

  • Free starting point and source of truth for delivery data: Meta Ads Manager.
  • Creative-level reporting for teams producing lots of ads: Motion.
  • Shopify brands wanting one blended revenue view: Triple Whale.
  • Higher-spend ecommerce brands that want their own attribution model: Northbeam.
  • Meta-heavy advertisers who want reporting and automation in one place: Madgicx.
  • Analysts piping Meta data into sheets, Looker Studio or a warehouse: Supermetrics.
  • Teams normalizing Meta with many other channels: Funnel.
  • Agencies sending branded multi-channel client reports: AgencyAnalytics.

What problem does a Meta reporting tool solve that Ads Manager doesn't?

Ads Manager shows what Meta delivered and what Meta attributes to itself, inside one ad account at a time. A reporting tool adds history you own, aggregation across accounts, creative grouping and a second opinion from your store or analytics data.

That second opinion matters. Meta's conversion counts depend on the attribution setting, on browser and device signal loss, and on modelled conversions, so they rarely match your order count. A good tool doesn't pretend to fix that. It shows the platform figure and the store figure side by side and labels each one.

Watch the API caveats too. Third-party tools read Meta's data through the Marketing API, which means they inherit its rate limits, its retention window for breakdowns, and any change Meta makes to which metrics or attribution settings are exposed. If you need multi-year history, start storing it now, because no tool can backfill what the API no longer returns.

What is each tool good at, and when should you skip it?

Each block below covers best fit, the strength that earns its place and the reason to skip it. Confirm plan limits, connector refresh rates and seat rules on the official pages before you commit.

Meta Ads Manager: best for the raw delivery truth

Ads Manager is where spend, reach, frequency and Meta-attributed results originate, and its native breakdowns are the reference every other tool reads from. Its built-in reporting covers a lot of basic needs without a separate tool fee. Its limits are structural: it reports one platform's view of attribution, it's awkward across many ad accounts, and it doesn't show store revenue that Meta didn't claim. Skip it as your only tool if you report blended results or manage several brands.

Motion: best for creative-led ecommerce teams

Motion is built around creative reporting: grouping ads by creative idea so you can see which ideas work rather than which ad IDs did. That fits brands and agencies shipping many variations a week, where Ads Manager's ad-level rows become unreadable. It isn't a revenue attribution system, so pair it with store data. Skip it if you run a handful of evergreen ads.

Triple Whale: best for Shopify brands wanting a blended view

Triple Whale is an ecommerce analytics and attribution platform closely associated with Shopify stores. Its appeal is putting ad spend and orders on one screen so blended efficiency sits next to platform ROAS. Consider another option if your store isn't on Shopify, or if you need agency-style multi-client reporting more than brand-level analytics.

Northbeam: best for higher-spend brands that want their own model

Northbeam focuses on multi-touch attribution and media mix-style analysis for ecommerce, so Meta is judged by a model you configure rather than by Meta itself. That's useful when Meta, Google and other channels all claim the same orders. The trade-off is effort: the model needs clean tracking, enough volume and time before its outputs are trustworthy. Skip it if your monthly spend is small or your tracking isn't in order yet.

Madgicx: best for Meta-first advertisers wanting reporting plus automation

Madgicx centres on Meta advertising, combining reporting with optimization and automation features. That suits a brand where Meta is most of the budget and one person both reports and optimizes. Its narrower focus is the limit: if TikTok, Google and email matter equally, a cross-channel tool will serve you better. Our sister guide to paid social management platforms covers the automation side in more depth.

Supermetrics: best for analysts who build their own reports

Supermetrics is a connector: it pulls Meta data into destinations such as spreadsheets, dashboards or a warehouse (confirm current destinations on the vendor's site), and you design the report. That gives you full control of the schema and, with a warehouse destination, history you own. You also own the maintenance when Meta renames a field. Skip it if nobody on the team is comfortable building reports.

Funnel: best for normalizing Meta with many other channels

Funnel collects data from many sources and focuses on mapping and normalizing it, so Meta's metrics line up with your other channels and your store under shared definitions. It's a good fit for multi-brand ecommerce groups and larger agencies. It's more than a single-brand, Meta-only team needs.

AgencyAnalytics: best for branded multi-client reports

AgencyAnalytics is a client reporting platform for agencies; check the current pricing basis and any trial on AgencyAnalytics' pricing page, which couldn't be fetched for this draft. It suits agencies that want Meta alongside search, SEO and other channels in branded client reports; confirm white-labelling, client access and scheduling options on the vendor's site. Creative analysis is shallower than in a Meta-first tool, so creative-heavy agencies often run both.

Which tool fits an ecommerce brand versus an agency?

A single ecommerce brand should prioritise blended revenue and creative insight, so it usually pairs a store-aware tool with Ads Manager. An agency should prioritise aggregation, permissions and repeatable client reports, so it usually starts with a connector or agency platform.

  • Shopify brand, Meta is the main channel: Ads Manager plus Triple Whale or Motion, depending on whether revenue or creative is the weaker view.
  • Brand spending heavily across Meta and Google: Northbeam or Funnel, because both platforms will over-claim together.
  • Agency with 10 to 40 small clients: AgencyAnalytics or a cross-channel reporting tool, with per-client logins rather than shared ad account access.
  • Agency with an analyst and a warehouse: Supermetrics into the warehouse, with Looker Studio on top.

Permissions deserve a check either way. Connect through Meta Business Manager with a system user or a named service account where the tool supports it, so access doesn't break when an employee leaves.

What should a weekly Meta Ads report include?

A useful weekly report has four layers: spend and delivery, results under a stated attribution setting, creative performance and a reconciliation line against store revenue. Keep the schema fixed week to week so trends mean something.

  1. Header fields: week start and end, account or brand, currency, time zone and the attribution setting used (for example, the click and view windows).
  2. Delivery: spend, impressions, reach, frequency, CPM and link click-through rate, by campaign objective.
  3. Results: purchases or leads, conversion value, cost per result and platform ROAS, each labelled "Meta-attributed".
  4. Creative: top and bottom concepts by spend, with hook rate or thumb-stop proxies if your tool provides them, and the number of new creatives launched.
  5. Audience and placement: spend share and efficiency by placement and by prospecting versus retargeting.
  6. Blended view: total store revenue, new-customer revenue and blended spend-to-revenue ratio, with the difference from Meta-attributed value shown, not hidden.
  7. Notes: changes made, tracking incidents, promotions and the one test planned for next week.

If naming is inconsistent, creative grouping falls apart, so fix campaign and ad names first with a UTM and naming governance tool.

What mistakes do buyers make with Meta reporting tools?

The most common mistake is buying a dashboard before deciding which attribution setting and revenue source the business will trust. The second is assuming the tool stores history forever when it may only re-query the API.

  • Comparing tools on chart design instead of connector refresh cadence and whether recent days are re-pulled as conversions arrive.
  • Mixing attribution settings across accounts, then comparing ROAS between them.
  • Granting tools admin access through a personal Facebook profile.
  • Reporting Meta ROAS to a client without the blended figure next to it.
  • Ignoring creative naming, which makes concept-level reporting impossible later.

What else do buyers ask about Meta Ads reporting?

Most questions come back to attribution, history and access. The short answers are below.

Why does Meta report more purchases than my store?

Meta counts conversions under its own attribution windows, includes modelled conversions and can credit sales other channels also claim. Your store counts each order once, so a gap is normal and should be shown, not hidden.

Do third-party tools get different data from Ads Manager?

They read the same underlying data through Meta's Marketing API, so differences usually come from attribution settings, time zones, currency or refresh timing. Reconcile one week before trusting a new tool.

How much Meta Ads history can a reporting tool show?

It depends on whether the tool stores data or re-queries the API, and on what Meta's API still returns for older breakdowns. If you need long history, store it in a warehouse or a tool that keeps its own copy.

Should agencies give reporting tools admin access to client ad accounts?

Give the least access the tool needs, ideally through Business Manager partner access or a system user rather than a personal profile. That keeps reporting working when staff change and makes access easy to revoke.

What's the verdict on Meta Ads reporting tools?

Start with Ads Manager, then add the one view it lacks: creative insight (Motion), blended store revenue (Triple Whale or Northbeam), or multi-client reporting (AgencyAnalytics, Supermetrics or Funnel). Don't buy a tool that promises to settle attribution, because none of them can.
