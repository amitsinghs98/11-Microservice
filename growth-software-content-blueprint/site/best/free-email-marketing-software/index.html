Quick answer

Three free plans were re-checked against their official pricing pages on 4 November 2026: MailerLite, Brevo and Mailchimp. All three cap something that a growing list will hit early, but they cap different things, which is why the right pick depends on your list size and send frequency rather than on a headline "free".

  • Small list, wants real automation on day one: MailerLite Free, within its 250-subscriber and 2,500-email monthly limits.
  • Larger contact file, low send frequency: Brevo Free, which limits daily sending rather than leading with a contact cap.
  • Creator newsletter that expects to grow past a few hundred readers: Kit's free newsletter plan or EmailOctopus, after confirming current caps.
  • Shopify store testing email before committing: Klaviyo's free tier, as a stepping stone rather than a long-term home.
  • Teams already in a CRM: HubSpot's free marketing tools, if you accept the branding and the sharp jump to paid tiers.

Mailchimp Free is the most recognisable name here but, based on its current limits, the least practical for anything beyond a test list.

Free-forever plans versus free trials

A free-forever plan stays at zero cost as long as you stay inside its limits. A free trial gives access to paid features for a fixed period, then either charges you or drops you back to a reduced plan. Several vendors run both at once, which blurs the line during signup.

MailerLite's pricing page, checked 4 November 2026, offers a Free plan alongside a 14-day trial of premium features; when the trial ends, premium features switch off but data stays in the account. Mailchimp's pricing page, checked the same day, pairs its Free plan with a 14-day trial of the Standard plan. Some platforms, such as ActiveCampaign, have historically been trial-only with no free-forever tier, so check before assuming a "start free" button means a permanent plan.

The practical risk: building automations during a trial that the free plan cannot run afterwards. Note which features are trial-only before you build anything that depends on them.

The free plans, one by one

MailerLite Free

Best for: a new list under 250 people that needs a welcome sequence, a signup form and a simple landing page.

What the free plan allows: MailerLite's pricing page (checked 4 November 2026) lists 250 subscribers, 2,500 monthly emails, 2 user seats, 3 active automations with up to 5 steps each, 3 active forms, 1 website and 1 landing page, with knowledge-base support. The same page says API and MCP access on Free is limited and does not include sending.

Upgrade trigger: the subscriber cap. The pricing FAQ states that when a free account exceeds 250 active subscribers, campaign and automation sending is locked until you upgrade or remove subscribers. The Comfort plan for up to 500 subscribers was listed at $12 per month billed monthly, or $10.80 per month billed yearly, with 5,000 monthly emails (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: unusually complete feature access for a free tier, and a pricing page that explains how subscribers are counted. Limitations: the subscriber cap is low, and the five-step automation limit stops most multi-branch flows. Logo removal appears only among paid-plan inclusions.

Skip it if you are importing an existing list of more than 250 people. Official site: MailerLite pricing.

Brevo Free

Best for: a business with a larger contact file that sends infrequently, or one that also needs transactional email from the same account.

What the free plan allows: Brevo's pricing page (checked 4 November 2026) describes Free as "free forever, no credit card needed" and states that once an account is approved for sending, it can send up to 300 emails per day. The page also says all plans include transactional email features. The free plan's contact allowance was not legible in the version we retrieved, so confirm it on the pricing page.

Upgrade trigger: the daily send ceiling, or branding. The Starter plan was listed at $9 per month billed monthly, from 5,000 emails per month, with removal of the Brevo logo sold as an add-on; the Standard plan adds multi-step marketing automation, A/B testing and logo removal (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: the volume-based model suits large, quiet lists. Limitations: a daily cap means one newsletter to a list of more than 300 people has to be split across days, and branded footers remain until you pay.

Skip it if you need to send one campaign to your whole list at the same moment. Official site: Brevo pricing.

Mailchimp Free

Best for: trying the Mailchimp editor and audience tools on a very small list before choosing a paid plan.

What the free plan allows: Mailchimp's pricing page (checked 4 November 2026) states the Free plan includes 1 seat, 1 audience with a maximum of 250 contacts, and up to 500 sends per month, with a maximum of 250 per day. It also notes that sending is paused if the contact or send limit is exceeded, and that free accounts have access to only some pre-built templates.

Upgrade trigger: almost immediate. At 500 sends a month, a 250-contact list can receive two campaigns. Essentials was shown as starting at $13 per month and Standard at $20 per month after introductory offers (checked 4 November 2026); both are priced by contact tier.

Strengths: a familiar interface and a large integration ecosystem. Limitations: the tightest send allowance among the plans we could confirm. If Mailchimp's paid tiers are the sticking point, our roundup of Mailchimp alternatives by switch reason covers the options.

Skip it if you plan to send weekly. Official site: Mailchimp pricing.

Kit (free newsletter plan)

Best for: writers and creators whose main job is a regular newsletter rather than ecommerce flows.

What the free plan allows: Kit, formerly ConvertKit, offers a free newsletter plan with landing pages and forms. Its free tier has historically allowed a larger subscriber count than MailerLite or Mailchimp, while reserving most automation and integrations for paid plans. We could not re-check the current cap for this draft.

Strengths: creator-oriented tools and simple broadcast workflows. Limitations: automation depth on the free plan is limited, so sequence-heavy funnels need a paid plan. Skip it if you need ecommerce event triggers. Official site: Kit pricing.

Sender Free

Best for: budget-conscious small businesses that want automation and a relatively roomy free tier.

What the free plan allows: Sender markets a free-forever plan that includes campaigns and some automation, with sender branding on emails. Contact and send caps were not re-checked for this draft. Limitations: a smaller ecosystem of integrations and agency resources than the larger platforms. Skip it if you rely on a niche integration; confirm it exists first. Official site: Sender pricing.

EmailOctopus free plan

Best for: straightforward newsletters where low cost at scale matters more than advanced segmentation.

What the free plan allows: EmailOctopus offers a free tier with EmailOctopus branding in emails and restricted reporting history compared with paid plans. Caps were not re-checked for this draft. Limitations: automation and template options are narrower than in all-in-one suites. Skip it if you need multi-branch journeys. Official site: EmailOctopus pricing.

Klaviyo Free

Best for: a new Shopify or WooCommerce store that wants to see abandoned-cart and post-purchase flows working before paying.

What the free plan allows: Klaviyo's free tier is sized for very small audiences, with email and limited SMS allowances; we could not re-check exact figures. Klaviyo counts active profiles, so imported customers can consume the allowance quickly. Limitations: paid pricing rises with profiles, and email and SMS are billed separately. Our ecommerce email platform shortlist compares the paid alternatives. Skip it if you sell offline or have no store integration. Official site: Klaviyo pricing.

HubSpot free marketing tools

Best for: teams already using HubSpot's free CRM who want simple forms and occasional emails on the same records.

What the free plan allows: HubSpot's free tools include marketing email with HubSpot branding and a monthly send allowance; check current limits on the official page. Limitations: automation sits mainly in paid Marketing Hub tiers, and the step from free to paid is steep for a small team. The HubSpot Marketing Hub and ActiveCampaign comparison covers what you get once you pay. Skip it if email is your main channel. Official site: HubSpot Marketing Hub pricing.

Modelling a 500-subscriber newsletter

Assumptions for this hypothetical: 500 active subscribers, one newsletter per week (about 4.3 sends a month), plus a three-email welcome sequence for roughly 40 new signups a month. That is about 2,150 newsletter emails and 120 welcome emails, or roughly 2,270 sends a month.

  • MailerLite Free: the 2,500 monthly emails would cover the volume, but 500 subscribers exceeds the 250-subscriber cap, so sending would lock. The first realistic plan is Comfort at the 500-subscriber tier.
  • Brevo Free: a 300-per-day ceiling means each weekly send must be split across two days. Workable for a newsletter that is not time-sensitive; awkward for launches.
  • Mailchimp Free: both the 250-contact cap and the 500 monthly sends rule it out.
  • Kit, Sender, EmailOctopus: these have historically offered free caps above 500 subscribers. Confirm the current numbers before relying on this, because they were not re-checked for this draft.

The lesson: at 500 subscribers, the first upgrade trigger is usually the contact cap, not the send cap. Migration warning: automations do not travel

Contacts export as a CSV on every platform here, but automations, segments built on platform-specific events, form designs and templates generally do not. A welcome sequence built in one tool has to be rebuilt by hand in the next, and engagement history is often lost or flattened into tags.

Before building on a free plan, keep a plain document of every automation: trigger, delays, conditions and email copy. Export contacts with consent source, signup date and tags at least quarterly. Mistakes and questions to ask

  • Counting all contacts instead of active ones. MailerLite, for example, counts any address active at any point in the billing cycle, even if deleted later (pricing FAQ, checked 4 November 2026).
  • Assuming free means unbranded. Ask whether a vendor footer or logo appears and what removing it costs.
  • Ignoring sending authentication. Free plans still need a properly authenticated sending domain; the deliverability tool roundup explains what to monitor.
  • Building flows during a trial that the free plan cannot run.

Questions for any vendor: What happens on the day I exceed the cap? Is sending paused or am I charged automatically? Which features are trial-only? Can I export automations in any form?

FAQs

Is there a free email marketing plan with no limits?

No. Every free plan reviewed limits contacts, sends, features or all three. The useful question is which limit you will hit first.

Do free plans hurt deliverability?

Not inherently. Free accounts usually send from shared infrastructure, and your own list quality and domain authentication matter more than the plan tier.

Can I use a free email plan for cold outreach?

Generally no. Marketing platforms expect opted-in lists and their acceptable-use policies typically prohibit purchased lists. Outbound needs different tooling, covered in our cold email software guide.

Should an agency put clients on free plans?

Only if the client owns the account and understands the upgrade trigger. Account ownership disputes are harder to fix than a small subscription.

Verdict

For a list under 250 that needs automation, MailerLite Free offers the most complete confirmed feature set. For a bigger, quieter list, Brevo Free's daily limit is easier to live with than a contact cap. Mailchimp Free works only as a sandbox. For creator newsletters aiming past 500 readers, verify Kit's and EmailOctopus's current caps; they may let you wait longest before paying.

Whichever you choose, document your automations from day one so that the eventual move to a paid plan, or a different platform, is a rebuild you planned for rather than a surprise.
