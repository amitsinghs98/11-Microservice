Which marketing data integration tool should you choose?

If your destination is a dashboard or spreadsheet, pick a marketing-specific connector tool such as Supermetrics, Windsor.ai or Funnel; if your destination is a warehouse that also holds CRM and product data, a general ELT tool such as Fivetran or Airbyte fits better. The split is about who maintains the pipeline: marketers or a data engineer.

  • Marketers reporting in Looker Studio, Sheets or Excel: Supermetrics or Windsor.ai.
  • Agencies and brands that want harmonized cross-channel ad data: Funnel.
  • Teams loading ads, CRM and app data into one warehouse with minimal upkeep: Fivetran.
  • Budget-conscious teams with engineering help, or a need to self-host: Airbyte.
  • Larger marketing teams needing governance and transformation before data lands: Adverity.

Do you need a connector tool or full data engineering?

A lean team needs a connector tool if the job is pulling ad, email, analytics and commerce data into one place on a schedule. You need data engineering when you're joining that data with CRM and product records, managing schema changes, and building models other tools depend on.

Marketing connector tools hide most of the complexity: they map each ad platform's metrics to common fields, handle API changes and refresh dashboards. General ELT tools copy raw tables into a warehouse and leave modeling to you, usually in SQL or dbt. That's more powerful and more work. If you also need to send warehouse results back into your CRM or ad platforms, that's a separate leg covered in our guide to reverse ETL tools.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The marketing-specific tools are faster to set up and easier for marketers to own. The general ELT tools are better foundations once a warehouse joins marketing with CRM and product data.

Supermetrics: best for marketers reporting in BI tools and spreadsheets

Supermetrics connects ad, analytics, social and CRM sources to destinations such as Looker Studio, Google Sheets and Excel. The pricing page sets out the current plans, which weren't confirmed for this draft, so check what each tier includes. Strength: marketers can run it without engineering. Limitation: check which plan includes warehouse destinations before you plan a move to a warehouse. Skip it if a warehouse is your main destination from day one.

Windsor.ai: best for small teams building simple multi-channel dashboards

Windsor.ai offers marketing connectors, and its pricing explainer describes plans based on the data sources and accounts you connect. Strength: a simple path to multi-channel dashboards. Limitation: fewer governance and transformation controls than enterprise tools, and you should check how the vendor counts ad accounts across clients. Skip it if you need a formal data model and audit trails.

Funnel: best for harmonized cross-channel ad data

Funnel stores marketing data, maps metrics across platforms and sends it to BI tools and warehouses. Its help article on Flexpoints explains how plans are sized in capacity units. Strength: harmonized naming across platforms saves analyst time. Limitation: check which plan includes warehouse exports and whether a minimum capacity applies. Skip it if you only report on two channels.

Fivetran: best for low-maintenance warehouse loading

Fivetran runs managed connectors that copy source tables into your warehouse and adjust to schema changes. Its usage-based pricing documentation bills on monthly active rows (MAR), a count of the distinct rows synced each month. Strength: little pipeline maintenance once set up. Limitation: ad and email tables with many updated rows can drive MAR up, and raw tables still need modeling. Skip it if nobody will write SQL on the other end.

Airbyte: best when you have engineering help or need to self-host

Airbyte offers a self-managed open-source version and a Cloud service. Its Cloud release notes track product and plan changes, so check current terms carefully. Strength: broad connector library and the option to run it yourself. Limitation: self-hosting shifts monitoring and upgrades onto your team, and connector maintenance levels vary, so check who supports the connectors you need. Skip it if you have no engineer to own it.

Adverity: consider it for governed, larger marketing teams

Adverity is a marketing data platform with transformation and data quality features before data reaches its destination. Its pricing page sets out its current plans, which weren't confirmed for this draft. Strength: governance features suit teams with many brands or markets. Limitation: its governance depth and onboarding are more than a lean team usually needs. Skip it if you're a team of one or two.

Do the numbers from these tools match the ad platforms?

Usually close, but rarely exact: attribution windows, time zones, currency conversion and late-arriving conversions all cause gaps between the connector's data and the platform's own interface. Treat small discrepancies as expected and document which source wins.

Which five source systems should you connect first?

Connect the systems that hold spend, traffic, leads and revenue first: your main ad platform, your web analytics, your CRM, your email platform and, for ecommerce, your store. Everything else can wait until those five reconcile.

  1. Primary ad platform (Google Ads, Meta or LinkedIn): spend, clicks, conversions by campaign.
  2. Web analytics (GA4 or an alternative): sessions and conversions by source and medium.
  3. CRM: leads, opportunities and revenue with original source fields.
  4. Email platform: sends, clicks and attributed conversions.
  5. Commerce or billing system: orders or subscriptions, the revenue of record.

For each, record the sync frequency you need, how far back to backfill, who can access the data, and which alert should fire on failure. Once the five reconcile, the analysis layer is where marketing analytics software earns its place.

What mistakes do lean teams make with data integration?

The usual mistakes are connecting every source at once, choosing on connector count, and ignoring schema changes until a dashboard breaks. Starting narrow and monitoring early avoids most of the rework.

  • Using an integration tool where a workflow tool fits. Moving single events between apps, like a form fill into the CRM, belongs in a tool from our list of workflow automation tools.
  • Skipping alerts. Expired ad-platform tokens are a common silent failure.
  • No backfill plan. Decide how much history you need before choosing a plan that limits it.
  • Forgetting warehouse costs. If you load into a warehouse, storage and query costs are a separate bill; see our guide to warehouses for small marketing analytics teams.

What else do buyers ask about marketing data integration tools?

These are the questions that come up most often once the first connectors are live. Confirm plan details on each official page.

Can I use Looker Studio connectors instead of a warehouse?

Yes, for straightforward reporting across a few channels. A warehouse becomes worthwhile when you need to join marketing data with CRM or revenue data, or keep long history.

What is the difference between ETL and ELT?

ETL transforms data before loading it, while ELT loads raw data into the warehouse and transforms it there. Most modern warehouse tools use ELT.

How often should marketing data sync?

Daily is enough for most reporting, with a few refreshes a day for active campaign management. Faster schedules can raise costs or hit plan limits.

Do these tools handle schema changes automatically?

Managed ELT tools generally adapt to new columns and some field changes, while connector tools map fields for you. Check how each tool alerts you when a source API changes.

What's the verdict?

Lean teams reporting in dashboards should start with Supermetrics or Windsor.ai, and move to Funnel when cross-channel harmonization matters. Teams building a warehouse should choose Fivetran for low maintenance or Airbyte when engineering time is cheaper than subscription spend.
