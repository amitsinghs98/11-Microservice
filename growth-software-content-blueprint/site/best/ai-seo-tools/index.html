Quick answer

  • One suite for research plus AI assistance: Semrush or Ahrefs, both built on their own keyword and link datasets.
  • SERP-based drafting and optimization: Surfer, with Frase as the lighter, lower-cost route to briefs.
  • Site-wide topic planning for large content libraries: MarketMuse.
  • Internal linking: InLinks for entity-driven linking, Link Whisper for WordPress sites.
  • AI-assisted analysis of crawl data: Screaming Frog SEO Spider connected to an AI provider with your own API key.

Pick by the job that eats most of your team's hours, not by how much AI a product advertises.

How we chose

We scored each product against six tasks from the client brief: keyword and SERP research, content briefs, on-page optimization, internal linking, content refreshes and AI search visibility. For each, we asked whether the feature relies on measured data (rankings, search volume estimates, crawls, links) or on generated output from a language model.

Evidence came from official product pages, pricing pages and documentation. No hands-on testing was performed. Prices change often in this category, and we could not verify current figures for these vendors on 4 November 2026, so we describe pricing models and link each official page. The order below follows the workflow, not a ranking, and no vendor paid for placement.

The tools

Semrush

Best for: teams that want keyword research, competitive data, content help and AI visibility reporting from one vendor.

What it does: Semrush pairs its keyword, ranking and audit databases with AI-assisted features, including an assistant that surfaces recommendations, content writing and optimization tools, and a separately sold toolkit for monitoring AI search visibility. Check which features sit in the core plan and which are add-ons.

Pricing: tiered per-user subscription with paid toolkits; see the Semrush pricing page.

Limitations: add-ons make total cost hard to predict. Consider another option if you only need a content editor.

Ahrefs

Best for: link-led SEO teams that want AI assistance on top of strong backlink and keyword data.

What it does: Ahrefs has added AI-assisted content features and Brand Radar, which reports how often a brand is mentioned in AI-generated answers. Brand Radar is positioned as a large dataset rather than a tracker of prompts you write yourself; confirm how it samples before relying on it.

Pricing: tiered subscription with paid add-ons; see the Ahrefs pricing page.

Limitations: content-editor workflows are less central than in Surfer or Clearscope.

Surfer

Best for: content teams that brief, draft and optimize articles against current search results.

What it does: Surfer's Content Editor scores a draft against patterns in top-ranking pages (terms, structure, length) and offers AI drafting. It also audits existing URLs for refresh opportunities and sells an AI visibility tracker.

Pricing: tiered subscription; see Surfer's pricing page.

Limitations: chasing a higher score can produce bloated, repetitive copy. Our page on content optimization tools and how to read their scores explains why scores are directional.

Frase

Best for: smaller teams that want fast SERP research and outlines without a large suite.

What it does: Frase pulls headings and questions from ranking pages into an outline builder, then adds AI writing and optimization guidance.

Pricing: tiered subscription; see the Frase pricing page.

Limitations: it does not replace a keyword database, so pair it with a research tool. Our roundup of AI tools for SEO content briefs compares it with alternatives.

MarketMuse

Best for: publishers and B2B teams with hundreds of pages who need to decide what to write, update or merge next.

What it does: MarketMuse models topics across a site's content inventory, highlights coverage gaps and produces briefs.

Pricing: tiered, with higher tiers through sales; see the MarketMuse pricing page.

Skip it if you publish a few posts a month; planning at this depth pays off on large libraries.

InLinks

Best for: sites that want entity-based internal links and schema suggestions applied at scale.

What it does: InLinks analyses the entities each page covers and proposes or inserts internal links and structured data.

Pricing: subscription tied to usage; see the InLinks website.

Before you choose: check how links are inserted (script versus CMS edits) and what happens to them if you cancel.

Link Whisper

Best for: WordPress publishers who want internal link suggestions inside the editor.

What it does: a plugin that suggests links between posts and reports orphaned content.

Pricing: licence by number of sites; see the Link Whisper website.

Skip it if your site is not on WordPress.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider

Best for: technical SEOs who want to run AI prompts against crawled pages, such as classifying page types or drafting missing meta descriptions.

What it does: the desktop crawler can connect to AI providers through your own API key, so prompts run on real crawl data. The AI provider bills usage separately.

Pricing: annual licence per user, with a limited free mode; see the SEO Spider page.

Limitations: setup requires technical confidence, and generated output still needs sampling and review.

Where the human checkpoint goes

Put a named reviewer between AI output and anything that ships. A practical sequence:

  1. After the brief: confirm search intent and that the SERP sample matches your market and language.
  2. After the draft: check every factual claim, statistic and product detail against a primary source.
  3. Before publishing: read for brand voice and remove term-stuffing added to raise a score.
  4. Before applying bulk links or meta changes: review a sample of 20 suggestions first.

Recommendations by scenario

In-house team of two or three

One research suite (Semrush or Ahrefs) plus one content editor (Surfer or Frase) covers most needs. Add internal-link tooling only after a crawl shows orphaned pages.

Agency producing content for many clients

Prioritise per-seat cost, shareable editor links and brief templates. Pull keyword data from your existing keyword research tool rather than paying twice.

Brand worried about AI answers

Treat AI visibility as a separate monitoring job with its own prompt set. Our comparison of GEO and AI search visibility trackers covers sampling and refresh cadence.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

  • Paying for duplicate AI writers. Many teams already have a general assistant; check what the SEO tool adds beyond it.
  • Treating a content score as a ranking forecast. It measures similarity to a sample.
  • Skipping the data question. Ask where keyword volumes, SERP samples and visibility answers come from, and how often they refresh.

Questions for sales: Which AI features use credits, and what happens when they run out? Can we choose the country and language of the SERP sample? Is our content used to train models?

FAQs

Are AI SEO tools safe to use for published content?

They can be, if a person checks facts and intent before publishing. The risk lies in unreviewed output, not in the tooling itself.

Do I still need a keyword research tool?

Yes, for measured demand and competition data. A language model can suggest keywords but cannot tell you reliable search volume.

What is the difference between AI SEO and GEO?

AI SEO here means using AI to help classic search work. GEO (generative engine optimization) refers to influencing how AI assistants mention and cite a brand, which needs its own measurement.

Verdict

Start from the task, not the label. A research suite plus one content editor solves most problems; MarketMuse earns its place on large libraries, InLinks or Link Whisper when internal linking is the bottleneck, and Screaming Frog when your team is technical. Whatever you choose, keep measured data and human review at the centre, and treat AI output as a draft.
