Which churn prediction software should you choose?

If you already have a customer success platform, start with its risk scoring before buying a separate prediction tool, because alerts are only useful when they trigger a playbook someone owns. Buy a dedicated predictive tool only when you have enough churn history to train on and a team ready to act on its output.

  • Large CS teams with complex segments: Gainsight.
  • Mid-market SaaS teams acting on usage drops: ChurnZero.
  • Teams that want modular journeys and risk workflows: Totango.
  • Product-led SaaS with good product data: Vitally or Planhat.
  • Teams with data but no data scientist: Pecan AI.
  • Product teams predicting churn from in-app behaviour: Amplitude.

What does churn prediction software actually predict?

Churn prediction software estimates the likelihood that an account will cancel, downgrade or fail to renew within a set window, based on patterns in usage, billing, support and relationship data. The output is a probability or risk band, and it's only as good as the signals and history behind it.

Typical signals fall into four groups:

  • Usage: logins, active users against seats purchased, adoption of key features, and trends over time.
  • Billing: failed payments, downgrades, discount dependence and upcoming renewal dates.
  • Support: ticket volume, unresolved escalations and negative sentiment.
  • Account: champion changes, missed meetings, survey responses and contract terms.

Data quality decides everything. If product events are inconsistent or customer records are duplicated across your CRM and billing system, the model learns noise. Fix identity matching first; our guide to CRM data quality and deduplication tools covers that groundwork.

What does each churn prediction tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The CS platforms are strongest at turning risk into assigned work; the analytics tools are strongest at modelling. Decide which half of the problem you're short on before you shortlist.

Gainsight: best for large CS teams with governed models

Gainsight is an established customer success platform with configurable health scoring, risk alerts and playbooks across segments. It suits organizations with CS operations staff who can maintain the model and its data feeds. Pricing is quote-based. Skip it if you have a small team and no one to administer the platform; the depth comes with overhead.

ChurnZero: best for acting on usage risk

ChurnZero focuses on customer success for subscription businesses, combining in-app usage tracking, health scores, alerts and automated plays. It's a sensible fit for mid-market SaaS teams that want risk tied to engagement campaigns. Skip it if you can't instrument your product, because usage signals are central to its value.

Totango: best for modular, journey-based workflows

Totango organizes customer success around journeys and modules, with health and risk tracking feeding playbooks. That modularity lets you start narrow and add scope. Map exactly which modules you need, because packaging affects cost. Skip it if you want one fixed, opinionated workflow out of the box.

Vitally: best for product-led SaaS with good data

Vitally is a customer success platform aimed at product-led and B2B SaaS teams, with health scores built from product, billing and CRM data and connections to data warehouses. It rewards teams whose data is already tidy. Skip it if your product data is scattered and nobody owns cleaning it.

Planhat: best for combining revenue and usage

Planhat offers a flexible customer data model that brings revenue, usage and relationship data together for health and risk views. The flexibility is useful when your accounts don't fit a standard template, but it takes design time. Skip it if you want a fixed scoring framework you can switch on quickly.

Pecan AI: best for building a predictive model without data scientists

Pecan AI is a predictive analytics platform that builds models, including churn prediction, from your own tables. It's a fit for teams with data in a warehouse but no in-house modelling skills. It produces predictions; the outreach, tasks and playbooks have to live in your CRM or CS tool. Skip it if you need an all-in-one CS workspace.

Amplitude: best for behaviour-based churn signals

Amplitude is a product analytics platform, and its predictive cohort features can flag users likely to stop using the product. That's valuable for product-led businesses where churn starts as declining usage. Check which predictive features your plan includes on the Amplitude pricing page. Skip it if your churn is driven by contracts and relationships more than in-app behaviour.

How often do churn risk alerts turn out to be wrong?

Every churn model produces false positives, healthy accounts flagged as at risk, and false negatives, departures it missed. How many depends on your data and threshold, and vendor materials can't tell you that for your business.

How do you validate churn alerts against what actually happened?

Back-test the model on a past period: score accounts as of a date several months ago, then compare those scores with which accounts actually churned. Report two things side by side, how many flagged accounts churned and how many churned accounts were never flagged.

  1. Pick a window: for example, risk at day 0 and churn within the next renewal cycle.
  2. Freeze the inputs: use only data available on the scoring date, or the test will flatter the model.
  3. Count both errors: false alarms waste CSM time; misses lose revenue. Choose a threshold that balances the two for your team size.
  4. Check explanations: for a sample of alerts, ask whether the stated reasons match what the account manager knew.
  5. Repeat quarterly: products and customer bases change, and so does what predicts churn.

Label any worked numbers you share internally as hypothetical until they come from your own back-test.

Which approach fits your subscription business?

The right tool depends on your churn volume, data maturity and who will act. Small businesses with few churn events are better served by transparent rules than by a model they can't validate.

  • Early-stage SaaS with few churned accounts: start with a simple, explainable score; see our guide to customer health scoring software.
  • Revenue-led questions about where churn comes from: pair prediction with subscription analytics software that shows churn by cohort and plan.
  • A CS team ready to run playbooks: compare the platforms in our customer success platforms guide.
  • Product-led businesses: start with behavioural data from your product analytics tool.

What else do buyers ask about churn prediction software?

Buyers mostly ask how much data they need, how accurate the models are and whether a spreadsheet would do. None of those have universal answers, so treat the guidance below as a starting point.

How much history do we need before churn prediction is useful?

Enough churned accounts to learn from, which small subscription businesses often don't have yet. If only a handful of customers have churned, start with transparent rules and revisit a model once you have more outcomes.

Is churn prediction the same as a health score?

They overlap but aren't the same. A health score is usually a weighted mix of signals you choose, while churn prediction estimates the likelihood of a specific outcome, often with a model trained on past churn.

What accuracy should we expect from a churn model?

There's no honest universal figure, because accuracy depends on your data, churn rate and prediction window. Ask vendors to back-test on your own history and report how many flagged accounts actually churned and how many churned accounts were missed.

Can churn prediction catch involuntary churn from failed payments?

Partly. Failed payments are better handled by billing and dunning tools, which act on the payment itself, while prediction tools are better at spotting customers who are deciding to leave.

Should customers ever see their churn risk score?

Generally no. Risk scores are internal working estimates that can be wrong, and showing them to customers invites confusion; share the underlying issues, such as low adoption, instead.

What's the verdict on churn prediction software?

Churn prediction pays off when three things are true: you have enough history, your data is clean, and someone owns the response to every alert. If any one is missing, fix it before you add a model, or you'll buy confident-looking scores nobody trusts.
