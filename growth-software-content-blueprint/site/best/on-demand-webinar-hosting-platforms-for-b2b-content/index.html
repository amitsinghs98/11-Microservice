Which on-demand webinar hosting platform should you choose?

Pick Wistia or Vidyard if your recordings will live on your own website behind your own forms, Brightcove if you need enterprise video governance, and ON24 or Goldcast if the on-demand library should keep the same engagement tracking as the live event. BrightTALK suits teams that want third-party audience reach more than ownership, and Vimeo suits teams that want flexible, general-purpose hosting.

The right on demand webinar hosting software makes a library discoverable, gated where it should be, measurable per viewer and easy to maintain. Most teams fail on the last point: recordings pile up, titles go stale, and nobody owns the refresh.

  • Marketing-site video with lead capture: Wistia
  • Sales and marketing video with viewer tracking: Vidyard
  • Enterprise video governance: Brightcove
  • Event-native on-demand hubs: ON24 and Goldcast
  • Syndicated audience reach: BrightTALK
  • Flexible general hosting: Vimeo

Who needs dedicated on-demand webinar hosting?

You need it once your recordings are a content asset rather than an event by-product: when prospects find them through search, sales sends them in sequences, and you want to know who watched what. A replay link inside the webinar tool is fine for a single follow-up email and inadequate for a library.

B2B content teams usually reach this point once a back catalogue of recordings builds up. At that stage, the questions become structural: where the library lives, how it is organised, and how viewing data reaches the CRM. Does gating on-demand webinars cost you viewers?

Gating trades reach for identification, and whether the trade pays depends on how strong your follow-up is; the vendor information we reviewed describes gating features but not their effect on viewing. Many teams gate the newest, most commercial recordings and leave older educational ones open.

What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

Wistia and Vidyard are natural fits for a marketing-owned library on your own domain, Brightcove for governed enterprise video, and ON24 or Goldcast for keeping live and on-demand data together. BrightTALK is a distribution choice more than a hosting one.

Wistia: best for libraries on your own site

Wistia is a marketing-focused video host with customisable embeds, in-player lead capture forms, viewer analytics and marketing-automation integrations. Strengths: recordings live on your domain, which helps search visibility and keeps visitors on your site. Limitations: check what counts towards your plan's limits. Skip it if you want a third-party audience rather than your own.

Vidyard: best for sales-led viewer tracking

Vidyard hosts marketing and sales video with viewer-level analytics and CRM integrations, and it's positioned for personalised sales video. Strengths: sales reps can send a recording and see who watched. Limitations: its centre of gravity is sales video, so library organisation may need extra work. Skip it if nobody in sales will use the viewing data.

Brightcove: best for enterprise governance

Brightcove is an enterprise video platform with extensive player, delivery and access-control options. Strengths: suits organisations where video spans marketing, training and media. Limitations: procurement and set-up are enterprise-sized. Skip it if the library is only a marketing asset.

ON24: best for event-native on-demand engagement

ON24 offers on-demand and always-on content experiences alongside its live webinars, carrying engagement tracking into replays. Strengths: one engagement record across live and on-demand. Limitations: the library sits inside a platform you'd need to keep licensing. Skip it if you don't already run live webinars on ON24.

Goldcast: best for recordings tied to account reporting

Goldcast pairs B2B events with tools for repurposing recordings into shorter clips and content hubs. Strengths: useful when one webinar should feed several assets. Limitations: it makes most sense as part of a wider Goldcast event stack. Skip it if you only need hosting.

BrightTALK: best for third-party audience reach

BrightTALK is a network where B2B professionals watch webinars, and vendors receive leads from viewers. Strengths: reach beyond your own list. Limitations: you don't own the page, and leads are network-sourced. Skip it if you want search traffic to your own domain.

Vimeo: best for flexible general hosting

Vimeo provides video hosting, live streaming and webinar options. Strengths: broadly familiar and adaptable. Limitations: B2B lead capture and CRM depth vary by plan, so check before assuming. Skip it if viewer-level CRM data is the main requirement.

What features make a webinar library discoverable and measurable?

Transcripts, chapters and on-page search make recordings discoverable; per-viewer analytics and CRM sync make them measurable. Without transcripts, a 45-minute recording is invisible to both search engines and visitors skimming for one answer.

  • Transcripts and captions: needed for accessibility and for indexable text on the page.
  • Chapters: let viewers jump to the segment they need, and show which segments hold attention.
  • Forms and access: gate selectively, and support pre-filled forms for known contacts.
  • Analytics: per-viewer watch time, not just play counts, synced to the contact record.
  • Storage and bandwidth: understand which limits apply, since long recordings consume them faster than short clips.

Where the library lives matters too. Hosting the player inside your CMS keeps the SEO value; see our CMS platforms for growing marketing teams for the website side.

How should you organise and refresh a webinar library?

Organise recordings by buyer problem first and format second, and review every recording on a fixed cycle so outdated product demos are retired or re-recorded. A library with no owner and no review date decays into a liability.

A simple taxonomy uses three tags per recording: the buyer problem it addresses, the funnel stage, and the product or feature it covers. Add a "review by" date to each entry. At review, decide whether to keep, update the description and chapters, cut into clips, or retire it.

What else do buyers ask about on-demand webinar hosting?

Most questions are about where recordings should live and whether to gate them. The answers below assume the library is a marketing asset you want to measure.

Can we keep on-demand webinars in our live webinar platform?

Yes, and many platforms offer replays, but check whether the replay page can be embedded on your site, indexed by search engines and tracked per viewer. If not, a dedicated video host is usually the better home for a library.

Should on-demand webinars be gated?

Gate recordings with clear commercial intent and leave broad educational ones open. Review the split periodically against identified viewers and pipeline rather than raw view counts.

Do we need transcripts for every recording?

Yes, for accessibility and discoverability. Captions help viewers who watch without sound, and an edited transcript gives search engines and readers text they can use.

How often should a webinar library be reviewed?

Set a review date for each recording based on how quickly its subject changes. Product demos need frequent checks, while strategy sessions can last longer between reviews.

BrightTALK is worth considering as a distribution channel alongside, not instead of, your own library.
