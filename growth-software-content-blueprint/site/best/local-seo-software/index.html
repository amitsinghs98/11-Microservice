Quick answer

  • Agencies and small multi-location brands that want one affordable toolkit: BrightLocal, which prices by location band and bundles rank tracking, a search grid, citation tracking and review tools.
  • Map-pack visibility analysis on a credit budget: Local Falcon, a geo-grid specialist that charges per grid point rather than per location.
  • Citation research and building with a hands-on service option: Whitespark.
  • Enterprise listings syndication across hundreds of locations: Yext or Uberall, both quote-based.
  • Review generation and reputation workflows across branches: Birdeye.
  • Teams already paying for an SEO suite: Semrush Local or Moz Local as add-ons, after checking country coverage.

Most multi-location teams pair one listings or reputation platform with one tracking tool.

Geo-grid tracking and its limits

Conventional rank tracking reports one position per keyword. Local results shift with the searcher's location, so geo-grid tools place a grid of points over a service area (say 5 by 5 or 9 by 9), check the map ranking at each point and draw a heat map. That shows where visibility drops off and why a competitor wins the next suburb. The limits:

  • It is a sample. Each point is one simulated query at one moment, without personalisation or search history.
  • Grid design changes the answer. A wide, sparse grid can make a strong business look weak. Fix grid size, spacing and keywords between scans.
  • Average grid rank is a summary, not a KPI. Pair it with GBP calls, direction requests and conversions.
  • Cost scales with points. A 9 by 9 grid is 81 checks per keyword per scan.

The tools

BrightLocal

Best for: local agencies and brands with up to about 100 locations that want tracking, citations and reviews in one login.

What it does: the pricing page lists three plans. Track covers local rank tracking for up to 100 keywords and four competitors per location, a geo-grid view for up to five keywords, citation tracking, GBP audits and a local AI visibility tracker. Manage adds listing sync and GBP post scheduling. Grow adds review monitoring, review requests by email and SMS, and a review widget.

Pricing: by location band. One location on Track is $41 per month billed monthly or $369 a year; 11–20 locations on Grow is $197 per month or $1,772 a year. Above 100 locations, pricing is on request. Citations are pay-as-you-go from $3.20 per site, or $2 with bulk credits (checked 4 November 2026, BrightLocal pricing).

Strengths and limits: transparent bands and a 14-day trial without a card. The vendor says the platform works in the USA, UK, Canada and Australia, so check coverage elsewhere, and confirm which plan carries white-label reports. Official site.

Local Falcon

Best for: repeatable map-pack scans across many listings without per-location fees.

What it does: geo-grid scans on Google and Apple Maps, grid sizes from 3 by 3 to 21 by 21, competitor and trend reports, scheduled scans and AI visibility tracking.

Pricing: one credit is one grid point, so a 5 by 5 scan uses 25 credits. Monthly packages run from $24.99 for 7,500 credits to $199.99 for 63,150; monthly credits expire each cycle, and Falcon AI analysis costs 25 credits per report (checked 4 November 2026, Local Falcon pricing).

Skip it if you need listings or review management: it measures, it does not fix. Official site.

Whitespark

Best for: agencies running citation audits and clean-up in competitive niches.

What it does: a citation finder, local rank tracker, review tooling and a managed citation-building service. Tools and services are priced separately; we did not verify current figures (see Whitespark's site).

Limitations: several separate tools are less convenient than one bundled suite.

Yext

Best for: enterprise brands with hundreds of locations and a central team pushing listing data to many publishers.

What it does: central management of structured business information, distributed to a publisher network, with review response and location pages. Quote-based (see Yext).

Skip it if you run a handful of sites. Ask what happens to synced listings if the contract ends.

Uberall

Best for: large franchises wanting listings, reviews, local posts and store-locator pages under one contract, with separate head-office and branch roles.

Pricing and limits: quote-based (see Uberall). Enterprise onboarding and contract terms may not suit agencies whose client roster changes often. Ask for a demo of the local manager's view.

Birdeye

Best for: service brands (healthcare, home services, automotive) where review volume and response speed drive bookings.

What it does: review requests and monitoring, customer messaging and surveys, with listings alongside. Quote-based, typically per location (see Birdeye).

Skip it if rankings, not reviews, are the bottleneck; you will still need a tracker.

Semrush Local

Best for: teams already on Semrush that want listings, GBP management and map rank tracking without another vendor.

Pricing and limits: a per-location add-on to a Semrush subscription; we did not verify current figures (see Semrush pricing). Listing distribution coverage varies by country, so confirm yours.

Moz Local

Best for: businesses that mainly need listing data distributed and monitored.

Pricing and limits: per-location tiers (see Moz Local). Simple to run, but lighter on review generation and grid analysis, so skip it if clients expect heat maps.

What location-based pricing looks like

Two published models illustrate the range. Take a 12-location dental group tracking 10 keywords per location.

  • BrightLocal Track, 11–20 locations: $936 per year billed annually, or $104 per month billed monthly (checked 4 November 2026). That includes conventional local rank tracking, with grid views limited to five keywords per location.
  • Local Falcon, weekly 7 by 7 scans: 12 locations × 10 keywords × 49 points is 5,880 credits per weekly round, or roughly 25,500 credits in a month of about 4.3 weeks. That fits inside the Pro monthly package of 31,250 credits at $99.99 per month (checked 4 November 2026), leaving headroom for competitor scans. Move to a 9 by 9 grid and the same plan would no longer cover it.

For quote-based platforms (Yext, Uberall, Birdeye), ask for a written per-location rate at your current count and at double it. These scenarios are illustrative; check each pricing page before you budget.

Recommendations by scenario

Single location

Use the free GBP dashboard plus a low BrightLocal tier or a small Local Falcon package. Enterprise syndication rarely pays at this size.

Franchise or multi-location brand

Prioritise permissions and governance: Yext or Uberall for listings, Birdeye where reviews drive revenue, and a grid tool for spot checks.

Local SEO agency

BrightLocal plus Local Falcon covers most needs at predictable cost. Feed the outputs into your client SEO reporting platform, and keep each client's locations, contacts and renewal dates in a CRM built for agency client accounts so churn risk is visible before reports go out. Rollout checklist for 10+ locations

  1. Export every GBP listing and reconcile name, address, phone, hours and categories against a single master spreadsheet.
  2. Confirm ownership: who holds primary owner access to each profile, and is it a company account rather than a former employee's?
  3. Define roles: head office edits core data; local managers answer reviews and post updates.
  4. Pick a fixed keyword set per location (5–15 terms) and a fixed grid size and radius per market type, urban or rural.
  5. Run a baseline scan and citation audit, scheduled at a fixed weekday and hour, before changing anything.
  6. Fix duplicates and closed listings first; they distort every later metric.
  7. Set review-response rules, including escalation for complaints and a target response time.
  8. Document what happens to listings and data if you cancel the tool.
  9. Review after 90 days and drop features nobody uses.

A monthly local reporting example

A hypothetical one-page monthly report for a 12-location client might contain:

  • Headline: two sentences on what changed and why, written by a person.
  • Visibility: grid heat maps for the three priority keywords, this month versus baseline.
  • Actions from GBP: calls, direction requests and website clicks by location, from GBP performance data.
  • Reputation: new reviews, average rating trend and median response time.
  • Listings health: open citation errors and duplicates resolved.
  • Next month: three planned actions, each tied to a location.

Lead with outcomes such as calls and bookings, and put average grid rank in context rather than at the top.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

  • Buying listings syndication for a two-location business. Manual GBP care may be enough.
  • Over-scanning. Daily 13 by 13 grids burn credits without adding insight.
  • Ignoring exit terms. Ask whether listing changes persist after cancellation.

Questions for sales: How is a "location" defined for service-area businesses? Which plan includes white-label reports? Can local managers be restricted to their own branch? What is the renewal price after the first term?

FAQs

Do I need local SEO software if I only have one Google Business Profile?

Not always. The free GBP dashboard and occasional grid checks can be enough. Software earns its cost once you need competitor tracking, citation audits or review requests at volume.

Is a geo-grid rank the same as what customers see?

No. It is a simulated snapshot from fixed points. Treat it as a directional map of coverage, not a precise record of every searcher's results.

How should agencies bill for local tools?

Model software cost per location inside the retainer so new branches do not quietly erode margin.

Verdict

For most agencies and brands under 100 locations, BrightLocal's published location bands make it the easiest place to start, with Local Falcon added when clients need detailed grid analysis. Large franchises with governance needs should run a quote process with Yext and Uberall, and reputation-led service brands should evaluate Birdeye. Whatever you pick, fix grid settings early and report outcomes before rankings.
