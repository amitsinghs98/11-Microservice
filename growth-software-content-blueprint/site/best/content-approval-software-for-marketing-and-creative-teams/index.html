Best Content Approval Software for Marketing and Creative Teams

Good content approval software gives every reviewer one place to comment on the current version and leaves a record of who signed off and when. Filestage, Ziflow and PageProof cover most file types with staged workflows, Frame.io leads for video, Planable for social posts, Markup.io for live web pages, and Wrike suits teams that want proofing inside project management.

Which approval tool should you choose?

Pick by the content type that causes the most review pain, then check the tool handles your other formats acceptably. A video-heavy team and a social-heavy team need different tools, even if both call it "approval".

  • Mixed marketing assets (PDFs, images, video, web) with staged sign-off: Filestage or Ziflow.
  • Regulated or detail-heavy proofing with tight version control: PageProof.
  • Video and motion work: Frame.io.
  • Social posts and calendars approved by clients or managers: Planable.
  • Feedback on live websites and landing pages: Markup.io.
  • Approval inside an existing project management tool: Wrike.

Why separate approval from task management?

Task tools track whether work is done; approval tools record what exactly was approved, on which version, by whom. Mixing them leads to sign-offs buried in comment threads and attachments nobody can match to the final file.

A task card can say "Banner: approved", but it can't show the pixel-level comments on version three, the reviewer who changed the headline, or proof that legal saw the final copy. That's what dedicated approval software adds: annotations on the asset itself, side-by-side version comparison, staged reviewers, due dates per stage and an audit trail. Keep tasks in your content workflow software and schedules in your editorial calendar, and link each task to its proof.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

General proofing suites handle the widest range of formats and workflows, while specialists do one format very well and the rest less so. Choose a suite if your reviews span formats, and a specialist if one format dominates.

Filestage: best for staged reviews across mixed assets

Filestage lets teams collect comments on documents, images, video and other files, with review steps and due dates so legal or brand only see a version once earlier reviewers are done. Our editorial assessment is that it's a good fit for in-house marketing teams and agencies working with outside reviewers. Limitation: check project, storage and integration limits on each tier. Skip it if nearly everything you review is video. See Filestage's official site.

Ziflow: best for complex creative workflows

Ziflow is an online proofing platform for creative operations, with multi-stage workflows, a broad set of file types and integrations into creative and project tools. Limitation: the richest automation and enterprise controls usually sit in higher plans. Skip it if you need only simple yes-or-no sign-off. See Ziflow's official site.

PageProof: best for detail-heavy and sensitive proofing

PageProof focuses on precise proofing with version comparison and workflow steps, and it emphasizes security, which matters to teams in regulated industries. Limitation: it's a proofing tool, so planning and tasks live elsewhere. Skip it if you want approvals bundled with project management. See PageProof's official site.

Frame.io: best for video review

Frame.io, owned by Adobe, is built for frame-accurate comments on video and integrates with video editing tools. That makes it the natural choice for teams where edits go back and forth with editors. Limitation: it's less natural for long documents and live pages. Skip it if video is a small share of your output. See Frame.io's official site.

Planable: best for social post approval

Planable shows posts as they'll look on each network and lets managers or clients comment and approve before scheduling. Limitation: it's built for social and short-form content, not print or long video. Skip it if social is a minor channel for you. See Planable's official site, and compare scheduling tools in our roundup of social content tools for agencies.

Markup.io: best for feedback on live web pages

Markup.io lets reviewers pin comments directly on websites, images and PDFs, which suits landing page and site launches. Limitation: staged approvals and audit depth are lighter than full proofing suites. Skip it if you need formal compliance sign-off. See Markup.io's official site.

Wrike: best for proofing inside project management

Wrike is a work management platform with proofing and approval features, so tasks and sign-off live in one place. Limitation: proofing depth varies by plan and is narrower than a dedicated suite. Skip it if you already run another project tool you won't replace. See Wrike's official site.

Who should approve what, internally and with clients?

Give each content type a fixed sequence: content owner, then subject expert, then brand, then legal or client, with only the final stage able to approve. Everyone else comments, which keeps sign-off accountable.

  • Blog posts and articles: editor approves; subject expert reviews facts; legal only for claims, comparisons or regulated topics.
  • Designs and display ads: creative lead, then brand; client approval for agency work; legal for offers and pricing.
  • Video: producer, then brand on a cut; client or executive on the final cut; music and talent rights confirmed before export.
  • Social posts: community or channel lead approves; client approval if contracted; crisis-sensitive posts go to comms.
  • Web pages and landing pages: content owner and SEO on staging; developer QA; final approval from the page owner before launch.
  • Campaign launch packs: marketing lead approves the set; each asset must already carry its own approval.

Write the matrix into the tool's workflow templates so reviewers are added automatically. For how approvals fit governance more widely, see our list of content governance tools and the content operations hub.

Do approval tools actually shorten review cycles?

They can, but only when the team also limits who approves and enforces due dates; a tool alone just moves the same slow reviewers to a new screen. The software removes version confusion and email chasing, and the process removes indecision.

What mistakes slow approvals down?

The biggest are too many approvers, reviewing before the brief is agreed, and allowing feedback outside the tool. Each one creates rework that no software can fix.

Others: no due dates per stage; legal reviewing early drafts that will change; reviewers commenting on old versions because links weren't updated; guest reviewers who need a login they never set up; and no rule for resolving conflicting feedback. Agree upfront who breaks ties. A clear brief prevents many late changes, which is why it's worth reading our guide to content brief and assignment tools.

What else do teams ask about content approval software?

The remaining questions are about clients, audit trails and overlap with project tools. Short answers follow.

Do clients need an account to review?

Many approval tools let guests review through a link, sometimes with an email check, but rules differ by vendor and plan. Confirm guest access and whether guests count as paid users before you buy.

What should an audit trail record?

At minimum: every version, every comment and its author, each approval decision with a timestamp, and who was asked to review. Regulated teams should also check how long the record is kept and whether it can be exported.

Can project management tools handle approvals on their own?

Some can, through built-in proofing features. Check whether they offer on-asset annotation and version comparison, because a simple approve button on a task isn't enough evidence for brand or legal review.

Is content approval software the same as creative proofing?

Largely yes; proofing is the core feature. Content approval as a category also covers drafts, social posts and web pages, while creative proofing tools are often tuned for design and video files.

What's the verdict?

Choose Filestage or Ziflow for staged approvals across mixed formats, PageProof for detail-heavy proofing, and Frame.io, Planable or Markup.io when video, social or web pages dominate. Choose Wrike if you'd rather keep approvals inside project management.
