Quick answer

  • Fastest to hand to account managers: Zapier, for its connector catalogue and approachable editor.
  • Most visual control per dollar: Make, if your team is comfortable with routers, iterators and credit maths.
  • Most control and predictable cost for complex flows: n8n, cloud or self-hosted, if someone technical owns it.
  • Workflows that need a human approval step: Relay.app.
  • Agencies with developers who prefer code: Pipedream.
  • Agencies standardised on Microsoft 365: Power Automate.

For a head-to-head on the three most common choices, see our Zapier vs Make vs n8n comparison.

How we chose

Agency criteria differ from in-house criteria. We weighted: separation of client workspaces or projects; shared and scoped credentials; connector coverage for marketing apps; error logs, retries and alerts; execution history retention; team permissions; and how the billing unit (tasks, credits or executions) behaves as volume grows.

Evidence came from official pricing pages and documentation. No hands-on testing was performed. We verified Zapier, Make and n8n pricing pages on 4 November 2026; other vendors' pricing is described by model only. The order reflects how many agency scenarios each tool covers well, not payment.

The tools

1. Zapier

Best for: Agencies that want account managers, not just ops specialists, to build and fix simple automations.

What it does: Trigger-and-action workflows (Zaps) across a very large app catalogue, plus Tables, Forms, Paths for branching and AI steps.

Pricing: Zapier's pricing page lists a Free plan with 100 tasks a month and two-step Zaps, Professional from $19.99 a month, Team from $69 a month with 25 users, shared Zaps and shared app connections, and Enterprise on quote; yearly billing is advertised as saving 33% (checked 4 November 2026, Zapier pricing). Prices rise by task tier. Triggers, Filter, Formatter and Paths steps do not use tasks, according to the same page.

Strengths: Connector breadth, low training cost, version history and custom error notifications on paid plans.

Limitations: Per-task billing makes multi-step, high-volume flows expensive. Free and Professional are single-user, so a real agency deployment starts at Team. When you exceed your tier, Zapier moves you to pay-per-task billing at a higher rate unless you turn it off.

Skip it if: most of your volume is batch data processing, such as syncing thousands of rows a day.

2. Make

Best for: Agencies with an ops lead who likes a visual canvas and wants more logic per dollar.

What it does: Scenario canvas with routers, filters, iterators, aggregators and error-handler routes.

Pricing: Make bills in credits; most module actions use one credit. Its pricing page listed a Free plan with 1,000 credits a month and two active scenarios, and, at the 10,000-credit tier, Core at $9, Pro at $16 and Teams at $29 a month, with a "save 15%" label for annual billing (checked 4 November 2026, Make pricing). Confirm which billing period the figure applies to at checkout.

Strengths: Error-handler and router modules do not consume credits, per Make's FAQ. Execution logs are kept 30 days on Core, Pro and Teams, with full-text log search from Pro.

Limitations: Team roles and shareable templates start on Teams. If credits run out, scenarios stop until you add more, though incoming webhooks queue up to your allowance. The canvas can intimidate non-technical staff.

3. n8n

Best for: Agencies with a technical owner, complex multi-step flows or data-residency requirements.

What it does: Node-based workflows with code steps, HTTP requests, webhooks, AI nodes and error workflows, available as n8n Cloud or self-hosted.

Pricing: n8n bills per execution, meaning one full workflow run however many steps it contains. Its pricing page listed Starter at $20 a month billed annually with 2,500 executions, Pro at $50 a month billed annually with 10,000 executions, and a self-hosted-only Business plan at $800 a month billed annually with 40,000 executions (checked 4 November 2026, n8n pricing). A self-hosted Community Edition is published on GitHub.

Strengths: Execution-based billing favours long workflows; cloud data is hosted in Frankfurt, per the pricing FAQ; automatic retries and error workflows are listed on all cloud plans.

Limitations: Starter allows one shared project and Pro three, which matters if you separate clients by project. SSO and Git-based version control sit on Business and Enterprise. Self-hosting moves patching, backups and uptime onto your team.

4. Relay.app

Best for: Content and reporting workflows that need a person to approve or edit before the next step.

What it does: Workflow automation built around human-in-the-loop steps, such as approvals, assignments and manual inputs, alongside AI steps.

Pricing: See Relay.app's pricing page.

Strengths: Makes client sign-off part of the workflow rather than an email thread.

Limitations: Smaller connector catalogue than Zapier or Make; confirm your clients' apps are covered.

5. Pipedream

Best for: Agencies with developers who would rather write Node.js or Python than drag modules.

What it does: Event-driven workflows mixing prebuilt actions with code steps.

Pricing: Usage-based; see Pipedream's pricing page.

Limitations: Hard to hand to non-developers, which concentrates maintenance in a few people.

6. Microsoft Power Automate

Best for: Agencies or clients already on Microsoft 365, SharePoint and Teams.

What it does: Cloud flows, desktop automation and approvals inside the Microsoft ecosystem.

Pricing: Microsoft licensing by user or by flow, with premium connectors on paid licences; see Power Automate's official site.

Limitations: Marketing SaaS connectors are thinner than Zapier's, and licensing is complex to explain to clients.

Four agency use cases

  • Lead routing: form or ad lead arrives, gets enriched, is written to the client's CRM and the right rep is alerted. If the CRM itself is the bottleneck, our list of CRMs built for agency client accounts covers that decision, and dedicated lead routing and scheduling software may beat a homemade flow when routing rules get complex.
  • Reporting: pull weekly ad and analytics figures into a sheet or dashboard and post a summary to the client channel. A purpose-built tool from our performance marketing analytics shortlist or our client SEO reporting roundup is often cheaper than maintaining a fragile multi-step flow.
  • Content operations: a brief approved in the project tool creates a doc, assigns a writer and schedules review. This is where Relay.app's approvals help.
  • Alerts: budget pacing, broken tracking or a form that stops submitting. Alerts are cheap in tasks but expensive when they fail silently.

Failure handling and maintenance ownership

Every agency automation needs a named owner, an alert route and a runbook. Before you buy, check:

  • Retries: automatic, manual replay, or both? Zapier lists Autoreplay; n8n lists automatic retries on all cloud plans.
  • Error routing: can a failure trigger its own workflow? n8n has error workflows; Make has error-handler routes.
  • Log retention: Make lists 30 days on paid self-serve plans; n8n lists 7 days on Starter and 30 on Pro.
  • Credential hygiene: whose login powers each client connection, and what happens when that person leaves?
  • Client offboarding: can you export or hand over workflows cleanly?

Mistakes and questions to ask

  • Building everything under one person's account. Ask whether shared connections and folder permissions are on your plan.
  • Ignoring overage behaviour. Ask whether flows pause, queue or bill extra at the limit.
  • Rebuilding what native integrations already do. Check the client's CRM and ad platforms first.
  • Not billing maintenance. Price automation upkeep into retainers.

FAQs

Should we run every client in one automation account?

Separate where you can, using folders, projects or teams, so a client's data and credentials stay isolated. For larger clients, consider having them own the account and grant you access.

Is self-hosted n8n cheaper for agencies?

The licence cost can be lower, but you pay in server management, security patching and on-call time. It suits agencies with an engineer who already runs infrastructure.

Do failed runs count against our allowance?

Zapier states that unsuccessful actions do not count as tasks. Check each vendor's documentation for retries and partial runs.

Verdict

Zapier is the safest default for agencies whose builders are not technical, as long as volume stays moderate. Make offers more logic per dollar for an ops-minded team. n8n is the strongest fit for complex, high-volume flows when someone owns its upkeep. Whichever you pick, budget for maintenance, not just tasks.
