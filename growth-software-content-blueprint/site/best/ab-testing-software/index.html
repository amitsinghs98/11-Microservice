Quick answer

The right A/B testing platform depends less on its feature list than on where your changes live. Marketing pages and storefront themes suit visual, client-side tools. Pricing logic, onboarding flows and app features suit server-side platforms with feature flags.

  • Marketer-led web testing at mid-market scale: VWO or Convert.
  • Web testing plus personalisation for larger brands: AB Tasty or Optimizely.
  • Product and engineering teams that want flags and experiments together: Statsig or PostHog.
  • Teams that want results computed in their own data warehouse: GrowthBook.
  • Shopify stores testing prices, shipping and offers: Intelligems.

If your traffic is modest, read the next section before buying anything.

Experiment basics and low-traffic limits

An A/B test randomly splits visitors between a control and one or more variants, then compares a primary metric. Three inputs decide how long that takes: the baseline conversion rate, the smallest lift worth detecting (the minimum detectable effect), and your tolerance for false positives and missed effects.

Illustrative arithmetic using the standard two-proportion formula at 95% confidence and 80% power:

Approximate visitors needed per variant (illustrative calculation, not vendor data)
Baseline conversion Relative lift to detect Visitors per variant
3% 20% (3.0% to 3.6%) About 13,900
3% 10% (3.0% to 3.3%) About 53,200

Halving the effect you want to detect roughly quadruples the sample. A SaaS pricing page with 5,000 visitors a month cannot realistically detect small lifts on paid conversions. Options for low-traffic teams: test bigger changes, use a metric closer to the change (such as clicks to sign-up rather than paid plans), run fewer variants, or skip testing and use qualitative research instead. Our landing-page CRO toolkit covers that research-first route.

Two more sample-size concerns. Stopping a test the moment it looks significant inflates false positives unless the platform uses sequential or Bayesian methods designed for continuous monitoring. And testing many metrics or segments at once makes a spurious "winner" more likely. Ask each vendor how its statistics handle both.

Client-side vs server-side testing

Client-side testing changes the page in the visitor's browser with a JavaScript snippet, usually through a visual editor. It is fast for marketers to launch, but it can cause flicker (the original briefly showing before the variant), it is affected by ad blockers and consent choices, and it struggles with logic that lives on the server.

Server-side testing assigns variants in your application code, typically through an SDK or feature flags. It suits pricing, checkout logic, onboarding and mobile apps, and avoids flicker. The cost is developer involvement for every experiment.

Many platforms now offer both. The question is which mode your team will actually use most, because that determines who owns the testing roadmap.

Guardrail metrics

A guardrail metric is one you do not try to improve but refuse to harm. Typical guardrails:

  • Ecommerce: average order value, refund or return rate, page load time, checkout errors.
  • SaaS: trial-to-paid rate, activation, support tickets, churn in the first billing cycle.
  • Lead generation: lead-to-opportunity rate and unsubscribe or spam-complaint rates for follow-up email.

Guardrails often live outside the testing tool, in product analytics, the CRM or the billing system. That is why integration with your marketing and performance analytics stack, and a sober view of what attribution can and cannot prove, matter as much as the test editor.

The platforms

VWO

Best for: marketing and ecommerce teams that want testing plus behaviour insights from one vendor. Consider another option if: your experiments are mostly in-app product changes.

VWO sells web testing, behaviour analytics, personalisation and server-side experimentation as separate products. Its visual editor lowers the barrier for marketers, and its documentation describes a Bayesian-based statistics engine. Pricing scales with traffic and modules, so a full bundle can become a substantial line item. See the VWO pricing page.

Convert

Best for: agencies and mid-sized teams that want a focused client-side tool with clear plan tiers. Consider another option if: you need native feature flags and server-side SDKs as your main workflow.

Convert Experiences concentrates on web experimentation, with audience targeting, integrations with common analytics tools and a privacy-focused positioning. Check its documentation for which statistical methods are offered and how flicker is handled. Plans are tiered by tested visitors; see the Convert pricing page.

AB Tasty

Best for: retail and consumer brands combining experimentation with personalisation and merchandising campaigns. Consider another option if: you want transparent self-serve pricing.

AB Tasty covers web experimentation, personalisation and feature management for product teams. Its strength is giving marketers campaign-style personalisation alongside tests. It is sold through sales conversations, so expect annual contracts. Start at the AB Tasty website.

Optimizely

Best for: enterprises running many concurrent experiments across web and product with dedicated analysts. Consider another option if: you have one marketer and a few tests a quarter.

Optimizely offers both web and feature experimentation, and its Stats Engine is designed for sequential monitoring so teams can check results without the classic peeking problem. Governance, programme management and personalisation are strengths. Implementation and contract size match its enterprise focus. See the Optimizely website.

Statsig

Best for: SaaS product and engineering teams that want feature gates, experiments and product metrics in one place. Consider another option if: marketers need a visual editor for landing pages.

Statsig's documentation covers feature gates, experiments, variance-reduction techniques and a warehouse-native deployment option. It is developer-oriented by design. Statsig announced in 2025 that it was being acquired by OpenAI, so ask about product roadmap and support commitments during evaluation. See the Statsig pricing page.

PostHog

Best for: startups that want product analytics, session replay, feature flags and experiments in one tool. Consider another option if: your tests are mostly marketing-page copy and layout changes owned by non-developers.

PostHog bundles experimentation with analytics, so guardrail metrics sit next to test results. Pricing is usage-based per product, with a monthly free allowance, which suits early-stage teams but needs monitoring as event volume grows. See the PostHog pricing page.

GrowthBook

Best for: data teams that want experiment analysis to run on their own warehouse data. Consider another option if: you do not have a warehouse or someone who can write metric definitions in SQL.

GrowthBook is open source and can be self-hosted or used as a cloud service. It offers feature flags, a visual editor and both Bayesian and frequentist analysis, according to its documentation. Because metrics come from your warehouse, revenue and retention guardrails are easier to include. See the GrowthBook pricing page.

Intelligems

Best for: Shopify brands testing prices, shipping thresholds, discounts and offers. Consider another option if: you are not on Shopify or mainly want layout tests.

Intelligems focuses on profit-related experiments for Shopify, including price and shipping tests that generic web tools handle poorly. Price testing raises customer-trust and legal questions, so agree a policy before launching one. See the Intelligems website.

Which platform for which team

  • Ecommerce brand on Shopify, marketer-led: Intelligems for offer and price tests; VWO or Convert for broader theme and landing-page tests.
  • Ecommerce brand with an in-house experimentation team: AB Tasty or Optimizely, with guardrails on order value and returns.
  • Early-stage SaaS: PostHog, with experiments limited to high-traffic steps such as sign-up and activation.
  • Scaling SaaS with engineers and a warehouse: Statsig or GrowthBook. Connect results to pipeline in a CRM suited to B2B SaaS sales teams.
  • Lifecycle and onboarding email tests: these usually belong in your email platform; see our guide to lifecycle email automation software.

Implementation questions

Ask these before signing, and ideally during a trial on a real page:

  • How does the snippet or SDK affect page speed, and what anti-flicker method is used?
  • How are visitors bucketed across devices and sessions, and how does consent affect bucketing?
  • Which statistical method is used, and is it safe to monitor results continuously?
  • Can we define guardrail metrics from our analytics, CRM or warehouse?
  • How are sample ratio mismatches detected and flagged?
  • What counts toward usage limits, and what happens on overage?
  • Who on our team will build variants, QA them and sign off results?

Pair experiment results with qualitative evidence: session replay and heatmap research often explains why a variant won or lost. If you are replacing analytics as well, our GA4 alternatives and complements page covers the measurement side.

FAQs

What is the minimum traffic for A/B testing?

There is no universal threshold. It depends on baseline conversion rate and the lift you need to detect. Run your numbers through a sample-size calculator before choosing a platform; if a test would take many months, invest in research first.

Should we choose Bayesian or frequentist statistics?

Either can work when applied correctly. What matters more is that the method matches how your team monitors and stops tests, and that results are reported with uncertainty, not just a winner label.

Do feature flags replace an A/B testing tool?

Flags control who sees a feature. They become an experimentation tool only when paired with randomised assignment and metric analysis, which some flag platforms include and others leave to you.

What replaced Google Optimize?

Google retired Optimize in 2023 and pointed users toward third-party testing tools that integrate with GA4. Any platform on this list can fill the gap, depending on your traffic and team.
