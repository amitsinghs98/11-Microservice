Quick answer

Agencies rarely regret a keyword tool because it lacked one more filter. They regret it when exports stop at a few thousand rows, when a suburb client shows no volume at all, or when every extra analyst needs a paid seat. With those failure points in mind:

  • Widest keyword and competitor research inside one suite: Semrush (Starter tier or above).
  • Intent labels and clustering alongside link data: Ahrefs Standard or above.
  • Agencies that need research plus daily tracking on a budget: SE Ranking.
  • In-house generalists: Moz Pro Keyword Explorer.
  • Freelancers who mainly need a dedicated keyword finder: Mangools KWFinder.
  • Pay-as-you-go volume data in the browser: Keywords Everywhere.
  • Free starting points: Google Keyword Planner and Google Search Console.

How to judge a keyword database

Vendors publish headline database sizes, but a bigger number does not tell you whether your client's market is covered. Judge a database by what it returns for your own queries:

  • Long-tail coverage in your markets. Search ten niche terms you know get traffic. Count how many return any volume.
  • Location depth. Check whether data stops at country level or reaches regions and cities.
  • Freshness. Look for recent trend data on a term that spiked in the last few months.
  • Consistency with first-party data. Compare estimated volumes against impressions in Search Console for a site you manage. Expect differences; look for obvious misses.
  • SERP context. Volume without the current results page, features and ranking domains tells you little about difficulty.

Every third-party volume figure is an estimate. Treat tools as consistent rulers for comparison, not as precise counters.

The tools

Semrush

Best for: agencies that want keyword, competitor and PPC research in one database. What it does: keyword discovery, competitor keyword analysis and gap comparison, with research reports usable across many countries.

Pricing: the Semrush plans page describes the $139-a-month SEO plan as offering "basic" keyword research, with fuller keyword research and optimization tools from Starter at $199 a month; Pro+ at $299 adds historical data and keyword cannibalization analysis. Extra users start at $45 a month (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: breadth, plus paid-search data that helps with commercial-intent terms. Limitations: the cheapest plan is not the research plan, and every analyst seat adds cost. Skip it if you only need volumes for a handful of local clients.

Ahrefs

Best for: teams that want keyword research tied closely to backlink and content data. What it does: Keywords Explorer with difficulty scores, SERP overviews and keyword lists, plus organic competitor research in Site Explorer.

Pricing: the Ahrefs pricing page lists Lite at $129 a month with 1,000 credits per user, and Standard at $249 with unlimited credits. Keyword clusters, search intents, AI suggestions and translations are listed as Standard-and-above features (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: strong competitor research and clustering on Standard. Limitations: Lite's credit limit and missing intent labels make it a weak fit for heavy research. Skip it if your budget only stretches to Lite and research is your main job.

SE Ranking

Best for: small agencies combining research with daily rank tracking. What it does: keyword and competitor research, SERP analysis and a Research API alongside tracking and audits.

Pricing: the SE Ranking plans page lists Core at $129 a month with keyword, competitor and backlink research described as unlimited, 50,000 data export rows and 25,000 Research API credits; Growth at $279 raises exports to 100,000 rows (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: predictable cost and exports suited to agency volumes. Limitations: verify database depth in your smaller markets during the trial. Skip it if you need the most extensive PPC research layer.

Moz Pro

Best for: in-house marketers who want a simple research interface. What it does: Keyword Explorer offers suggestions, difficulty, SERP analysis and keyword lists.

Pricing: tiered plans with keyword query limits; figures not verified for this draft. See the Moz Pro pricing page.

Strengths: approachable for non-specialists. Limitations: query caps can constrain agencies researching many clients each month. Skip it if you need bulk exports or API access on a small plan.

Mangools KWFinder

Best for: freelancers and small agencies who want a focused keyword finder rather than a full suite. What it does: KWFinder is sold as part of the Mangools bundle, which also includes SERP, rank-tracking and link tools.

Pricing: plans are limited by keyword lookups and related allowances; see the Mangools plans page. Figures not verified for this draft.

Strengths: a gentle learning curve and local search options. Limitations: lighter competitor and backlink depth than the big suites. Skip it if you manage large ecommerce catalogues.

Keywords Everywhere

Best for: consultants who want volume data while browsing search results. What it does: a browser extension that overlays keyword metrics on search pages and other sites.

Pricing: credit-based; see the Keywords Everywhere site. Figures not verified.

Strengths: low commitment for occasional use. Limitations: not a project workspace, so no shared lists or client separation. Skip it if several analysts need to collaborate.

Google Keyword Planner

Best for: checking advertiser-side demand for free. What it does: suggests keywords and shows forecast data inside Google Ads Keyword Planner. It requires a Google Ads account, and accounts with little ad spend may see volume ranges rather than precise figures.

Limitations: no organic difficulty or SERP context. Skip it if you need competitor data.

Google Search Console

Best for: finding the queries a client already appears for. The performance report in Search Console shows real impressions and clicks for verified sites, which makes it the best source for "striking distance" opportunities. Limitations: only sites you control, and no discovery beyond them.

Local, ecommerce and multi-country workflows

A local service client

Volumes for "plumber in [suburb]" are often tiny or missing. Check city-level data in your chosen tool, combine it with Search Console impressions, and expect to group local variants into one page target. Location-level tracking and Google Business Profile work belong to a separate tool category; see our local SEO software for multi-location businesses.

An ecommerce store

Category and product research generates thousands of rows. Export caps, clustering and intent labels matter most here, which favours Ahrefs Standard, Semrush Starter or above, or SE Ranking with higher export rows.

A multi-country site

Check that each target country has its own database, that translations or local-language suggestions are available, and that per-country exports do not each consume a separate report credit you cannot afford.

A sample research workflow

  1. Pull current queries and impressions from Search Console for the client's site.
  2. Run three to five competitor domains through a suite's organic competitor and gap reports.
  3. Expand seed terms with keyword suggestions and question variants.
  4. Cluster terms by shared ranking URLs or intent, and map each cluster to an existing or new page.
  5. Prioritise by business value, difficulty and current position, then hand clusters to writers with a brief. Our roundup of AI content brief tools covers that hand-off.
  6. Add priority terms to rank tracking and report progress through your client SEO reporting software.

Pricing units and export caps

Keyword tools charge in different units, and the unit decides what an agency can actually do in a month. All figures below were checked on 4 November 2026 on the vendors' official pricing pages.

  • Ahrefs: maximum rows per report of 2,500 on Lite, 30,000 on Standard and 75,000 on Advanced; export rows per month of 500,000, 1.5 million and 4 million; keyword lists of 50, 100 and 200.
  • SE Ranking: 50,000 export rows on Core and 100,000 on Growth, plus Research API credits of 25,000 and 100,000 a month.
  • Semrush: the free account allows 10 reports a day across tools. Paid-plan report and export limits vary by plan; check the comparison table on the official page.
  • Credits and lookups: tools such as Keywords Everywhere and Mangools meter usage by credits or daily lookups. Estimate your monthly lookups before choosing a tier.

Seats are the other unit. If three people research daily, multiply any per-user add-on by three before comparing plans.

Trial checklist and buying mistakes

Use a free trial or free tier to answer specific questions, not to browse:

  • Run the same 20 seed terms for a local, an ecommerce and an international client in each tool.
  • Export a full keyword list and note where the row cap stops you.
  • Check whether intent labels and clusters are on the plan you would buy, not just the trial.
  • Confirm how many people can log in and what each extra seat costs.
  • Check whether API access is included or a separate add-on.

The commonest mistake is paying for a dedicated keyword tool when your suite already covers the job. FAQs

Why do keyword volumes differ between tools?

Each vendor builds estimates from its own data sources and models. Pick one tool as your reference so comparisons stay consistent over time.

Is Google Keyword Planner enough for SEO?

It is useful for demand checks but lacks organic difficulty, SERP features and competitor data, so most agencies pair it with a paid tool.

Do I need keyword clustering?

For ecommerce and content-heavy sites, yes: clustering prevents several pages competing for the same intent. For small local sites, manual grouping is often enough.

Can AI chat tools replace keyword databases?

They can brainstorm topics, but they do not supply measured search volume. Use them for ideation and verify demand in a keyword tool, as our AI content workflow guide recommends.

Verdict

Our editorial assessment: Semrush and Ahrefs give the deepest competitor-led research, but only on the right tier, since the entry plans of both are limited for research-heavy agency work. SE Ranking is the pragmatic choice when research, tracking and exports must fit one modest budget. Mangools and Keywords Everywhere suit lighter, individual use, and Google's free tools belong in every workflow. Run the trial checklist before committing, because database coverage in your clients' markets matters more than any vendor's headline numbers.
