Best Checkout Optimization Software for Online Stores

The checkout optimization software ecommerce teams should buy is the kind that removes a specific, measured source of friction rather than adding another layer to the checkout. Start with your platform's native checkout and accelerated wallets, then add address, shipping, orchestration or fraud tools only where your own funnel data points to a problem.

Which checkout optimization tool should you choose?

Choose by the friction you can actually see: wallets and one-click tools for repeat-buyer speed, address and shipping tools for form errors and surprise costs, orchestration for payment failures, and fraud tools for false declines. Most stores on Shopify or BigCommerce should exhaust native checkout settings before buying anything.

  • Shopify Plus stores that need branded checkout logic: Shopify Checkout Extensibility.
  • Stores that want hosted payments with saved-detail autofill: Stripe Checkout and Link.
  • Guest-heavy stores where many buyers already use PayPal: PayPal Fastlane.
  • Brands chasing a one-click experience across a shopper network: Bolt.
  • Multi-processor or multi-market merchants with payment failures to fix: Primer.
  • Higher-risk catalogs losing revenue to declines and chargebacks: Signifyd.
  • Stores shipping internationally with address errors: Loqate.
  • Complex shipping rules that make rates unclear at checkout: ShipperHQ.

What problem does checkout optimization software actually solve?

It reduces the drop-off between "start checkout" and "order placed" by making payment faster, forms shorter, costs clearer and approvals more reliable. It doesn't fix weak product pages, poor traffic quality or a price that's too high, which is why diagnosis comes before purchase.

Checkout friction usually falls into five buckets: payment (missing wallets, declines, slow card entry), address capture (typos, failed validation, international formats), shipping (late-revealed costs or vague delivery dates), trust (unfamiliar payment pages, missing policies) and extensibility (you can't add the field, message or upsell you need). Each bucket has its own category of tool, and buying the wrong one is common.

If shoppers are leaving before checkout, the answer is more likely on the product and cart pages, covered in our guide to Shopify apps for improving store conversion. If they start checkout and leave, recovery is a separate job, covered in abandoned cart recovery platforms. For the wider category map, see the ecommerce growth software hub.

What does each checkout tool do well, and when should you skip it?

Each tool below solves one friction bucket well and adds cost or complexity elsewhere. Read the "skip it if" line as carefully as the strengths.

Shopify Checkout Extensibility: best for Shopify Plus brands that need checkout logic

Shopify's checkout extensibility framework lets developers and apps add UI extensions, functions for discounts and delivery logic, and branding settings without editing the checkout's core code. That keeps you on Shopify's maintained, upgrade-safe checkout, which is the main strength. The limitation is that the richest customization sits on Shopify Plus, and anything outside the extension points simply isn't possible. Skip it if you're on a standard Shopify plan and only need minor tweaks that the checkout editor already handles.

Stripe Checkout and Link: best for hosted payments with saved-detail autofill

Stripe's hosted checkout and its Link saved-payment feature aim to shorten card entry for returning shoppers across Stripe merchants. It's a strong fit for custom-built or headless stores that want a maintained payment page and broad wallet support with little front-end work. Pricing is per transaction, with the current rates on the official pricing page. Skip it if your platform already bundles payments and you'd be adding a second processor for no clear reason.

PayPal Fastlane: best for speeding up guest checkout

Fastlane recognizes guest shoppers who've saved details with PayPal and fills them in, which targets the stores where account creation is low. It's worth evaluating if a large share of your orders are guest orders. Its value depends on how many of your shoppers are recognized, and availability varies by market and platform. Skip it if most buyers already log in or use a wallet you support.

Bolt: best for brands that want a one-click network experience

Bolt positions itself as a one-click checkout that remembers shoppers across participating merchants. The appeal is speed for repeat network shoppers; the trade-off is that a third party owns more of your checkout and shopper relationship. Check which platforms and payment processors it currently supports, because that has changed over time. Skip it if you want to keep checkout fully native or your platform restricts third-party checkouts.

Primer: best for merchants with several processors or markets

Primer is a payment orchestration layer: one integration that routes payments across processors and methods, with workflows for retries and fallbacks. It suits merchants selling across markets where local payment methods and processor performance vary. It needs engineering time and only pays off with meaningful volume and more than one processor. Skip it if a single processor already covers your markets.

Signifyd: best for stores losing revenue to fraud and false declines

Signifyd makes order-level fraud decisions and markets a guarantee model for approved orders, which shifts chargeback risk away from you. That matters most for high-value or frequently targeted catalogs, where manual review slows fulfilment. Pricing is sales-led. Skip it if fraud is rare for you and your platform's built-in risk analysis is enough; alternatives like Riskified sit in the same space if you want a second quote.

Loqate: best for international address capture

Loqate offers address autocomplete and verification across many countries, which shortens typing and reduces failed deliveries. It's a narrow tool that does one job. Skip it if you sell domestically and your platform's address autocomplete already works well.

ShipperHQ: best for stores whose shipping rules confuse buyers

ShipperHQ manages rate rules, carrier options, delivery dates and in-store pickup so checkout shows the right options for each cart. Surprise or unclear shipping costs are a common friction point, so this can matter more than payment tweaks. It requires careful setup of rules and product attributes. Skip it if you offer flat-rate or free shipping across the board.

How do you diagnose checkout friction before buying a tool?

Measure step-by-step drop-off from cart to order, then watch real sessions on the step with the biggest loss before choosing a category of tool. Most stores find one dominant cause, and that cause decides the purchase.

  1. Map the funnel by step: cart, contact, shipping, payment, review, order. Note where the largest relative drop sits, by device.
  2. Check payment failures: pull decline and error rates from your processor dashboard by card type and country. High declines point to orchestration or fraud tuning, not design.
  3. Check address errors: count failed validations and returned-to-sender parcels. That points to address capture.
  4. Check shipping surprises: confirm shipping costs and delivery dates appear before checkout. If they don't, fix that first.
  5. Check wallet coverage: compare the wallets you offer with what your mobile traffic expects.
  6. Check trust signals: returns policy, contact details and recognizable payment marks visible at payment.
  7. Watch sessions: a session replay tool shows rage clicks and stalled fields on the losing step.
  8. Test one change at a time: use A/B testing software or your platform's own testing where checkout allows it, and run tests long enough to cover weekday patterns.

Broader conversion research methods are in our list of conversion rate optimization tools. Remember that adding checkout scripts can slow pages, so track load times with website performance monitoring tools after each install.

What mistakes do stores make with checkout optimization?

The biggest mistake is stacking several checkout apps that each add a script, a field or a redirect. The second is judging a change by a few days of data or by a vendor case study.

  • Buying a one-click tool when the real issue is late-revealed shipping costs.
  • Adding upsells at payment, where they compete with the one action that matters.
  • Running two fraud tools, or a fraud tool on top of platform risk scoring, without clear ownership of decisions.
  • Ignoring declines: payment failures don't show up as design problems in heatmaps.
  • Forgetting that the platform decides what's possible; check our comparison of ecommerce platforms for growing DTC brands before committing to a checkout strategy.

What else do buyers ask about checkout optimization software?

Most questions come down to platform limits and how to prove a tool worked. The short answers are below.

Can I customize Shopify checkout without Shopify Plus?

Partly. Standard plans offer branding and app-based extensions in some areas, but the deepest checkout customization is tied to Shopify Plus, so check Shopify's current checkout documentation for your plan.

Is payment orchestration worth it for a small store?

Usually not. Orchestration pays off when you use several processors or sell across markets with different payment methods; a single-market store on one processor rarely needs it.

Will adding more wallets always help conversion?

No. Wallets help when your shoppers already use them, and too many buttons can clutter the page, so check which wallets your mobile traffic actually expects.

How long should a checkout test run?

Long enough to cover at least full weekly cycles and reach a sample your testing tool considers reliable. Short tests on low traffic often produce false winners.

Do fraud tools reduce conversion?

They can, if they decline good orders. Judge a fraud tool on both chargebacks avoided and good orders approved, and review its decisions in the first weeks.

What's the verdict on checkout optimization software?

Start native, measure step-by-step drop-off, and buy for the bucket that's leaking: wallets for speed, address and shipping tools for clarity, orchestration and fraud tools for failed payments. Treat any promised lift as a hypothesis to test on your own traffic.
