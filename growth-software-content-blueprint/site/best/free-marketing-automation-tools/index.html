Quick answer

Most free tiers automate one thing well: a welcome email, a form-to-CRM handoff or a two-step sync. Very few run branching lifecycle journeys without payment, so pick the tool whose ceiling you are least likely to hit in six months.

  • Small list needing real email automation: MailerLite Free, which lists automations on its free tier.
  • CRM-first B2B team: HubSpot's free tools, if one automated email action is enough for now.
  • Large list, infrequent sends: Brevo Free, which caps daily sends.
  • Connecting existing apps: Zapier Free for two-step automations, Make Free for multi-step scenarios.
  • Teams with a technical owner: n8n Community Edition or Mautic, free to self-host but not free to run.

Free plan, trial or self-hosted

  • Free plan: no end date, but hard caps on contacts, sends, tasks or users, and usually vendor branding.
  • Free trial: paid features for a limited period. Fine for evaluation, risky for production, because automations stop or downgrade on expiry. Some well-known platforms, ActiveCampaign among them, have historically offered trials rather than a permanent free plan; check current offers.
  • Self-hosted community edition: no licence fee, but you pay for servers, email sending, updates and someone's time.

Every tool below has a permanent free option.

The tools

MailerLite Free

Best for: a solo operator or early-stage business that wants a genuine welcome sequence on a small list.

What it offers: campaigns, forms, landing pages and a visual automation builder. The MailerLite pricing page lists Free at 250 subscribers, 2,500 monthly emails and 2 seats, with automations included (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: automation is part of the free tier, and the next step is modest: Growing Business is listed at $12 a month billed monthly, or $10.80 a month billed yearly, for 500 subscribers, 5,000 emails, 3 seats, 50 automations and logo removal (checked 4 November 2026).

Limitations: 250 subscribers is a low ceiling, logo removal is a paid feature, and there is no sales pipeline. Skip it if you need a CRM in the same tool.

HubSpot free tools

Best for: B2B teams wanting free CRM, forms and basic email in one place, with a plan to grow into paid HubSpot.

What it offers: the Marketing Hub pricing page lists the free tier for up to 2 users, 2,000 email sends per calendar month with HubSpot branding, and 1 automated action (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: forms, contacts and email activity share one record, which makes a later move to paid automation cleaner.

Limitations: one automated action covers a single follow-up, not a sequence. Starter raises this to up to 10 automated actions and removes branding; Professional, where fuller workflows sit, lists a required one-time onboarding fee of $3,000 (checked 4 November 2026). Our ActiveCampaign and HubSpot comparison weighs that jump.

Brevo Free

Best for: larger contact lists that send occasional campaigns.

What it offers: email, SMS, a basic CRM and workflows. The Brevo pricing page lists Free at up to 300 emails per day, and lists logo removal on Starter, from $9 a month on monthly billing (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: pricing follows send volume, so a large, quiet list stays cheap.

Limitations: a daily cap means a 1,500-person newsletter takes five days. Confirm the current free contact cap and automation allowance before relying on it.

Mailchimp Free

Best for: very small senders who want a familiar editor and will stay simple.

What it offers: campaigns, forms and landing pages, with reduced automation compared with paid plans. Mailchimp has revised its free plan several times, so check the official Mailchimp pricing page rather than remembered figures.

Limitations: in our editorial assessment, the free tier is campaign software with a starter automation, not a lifecycle platform. Our guide to Mailchimp alternatives covers common switch reasons.

Kit free plan

Best for: creators and consultants growing through lead magnets and landing pages.

What it offers: Kit (formerly ConvertKit) has a free plan with forms, landing pages and broadcasts; richer sequences sit on paid plans. Confirm current limits on the Kit pricing page.

Limitations: built for creators, with no deal pipeline. Skip it if automation must hand leads to sales reps.

Zapier Free

Best for: connecting two apps, such as a form to a spreadsheet or CRM.

What it offers: the Zapier pricing page lists Free at 100 tasks per month, two-step Zaps, 15-minute updates and 1 user (checked 4 November 2026).

Limitations: two steps means one trigger and one action, so "add to CRM and notify Slack" already needs Professional, listed from $19.99 a month (checked 4 November 2026). Our Zapier, Make and n8n comparison explains how each meters usage.

Make Free

Best for: operators who want branching, multi-step workflows on a visual canvas.

What it offers: scenarios with routers, filters and data mapping, within a monthly usage allowance listed on the Make pricing page.

Limitations: every module run consumes usage, so frequent polling can exhaust a free allowance quickly. The canvas asks more of non-technical marketers.

n8n Community Edition

Best for: teams with a developer or technical marketer to host and maintain it.

What it offers: workflow automation with code steps and webhooks. The community edition can be self-hosted without a licence fee under n8n's source-available licence; Cloud and enterprise editions are paid. See n8n pricing and its hosting documentation.

Limitations: you own uptime, backups, upgrades and security, and some governance features are paid-only. If that sounds heavy, our no-code automation platforms guide covers hosted options.

Mautic

Best for: organisations wanting open-source campaigns, segments, forms and lead points with full data control.

What it offers: Mautic is downloaded, run on your own server and connected to an email sending service.

Limitations: setup, scheduled jobs, updates and deliverability are yours. Without a technical owner it can cost more in time than a paid hosted tool.

Also considered: Klaviyo and Omnisend offer free entry tiers for online stores; see our ecommerce email platform shortlist. Trial-only platforms were excluded.

Modelling your first upgrade trigger

Ask which limit you will hit first. Write down contacts in six months, emails per month and automated steps per new lead.

Hypothetical example: a consultancy with 150 subscribers adds 40 leads a month, runs a three-email welcome sequence and sends one monthly newsletter.

  • MailerLite Free: the 250 cap arrives in about three months; email volume is not the constraint.
  • HubSpot free tools: the sequence needs more than one automated action, so the trigger arrives on day one.
  • Zapier Free: form-to-CRM-plus-notification needs three steps, forcing a paid plan or two Zaps sharing 100 tasks.

Then price the trigger: our marketing automation pricing guide models the move from free to paid across contact-, task- and seat-based plans.

Export and lock-in risks

  • Contacts: confirm export includes tags, custom fields and consent status, not just addresses.
  • Workflows: automation logic rarely exports portably, so document triggers, delays and conditions as you build.
  • Engagement history: opens and clicks often stay behind, resetting segments.
  • Hosted pages: vendor-hosted form and landing page URLs break when you leave; use your own domain where possible.
  • Consent proof: keep opt-in records independent of the tool.

Recommendations by scenario

  • Newsletter plus welcome sequence: MailerLite Free, then its first paid plan.
  • B2B lead capture: HubSpot's free tools, with a budget for the tier you will need. If the CRM matters more than email, compare free CRM plans and their limits.
  • Email-only needs: see what free email marketing plans actually allow for deeper sending-limit detail.
  • Agency client work: free tiers suit proofs of concept only; the workflow automation tools agencies use handle multi-client set-ups.

Mistakes and questions to ask

Common mistakes

  • Choosing by headline contact count while ignoring per-workflow step limits.
  • Running production automations on a trial.
  • Treating self-hosted software as zero cost.

Questions to ask

  • Which features disappear if we downgrade from paid back to free?
  • Do unsubscribed or inactive contacts count toward the cap?
  • Is any support channel included on free, or documentation only?

FAQs

Can a free tool run a proper lead nurture sequence?

On small lists, some can. MailerLite lists automations on Free, while HubSpot's free tier allows one automated action. Branching nurture with scoring generally needs a paid plan.

Is Zapier's free plan enough for marketing automation?

It is a connector rather than a campaign tool: 100 tasks a month and two-step Zaps (checked 4 November 2026).

Is self-hosting n8n or Mautic really free?

The licence is; hosting, email sending, maintenance and security are not. Budget for them explicitly.

Verdict

Our editorial assessment: MailerLite Free is the most complete free email automation tier for small lists, HubSpot's free tools are the better base for CRM-led B2B teams who accept a costly next step, and Zapier or Make fill integration gaps. Self-hosted options reward technical teams and punish everyone else. Identify your first upgrade trigger and exit path before building the second workflow.
