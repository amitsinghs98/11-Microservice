Which free marketing automation tool should you choose?

Pick the free tool whose ceiling you will hit last, not the one with the longest feature list: an email platform with a built-in automation builder for a small list, a CRM's free tier if leads must reach sales, or a connector such as Zapier or Make if the job is moving data between apps. Free tiers automate one or two jobs well, and almost none of them run branching lifecycle journeys without payment.

  • Small list that needs a welcome sequence: MailerLite or Kit, both of which have offered automation on their free plans. Confirm the current subscriber cap before you build.
  • B2B team that wants leads, forms and email on one record: HubSpot's free tools, if a single automated follow-up is enough for now and you accept that the next tiers cost much more.
  • Large list that sends rarely: Brevo's free plan, which caps sending per day rather than per month.
  • Connecting two apps you already pay for: Zapier Free for simple trigger-and-action jobs, or Make Free if you need branches.
  • A team with a technical owner: n8n's self-hosted Community Edition or Mautic, which carry no licence fee but are not free to run.

Is it a free plan, a trial or a self-hosted edition?

A free plan has no end date but hard caps; a trial unlocks paid features for a fixed period and then stops or downgrades your automations. Self-hosted editions remove the licence fee and hand you the servers, email sending, upgrades and security instead.

The distinction matters most for production work. A welcome sequence built on a trial will break the day the trial ends, and the subscriber who signed up that morning gets nothing. Some well-known platforms, ActiveCampaign among them, are better known for trials than permanent free plans, so they are excluded from this list. Check the vendor's current offer if you are considering one.

What does each free tier actually let you automate?

Email platforms let you automate a sequence for a small list, CRM-led suites let you automate a single follow-up on a shared contact record, and connectors let you move data between apps within a monthly usage allowance. The blocks below give best-for and skip-it-if guidance for each.

MailerLite Free: best for a first welcome sequence

MailerLite's free plan has included its visual automation builder alongside campaigns, forms and landing pages, which is unusual at this price point. The ceiling is the subscriber cap and the MailerLite logo on emails, both stated on the MailerLite pricing page. Skip it if you need a sales pipeline in the same tool.

Kit free plan: best for creators and consultants

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) is built around lead magnets, landing pages and broadcasts. Its free plan suits a consultant growing a newsletter, but richer sequences and reporting sit on paid plans. Check the current subscriber allowance on the Kit pricing page. Skip it if automation must hand leads to sales reps, because there is no deal pipeline.

Mailchimp Free: best for very simple senders

Mailchimp's free tier is campaign software with a starter automation, not a lifecycle platform, in our assessment. Mailchimp has revised its free plan several times, so rely on the Mailchimp pricing page rather than remembered figures. If you are already outgrowing it, our guide to Mailchimp alternatives for growing businesses covers the usual reasons teams move.

Brevo Free: best for a large list that sends occasionally

Brevo meters its free plan by emails per day rather than contacts, so a large but quiet list stays free. The trade-off is pace: a newsletter to several thousand people can take days to go out under a daily cap. Confirm the current daily limit and automation allowance on the Brevo pricing page. Skip it if you send time-sensitive campaigns to your whole list.

HubSpot free tools: best for CRM-first B2B teams

HubSpot's free tools put forms, contacts and email activity on one record, which makes a later move into paid automation cleaner than stitching tools together. The automation allowance on the free tier is very small: enough for a single follow-up, not a sequence. Check the current limits on the HubSpot Marketing Hub pricing page. Skip it if you need multi-step nurture soon and cannot budget for the paid tiers; our ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot comparison weighs that jump.

Zapier Free: best for one trigger and one action

The Zapier pricing page lists the Free plan at 100 tasks a month with two-step Zaps and 15-minute polling (checked 4 November 2026). Two steps means one trigger and one action, so "add the lead to the CRM and alert Slack" already needs a paid plan. Zapier's pricing FAQ says triggers, filters and failed actions do not use tasks (checked 4 November 2026), which stretches the allowance further than the headline suggests. Skip it if your workflows branch.

Make Free: best for branching workflows on a budget

The Make pricing page lists its Free plan at 1,000 credits a month, 2 active scenarios and a 15-minute minimum interval (checked 4 November 2026). Routers and filters let you branch for free, which Zapier does not. The catch is that most module actions use a credit, so a scenario that polls often can drain the allowance. The canvas also asks more of non-technical marketers.

n8n Community Edition: best for teams with a developer

n8n publishes a self-hosted Community Edition alongside paid cloud plans, as its pricing page shows (checked 4 November 2026). There is no licence fee, but you own uptime, backups, upgrades and security. n8n's licence is source-available rather than a conventional open-source licence, so read its terms if you plan to embed it in client work.

Mautic: best for full data control

Mautic is open-source marketing automation with campaigns, segments, forms and lead points. You download it from the Mautic project site, run it on your own server and connect an email sending service. Skip it unless someone on the team will own server maintenance and deliverability, because without that owner it costs more in time than a paid hosted tool.

Also considered: Klaviyo and Omnisend have free entry tiers for online stores, covered in our ecommerce email platform shortlist.

Based on the product information reviewed, free tiers fail in predictable places: a second workflow step, a subscriber cap reached mid-campaign, branding on client-facing emails, and support limited to documentation. None of these is hidden, but they are easy to overlook when the first automation works.

How do you work out your first upgrade trigger?

Write down three numbers for six months from now: contacts, emails per month and automated steps per new lead. Whichever limit those numbers cross first is your upgrade trigger, and its price is your real cost of "free".

Hypothetical example: a consultancy with 150 subscribers adds 40 leads a month, runs a three-email welcome sequence, and wants each new lead added to a spreadsheet and announced in Slack.

  • Email platform: list size grows by 240 in six months, so the trigger is whichever month crosses the free subscriber cap. Email volume is rarely the constraint at this size.
  • HubSpot free tools: a three-email sequence needs more than the free automation allowance, so the trigger arrives on day one.
  • Zapier Free: spreadsheet plus Slack is two actions after the trigger, which exceeds a two-step Zap. You would need two Zaps sharing 100 tasks, or the Professional plan, listed from $19.99 a month (checked 4 November 2026) on the Zapier pricing page.
  • Make Free: about three credits per lead (the trigger module counts) is roughly 120 credits a month, well inside 1,000.

Then price the trigger. Our marketing automation pricing guide converts contact-, task- and seat-based plans into an annual figure.

What should you check about export and lock-in before you build?

Check that you can export contacts with their tags, custom fields and consent status, and document every workflow yourself, because automation logic rarely exports in a form another tool can import. Free tiers make it cheap to start and surprisingly costly to leave.

  • Contacts: confirm export includes consent timestamps and source, not just email addresses.
  • Workflows: keep a plain document of each trigger, delay and condition as you build it.
  • Engagement history: opens and clicks often stay behind, which resets your engagement segments after a move.
  • Hosted pages: vendor-hosted form and landing page URLs break when you leave, so publish them on your own domain where you can.

Which free tool fits your scenario?

Match the tool to the job you need automated first, and plan the paid step before you need it. Free tiers are a proof of concept for agencies and a starting point for founders, not a long-term home for revenue-critical workflows.

  • Newsletter plus welcome sequence: an email platform's free tier; compare the caps in our guide to what free email marketing plans allow.
  • B2B lead capture into a CRM: HubSpot's free tools, or compare free CRM plans and their limits if the pipeline matters more than email.
  • Glue between existing apps: Zapier or Make, then the no-code automation platforms shortlist when volume grows.
  • Agency client work: free tiers suit a pilot only; the workflow automation tools agencies use handle multi-client set-ups.

What else do buyers ask about free marketing automation?

Most questions come down to caps and hidden costs. The short answers are below, with the detail in the sections above.

Can a free tool run a proper lead nurture sequence?

On a small list, some can. Email platforms such as MailerLite have included automation on their free plans, while HubSpot's free tier allows only a very small automation allowance. Branching nurture with lead scoring generally needs a paid plan.

Is Zapier's free plan enough for marketing automation?

It is a connector, not a campaign tool. Zapier's pricing page lists 100 tasks a month and two-step Zaps on Free (checked 4 November 2026), which covers simple handoffs between apps but not multi-step workflows.

Is self-hosting n8n or Mautic really free?

The licence is free; hosting, email sending, maintenance, backups and security are not. Budget for the server and for the person who keeps it updated before you compare it with a paid hosted plan.

What's the verdict on free marketing automation tools?

Our editorial assessment: an email platform's free tier is the most practical start for a small list, HubSpot's free tools are the better base for CRM-led B2B teams that accept a costly next step, and Zapier or Make fill integration gaps. Self-hosted options reward technical teams and punish everyone else.
