Quick answer

  • Inbound-led agencies that run their own content and email: HubSpot, for attribution from first form fill to signed proposal.
  • Founder-led agencies that want a fast, clear deal board: Pipedrive.
  • Agencies that want sales and delivery boards in one workspace: monday CRM.
  • Agencies that want pipeline, budgets and resourcing in an agency-specific tool: Productive.
  • Smaller agencies that want CRM plus basic projects: Insightly.
  • Price-sensitive agencies with someone to configure it: Zoho CRM.

This page narrows our broader small-business CRM comparison to agency needs. If you would rather work through requirements before looking at vendors, start with the step-by-step guide to choosing an agency CRM.

The agency pipeline we modelled

We assessed each CRM against a hypothetical agency of 5 to 10 people selling SEO, paid media and content retainers. Its pipeline has five stages:

  1. Lead: a referral, inbound form or outbound reply. Source must be recorded.
  2. Discovery: a call booked, notes and a qualification checklist.
  3. Proposal: scope, pricing and a proposal document sent and tracked.
  4. Negotiation and signature: revisions, contract and start date.
  5. Onboarding handoff: the won deal creates a client record and a kickoff task in the delivery tool.

Two further needs separate agencies from other small businesses. First, one client account often holds several deals at once, for example a retainer renewal and a separate website project, so you need multiple pipelines and clean account-to-contact relationships. Second, partners and referrers matter, so the CRM should let you track who introduced a lead.

Six CRMs for agencies

HubSpot

Best for: agencies whose new business comes from their own content, webinars and email.

HubSpot connects form submissions, email engagement and deal records on one timeline, so you can see which campaign produced a signed retainer. Sales Hub tiers add sequences, quotes and more pipelines; Marketing Hub adds the attribution reporting. Many agencies also know HubSpot from client work, which shortens training.

Pricing: free tools, then per-seat tiers by hub; see the HubSpot Sales Hub pricing page. Limitation: the attribution story depends on Marketing Hub, and the Professional tiers are a large step up. Skip it if nearly all your work comes from referrals and you will not use the marketing side.

Pipedrive

Best for: founder-led agencies where two or three people run new business.

Pipedrive's deal board maps neatly onto lead, discovery, proposal and signature, and it supports several pipelines, so retainers and one-off projects can sit apart. Its pricing page lists a Projects add-on for post-sale work and a Smart Docs add-on for proposals and documents (checked 4 November 2026).

Pricing: published per seat on the Pipedrive pricing page; see the cost model below. Limitation: marketing attribution is light, and add-ons for documents and projects increase the per-seat cost. Skip it if you need delivery resourcing and margin tracking.

monday CRM

Best for: agencies that already run delivery on monday.com.

Because monday CRM sits on the same work-management platform, a won deal can trigger an automation that creates a client project board. That is the cleanest handoff on this list if your delivery team already lives there.

Pricing: per seat, sold in seat bundles; check the monday CRM pricing page. Limitation: you design your own pipeline structure, and attribution reporting needs extra setup. Skip it if delivery runs in another tool such as Asana or ClickUp.

Productive

Best for: agencies that care about profitability per client as much as pipeline.

Productive is agency management software rather than a pure CRM. Its sales pipeline sits alongside budgeting, time tracking, resource planning and invoicing, so a proposal can become a budget and project without leaving the tool.

Pricing: per user across tiers; see the Productive pricing page. Limitation: sales automation, email sequencing and marketing attribution are not its focus. Skip it if outbound prospecting is a big part of your growth plan.

Insightly

Best for: small agencies that want a CRM with built-in project records.

Insightly's CRM converts won opportunities into projects, keeping the contact, account and delivery history linked. The vendor also sells marketing and service products separately.

Pricing: per user, tiered; see the Insightly pricing page. Limitation: project features are simpler than dedicated delivery tools, and its integration ecosystem is smaller than HubSpot's. Skip it if you already have a delivery platform you like.

Zoho CRM

Best for: cost-conscious agencies with an ops-minded person to configure it.

Zoho CRM handles multiple pipelines, custom modules for partners and referrers, and workflow rules. Zoho Projects and Zoho Campaigns can cover delivery and email if you want one vendor.

Pricing: per user by edition; see the Zoho CRM pricing page. Limitation: setup takes deliberate effort, and the experience across Zoho apps is less unified than a single product. Skip it if no one has time to own configuration.

Keeping sales CRM separate from delivery

Most agencies run two systems: a CRM for winning work and a project or agency-management tool for doing it. Trying to make one tool do both usually weakens one side. In our assessment, the cleaner pattern is:

  • The CRM owns leads, contacts, accounts, deals, proposals, renewal dates and lead source.
  • The delivery tool owns tasks, time, budgets, deliverables and client communication about work.
  • The handoff is one automated event: "deal won" creates the client and a kickoff project, carrying scope, value, start date and the contact list.
  • Upsells flow back: account managers log expansion opportunities in the CRM, not in a project comment.

Native integrations or a workflow tool can carry the handoff; our workflow automation tools for agencies roundup covers the connectors. Client performance reporting belongs in neither system; see the client reporting software for agencies list instead.

Mistakes and questions to ask

  • Mixing client contacts and prospects without a status field. Your newsletter list and outreach lists will collide.
  • Leaving lead source optional. Make it required at deal creation, or attribution reports will be empty.
  • Buying a CRM for client reporting. It will not replace your reporting tool.

Ask vendors: Can one account hold several open deals in different pipelines? Can a won deal create a record in our delivery tool natively? Which tier includes proposal or quote documents? Can non-sales staff get free or cheaper seats?

FAQs

Do agencies need a separate CRM for each client?

No. Your CRM is for your own new business and account growth. If you manage pipelines on behalf of clients, that work belongs in the client's own CRM.

Can a project management tool replace an agency CRM?

For a very small agency with few leads, sometimes. Once you need lead source, proposal tracking and renewal dates, a dedicated CRM or an agency platform with a pipeline is easier to report from.

How should agencies track referral partners?

Create partners as companies or a custom object, and link every referred deal to the partner record so you can report on partner-sourced revenue.

Verdict

There is no single agency CRM. Based on the product information reviewed, HubSpot suits agencies that win work through their own marketing, Pipedrive suits founder-led new business, and monday CRM or Productive suit agencies that want sales and delivery closely joined.
