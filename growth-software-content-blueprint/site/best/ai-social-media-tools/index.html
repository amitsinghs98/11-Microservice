Quick answer

Based on the product information reviewed, these are our picks by agency situation. Each one suits a different buyer, and none of them wins everywhere.

  • Client approvals come first: Planable, because review and sign-off are the core of the product rather than an add-on.
  • Large agency with an inbox and reporting workload: Sprout Social or Hootsuite, where AI writing sits inside a full management suite.
  • Small team on a tight budget: Buffer, for simple scheduling with an AI assistant built into the composer.
  • Many brands and a content-discovery habit: ContentStudio, which pairs AI writing with multi-workspace planning.
  • Visual-first channels such as Instagram and TikTok: Later.
  • Designing on-brand graphics fast: Canva, with brand kits and AI design features.
  • Generating complete posts (image and caption) from a prompt: Predis.ai, if you accept heavier editing.

Generation tools vs scheduling tools

The market splits into three groups, and mixing them up is the most common reason agencies end up paying twice.

  • Scheduling suites with AI writing: Hootsuite, Sprout Social, Buffer and Later. Their value is the calendar, publishing, analytics and permissions. AI drafts captions and suggests ideas inside the composer.
  • Collaboration-first planners: Planable and, to a degree, ContentStudio. Their value is the review loop between agency and client, with AI help along the way.
  • Generators: Predis.ai and Canva produce the creative itself, meaning layouts, images and sometimes video, with scheduling as a secondary feature.

General-purpose writing assistants also produce good caption drafts. If your team already pays for one, our roundup of AI writing tools for marketing teams explains where a chatbot is enough and where a dedicated platform earns its fee.

How we chose

We assessed each tool against the jobs an agency actually does every week:

  • Idea generation and post copy: whether AI works inside the composer and can adapt drafts per network.
  • Brand voice: whether you can store tone, examples or brand rules per client rather than per account.
  • Calendars and publishing: native scheduling, supported networks and bulk handling.
  • Approval workflows: internal review, external client sign-off and comment history.
  • Visual generation: native image or template creation versus integration with a design tool.
  • Multi-brand permissions: separate workspaces, per-client roles and seat costs.

The tools

Planable

Best for: agencies whose bottleneck is getting clients to approve posts on time.

Planable presents posts as they will appear in each network, and clients comment and approve in context. The vendor describes multi-level approval workflows and separate workspaces per client, plus an AI assistant for drafting and rewriting copy. Planable uses tiered plans; check the Planable pricing page for how users and workspaces are counted.

Strengths: clear client-facing review, version history and a workflow that matches agency sign-off. Limitations: analytics and social inbox features are lighter than in full suites, and AI generation is assistance rather than a creative engine. Skip it if you need deep reporting or community management in the same tool. Planable official site.

Sprout Social

Best for: larger agencies and in-house teams that want publishing, engagement, listening and reporting under one contract.

Sprout Social's AI Assist features help draft and adjust post copy and replies inside the platform. The product's broader strength is its reporting, social inbox and approval workflows. Pricing is per seat, which gets expensive as headcount grows; see Sprout Social's pricing page.

Strengths: mature reporting and permissions. Limitations: per-seat cost and an AI layer that supports the suite rather than replacing a design or writing tool. Skip it if you are a small team mainly looking for faster caption writing. Sprout Social official site.

Hootsuite

Best for: agencies already standardised on Hootsuite that want AI drafting without adding a separate tool.

Hootsuite's OwlyWriter AI generates captions, post ideas and rewrites in the composer, alongside scheduling, inbox and analytics. Plans are priced per user, with team and enterprise tiers sold through sales; see Hootsuite's plans page.

Strengths: broad network support and an established agency footprint. Limitations: seat costs rise quickly, and brand-voice controls per client deserve a close look during a trial. Skip it if you are switching away for cost reasons; our review of Hootsuite alternatives for agencies covers that path. Hootsuite official site.

Buffer

Best for: solo creators and small agencies that want a simple calendar with AI writing help.

Buffer includes an AI assistant in the composer for generating ideas, rewriting and repurposing posts across networks. Pricing is based on the number of connected channels, which makes it predictable for small portfolios; see Buffer's pricing page.

Strengths: low complexity and transparent channel-based pricing. Limitations: approvals and client permissions are basic compared with Planable or Sprout Social, and per-channel pricing scales poorly for agencies with many client profiles. Skip it if clients need structured sign-off. Buffer official site.

ContentStudio

Best for: agencies that curate as well as create and want many workspaces in one account.

ContentStudio combines content discovery, an AI writing assistant, AI image generation, a planner and approval workflows, with agency-oriented plans that support multiple workspaces. Plan limits vary; see the ContentStudio pricing page.

Strengths: a broad feature set for the price tier it targets. Limitations: breadth can mean a steeper learning curve, and AI credit allowances should be checked against your monthly volume. Skip it if you want a minimal interface. ContentStudio official site.

Later

Best for: visual brands on Instagram, TikTok and Pinterest.

Later's visual planner and grid preview suit image- and video-led channels, and its AI caption tools help produce copy variants. Plans are tiered by social sets and users; see Later's pricing page.

Strengths: strong visual planning. Limitations: less suited to LinkedIn-heavy B2B programmes, and approval depth varies by plan. Skip it if most of your clients are B2B. Later official site.

Canva

Best for: teams producing a high volume of branded graphics, carousels and short videos.

Canva's AI features (marketed as Magic Studio) cover text generation, design generation, image generation and resizing designs for other formats. Brand kits keep fonts, colours and logos consistent per client, and Canva offers a content planner for scheduling to some networks. Pricing is per person on paid plans; see Canva's pricing page.

Strengths: design speed and brand kits. Limitations: its scheduling is not a replacement for a management suite, and generated imagery needs a rights and likeness review. Skip it if you need approvals and reporting in one place. Canva official site.

Predis.ai

Best for: small brands and solo creators who want finished drafts (creative plus caption) from a single prompt.

Predis.ai generates social posts, carousels and short videos from text or product inputs, with captions and hashtags, and can schedule them. It uses credit-based tiers; see the Predis.ai pricing page.

Strengths: fast first drafts. Limitations: output tends towards template looks, and agencies with strict brand guidelines should expect heavy editing. Skip it if your clients have a mature design system. Predis.ai official site.

Workflow examples: solo creator and agency

Solo creator or freelancer (hypothetical)

A consultant posts to LinkedIn and Instagram four times a week. A lean stack is a scheduler with built-in AI (Buffer or Later) plus Canva for graphics. AI drafts three caption options; the creator picks one, edits it in their own voice and schedules it. Seat limits rarely matter here, but channel counts do, so check how each extra profile changes the bill.

Agency with ten clients (hypothetical)

An agency runs ten brands across three networks each. The account manager drafts a monthly calendar with AI ideas, a designer builds visuals from each client's brand kit in Canva, and posts move into Planable or Sprout Social for internal review and then client approval. Here the cost drivers are seats for designers, account managers and client reviewers, plus per-channel or per-workspace limits. Ask each vendor whether client approvers count as paid seats.

For visual production at volume, compare these options with dedicated AI image generators for ad creative and AI video editors that cut short-form clips.

A repurposing task to run in every trial

We did not run this task ourselves. It is a repeatable trial you can give every shortlisted tool so the comparison is fair:

  1. Take one published blog post of around 1,200 words from a real client.
  2. Ask each tool for a LinkedIn post, an Instagram carousel outline, three short X posts and one short-video script.
  3. Apply the client's brand voice notes, and see where the tool stores them.
  4. Score each output for factual fidelity to the source, voice match, network fit and editing time.
  5. Push one post through the full approval workflow to a test client account.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

  • Paying for two calendars. If you already schedule in one suite, add a design or generation tool rather than a second scheduler.
  • Ignoring client seats. Ask whether external approvers are free, limited or billed.
  • Assuming brand voice is per client. Some tools store voice settings per account, not per workspace.
  • Skipping human review. AI captions can misstate offers, prices or claims. Build a review step, as described in our AI content workflow with editorial checks.
  • Overlooking reporting. AI creation tools rarely replace social media analytics for multi-brand teams.

Questions for sales: How are AI credits counted and do they roll over? Which networks support direct publishing versus reminders? Can we set per-client roles? What happens to drafts if we downgrade?

FAQs

Can an AI social tool replace a social media manager?

No. These tools speed up drafting and resizing, but strategy, community judgement and client relationships still need a person. Treat AI output as a first draft.

Should agencies buy a generator or a scheduler first?

Usually the scheduler with approvals, because missed sign-offs cost more time than caption writing. Add a generator when design capacity becomes the constraint.

Are AI-generated social images safe to use for clients?

Check each vendor's commercial-use terms and avoid generating real people, logos or trademarks you lack rights to. When in doubt, get legal review.

How do I compare AI usage limits?

Convert each plan's credits or generation limits into your monthly post volume per client, then compare. Credits for images and video usually deplete faster than text.

Verdict

In our editorial assessment, agencies should choose by bottleneck. If approvals slow you down, Planable fits. If you need one suite for everything, Sprout Social or Hootsuite justify their seat costs at scale. Small teams get most value from Buffer or Later plus Canva. Predis.ai suits volume over polish.
