Which AI video editing tool should your marketing team choose?

Choose Descript for webinars, podcasts and talking-head edits, Opus Clip for cutting long recordings into short clips, and VEED or Kapwing for browser-based team editing with subtitles. Bring in Premiere Pro only if you have trained editors, and treat Runway and Synthesia as specialist add-ons rather than your main editor.

  • Speech-led content: Descript, which edits video through its transcript.
  • Long-to-short repurposing: Opus Clip.
  • Shared browser editing: VEED or Kapwing; trial both, buy one.
  • Trend-led social with templates: CapCut, once legal has read its asset terms.
  • Professional finishing: Adobe Premiere Pro.
  • Generated B-roll and concept shots: Runway.
  • Presenter-led training without filming: Synthesia.

Should you buy an editor or a text-to-video generator?

Buy an editor first, because most marketing video starts from footage you already have: demos, interviews, webinars and customer calls. Add a generator only when there's a recurring need for footage you can't film.

Editing-first tools apply AI to transcription, captions, silence removal, vertical reframing and clip selection. The output is still your footage, so most rights questions are settled before you start. Generators create new footage from a prompt, image or script, sometimes with a synthetic presenter. They save shoot days but raise questions about likeness, product accuracy, disclosure and the vendor's commercial terms. Treat their output like stock footage that needs a license check.

Which editing-first tools fit marketing work?

Descript and Opus Clip cover the most common marketing jobs, editing recorded talk and cutting it into clips. VEED, Kapwing and CapCut suit generalists, and Premiere Pro suits specialists.

Descript: best for webinars, podcasts and interviews

Descript transcribes a recording and lets you cut video by deleting words in the transcript. Based on the product information reviewed, it also offers filler-word removal, Studio Sound audio clean-up, AI voice features and an AI editing assistant. Plans are per seat with transcription and AI allowances; see Descript's pricing page.

Strengths: non-editors become productive quickly. Limitations: less suited to motion-heavy ads, and AI voice features need consent rules. Skip it if your output is mostly product B-roll set to music. Visit Descript.

Opus Clip: best for long-to-short repurposing

Opus Clip analyzes a long video, proposes short clips, reframes them vertically and adds animated captions. Pricing is credit-based, with credits tied to processing; see Opus Clip's pricing page.

Strengths: it automates finding the moments, which is the slowest part of clipping. Limitations: automated picks can cut mid-argument, so someone must watch every clip. Skip it if you rarely record long-form. Visit Opus Clip.

VEED: best for a browser editor with subtitles and brand kits

VEED is an online editor with automatic subtitles, subtitle translation, brand kits, templates and AI avatar features. Plans are per seat; see VEED's pricing page for export and watermark rules by plan.

Strengths: no install and a gentle learning curve. Limitations: long or high-resolution projects can strain a browser editor. Skip it if professional editors already work in desktop software. Visit VEED.

Kapwing: best for distributed teams that co-edit

Kapwing offers collaborative editing, auto subtitles, silence removal and repurposing in shared workspaces, priced per member; see Kapwing's pricing page.

Strengths: simple shared review. Limitations: it overlaps with VEED, so paying for both rarely makes sense. Skip it if one person does all the editing. Visit Kapwing.

CapCut: best for trend-led short-form

CapCut provides auto captions, templates and effects across desktop, web and mobile, with paid plans adding features and storage. Before using its templates, music or effects in paid campaigns, check which assets CapCut's official site and terms clear for commercial use. Some organizations also send apps from its owner, ByteDance, through extra procurement review.

Strengths: speed and a low entry cost. Limitations: lighter team governance and asset rights that need checking. Skip it if legal needs clear, contractual asset rights.

Adobe Premiere Pro: best for trained in-house editors

Premiere Pro adds AI assistance to a professional editor, including speech-to-text captions, speech enhancement and Firefly-powered generative features such as extending a clip. Access is by Creative Cloud subscription, and generative features draw on credits; see Adobe's Premiere Pro page.

Strengths: export, color and audio control. Limitations: far steeper learning curve than browser tools. Skip it if your editors are marketers rather than video specialists.

When does a text-to-video generator earn its place?

A generator earns its place when you need the same kind of unfilmable footage every month: concept shots, abstract B-roll or localized presenter videos. It doesn't replace an editor, and its output needs more review than an edit.

Runway: best for concept footage and stylized B-roll

Runway generates video from text and images and offers video-to-video and AI editing tools. Usage is credit-based by plan; see Runway's pricing page.

Strengths: creative range for mood films and visual experiments. Limitations: clips are short, consistency across shots takes effort, and generated products may not match reality. Skip it if you need accurate product demonstrations. Visit Runway.

Synthesia: best for training and explainer videos in many languages

Synthesia turns a script into a video with an AI avatar and voice, with templates and brand settings. Plans include video-minute allowances, with enterprise tiers by quote; see Synthesia's pricing page. HeyGen is a comparable avatar tool worth trialing alongside it.

Strengths: localization without reshoots. Limitations: avatar video can feel impersonal in advertising, and custom avatars of real staff need documented consent. Skip it if authenticity is the point of the content. Visit Synthesia.

How far can you trust auto captions and export settings?

Not far enough to publish unchecked: none of these vendors' pages we reviewed promise an accuracy rate, and audio quality, accents and product names all affect results. Export resolution and watermark rules also vary by plan, so confirm both before you commit.

Is per-seat or credit pricing cheaper for your volume?

Per-seat pricing is usually cheaper for a team that edits every day but generates rarely. Credit pricing suits occasional heavy bursts, but only if you model credits against a real month of briefs.

Credits deplete according to output length, resolution and model, so the same plan can cover a month for one team and a week for another. When comparing plans, check four things: export resolution, watermark removal, the monthly AI or credit allowance and whether unused credits roll over. What review does generated footage need before it's published?

Generated footage needs a caption proofread, a likeness and voice consent check, a product-truth check, a rights check and a disclosure decision. Edited footage of your own recordings needs the first and usually the third.

  • Captions: proofread names, product terms and numbers in every subtitle.
  • Likeness and voice: get written consent before cloning anyone's face or voice.
  • Product truth: footage must not show features or results your product doesn't deliver.
  • Rights: confirm the vendor's commercial terms and the licenses on music, stock and templates.
  • Disclosure: follow each platform's rules on labeling synthetic or altered media.

What else do marketing teams ask about AI video editing tools?

The recurring questions are about ads, accuracy and resolution. Each answer below is short on purpose.

Do we need both a video editor and a text-to-video generator?

Usually not at first. Start with an editor, and add a generator only when you have a monthly need for footage you can't film.

Can AI-generated video run in paid ads?

Often it can, subject to the vendor's commercial terms, each ad platform's policies on synthetic media and your own legal review of likeness and trademarks.

Will a browser-based editor export in 4K?

It depends on the plan and the project length, so check the export resolution listed on the vendor's pricing page before you buy.

Who should approve AI-edited video before it goes live?

At minimum, the content owner checks captions and claims, and anyone whose face or voice appears, or is synthesized, signs off on its use.
