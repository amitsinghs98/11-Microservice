Most marketing video work is cutting, captioning and resizing footage you already have. Generating footage from a prompt is a separate job with separate risks. This guide keeps the two apart.

Quick answer

  • Webinars, podcasts and talking-head edits: Descript, for editing video by editing its transcript.
  • Long recordings into short clips: Opus Clip.
  • Browser-based team editing with subtitles: VEED or Kapwing.
  • Fast short-form with templates: CapCut, after checking commercial-use terms for its assets.
  • Professional editors who need AI inside a full NLE: Adobe Premiere Pro.
  • Generated B-roll and concept footage: Runway.
  • Presenter-led training or explainer videos without filming: Synthesia.

Based on the product information reviewed, no single tool covers editing, clipping and generation well, so most teams pair one editor with one specialist.

Editing-first tools vs text-to-video generators

Editing-first tools start from footage you own: recordings, product demos, interviews. AI handles transcription, captions, silence removal, reframing for vertical formats and clip selection. The output is your footage, so rights questions are mostly settled before you begin.

Generators create new footage from a prompt, an image or a script, including synthetic presenters. They save filming time but introduce questions about likeness, accuracy, disclosure and the vendor's commercial-use terms. Treat them like stock footage that needs a licence check, not like an edit.

How we chose

Criteria, in order of weight for a marketing team:

  • Short-form editing speed and vertical reframing.
  • Captions: auto-subtitle workflow, editing of the transcript and caption styling.
  • Repurposing: turning long recordings into clips.
  • Brand kits and templates for consistent output across editors.
  • Collaboration: comments, shared projects and review links.
  • Export quality and limits: resolution, watermarks and export caps by plan.
  • Commercial-use terms for generated footage, music and template assets.

What we checked: official product, pricing and help pages. We performed no hands-on testing, so we make no claims about subtitle accuracy rates or render quality. Editing-first tools

Descript

Best for: content teams turning webinars, podcasts and interviews into polished edits.

Descript transcribes recordings and lets you cut video by deleting words in the transcript. The vendor also lists filler-word removal, audio clean-up (Studio Sound), AI voice features and an AI editing assistant. Plans are per seat with transcription and AI usage allowances; see Descript's pricing page.

Strengths: very fast for speech-led content, and non-editors pick it up quickly. Limitations: less suited to motion-heavy or effects-driven ads, and AI voice features need consent and policy controls. Skip it if your output is mostly product B-roll set to music. Descript official site.

Opus Clip

Best for: repurposing long videos into short vertical clips at volume.

Opus Clip analyses a long recording, proposes short clips, reframes them for vertical formats and adds animated captions. Pricing is credit-based, with credits tied to processing time; see Opus Clip's pricing page.

Strengths: removes the slowest part of clipping, which is finding the moments. Limitations: automated clip selection can miss context or cut mid-argument, so every clip needs a human watch. Skip it if you rarely produce long-form source video. Opus Clip official site.

VEED

Best for: marketing teams that want a browser editor with subtitles, translation and brand kits.

VEED is an online editor with automatic subtitles, subtitle translation, brand kits, templates and AI avatar features. Plans are per seat; see VEED's pricing page.

Strengths: no install, and approachable for generalists. Limitations: browser editing can strain on long or high-resolution projects; check export resolution and watermark rules per plan. Skip it if you have professional editors who already use desktop software. VEED official site.

Kapwing

Best for: distributed teams that co-edit and review in the browser.

Kapwing offers collaborative editing, auto subtitles, silence removal and repurposing features, with shared workspaces. Pricing is per member; see Kapwing's pricing page.

Strengths: real-time collaboration and simple review. Limitations: overlaps heavily with VEED, so trial both rather than buying both. Skip it if one person does all editing. Kapwing official site.

CapCut

Best for: social teams producing trend-led short-form with templates and effects.

CapCut provides auto captions, templates, effects and quick vertical editing on desktop, web and mobile, with paid plans adding more features and storage. Before using its templates, music or effects in paid campaigns, read which assets are cleared for commercial use in CapCut's official terms and plan pages. Some organisations also put apps from its owner, ByteDance, through extra procurement review.

Strengths: speed and a low entry cost. Limitations: asset licensing for commercial use needs checking, and team governance is lighter than enterprise tools. Skip it if your legal team needs clear, contractual asset rights.

Adobe Premiere Pro

Best for: in-house video teams and agencies with trained editors.

Premiere Pro adds AI assistance to a professional editor, including speech-to-text transcription and captions, speech enhancement and Firefly-powered generative features such as extending clips. Access is by Creative Cloud subscription, and generative features draw on credits; see Adobe's Premiere Pro page.

Strengths: export control, colour and audio depth. Limitations: a learning curve far above browser tools. Skip it if your editors are marketers, not video specialists.

Text-to-video and avatar generators

Runway

Best for: creative teams generating concept footage, B-roll and stylised shots.

Runway generates video from text and images and offers video-to-video and AI editing tools. Usage is credit-based by plan; see Runway's pricing page.

Strengths: creative range for mood films and visual experiments. Limitations: clips are short, consistency across shots takes effort, and generated products or people may not match reality. Skip it if you need accurate product demonstrations. Runway official site.

Synthesia

Best for: training, onboarding and explainer videos with a synthetic presenter in many languages.

Synthesia turns a script into a video with an AI avatar and voice, with templates and brand settings. Plans include video-minute allowances, with enterprise tiers by quote; see Synthesia's pricing page. HeyGen is a comparable option worth trialling alongside it.

Strengths: fast localisation without reshoots. Limitations: avatar video can feel impersonal in brand advertising, and custom avatars of real staff require documented consent. Skip it if authenticity is the point of the content. Synthesia official site.

Per-seat vs credit pricing

Editing-first tools mostly charge per seat, with allowances for transcription hours, storage or AI actions. Generators and clipping tools mostly charge in credits, which deplete according to output length, resolution and model. A team that edits daily but generates rarely should favour seats; a team that generates heavily should model credits against a month of real briefs.

When comparing plans, check four limits: export resolution, watermark removal, monthly AI or credit allowance, and whether unused credits roll over. Our cost model for AI content tools shows how to convert these into a cost per asset.

Review requirements for generated footage

  • Captions: proofread every auto-subtitle, especially names, product terms and numbers. Accuracy varies with audio quality and accents.
  • Likeness and voice: get written consent before cloning any real person's face or voice.
  • Product truth: generated footage must not show features or results your product does not deliver.
  • Rights: confirm the vendor's commercial-use terms and the licence on music, stock and templates.
  • Disclosure: follow platform rules on labelling synthetic or altered media.

FAQs

Do I need both an editor and a generator?

Most marketing teams need an editor first. Add a generator only when you have a recurring need for footage you cannot film.

Can I use AI-generated video in paid ads?

Often yes, subject to the vendor's commercial terms, ad platform policies and your own legal review of likeness and trademarks.

How accurate are automatic captions?

We did not measure accuracy. Treat auto captions as a draft and budget proofreading time for every video.

Will a browser editor handle 4K exports?

It depends on plan and project length. Check export resolution limits on the vendor's pricing page before committing.

Verdict

In our editorial assessment, choose an editing-first tool around your most common source material: speech-led recordings point to Descript, long-form repurposing to Opus Clip, team editing to VEED or Kapwing, and professional work to Premiere Pro.
