Which review and UGC platform should you choose?

Choose by where your reviews need to appear: on your own store only, or also on retailer and marketplace pages. Shopify apps such as Judge.me, Loox and Okendo cover your own store well, while Bazaarvoice and PowerReviews are built for syndication to retailers.

  • Fast Shopify launch with a free plan: Judge.me.
  • Photo-led brands: Loox.
  • Reviews plus surveys and product attributes: Okendo.
  • Reviews and loyalty from one vendor: Yotpo or Stamped.
  • Product and company reviews together: REVIEWS.io.
  • Retailer syndication at scale: Bazaarvoice or PowerReviews.

What makes reviews trustworthy, and what does the law say?

Trustworthy reviews come from verified buyers, are published whether positive or negative, and disclose any incentive. In the US, the FTC's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) has sections on fake or false reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, insider reviews and review suppression (checked 4 November 2026).

In practice, ask each platform how it handles four safeguards:

  • Verification: does it mark reviews from confirmed purchases, and how?
  • Moderation: are rejections based on published content rules (profanity, personal data, off-topic) rather than on star rating?
  • Incentives: if you offer points or discounts for reviews, is the incentive offered regardless of sentiment and labelled on the review?
  • Display: can shoppers sort and filter to see negative reviews, or does the widget bury them?

This isn't legal advice; rules differ by country, so have counsel check your incentive and moderation policy.

Vendors commonly claim they do, but the effect varies by category, price point and how many reviews a product already has. Treat any vendor uplift figure as a claim to test on your own store, not a forecast.

What are the strengths and limits of each platform?

Collection and widgets are similar across the field; the real differences are syndication reach, data capture and whether you also want loyalty from the same vendor. Each block names who should choose the tool and who should skip it.

Okendo: best for Shopify brands that want rich review content and surveys

Okendo positions itself around product reviews with photos and video, attributes such as fit or skin type, and Q&A, plus customer surveys and other zero-party data features; confirm which of these sit on your plan. It suits Shopify brands that want reviews to double as product data.

Pricing: not quoted here; see the official Okendo site for current plans and what each tier unlocks.

Limitations: it's Shopify-centred, and the broader feature set means more to configure than a basic reviews app.

Skip it if you only need star ratings and a review email.

Yotpo: best for brands that want reviews and loyalty from one vendor

Yotpo offers product reviews and visual UGC alongside separate loyalty and other retention products. Its reviews product covers collection, moderation, on-site widgets and syndication options.

Pricing: not quoted here; see the official Yotpo site for current plans and what each tier unlocks.

Limitations: buying several Yotpo products is a bigger commitment than a single-purpose app, so price each module and check whether you need the bundle.

Skip it if you want a single, inexpensive reviews app with no wider suite.

Judge.me: best for small and mid-sized Shopify stores on a budget

Judge.me is a Shopify reviews app whose pricing page lists a free plan with photo and video reviews, and a paid Awesome plan at $15 a month (checked 5 November 2026). It also covers review request emails and storefront widgets. It's the sensible default when you need reviews running this week.

Pricing: free and paid plans as above; confirm current terms and currency on the official Judge.me site.

Limitations: lighter syndication and enterprise moderation than Bazaarvoice or PowerReviews.

Skip it if you need reviews syndicated to major retailer sites.

Loox: best for visual, photo-led brands

Loox focuses on photo and video reviews, with display widgets designed around visual content. It suits fashion, beauty and lifestyle brands where a customer photo sells better than text.

Pricing: not quoted here; see the official Loox site for current plans and what each tier unlocks.

Limitations: less depth for text-heavy categories and for Q&A or detailed product attributes.

Skip it if your products are technical and buyers read long written reviews.

Stamped: best for brands wanting reviews plus loyalty from one vendor

Stamped positions itself around product and site reviews, photo and video UGC, Q&A and a loyalty product; confirm which ecommerce platforms it supports for your store. It's an alternative to Yotpo for brands that want both in one place at a smaller scale.

Pricing: not quoted here; see the official Stamped site for current plans and what each tier unlocks.

Limitations: as with any combined suite, check each module's depth against single-purpose tools.

Skip it if you already have a loyalty app you're happy with.

REVIEWS.io: best for brands that want product and company reviews together

REVIEWS.io collects both product reviews and company (service) reviews, with UGC display; ask which search and marketplace channels it can share reviews to in your markets. It suits brands that care about seller reputation as well as product pages.

Pricing: not quoted here; see the official REVIEWS.io site for current plans and what each tier unlocks.

Limitations: check which syndication and search-display options apply in your markets before relying on them.

Skip it if you only sell on your own site and need product reviews alone.

Bazaarvoice: best for brands that sell through major retailers

Bazaarvoice is an enterprise ratings, reviews and UGC platform whose retail syndication product distributes a brand's reviews to participating retailer sites (checked 5 November 2026), and it also sells sampling programmes. It suits manufacturers that need reviews to appear on retailer product pages, not only on their own site.

Pricing: not quoted here; see the official Bazaarvoice site for current plans and what each tier unlocks.

Limitations: enterprise pricing and implementation; small DTC brands won't use most of it.

Skip it if you sell only through your own store.

PowerReviews: best for mid-market and enterprise brands wanting syndication and analytics

PowerReviews offers ratings and reviews, Q&A, visual UGC, sampling and syndication, with an emphasis on review analytics. It's an alternative to Bazaarvoice for brands that want retailer reach.

Pricing: not quoted here; see the official PowerReviews site for current plans and what each tier unlocks.

Limitations: sales-led and heavier to implement than Shopify apps.

Skip it if you're a small Shopify brand without retail distribution.

How should you collect and moderate reviews?

Ask every buyer at the moment they've used the product, not the day it ships, and moderate against published content rules rather than sentiment. That gives you more reviews and a more credible mix of ratings.

Set the request delay by product type: days for a consumable, weeks for apparel that needs wearing. Send one reminder, not five. Route product defects and complaints from reviews to your support and product teams; a structured approach to that loop is covered in our list of customer feedback management software, which is written for B2B teams but follows the same principles. Answer negative reviews publicly and specifically.

What mistakes do brands make with review platforms?

The main mistake is paying for enterprise syndication with no retail distribution to syndicate to. The second is treating reviews as a widget rather than a data source for merchandising and product decisions.

  • Offering incentives only for positive reviews, or hiding negative ones.
  • Reusing customer photos in paid ads without recorded permission.
  • Ignoring page speed: several widgets from several apps can slow product pages.
  • Buying a reviews suite and a separate loyalty app that overlap; compare against our notes on loyalty and rewards platforms.

What else do buyers ask about ecommerce product review UGC platforms?

Most questions are about importing old reviews, incentives and Google display. Short answers follow; confirm specifics with each vendor.

Can I import my existing reviews when I switch platforms?

Most review platforms offer imports from other apps or CSV files. Check that verified-buyer status, photos and dates carry over, because losing them weakens the reviews' credibility.

Is it legal to give customers points for writing a review?

Incentives can be acceptable if they're offered regardless of whether the review is positive or negative and are disclosed. Rules vary by country, so check with counsel before launching an incentive.

Do product reviews appear in Google search results?

Reviews can feed review snippets and Google Shopping when they're marked up or shared through the right programmes. Ask each vendor which Google programmes it supports in your markets.

Should I moderate negative reviews?

Moderate for content rules such as profanity or personal data, not for sentiment. Suppressing negative reviews misleads shoppers and can breach review regulations.

What's the verdict?

Small and mid-sized Shopify brands should start with Judge.me, Loox or Okendo depending on whether price, photos or customer data matter most. Brands that sell through retailers should evaluate Bazaarvoice and PowerReviews for syndication.

Whichever you pick, write down your verification, moderation and incentive policy before launch and make sure the platform can enforce it.
