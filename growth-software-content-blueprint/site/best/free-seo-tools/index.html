Quick answer

An agency with two or three clients can run competent SEO without paying for software, as long as it accepts manual work and can only see sites it has access to. The free tools are strong on first-party data (what Google and Bing already know about a client's site). They are weak on competitor research, rank tracking at scale and white-label reporting.

  • Best for first-party search data: Google Search Console, with Bing Webmaster Tools as a second opinion.
  • Best for technical crawling on small sites: the free version of Screaming Frog SEO Spider.
  • Best for backlink and audit data on sites you own: Ahrefs Free, which needs ownership verification.
  • Best for occasional competitor lookups: Semrush's free account, which is capped daily.
  • Best for client reporting at no cost: Looker Studio connected to Search Console and GA4.
  • Best for local presence: Google Business Profile itself.

Who this guide is for

It's written for freelancers and agencies of one to three people who are signing their first retainers and want to know what they can deliver before committing to a subscription. It also suits in-house marketers who need to justify a paid tool by first showing what free tools can't do.

If you already manage ten or more clients, the free stack will cost you more in hours than a subscription costs in money. Start with our guide to choosing a full SEO platform by team type, or browse the wider SEO software category.

How we chose

We included a product only if an agency can use it for real client work at no cost, without a credit card, for longer than a trial period. Each tool also had to cover a distinct job: keyword discovery, technical checks, performance, local presence or reporting. Trials are listed separately so they're never mistaken for free tools.

Evidence came from official product pages and documentation. No hands-on testing was performed for this draft. Where a cap or limit could not be confirmed from an official page on 4 November 2026, we describe it in general terms and flag it in our research log. Products appear in workflow order, not by rank, and no vendor paid to be included or placed.

The tools

Google Search Console

Best for: every client, from day one. What it does: shows how Google crawls, indexes and displays a verified site, including the queries that produce impressions and clicks.

Key capabilities: performance reports by query, page, country and device; URL inspection; indexing and sitemap reports; Core Web Vitals based on field data. Pricing model: free from Google.

Strengths: it's Google's own data on the client's site, which no third-party estimate can replace. Limitations: you see nothing about competitors, history is kept only for a limited window, and interface exports are capped, so larger pulls need the API or a dashboard connector. Before you choose: get Owner or Full access added during client onboarding, not halfway through the first month. Search Console official site.

Bing Webmaster Tools

Best for: a second index view and clients with meaningful Bing or Microsoft-ecosystem traffic. What it does: Bing's counterpart to Search Console, with crawl, indexing and query data for verified sites.

Key capabilities: search performance, a site scan for on-page issues, keyword research and backlink views, according to Microsoft's product information. Pricing model: free. Strengths: setup is quick if you import sites from Search Console, and the site scan gives a basic audit at no cost. Limitations: keyword data reflects Bing, not Google, so don't present it as Google demand. Before you choose: treat it as a supplement, not a research tool. Bing Webmaster Tools official site.

Google Analytics 4

Best for: connecting organic sessions to leads and sales. What it does: event-based web analytics with conversion tracking. Pricing model: the standard product is free, and Google sells an enterprise tier separately.

Strengths: it's the default data source most clients already have, and it connects natively to Looker Studio. Limitations: setup takes real effort, some reports apply data thresholds, and exploration data retention defaults to a short window unless someone changes the setting. Before you choose: check the client's key events and retention setting before you report anything. If you're weighing lighter options, see our look at GA4 alternatives and complementary analytics tools. Google Analytics official site.

PageSpeed Insights

Best for: diagnosing slow templates and explaining Core Web Vitals to clients. What it does: runs a Lighthouse lab test and shows real-user field data where Google has enough of it. Pricing model: free, with an API for batch checks.

Strengths: its recommendations name specific resources, which gives developers something concrete to act on. Limitations: the interface tests one URL at a time, and lab scores vary from run to run, so test representative templates rather than chasing a single number. PageSpeed Insights.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider (free version)

Best for: technical audits of small brochure sites. What it does: a desktop crawler that finds broken links, redirects, duplicate titles, missing metadata and other on-site issues.

Pricing model: a free version with a URL cap per crawl, plus a paid annual licence that removes the cap and unlocks features such as saved crawls, JavaScript rendering, scheduling and integrations. Check Screaming Frog's SEO Spider page for the current cap and licence terms. Strengths: it shows the raw data a crawler sees, which is the best way to learn technical SEO. Limitations: it runs on your own machine and needs some technical skill. Any site larger than the cap needs the licence. Our technical audit tools guide for larger websites covers what comes next.

Ahrefs Free

Best for: backlink and site-health checks on client sites you can verify. What it does: according to Ahrefs' pricing page (checked 4 November 2026), it gives website owners free, limited access to Site Explorer and Site Audit.

Strengths: Ahrefs' own link index at no cost, plus a cloud crawler that runs without a desktop app. Limitations: it's built around sites you own, not competitor research, and the same page says Ahrefs never runs discounts. The cheapest paid step is the Starter plan, listed at $29 per month on 4 November 2026. Before you choose: verify each client site through Search Console or DNS. Ahrefs Free official page.

Semrush Free

Best for: occasional competitor and keyword lookups during pitches. What it does: limited access to the Semrush toolset. Pricing model: Semrush's pricing page lists the free account at $0 with no credit card, 1 demo project and 10 reports per day across its core tools (checked 4 November 2026).

Strengths: competitor visibility that first-party tools can't provide. Limitations: ten reports a day runs out quickly during real research, and one project can't cover multiple clients. Skip it if you need repeatable monthly competitor tracking, because the cap makes that impractical.

Google Keyword Planner

Best for: sizing commercial keyword themes, especially for clients who also run Google Ads. What it does: generates keyword ideas with search volume and bid estimates inside Google Ads. Pricing model: free to use, but it requires a Google Ads account.

Limitations: it's designed for advertisers, so it groups close variants and may show broad volume ranges rather than precise figures, depending on the account. It has no organic difficulty score. Use it for direction, not precision. Our comparison of keyword research tools for agencies explains what paid databases add. Keyword Planner official page.

Google Business Profile

Best for: any client with a physical location or service area. What it does: manages the listing that appears in Maps and the local pack, including categories, hours, photos, posts, reviews and performance insights. Pricing model: free.

Limitations: it reports on your own listing only, with no rank grids, citation audits or competitor views. Multi-location clients will soon want the dedicated tools covered in our local SEO software guide for multi-location businesses. Google Business Profile official site.

Looker Studio

Best for: monthly client dashboards built from Search Console and GA4. What it does: Google's free dashboarding tool, with native connectors for Google data sources. Pricing model: free, with a paid Pro tier for team and enterprise management.

Strengths: once a template is built, it can be copied for each client. Limitations: connectors for non-Google sources are often third-party and paid. Building templates takes time, and a broken data source can go unnoticed until a client asks. Looker Studio official site.

A zero-budget client workflow

  1. Onboarding (week 1): get access to Search Console, GA4 and Google Business Profile, and verify the site in Bing Webmaster Tools and Ahrefs Free.
  2. Baseline audit: crawl with the Screaming Frog free version (or Ahrefs Free's Site Audit if the site exceeds the cap), then test the five most important templates in PageSpeed Insights.
  3. Keyword discovery: export Search Console queries with high impressions and low click-through rate, expand them with Keyword Planner, and use a few of the Semrush free reports to check competitor gaps.
  4. Monthly reporting: a Looker Studio template showing Search Console clicks and impressions, GA4 organic key events, and a short written commentary.
  5. Rankings: rely on Search Console average position by query and page. Explain to the client that it's an average across impressions, not a daily rank check.

No-cost substitutes for paid tasks

  • Rank tracking: Search Console's query and page filters, compared month on month. This is less precise, but it reflects real impressions.
  • Content gap research: Google's own results, People Also Ask and Google Trends, reviewed by hand.
  • Schema validation: Google's Rich Results Test.
  • Content briefs: a free AI assistant, carefully edited. See our guide to free AI writing tools and their limits.

When a paid plan becomes necessary

In our assessment, there are four clear triggers. The first is a client who needs daily rank tracking for a defined keyword set. The second is when Search Console averages are no longer enough to explain performance. The third is pitching prospects whose sites you can't access, which makes competitor data essential. The fourth is reaching roughly four or five clients, the point at which building reports by hand starts eating billable time.

At that point, compare suites rather than buying single-purpose tools one at a time. Our SEO software pricing scenarios for 5, 10 and 20 clients show the real monthly cost. The guide to building an SEO stack without duplicate features explains which free tools to keep alongside a paid suite.

Mistakes and questions to ask

  • Using your personal Google account for client properties. Use an agency account with documented access.
  • Treating a 14-day trial as a free tool. Before it ends, export everything you'll need.
  • Presenting Keyword Planner ranges or third-party estimates as exact traffic.
  • Ask each vendor: does the free tier allow commercial client work, what data can I export, and what happens to my projects when a trial ends?

FAQs

Can an agency run client SEO entirely on free tools?

For a small number of clients with sites you can access, yes. The gaps are competitor research, reliable rank tracking and polished white-label reporting.

Is Ahrefs Free the same as an Ahrefs trial?

No. Ahrefs describes it as free, limited access to Site Explorer and Site Audit for website owners. It isn't a time-limited trial of the paid plans.

Which free tool should a new agency set up first?

Search Console. Every other step in the workflow depends on the query and indexing data it provides.

Verdict

The free stack is genuinely useful because it's built on first-party data, but it breaks down on competitor research and scale. Use it to prove value on early retainers. Then upgrade when the hours spent on workarounds cost more than a subscription would.
