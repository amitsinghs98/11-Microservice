Which marketing automation platform should a small team choose?

Pick HubSpot Marketing Hub if marketing and sales must share one contact record, and ActiveCampaign if you want deep branching automation at a lower entry point. Pick Klaviyo for a store, Customer.io for in-app behavior, Zoho Marketing Automation if you already run Zoho CRM, and Brevo or Mailchimp only when email is most of the job.

  • B2B team that wants one shared record: HubSpot Marketing Hub, provided you can fund the edition with the workflows you need.
  • SMB that wants branching automation without a suite bill: ActiveCampaign.
  • Large list, low send frequency, tight budget: Brevo.
  • Shopify or other ecommerce store: Klaviyo.
  • Product-led SaaS with event data: Customer.io.
  • Existing Zoho CRM or Zoho One customer: Zoho Marketing Automation.
  • Marketing-led team that wants journeys and reporting in one screen: Ortto.
  • Newsletter first, a welcome series second: Mailchimp.

If what you actually need is to move data between apps, such as a form to a CRM to Slack, you're shopping for a connector tool, not a marketing automation suite. Our Zapier, Make and n8n comparison covers that layer.

Are you buying as a startup, an SMB or an agency?

Each buyer needs a different amount of platform: a startup needs speed, an SMB needs sales handoff, and an agency needs something its staff can run across many accounts. Decide which one you are before you compare features, because the same tool can be right for one and wasteful for another.

Startup with one marketer

You need a welcome sequence, a lead capture form and a basic re-engagement flow live within weeks, and you probably haven't settled your CRM. Favor low setup effort and a pricing unit that won't punish a growing list. Our review of free marketing automation plans and their upgrade triggers shows how far a startup can go before paying.

SMB with a sales team

Your leads come from several sources and manual follow-up is leaking pipeline. Lead scoring, CRM field updates and owner alerts matter more than template libraries. The CRM decision often settles the automation decision, so read our small-business CRM shortlist alongside this page.

Agency building automation for clients

You'll standardize where you can and work inside each client's existing platform where you can't. Staff need fluency in two or three tools, and you'll often pair them with a connector layer. Our shortlist of workflow automation tools for multi-client agencies covers that second purchase.

When is an email tool not really marketing automation?

An email tool becomes marketing automation when its workflows can branch on behavior and data beyond email, update records and alert people, not just send a timed sequence. If a builder can only wait, send and branch on opens or clicks, treat it as an email platform with autoresponders.

Use four questions to tell them apart. Can a workflow branch on CRM fields and website activity? Can it change a lifecycle stage or score? Can it notify a salesperson or create a task? Can a contact exit when they hit a goal, such as booking a demo? Several products below can do all four, but some only on higher editions. Check the edition, not the brand. What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

HubSpot and ActiveCampaign are the generalists; the other six win in a narrower lane. Each block below gives the fit, the pricing model, the real limitation and the reason to skip it.

HubSpot Marketing Hub: best for B2B teams sharing one record with sales

What it does: email, forms, landing pages, lists, workflows, lead scoring and attribution reporting, all on HubSpot's CRM. Sales sees every form fill and email click without an integration to maintain.

Pricing model: Free, Starter, Professional and Enterprise editions, priced by seats and marketing-contact tiers. As we understand the packaging, the multi-branch workflow builder sits in Professional and above, and higher editions have historically carried a one-time onboarding fee. Confirm both on HubSpot's Marketing Hub pricing page.

Strengths: native CRM, mature reporting, a large app marketplace and many agency partners. Limitations: the Starter-to-Professional jump is the main cost event for most small teams. Skip it if you only need sequences and newsletters. Our ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot breakdown lays out the CRM and cost trade-offs side by side.

ActiveCampaign: best for SMBs that want branching automation

What it does: a visual builder with triggers, conditions, goals and waits, plus email, forms, segmentation and a deals CRM on higher plans. Its prebuilt automation recipes give a lean team a head start.

Pricing model: plan tiers by contact count, with users, CRM features, scoring and reporting added as you move up. See ActiveCampaign's pricing page.

Strengths: automation depth for the money and wide integration coverage. Limitations: reporting and CRM are lighter than HubSpot's, and complex automations become hard to hand over without documentation. Skip it if sales needs a full CRM with forecasting.

Brevo: best for large lists on a tight budget

What it does: email and SMS campaigns, transactional email, a workflow builder, forms and a basic sales CRM in one account.

Pricing model: Brevo has historically weighted its marketing plans toward monthly email volume rather than stored contacts, which can suit a big list you email rarely. Confirm the current basis on Brevo's pricing page.

Strengths: marketing and transactional email under one roof. Limitations: scoring, attribution and reporting are shallower than the suites. Skip it if lead scoring and sales handoff are the main reasons you're buying.

Klaviyo: best for ecommerce brands

What it does: email and SMS flows built on store data, such as abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase and win-back, with purchase-based segments and revenue reporting.

Pricing model: based on active profiles, with SMS priced separately. See Klaviyo's pricing page.

Strengths: a data model shaped around how stores make money. Limitations: it isn't built for B2B pipeline or sales handoff. Skip it if you sell services or long-cycle B2B deals. Store owners should also see our ecommerce email platform comparison.

Customer.io: best for product-led SaaS

What it does: journeys triggered by events and attributes your product sends, across email, push, SMS and in-app messages.

Pricing model: plan tiers by profiles and features. See Customer.io's pricing page.

Strengths: flexible logic for onboarding, activation and reactivation. Limitations: it depends on engineers sending clean event data, and there's no native sales CRM. Skip it if nobody on the team can own the event schema. Our lifecycle email automation shortlist goes deeper on this lane.

Zoho Marketing Automation: best for teams already on Zoho CRM

What it does: journeys, email, lead scoring and lead management tied to the Zoho CRM record.

Pricing model: contact-based tiers, and Zoho One bundles can change the economics. See Zoho Marketing Automation pricing.

Strengths: little integration work if Zoho is already your system of record. Limitations: the interface has a learning curve. Skip it if your CRM isn't Zoho.

Ortto: best for journeys and reporting in one interface

What it does: an audience data layer, a journey builder, email and SMS, and built-in journey and audience reporting.

Pricing model: contact-based tiers. See Ortto's pricing page.

Strengths: journey visualization and analytics in one place. Limitations: a smaller partner network, so outside help is harder to find. Skip it if you'll rely on an agency to run it.

Mailchimp: best for newsletter-first small businesses

What it does: campaigns, signup forms, landing pages and a customer journey builder whose depth depends on the plan.

Pricing model: contact-based tiers; check how audience contacts are counted on Mailchimp's pricing page.

Strengths: quick to start and familiar to most marketers. Limitations: it comes last because, in our assessment, it's an email platform first; branching, scoring and sales handoff are lighter. Skip it if you need workflow 2 below.

How do contact limits and automation tiers change the price?

Two things move the bill: the contact or profile count you're charged for, and the edition that unlocks the automation features you need. The entry price rarely matters; the tier you're forced into by workflow 2 or by list growth does.

Before you compare quotes, answer these for each shortlisted platform:

  • What counts as a billable contact? HubSpot separates marketing and non-marketing contacts; others may count unsubscribed or inactive records. Ask how each is treated.
  • Which edition unlocks branching, scoring and CRM updates? This is the edition you're really buying.
  • How many seats are included? A three-person team can cross a seat boundary before it crosses a contact one.
  • What does the plan cost at twice today's list? Contact pricing rewards regular pruning.
  • Is the first-year price promotional? Ask for the renewal rate in writing.

Which three workflows should you build first?

Build a welcome-and-nurture flow, a score-to-sales handoff and a re-engagement flow. If a platform can't express all three cleanly in its documentation or trial, it won't handle the fourth.

Workflow 1: welcome and nurture

  1. Trigger: form submission or new subscriber.
  2. Send the promised asset immediately.
  3. Wait two days, then branch on whether the asset link was clicked.
  4. Clicked: send a proof email such as a customer story. Not clicked: resend with a new subject line.
  5. Goal: a demo request or purchase removes the contact from the sequence.

Workflow 2: score-to-sales handoff

  1. Add points for pricing-page visits, demo-page views and email replies; subtract for inactivity.
  2. When the score crosses your threshold, update lifecycle stage in the CRM.
  3. Create a task for the owner and send an internal alert.
  4. If no sales activity is logged within three working days, alert the sales lead.

Scores are only as good as the signals behind them. Our guide to lead scoring tools for small revenue teams covers how to set thresholds you can defend.

Workflow 3: re-engagement and list hygiene

  1. Trigger: no clicks in 90 days. Treat open data cautiously, because mail privacy features inflate it.
  2. Send two re-permission emails a week apart.
  3. No response: tag as inactive and suppress from campaigns.
  4. Review the inactive segment quarterly and archive or delete it to cut contact-based cost.

How much work is migration and implementation?

Plan on days for an email-first tool, a few weeks for an SMB automation platform, and one to three months for a suite rollout that includes a CRM migration. Those are editorial planning assumptions, not vendor commitments, and data clean-up is usually the longest step.

  • Data clean-up: deduplicate, standardize fields and decide who to leave behind. Migrating dead contacts into contact-priced software means paying to store a problem.
  • Sending domain: SPF, DKIM and DMARC for your sending domain, plus a gradual ramp if you move to new sending infrastructure.
  • CRM mapping: field mapping, lifecycle stage definitions and owner assignment rules.
  • Rebuilding automations: workflows rarely export between vendors, so document each trigger, branch and exit before you rebuild.
  • Reporting baseline: record current conversion rates before you switch, then measure pipeline rather than opens with suitable marketing analytics software.

What buying mistakes should you avoid?

The costly mistakes are buying an email tool and expecting automation, pricing on today's list, and leaving the connector layer unplanned. Each is cheap to avoid during evaluation and expensive to fix after migration.

  • Treating every email platform as full automation. Run the four questions above against each shortlisted tool.
  • Buying the entry edition on paper. Price the edition that runs workflow 2.
  • Forgetting the glue. Apps your platform doesn't integrate with need a connector tool; our guide to no-code automation platforms for marketing ops compares the options.
  • Buying outside the wider stack plan. Our walkthrough on building a full-funnel growth stack for a small B2B team shows where automation sits next to CRM, analytics and content.

What else do buyers ask about marketing automation software?

Most remaining questions are about connector tools, list budgeting and running clients on one platform. Short answers follow.

Do we need Zapier if our platform has native integrations?

Often not at first, because native integrations cover the common syncs. A connector tool earns its cost when you link apps your platform doesn't support or need logic that lives outside it.

How many contacts should we budget for?

Budget for your cleaned list plus a year of expected growth. Contact-based pricing rewards pruning inactive records every quarter.

Can an agency run every client on one automation platform?

It can for clients you onboard from scratch. Established clients usually keep their existing platform, so agency staff need working fluency in two or three tools.

Is a free plan enough to start marketing automation?

A free plan can cover a welcome series and basic forms. Scoring, CRM updates and multi-branch workflows usually sit in paid editions, so check where those features start before you build on a free tier.
