Best Marketing Automation Software for Small Teams

A lean team rarely fails at marketing automation because it picked the wrong logo. It fails because it bought a builder it never had time to configure, or an email tool it mistook for automation. This guide sorts eight platforms by the work they actually take on.

Quick answer

There is no single winner here, because a five-person startup, a 40-person services firm and a three-client agency need different things. Based on the product information reviewed, these are our editorial picks by situation:

  • B2B team that wants marketing and CRM in one record: HubSpot Marketing Hub, if you can afford the tier that unlocks the workflow builder.
  • SMB that wants deep automation without an enterprise bill: ActiveCampaign.
  • Budget-sensitive team with large but low-frequency lists: Brevo.
  • Shopify or ecommerce brand: Klaviyo.
  • Product-led SaaS sending behaviour-triggered messages: Customer.io.
  • Business already running Zoho CRM: Zoho Marketing Automation.
  • Team that mainly needs newsletters plus a welcome series: Mailchimp, accepting that its automation is lighter than the dedicated platforms.

If your real need is moving data between apps rather than nurturing contacts, start with our comparison of Zapier, Make and n8n as connective tissue instead.

Who this guide is for

This is the pillar page for the automation part of our marketing automation and analytics software hub. It is written for three buyers:

  • Startups with one marketer, a CRM decision still open and a need for a welcome sequence and lead capture this quarter.
  • SMBs with a sales team, several lead sources and enough volume that manual follow-up is leaking pipeline.
  • Agencies building automation for clients, where the question is whether to standardise on one platform or work inside each client's stack.

A useful line to draw early: an email platform that sends a timed sequence is not the same as a marketing automation platform that branches on behaviour, scores leads, updates a CRM and alerts sales. Several products below can do both; some only do the first well.

How we chose

We looked for platforms that a small team can realistically run, then compared them on seven criteria: workflow builder depth (branching, goals, wait conditions), email and forms, segmentation, native CRM or CRM integration, reporting on automation performance, pricing unit, and setup complexity.

Evidence came from official pricing pages, product documentation and vendor materials. No hands-on testing was performed for this draft, and we did not run campaigns on any platform. The platforms

1. HubSpot Marketing Hub

Best for: B2B teams of three or more who want marketing, sales and service working from the same contact record.

What it does: Email, forms, landing pages, lists, workflows, lead scoring and attribution reporting sit on top of HubSpot's free CRM. The draw is that sales sees every form fill and email click without an integration.

Pricing model: Tiered (Free, Starter, Professional, Enterprise), priced by seats and by marketing contacts. Based on the product information reviewed, the full multi-branch workflow builder sits in Professional and above, and higher tiers have historically carried a one-time onboarding fee. Check both on HubSpot's Marketing Hub pricing page.

Strengths: Native CRM, strong reporting, a large integration marketplace and plenty of agency partners who know it.

Limitations: The jump from Starter to Professional is the main cost event for most small teams, and marketing-contact pricing rises as your database grows. Consider another option if you only need email sequences.

Before you choose: Price the tier that includes the workflows you actually need, not the entry tier. Our ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot breakdown models seat and contact costs side by side.

2. ActiveCampaign

Best for: SMBs and lean B2C or B2B teams that want sophisticated branching automation at a lower entry cost than an all-in-one suite.

What it does: A visual automation builder with triggers, conditions, goals and waits, plus email, forms, segmentation and a built-in sales CRM on higher plans. Its automation recipe library gives a useful head start.

Pricing model: Priced by contact count, with plan tiers that add users, CRM features, lead scoring and reporting. See ActiveCampaign's pricing page.

Strengths: Automation depth for the price, a mature builder and wide integration coverage.

Limitations: Reporting and CRM are less comprehensive than HubSpot's, and contact-based pricing climbs quickly with large, inactive lists. Complex automations need documentation or they become hard for the next person to maintain.

Before you choose: Confirm which tier includes lead scoring and the CRM features you expect, and prune inactive contacts before you migrate.

3. Brevo

Best for: Budget-conscious SMBs with large lists that they email infrequently.

What it does: Email and SMS campaigns, transactional email, a workflow builder, forms, and a basic sales CRM under one account.

Pricing model: Brevo has historically priced its marketing plans mainly on monthly email volume rather than stored contacts, with a free plan that caps daily sends. Confirm the current basis on Brevo's pricing page.

Strengths: Cost can stay predictable when your list is large but your send frequency is low; transactional and marketing email in one place.

Limitations: Automation and reporting are capable but not as deep as ActiveCampaign or HubSpot. Teams that need complex scoring or advanced attribution may outgrow it.

Before you choose: Model your annual send volume, including automated emails, because those count toward volume too.

4. Klaviyo

Best for: Ecommerce brands, especially on Shopify, that want flows driven by browsing, cart and order data.

What it does: Email and SMS flows (abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase, win-back), segmentation on purchase behaviour, and revenue reporting tied to store data.

Pricing model: Based on active profiles, with SMS priced separately. See Klaviyo's pricing page.

Strengths: Deep ecommerce data model and prebuilt flows that match how stores actually make money.

Limitations: Not designed for B2B sales handoff or pipeline management. Skip it if you sell services or long-cycle B2B deals.

Before you choose: Check how active profiles are counted and what happens to cost as your customer file grows.

5. Customer.io

Best for: Product-led SaaS teams that need messages triggered by in-app behaviour.

What it does: Journeys driven by events and attributes sent from your product, across email, push, SMS and in-app messages.

Pricing model: Tiered by profiles and features. See Customer.io's pricing page.

Strengths: Flexible event-based logic for onboarding, activation and reactivation.

Limitations: You need an engineer to send clean event data. There is no native sales CRM, and marketers without data support will struggle. For journey mapping with event data, see our guide to lifecycle email automation platforms.

6. Zoho Marketing Automation

Best for: Businesses already using Zoho CRM or Zoho One.

What it does: Journeys, email, lead scoring and lead management connected to the Zoho CRM record.

Pricing model: Contact-based tiers; Zoho One bundles may change the economics. See Zoho Marketing Automation pricing.

Strengths: Lower cost of integration if you already run Zoho apps.

Limitations: The value case weakens if your CRM is not Zoho, and the interface has a learning curve.

7. Ortto

Best for: Marketing-led SMBs that want journeys, audience data and analytics in one tool.

What it does: A customer data layer, journey builder, email and SMS, plus built-in reporting on journeys and audiences.

Pricing model: Contact-based tiers. See Ortto's pricing page.

Strengths: Journey visualisation and analytics in the same interface.

Limitations: Smaller ecosystem and partner network than HubSpot or ActiveCampaign, so agency support is thinner.

8. Mailchimp

Best for: Small businesses whose priority is newsletters, with a welcome series and a few simple triggers.

What it does: Email campaigns, signup forms, landing pages and a customer journey builder whose depth depends on plan.

Pricing model: Contact-based tiers. Check how audience contacts are counted on Mailchimp's pricing page.

Strengths: Easy to start, familiar to most marketers, wide integrations.

Limitations: We list it last because, in our assessment, it is an email platform first. Branching, scoring and sales handoff are lighter than the dedicated platforms. If email is your main channel, our small-business email marketing comparison is the better starting point.

Three starter workflows

Before committing, sketch these three flows in each shortlisted builder's documentation or trial. If a platform cannot express them cleanly, it will not handle your fourth.

Workflow 1: New lead welcome and nurture

  1. Trigger: form submission or new subscriber.
  2. Send a welcome email immediately with the promised asset.
  3. Wait two days; branch on whether the asset link was clicked.
  4. Clicked: send a case-study email. Not clicked: resend with a new subject line.
  5. Goal: a demo request or purchase exits the contact from the sequence.

Workflow 2: Lead score handoff to sales

  1. Add points for pricing-page visits, demo-page views and email replies; subtract for inactivity.
  2. When the score crosses your threshold, update lifecycle stage in the CRM.
  3. Create a task for the owner and send an internal alert.
  4. If no sales activity is logged in three working days, notify the sales lead.

Scoring models are only as good as the signals feeding them; our guide to lead scoring tools for small revenue teams covers how to set thresholds without guessing.

Workflow 3: Re-engagement and list hygiene

  1. Trigger: no opens or clicks in 90 days (treat open data cautiously, because privacy features distort it).
  2. Send two re-permission emails a week apart.
  3. No engagement: tag as inactive and suppress from campaigns.
  4. Review the inactive segment quarterly; delete or archive to cut contact-based costs.

Migration and implementation effort

Implementation is where small teams underestimate cost. Plan for these workstreams regardless of vendor:

  • Data clean-up: deduplicate, standardise fields and decide which contacts to leave behind. Migrating dead contacts into contact-priced software is paying to store a problem.
  • Domain authentication: SPF, DKIM and DMARC records for your sending domain, plus a warm-up period if you move to new sending infrastructure.
  • CRM mapping: field mapping, lifecycle stage definitions and owner assignment rules. If your CRM is still undecided, read our small-business CRM shortlist first, because the CRM choice often decides the automation choice.
  • Rebuilding automations: workflows rarely export between vendors. Document each one (trigger, branches, exit criteria) before you rebuild.
  • Reporting baseline: capture current conversion rates before switching so you can compare honestly afterwards.

As a rough editorial guide, an email-first tool can be live in days, an SMB automation platform in a few weeks, and a suite rollout with CRM migration over one to three months. These are planning assumptions, not vendor commitments.

Recommendations by team type

Startup with one marketer

Start with the cheapest tool that can run workflows 1 and 3. Brevo or ActiveCampaign's lower tiers are reasonable; HubSpot's free CRM plus Starter works if you expect to grow into Professional. SMB with a sales team

You need workflow 2. HubSpot Professional and ActiveCampaign's CRM tiers are the most direct paths. If you are weighing those two specifically, compare them on CRM alignment, lead scoring and sales handoff.

Agency serving clients

Standardise where you can, but expect to work in clients' existing platforms. Many agencies pair a primary automation platform with Zapier, Make or n8n for cross-client glue; our shortlist of workflow automation tools for agencies covers that layer, and our guide to no-code automation platforms for marketing ops compares the technical options.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

  • Treating every email platform as full automation. Ask: can a workflow branch on CRM fields and website behaviour, not just email opens?
  • Pricing on today's list size. Ask: what does the plan cost at twice our contacts, and do unsubscribed or inactive records count?
  • Ignoring seats. Ask: how many users are included, and what does each additional seat cost?
  • Skipping measurement. Ask: can automation results feed our reporting? Pair your platform with the right marketing analytics software so you measure pipeline, not just opens.
  • Missing the renewal. Ask: is the first-year price a promotion, and what is the renewal rate?

FAQs

What separates marketing automation from email marketing?

Email marketing sends campaigns and simple sequences. Marketing automation adds behaviour-based branching, lead scoring, CRM updates and internal alerts. Many products do both at different plan levels, so check the tier, not the brand.

Do we need Zapier if our platform has native integrations?

Often not at first. Native integrations handle common syncs. A general automation tool becomes useful when you connect apps the platform does not support, or when you need logic outside the marketing platform.

How many contacts should we budget for?

Budget for your cleaned list plus 12 months of expected growth. Contact-based pricing rewards regular pruning of inactive records.

Can an agency run every client on one platform?

It can for clients you onboard from scratch. Established clients usually keep their existing platform, so agencies need staff comfortable in two or three tools.

Verdict

For B2B teams that want a single customer record and can fund the workflow tier, HubSpot Marketing Hub is the most complete option on this list. For SMBs that want strong automation at lower entry cost, ActiveCampaign is our editorial pick. Ecommerce brands should look first at Klaviyo, product-led SaaS at Customer.io, and Zoho shops at Zoho Marketing Automation. Choose the platform that can run your three starter workflows today and still fits the budget at twice your current list.
