Best Invoicing Software for Freelancers and Digital Agencies

Most invoicing software for digital agencies handles the basics well; the differences show up in recurring retainers, billable time and expenses, multi-currency clients and how cleanly paid invoices reach your books. FreshBooks and Harvest suit time-based billing, Zoho Invoice and Invoice Ninja suit cost-conscious teams, Stripe Invoicing suits payment-first billing, and Xero or QuickBooks Online suit agencies ready for full accounting.

Which invoicing tool should you choose?

If you bill from tracked hours, start with FreshBooks or Harvest; if you bill fixed monthly fees and want card or bank payments collected automatically, Stripe Invoicing or Zoho Invoice fits. If your accountant already wants a general ledger, invoice from Xero or QuickBooks Online and skip a separate tool.

  • Freelancers and small agencies billing time and expenses: FreshBooks.
  • Agencies that track time against budgets and bill from it: Harvest.
  • Cost-conscious teams that want capable invoicing without a full accounting suite: Zoho Invoice.
  • Teams that want open-source or self-hosted invoicing: Invoice Ninja.
  • Solo consultants who want proposals, contracts and invoices together: Bonsai.
  • Agencies that already take payments through Stripe: Stripe Invoicing.
  • Agencies with an accountant and a need for full books: Xero or QuickBooks Online.

Do you need invoicing software or full accounting?

Invoicing software creates, sends and chases invoices and records payments; accounting software also keeps the ledger, reconciles bank feeds, handles bills and produces the reports your accountant files. A freelancer can often live on invoicing alone, but most agencies with staff need accounting underneath, with invoicing either built in or synced.

The cleanest set-up is one place where invoices are created and one place where they're reconciled. If you invoice in Harvest and reconcile in Xero, make sure the sync is one-way and reliable, or you'll chase phantom differences at month end. If your billing is mostly fixed monthly retainers with rollover rules, read our guide to retainer billing software for service businesses, which goes deeper on schedules and overages.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

FreshBooks and Harvest are strongest when invoices come from tracked work, Zoho Invoice and Invoice Ninja appeal to cost-conscious teams, Stripe is strongest on collecting payment, and Xero and QuickBooks are strongest on the books. Each gives something up to get there.

FreshBooks: best for freelancers and small agencies billing time and expenses

FreshBooks combines invoicing with time tracking, expenses, recurring invoices, online payments and accounting features aimed at small service businesses. It's a good fit for a freelancer or small agency that wants one tool rather than an invoicing app plus accounting. The limitation is that price can rise with growth, so check whether your tier caps billable clients and how added team members are charged. Skip it if you already have an accountant committed to Xero or QuickBooks. Check current limits on the FreshBooks pricing page.

Harvest: best for agencies that bill from tracked time

Harvest is a time tracker first, with project budgets, expenses and invoices generated from tracked hours, plus integrations with accounting tools. That makes it a natural choice for agencies with hourly or capped-hours work. It isn't an accounting system, so you'll still need Xero, QuickBooks or similar behind it. Consider another option if you bill only fixed fees. See the Harvest pricing page, and our guide to time tracking software for marketing agencies for the tracking side.

Zoho Invoice: best for cost-conscious teams

Zoho Invoice offers quotes, recurring invoices, a client portal, payment reminders, expenses and time tracking. It's a good fit for freelancers and small agencies that want more than a template; check current plan options on the official page. Availability and features may vary by region, and it connects most smoothly with Zoho Books. Skip it if you need deep integration with non-Zoho accounting. Plans are on the Zoho Invoice pricing page.

Invoice Ninja: best for open-source or self-hosted invoicing

Invoice Ninja is open-source invoicing software available as a hosted service or for self-hosting, with recurring invoices, a client portal, quotes, expenses and many payment gateways. Its appeal is control and cost, especially for technically confident teams. Self-hosting shifts updates, backups and security to you. Consider the hosted version, or another tool, if nobody on staff wants that job. See the Invoice Ninja pricing page.

Bonsai: best for solo consultants who want one tool

Bonsai bundles proposals, contracts, time tracking and invoicing for independents and small studios. Fewer tools means fewer hand-offs between a signed proposal and the first invoice. Its accounting depth is limited compared with Xero or QuickBooks. Skip it once you have staff, multiple entities or an accountant asking for a ledger. See the Bonsai pricing page.

Stripe Invoicing: best for payment-first billing

Stripe Invoicing creates and sends invoices with a hosted payment page, automatic reminders and card or bank payment options, and it can feed recurring billing through Stripe Billing. It's a good fit for agencies that already process payments through Stripe and want invoices paid online. It doesn't track time or expenses, and you'll still need accounting software. Consider another option if most clients pay by manual bank transfer. Fees are on the Stripe Invoicing pricing page.

Xero: best for agencies ready for full accounting

Xero is cloud accounting with invoicing, bank reconciliation, bills, multi-currency support and an app marketplace; check which plan includes multi-currency. Invoicing directly from Xero removes a sync. The trade-off is that you should confirm any invoice or bill limits on the entry plan, and the interface is built for bookkeepers more than account managers. Skip it as a freelancer who only needs invoices. Plans are on the Xero pricing page.

QuickBooks Online: best where your accountant already uses it

QuickBooks Online offers invoicing, recurring transactions, bank feeds, expenses and reporting, with plans that scale by users and features. Its strength is familiarity if your accountant already works in it. Confirm user counts and features per tier on the official page. Consider another option if you want lightweight invoicing without a ledger. See QuickBooks pricing.

How would a monthly retainer bill in practice?

Set a recurring invoice on the first of each month for the fixed fee, and add separate line items for approved extra hours and pass-through expenses before it's sent. The hypothetical example below shows why the tool must allow edits to a recurring invoice before it goes out.

Hypothetical, using stated assumptions: an SEO client pays a fixed fee of 4,000 per month in its own currency, covering a set allowance of hours. In one month, the client approves 5 extra hours at an agreed rate of 120, and you pay 300 for a third-party tool on their behalf. The invoice therefore carries three lines: 4,000 retainer, 600 extra hours (5 × 120) and 300 reimbursable expense, totalling 4,900 before tax. These figures are illustrative assumptions, not prices from any vendor.

To bill that cleanly, the tool needs four things: a recurring schedule, a way to add one-off lines to this month's draft, expenses that can be marked billable and pulled in, and tax fields that apply the right rate to each line. If extra hours come from time tracking, the time tool should push approved hours to the draft rather than make you retype them. Do automatic reminders really get invoices paid faster?

Automatic reminders remove the awkward manual chase and make late payment harder to ignore, but they won't fix an invoice the client disputes or a purchase order that's missing. Clear payment terms and the right PO number on the invoice usually matter as much as the reminder schedule.

What mistakes do agencies make with invoicing?

The biggest mistake is running two systems that both think they're the source of truth for invoices. The second is ignoring payment-processing fees and currency conversion, which quietly erode margin on international clients.

  • Recurring invoices on autopilot: automatic sending is risky when monthly extras need adding; send drafts for review instead.
  • Missing client tax details: capture tax IDs and addresses at onboarding, not at the first invoice.
  • One currency for everyone: billing foreign clients in your currency can slow payment; check multi-currency support.
  • Unbilled expenses: mark pass-through costs billable when you record them, or they'll be forgotten.
  • No link to the signed scope: invoice lines should match the SOW from your proposal software, so disputes are easy to settle.

What else do freelancers and agencies ask about invoicing?

Questions tend to cover payments, currencies and when to move to accounting software. Check specifics on the vendors' official pages, and ask your accountant about tax.

Can invoicing software collect card and bank payments?

Most invoicing tools connect to payment gateways so clients can pay online by card and, in some regions, by bank transfer or direct debit. Each payment carries a processing fee set by the gateway, so check the fee schedule before switching it on.

When should a freelancer move to accounting software?

Move when you hire staff, register for sales tax or VAT, pay regular supplier bills or your accountant asks for a ledger. Until then, a good invoicing tool with clean exports is usually enough.

How should agencies bill clients in other currencies?

Use a tool that can issue invoices in the client's currency and record the exchange difference when paid. Agree in the contract which currency applies and who bears conversion costs.

Should retainer invoices be sent in advance or in arrears?

Many agencies bill fixed retainers in advance and extra hours in arrears. That protects cash flow while keeping variable work tied to what was actually delivered and approved.

Do we need a client portal for invoices?

A portal helps clients find past invoices and pay without emailing you. It's useful for agencies with many clients; freelancers with a few clients can manage without one.

What's the verdict?

Choose FreshBooks or Harvest if your invoices come from tracked time, Zoho Invoice or Invoice Ninja if keeping costs down is the priority, and Stripe Invoicing if online payment is the main job. Move to Xero or QuickBooks Online once your agency needs real books behind its invoices.
