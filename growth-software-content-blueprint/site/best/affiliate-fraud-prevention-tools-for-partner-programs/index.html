Which affiliate fraud prevention tool should you choose?

Start with the fraud controls inside your affiliate platform, such as impact.com, Everflow, TUNE or Scaleo, because they see clicks, conversions and payouts together. Add a specialist detection layer such as Anura, FraudScore or CHEQ when you see lead or traffic quality problems your platform's rules can't explain.

  • Brands and SaaS companies on an enterprise partnership platform: impact.com's built-in protections.
  • Performance programs needing configurable rules and partner-level controls: Everflow.
  • Networks and advertisers running their own tracking stack: TUNE.
  • Affiliate networks and agencies wanting an all-in-one platform with anti-fraud settings: Scaleo.
  • Lead-generation programs worried about fake form fills: Anura.
  • Mobile and performance programs scoring traffic quality: FraudScore.
  • Brands protecting paid and affiliate traffic plus forms with one vendor: CHEQ.

What kinds of affiliate fraud should you watch for?

The five patterns behind most affiliate fraud are cookie stuffing, fake leads, self-referrals, coupon hijacking and incentivized traffic that breaks your terms. Each leaves different evidence, so each needs a different check.

  • Cookie stuffing: a partner drops its tracking cookie without a real click, often through hidden frames or forced redirects, so it gets credit for sales it didn't drive. Watch for very short click-to-conversion times and clicks with no page engagement.
  • Fake leads: bots or paid click farms fill in forms to trigger cost-per-lead commissions. Signals include repeated device fingerprints, disposable emails and leads that never answer.
  • Self-referrals: a partner buys through its own link to earn commission or a discount. Matching payment details, addresses or devices between partner and customer is the usual tell.
  • Coupon hijacking: a partner or browser extension inserts its code or link at checkout, taking credit from the partner that actually influenced the sale.
  • Incentivized traffic: visitors are paid or rewarded to click or sign up, which may be allowed in some programs and banned in others. Your terms decide.

What are the strengths and limits of each tool?

Platform controls are the cheapest first line because they act directly on commissions, while specialist tools add evidence you can't get from tracking data alone. Each block names who should skip it.

impact.com: best for enterprise partnership programs

Based on the product information reviewed, impact.com is a partnership management platform that includes tracking, contracts, payouts and protections against suspicious traffic and conversions. Its advantage is that fraud signals and payout holds live in the same system. Skip it if you only want a fraud layer on top of another platform.

Everflow: best for configurable performance rules

Everflow is a partner marketing platform for performance programs, with tracking, partner management and fraud-related controls such as traffic rules and conversion review. It suits teams that want to define their own thresholds per partner. Skip it if nobody on your team will own and tune the rules.

TUNE: best for teams running their own tracking

TUNE provides a partner marketing and tracking platform used by networks and advertisers, with fraud-prevention features that can be configured against your own conversion logic. Skip it if you want a managed, low-setup program.

Scaleo: best all-in-one for networks and agencies

Scaleo offers affiliate tracking and network management with built-in anti-fraud settings. It's a practical choice for smaller networks and agencies that want one platform. Skip it if you've already seen sophisticated lead fraud; add a specialist.

Anura: best for fake leads and form fraud

Anura focuses on ad and lead fraud detection, assessing visitors and form fills for signs of bots, malware and human fraud farms. It's most useful in cost-per-lead programs. Skip it if your program pays only on verified purchases with a refund window; the risk profile is different.

FraudScore: best for traffic-quality scoring

FraudScore provides fraud detection and traffic scoring for performance and mobile marketing, flagging suspicious sources so you can block or review them. Skip it if you run a small coupon-led ecommerce program where the main issue is code leakage.

CHEQ: best for one vendor across paid and partner traffic

CHEQ offers protection against invalid traffic across paid channels, forms and analytics. It suits brands that want one vendor across paid media and partners. Skip it if affiliate is your only channel; a platform-native control may be enough.

Which detection signals and controls matter most?

The signals that matter most are device and IP patterns, click-to-conversion timing, conversion quality after the sale and coupon usage outside the partner's audience. The controls that matter most are payout holds, partner-level rules and alerts that reach a person.

  • Device signals: repeated fingerprints, data-center IPs and mismatched locations across many conversions from one partner.
  • Click patterns: spikes outside normal hours, conversions seconds after a click, or clicks with no engagement.
  • Conversion checks: refunds, chargebacks, unanswered leads and trials that never activate, joined back to the partner.
  • Coupon rules: partner-specific codes, single-use codes for high-value offers, and alerts when a code appears on a site that isn't the partner's.
  • Anomaly alerts: notifications when a partner's conversion rate or average order value moves sharply.
  • Evidence export: the raw click and conversion records you'll need to show a partner.

How many of the flagged conversions will turn out to be real fraud?

Some share of flagged conversions will be legitimate, because unusual isn't the same as fraudulent: a partner with a viral post or a corporate audience on one network can trip the same rules as a bot. How do you run a fair investigation and partner appeal?

Hold the disputed commissions, gather evidence, tell the partner what you found and give them a fixed window to respond before you decide. Publishing that process in your partner terms protects honest partners and makes your decisions defensible.

  1. Hold, don't reverse: move flagged conversions to pending during your lock period.
  2. Gather evidence: export click, device, conversion and refund records for the partner and period.
  3. Compare against baseline: check the partner's normal patterns and similar partners.
  4. Notify the partner: share the specific pattern and the affected conversions, without revealing every detection rule.
  5. Appeal window: give a set number of days for the partner to explain traffic sources or provide proof.
  6. Decide and record: approve, partially reverse or reverse, citing the term that applies, and log the outcome.
  7. Escalate repeat cases: warnings, tighter rules, then removal from the program.

What mistakes do teams make with fraud prevention?

The worst mistake is buying a tool that promises to eliminate fraud and then switching off manual review. The second is reversing commissions without evidence or a published process, which drives away good partners.

  • Leaving refunds and cancellations out of commission logic.
  • Using one public discount code for every partner.
  • Setting the lock period shorter than the refund window.
  • Ignoring incentivized traffic rules until a dispute forces the question.

What else do program managers ask about affiliate fraud?

These answers cover the questions that come up when a program first finds suspicious conversions. They're general guidance; your partner terms and local law decide what you can withhold.

Can software stop all affiliate fraud?

No. Detection tools flag patterns and score risk, but determined fraudsters adapt and some legitimate activity looks unusual. Plan for a mix of automated rules, manual review and clear partner terms.

Should I reverse commissions automatically when fraud is flagged?

Hold rather than reverse. Put flagged conversions into a pending state, review the evidence, and reverse only when the case is clear and allowed by your terms.

Is coupon leakage the same as fraud?

Not always. A partner posting a code you gave them may be breaking your terms, but a code spreading to coupon sites on its own is leakage, and the response should match the cause.

How long should the commission lock period be?

Long enough to cover your refund or cancellation window and your review time. Publish it in your partner terms so partners know when commissions become final.

What's the verdict?

Use the fraud controls in your affiliate platform first, whether that's impact.com, Everflow, TUNE or Scaleo, then add Anura, FraudScore or CHEQ when lead or traffic fraud outgrows platform rules. Whatever you buy, keep manual review, payout holds and an appeal process in place.
