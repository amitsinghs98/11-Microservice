Quick answer

Most enrichment projects fail quietly. A few thousand records light up with job titles and headcounts, and six months later the CRM holds three versions of the same company and a field called "Industry (old)". The tool is rarely the cause. The missing piece is a plan for which fields to fill, from which source, and when to stop overwriting.

With that plan in place, these are our editorial picks based on the product information reviewed:

  • Most control over sources and logic: Clay, for teams that want multi-provider waterfalls and per-field rules.
  • Enrichment bundled with a database and outreach: Apollo.io, for small sales teams that want one bill.
  • Deep firmographic, technographic and intent coverage: ZoomInfo, for mid-market and enterprise teams with budget for an annual contract.
  • Native enrichment inside the CRM: HubSpot's enrichment (built on its Clearbit acquisition), if HubSpot is already your system of record.
  • European mobile numbers: Cognism, for teams calling into the UK and EU.
  • Raw data for builders: People Data Labs, when engineers want an API rather than a sales tool.

When enrichment helps, and when it doesn't

Enrichment pays for itself in three situations. Inbound forms can ask for an email address alone and fill company size and industry behind the scenes. Routing and scoring rules need firmographic fields that reps never type in reliably. Outbound lists need verified contact details before anyone writes a sequence.

It doesn't help much when your CRM has no agreed account model, when reps already know every buyer in a narrow niche, or when you plan to enrich everything once and never refresh it. The tools

Clay

Best for: RevOps leads and GTM engineers who want to decide which provider fills each field and in what order.

Clay works like a spreadsheet where each column can call a data provider, a formula or its AI research agent, Claygent. Its pricing page says it connects to more than 150 providers and that every plan, including Free, can run multi-provider waterfalls.

  • Per-column waterfalls: try provider A, fall back to B, stop at the first verified result.
  • Bring-your-own API keys for providers you already license.
  • Signals such as job changes on paid plans.

Pricing (checked 4 November 2026): the Clay pricing page lists a Free plan with 100 data credits and 500 actions a month and tables of up to 200 rows. Launch shows $167 a month on annual billing (the page's FAQ quotes "starting at $185/mo") with 2,500 data credits and 15,000 actions a month. Growth shows $446 a month on annual billing (FAQ: from $495) with 6,000 data credits and 40,000 actions. Clay states that data credits start at $0.05 each and that a lookup returning no result isn't charged.

Strengths: a high degree of flexibility, per-step cost visibility and unlimited seats on every plan. Limitations: two meters (actions and data credits) make forecasting harder, and CRM auto-sync and HTTP API integrations start at Growth, so Launch is a list-building tool rather than a CRM hygiene tool.

Skip it if nobody on the team wants to build and maintain tables. Visit Clay.

Apollo.io

Best for: small sales teams that want a contact database, enrichment and sequencing under one subscription.

Apollo combines its own people and company database with CSV, CRM and API enrichment. Paid plans add waterfall enrichment through partner sources and automated CRM enrichment, according to its pricing page.

  • Enrichment of CSV files, CRM records and API calls.
  • Job-change enrichment and intent topics.
  • Salesforce, HubSpot and Pipedrive integrations.

Pricing (checked 4 November 2026): the Apollo pricing page lists Free at $0 with 900 credits per seat per year, granted monthly, and CRM enrichment capped at 100 a month. Basic is $49 per seat per month billed annually with 30,000 credits per seat per year. Professional is $79 with 48,000 credits. Organization is $119 with a three-seat minimum and 72,000 credits. An email costs 1 credit and a phone number 8. Credits are pooled across the team, granted upfront and don't roll over.

Strengths: low entry cost and one place to find, enrich and contact. Limitations: phone numbers consume credits eight times faster than emails, every seat must sit on the same plan, and the page notes that standard plans are for internal business use only.

Skip it if you already pay for a sequencer and only need enrichment, because you'd be paying for overlap. Our Clay versus Apollo comparison goes deeper, and our Apollo alternatives roundup covers other options. Visit Apollo.io.

ZoomInfo

Best for: mid-market and enterprise teams that want firmographic, technographic, intent and org-chart data kept current inside the CRM automatically.

ZoomInfo is a broad sales intelligence platform. Its enrichment features update CRM and marketing automation records on a schedule and through form enrichment, based on the vendor's product materials.

Pricing model: quote-only, typically on annual contracts. See ZoomInfo's pricing page to request a quote.

Strengths: depth of company-level data in a single vendor relationship. Limitations: cost and contract length put it out of reach for most small teams, and a single-source database can't fall back to another provider on a miss.

Skip it if you have fewer than a handful of reps or no admin to manage field mappings. Our roundup of ZoomInfo alternatives for SMB teams lists lighter options. Visit ZoomInfo.

HubSpot enrichment (formerly Clearbit)

Best for: teams whose system of record is already HubSpot and who don't want middleware.

HubSpot acquired Clearbit and has folded its company and contact enrichment into HubSpot itself. The branding and credit packaging have changed since the acquisition, so check HubSpot's current product pages for what your tier includes.

Pricing model: tied to HubSpot subscriptions and HubSpot's credit system. See HubSpot pricing.

Strengths: no integration to maintain, and enriched fields are available to HubSpot forms, workflows and scoring straight away. Limitations: it only helps inside HubSpot, and you can't add a second provider as a fallback.

Skip it if you run Salesforce, Pipedrive or another CRM. Visit HubSpot.

Cognism

Best for: sales teams calling decision-makers in the UK and continental Europe.

Cognism markets person-level contact data with an emphasis on mobile numbers it says have been checked by phone, and on GDPR-aware data handling. It enriches CRM records through integrations and a browser extension.

Pricing model: quote-based. See Cognism's pricing page.

Strengths: a clear regional focus. Limitations: less suited to very small budgets, and North American coverage should be judged on your own sample rather than vendor claims.

Skip it if your buyers are almost all in the US and email is your only channel. Visit Cognism.

Lusha

Best for: individual reps and small teams that need quick contact lookups while browsing or working in the CRM.

Lusha provides emails, phone numbers and basic company details through a browser extension, a web app and CRM integrations, based on its product pages.

Pricing model: credit-based tiers with a free plan. See Lusha pricing.

Strengths: quick to adopt. Limitations: lighter on technographic depth and bulk governance controls than platform tools.

Skip it if you need scheduled, rules-based CRM enrichment across tens of thousands of records. Visit Lusha.

People Data Labs

Best for: product and data teams that want person and company records through an API or bulk files and will build matching themselves.

People Data Labs is a developer-first data provider rather than a sales app. It returns structured records you can store, match and join in your own warehouse or application.

Pricing model: usage tiers based on API calls or records, with a free developer tier. See People Data Labs pricing.

Strengths: flexible and warehouse-friendly. Limitations: no CRM interface, so writeback rules, deduplication and governance are your job, and which contact fields come back depends on plan and policy.

Skip it if you don't have engineering time. Visit People Data Labs.

FullEnrich

Best for: teams that want waterfall email and phone finding without building it in Clay.

FullEnrich positions itself as an aggregator that queries several contact-data providers in sequence and returns the result it finds.

Pricing model: credits. Check FullEnrich pricing for how unsuccessful lookups are billed.

Strengths: waterfall coverage with little setup. Limitations: narrow scope (contact details, not firmographic or technographic depth), and it depends on upstream providers you don't contract with directly.

Skip it if you need company-level fields for routing and scoring. Visit FullEnrich.

Credit use at 1,000 and 10,000 records

Credit systems make sticker prices hard to compare, so we modelled two hypothetical jobs. Assumptions: every record gets a work email lookup, 30% also get a mobile number, and nothing is re-enriched within the year. Apollo's unit costs are from its pricing page (checked 4 November 2026). Clay doesn't list a single per-email price because cost depends on which providers run, so its per-lookup figures below are our illustrative assumptions, not Clay figures: 2 data credits per found email, 10 per found phone, 70% email find rate and 40% phone find rate.

Field-level governance and duplicate prevention

Before switching on any automated writeback, agree a short rulebook. This checklist keeps enrichment from creating more cleanup than it saves:

  • Source of truth per field. Decide whether rep-entered, form-submitted or vendor data wins for each field, such as job title, employee count and industry.
  • Overwrite policy. Pick "fill blanks only", "overwrite if older than 180 days" or "never overwrite" for each field. Never let enrichment overwrite owner, lifecycle stage or opt-out status.
  • Provenance fields. Store an enrichment source and a last-enriched date next to key fields so you can audit and refresh.
  • Match keys. Match companies on normalised domain, not name. Match people on email first, then a profile URL.
  • Deduplicate before you enrich. Enriching duplicates doubles the bill and makes merges harder.
  • No record creation from enrichment. Enrichment should update records, not create new companies from a parent-company lookup.
  • Quarantine low-confidence results in a review view rather than writing them straight to live fields.

Scored and routed leads depend on these fields, which is why we cover the downstream side in our lead scoring software picks. If enrichment runs through an automation layer instead of a native integration, our guide to no-code automation platforms for marketing ops explains the trade-offs.

Match rates, fallbacks and data decay

Vendors publish their own coverage and accuracy claims, and they rarely match your segment. Coverage varies by country, seniority, company size and industry. The only reliable test is a sample: take 200 records you already know are correct, run them through each shortlisted tool, and score fill rate and accuracy field by field.

Waterfalls raise fill rates by trying a second or third source when the first misses, but they also multiply the number of providers whose data terms you inherit. Check how each provider handles opt-outs and what you may store after cancelling.

Data also decays as people change jobs and companies get acquired, so treat enrichment as a recurring process. Re-enrich active pipeline accounts more often than cold records, and verify emails close to send time with one of the list cleaning and email verification tools we cover. Recommendations by scenario

  • Seed-stage SaaS with a HubSpot CRM and inbound forms: start with HubSpot's native enrichment, and add Clay only if a specific field keeps coming back empty.
  • Five-rep outbound team on a tight budget: Apollo Basic or Professional, with mobiles enriched only for top-tier accounts.
  • RevOps team feeding scoring and routing: Clay Growth for CRM sync, with documented overwrite rules.
  • Team selling into Germany, France and the UK by phone: trial Cognism against your sample before signing.
  • Product-led company enriching sign-ups in a warehouse: People Data Labs or Clay's warehouse sync.

Mistakes and questions to ask

The common mistakes are enriching every record instead of those that pass basic fit rules, buying phone credits nobody dials, and letting two tools write to the same field. Ask vendors:

  • Are we charged when a lookup returns nothing, or returns a result we reject?
  • Which fields can we lock against overwrite, and is there a change log?
  • Do unused credits roll over, and what do top-ups cost?
  • Which sub-processors supply the data, and how are opt-out requests propagated?
  • What may we keep in our CRM after the contract ends?

FAQs

Is lead enrichment the same as a contact database?

They overlap. A contact database is where you search for new people. Enrichment fills gaps in records you already hold. Many vendors do both, but you pay differently depending on which you use more.

How often should records be re-enriched?

There's no universal interval. A sensible default is to refresh open-pipeline accounts quarterly and the rest of the database once or twice a year, plus on demand when a record enters a sequence.

Can enrichment replace form fields?

Partly. Many teams ask only for a work email and enrich company size and industry. Keep any field you need for consent or qualification that a vendor can't know, such as the buyer's timeline.

Do small teams need a waterfall?

Only if one provider leaves important gaps in your segment. Run the 200-record sample first. If a single source fills most fields correctly, a waterfall adds cost and complexity for little gain.

Is enriched data automatically GDPR-compliant?

No. You remain responsible for your lawful basis, transparency and handling objections. Review each vendor's data processing terms with your own counsel.

Verdict

In our editorial assessment, Clay is the strongest choice when someone on the team will own the logic, and Apollo is the practical default for small teams that also need a database and outreach. ZoomInfo and Cognism earn their cost only where their coverage fits your market. HubSpot's native enrichment is the simplest route for HubSpot shops. Whatever you pick, write the field rules first. That decision does more for data quality than the choice of vendor.
