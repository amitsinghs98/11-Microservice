The shared copy brief and quality criteria

Feed the identical brief into each tool on your shortlist during its trial. Here is a hypothetical example to adapt:

  • Product: a reusable insulated water bottle, 750 ml, keeps drinks cold for a stated number of hours (use your real, substantiated figure).
  • Audience: commuters aged 25 to 40.
  • Voice: plain, warm, no exclamation marks, no "revolutionary" claims.
  • Outputs: five search ad headlines within the ad platform's character limit, one landing-page hero section (headline, subhead, three benefit bullets), one 80-word product description, and one promotional email subject line plus preview text.
  • Constraint: mention no claims beyond the supplied facts.

Score each output on:

  1. Accuracy: did it invent features, certifications or statistics?
  2. Constraint adherence: character limits, banned words, required terms.
  3. Voice fit: would your brand lead approve it with light edits?
  4. Distinctiveness: are the five variants genuinely different angles or one idea reworded?
  5. Edit time: minutes from output to publishable copy.

The tools

Anyword

Best for: paid social and search teams that generate many variants and need help choosing between them.

Anyword's distinguishing feature is a predictive performance score attached to each generated variant, which the vendor describes as based on marketing performance data. Higher tiers can connect to your own channel data so scores reflect your audience.

  • Strengths: adds a prioritisation signal to variant generation; brand rules and voice settings.
  • Limitations: a predicted score is not a live test result; validate winners with real A/B tests, using tools from our A/B testing software guide.
  • Pricing: tiered plans with enterprise options. See Anyword's pricing page.
  • Skip it if: you run few ads and rarely test variants.

Official site: Anyword.

Jasper

Best for: in-house teams that need ads, landing pages and emails to sound like the same brand.

Jasper stores brand voices and company knowledge centrally, so a new campaign starts from approved context rather than a blank prompt. It offers marketing templates and team collaboration.

  • Strengths: shared brand context and campaign-oriented organisation.
  • Limitations: per-seat economics are hard to justify for one or two writers; no native performance prediction.
  • Pricing: per seat, with a trial and custom business tier. See Jasper pricing.
  • Skip it if: your copy volume is low or you need mostly product descriptions.

Official site: Jasper. If Jasper is the incumbent you are weighing, our options for teams moving off Jasper may help.

Hypotenuse AI

Best for: ecommerce teams writing or refreshing hundreds of product pages.

Hypotenuse AI focuses on product content: generating descriptions in bulk from catalogue data, with brand voice settings and integrations with ecommerce platforms, according to its product pages.

  • Strengths: bulk workflows built around product attributes rather than one prompt at a time.
  • Limitations: output is only as accurate as your product data; less suited to ad concepting.
  • Pricing: tiered with custom plans for larger catalogues. See Hypotenuse AI pricing.
  • Skip it if: your catalogue is small enough to write by hand.

Official site: Hypotenuse AI.

Copy.ai

Best for: teams that want copy generated as one step inside a repeatable go-to-market workflow.

Copy.ai has shifted from a template library toward a GTM AI platform with chained workflows, shared brand voice and company knowledge.

  • Strengths: automating high-repetition tasks such as account-specific messaging.
  • Limitations: more setup than a simple copy generator; may be more platform than a small ecommerce team needs.
  • Pricing: tiered plans. See Copy.ai prices.
  • Skip it if: you want a quick headline generator.

Official site: Copy.ai. How it compares with Jasper and Writesonic is covered in our three-way platform comparison.

Persado

Best for: large consumer brands with the traffic to run structured message experiments.

Persado is an enterprise platform that generates and optimises marketing language, positioned around understanding which words and emotional framing drive response. It is sold through a sales-led process.

  • Strengths: built for measured message optimisation at scale.
  • Limitations: enterprise commitment; not a self-serve tool for small teams.
  • Pricing: quote only, via Persado's website.
  • Skip it if: you lack the volume for statistically meaningful tests.

ChatGPT

Best for: marketers comfortable writing detailed prompts who do not need specialist features.

A general assistant can produce every output in the shared brief if you supply the constraints and examples. Projects or custom GPTs can hold your voice guide for reuse.

  • Strengths: flexibility and low marginal cost if the team already has seats.
  • Limitations: no performance feedback, no catalogue workflows, and character limits need checking manually.
  • Pricing: free tier and per-user plans. See ChatGPT pricing.
  • Skip it if: several people write copy and voice consistency is already a problem.

Official site: ChatGPT.

Rytr

Best for: freelancers and very small businesses that need occasional short copy cheaply.

Rytr offers use-case templates and tone options in a simple interface, with a free tier and low-cost paid plans.

  • Strengths: low cost and minimal learning curve.
  • Limitations: light on brand governance, collaboration and performance features.
  • Pricing: free tier with usage caps plus paid plans. See Rytr pricing.
  • Skip it if: you need team workflows or approvals.

Official site: Rytr.

Limitations of AI copywriting

Short copy concentrates risk. A single invented claim in a headline can breach advertising rules or platform policies, and product descriptions can state attributes your product does not have. Predictive scores and "best variant" labels estimate, they do not measure. Many tools also converge on the same phrasing, so competing brands using similar prompts can end up sounding alike.

Keep a human approver for every claim, maintain a list of substantiated facts the tools may use, and treat live test results as the only real performance evidence.

Buying mistakes and questions to ask

  • Judging tools on a demo prompt rather than your own brief.
  • Paying for a specialist and a general assistant that do the same job.
  • Overlooking seat minimums and word or credit allowances. Our pricing guide for AI content tools models these costs.

Ask vendors: Where does the performance score's data come from? Can we restrict outputs to an approved fact list? Can we export templates and brand settings? What usage counts toward limits?

FAQs

Are predicted performance scores reliable?

Treat them as a way to prioritise which variants to test, not as results. Only live tests with your audience show what actually performs.

Is a general assistant good enough for ad copy?

Often, for one or two writers with clear briefs. Specialist tools add value through shared brand settings, bulk workflows or performance signals.

Can AI write product descriptions without errors?

Only if it works from accurate structured product data, and even then a person should check claims about materials, dimensions and certifications.

Verdict

Pick by output type. Anyword suits variant-heavy ad teams, Jasper suits brand-conscious campaign teams, Hypotenuse AI suits large catalogues, Copy.ai suits workflow automation, and Persado suits enterprises with test volume. For small teams, a general assistant plus a disciplined brief may be all you need. Run the shared brief, count edit minutes, and let live tests settle which copy wins.
