Which conversation intelligence software should you choose for sales coaching?

For a coaching program with several managers and a large call volume, Gong and Chorus are built for deeper call and deal analysis; for a mid-sized team that wants scorecards without an enterprise contract, Avoma, Jiminny and Zoom Revenue Accelerator are the practical shortlist. Fathom and Fireflies suit teams that mainly need recordings, transcripts and summaries, with coaching added later.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • Large teams coaching from deal outcomes: Gong.
  • ZoomInfo customers: Chorus, added to the existing contract.
  • Teams that sell mostly over Zoom: Zoom Revenue Accelerator.
  • Mid-sized teams wanting published per-seat pricing: Avoma.
  • Managers who coach but don't record: Jiminny, with separate seat types for recorders and reviewers.
  • Budget-first teams starting with recordings and notes: Fathom or Fireflies.

What does conversation intelligence do for sales coaching?

Conversation intelligence records sales calls, transcribes them, detects topics such as pricing, competitors and next steps, and surfaces moments a manager should review. For coaching, the value is turning hundreds of hours of calls into a short list of specific moments worth discussing with each rep.

The coaching features that matter are scorecards managers fill against a defined call framework, searchable libraries of good calls, comments at a timestamp, and talk-pattern metrics used as prompts rather than targets. Deal insights (risk, missing stakeholders, competitor mentions) bridge into revenue intelligence platforms, which add pipeline and forecast signals on top. Recording is the sensitive part. Consent and notice rules vary by country and state and by how calls are made, so agree a recording policy with legal counsel before rollout. This page describes product options, not legal requirements.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The order follows our criteria for a sales coaching program: depth of coaching and deal analysis first, then pricing transparency and ease of rollout for smaller teams. Payment doesn't affect inclusion or order.

Gong: coaching from deal outcomes at scale

Gong analyzes calls, emails and meetings and links them to deals, so managers can coach on what happens in won and lost opportunities. Its pricing page describes per-user licenses and a platform fee that scales with users.

Pricing model: quote-based per-user licenses plus a platform fee.

Strengths: Deep analysis and search, with coaching moments tied to deal results.

Limitations: Expensive for small teams, and the breadth can overwhelm managers who just want a scorecard.

Skip it if: you have a handful of reps and one manager; a lighter tool covers coaching.

Chorus: conversation intelligence for ZoomInfo customers

Chorus is ZoomInfo's conversation intelligence product. ZoomInfo's Chorus pricing page lists custom plans, and the product can be bought on its own or bundled into a ZoomInfo contract.

Pricing model: custom plans; no public list price for Chorus.

Strengths: Commercially convenient if ZoomInfo data is already in your stack, with call analysis and deal insights.

Limitations: Pricing is opaque, and the value case rests heavily on the wider ZoomInfo relationship.

Skip it if: you don't use ZoomInfo and want a standalone tool.

Zoom Revenue Accelerator: teams that sell mostly over Zoom

Zoom's Revenue Accelerator pricing page publishes per-user tiers billed annually, with higher tiers adding AI features and pooled AI credits. It builds conversation analysis into the meeting platform many teams already use.

Pricing model: published per-user tiers billed annually, with AI credits on higher tiers.

Strengths: Analysis sits inside the meeting platform many teams already use, and published prices make budgeting easier.

Limitations: Coverage outside Zoom meetings is worth testing, and Zoom notes that packaging can change.

Skip it if: much of your selling happens on Teams, Google Meet or the phone.

Avoma: scorecards for mid-sized teams with transparent pricing

Avoma sells meeting recording and notes per recorder seat, with conversation intelligence (call scoring and coaching recommendations) as a separate per-seat add-on. Its help center explains that viewers are free and all paid recorders sit on the same base plan.

Pricing model: published per-seat base plan plus per-seat coaching add-on; free viewer seats.

Strengths: Clear pricing and free viewers, which suits managers and enablement staff who only review.

Limitations: Coaching sits in an add-on, so the base plan alone won't give you scorecards.

Skip it if: you need the deepest deal-level analytics across a large team.

Jiminny: coach-heavy teams that want predictable fees

Jiminny prices by seat type: recording seats for reps, insights seats for managers and coaches, and free listener seats. It says it charges no separate platform fee; its own pages describe setup fees and contract terms differently, so confirm both in your quote.

Pricing model: seat types, including free listener seats, with no separate platform fee; term and any setup charge confirmed in a quote.

Strengths: Seat types match how coaching actually works, and the flat-fee positioning reduces usage surprises.

Limitations: Seat prices and contract terms aren't consistent across the vendor's pages, so get them in writing before you size the contract.

Skip it if: your managers only need recordings and summaries; a meeting assistant may cost less.

Fathom: recordings and summaries on a budget

Fathom's pricing page lists a free plan for individuals and per-user Team and Business plans with a minimum seat count; newer team plans no longer include free seats.

Pricing model: free individual plan; per-user team plans with a seat minimum.

Strengths: Easy start for reps who mainly need recordings, transcripts and CRM-ready summaries.

Limitations: Coaching scorecards and deal analytics are lighter than the dedicated platforms.

Skip it if: managers need structured scorecards and team-wide coaching analytics.

Fireflies: broad meeting capture with coaching on higher tiers

Fireflies' pricing page lists a permanent free plan and per-seat paid tiers, with conversation intelligence and team analytics starting on its Business tier.

Pricing model: free plan plus per-seat paid tiers; AI credits sold separately for some features.

Strengths: Works across many meeting platforms and suits company-wide note-taking as well as sales.

Limitations: Sales coaching is one use among many, so scorecards are less specialized.

Skip it if: you want a sales-specific coaching workflow out of the box.

Will reps see this as coaching or as surveillance?

That depends on how managers use it, not on the tool: programs that review calls with the rep, share good examples and avoid ranking people on raw talk metrics are more likely to be accepted. Publish what's recorded, who can see it and how long it's kept before the first call is captured.

How do you build a coaching workflow rather than a surveillance system?

Coach a few behaviors at a time, against a written framework, with the rep in the room. Use the software to find moments and track progress, not to score everyone on every call.

  1. Pick two skills per quarter: for example discovery questions and next-step commitment, and build a short scorecard for each.
  2. Agree the policy: what's recorded, notice and consent wording, retention period and who can access recordings, reviewed with legal and HR.
  3. Weekly review: each manager reviews two calls per rep using the scorecard and leaves timestamped comments.
  4. One-to-one: discuss one moment that went well and one to change; the rep picks the next call to review.
  5. Library: enablement curates strong examples by stage, which feeds onboarding and sales enablement software.
  6. Quarterly check: look at scorecard trends and win rates together, and retire metrics that don't change behavior.

If the same team runs sequences, check whether your sales engagement platform already records dialer calls, to avoid paying twice. Deal-risk signals from calls can also inform sales forecasting software.

What mistakes do teams make with conversation intelligence?

The commonest mistake is recording everything and reviewing nothing. The second is treating talk-ratio and filler-word metrics as performance targets.

  • No policy before rollout: consent, notice and retention decisions made after the fact damage trust.
  • Ignoring languages: confirm transcription quality for every language your reps sell in.
  • Unmapped CRM sync: decide which fields summaries update, so notes don't overwrite rep entries.
  • Paying for unused seats: separate recorder seats from reviewer seats where the vendor allows it.
  • Overlap: meeting assistants, dialers and engagement platforms may already record the same calls.

What else do buyers ask about conversation intelligence software?

Buyers ask most about consent, seat types and which metrics are worth tracking. Short answers follow.

Is conversation intelligence legal to use on sales calls?

Recording rules depend on where you and the other party are and how the call is made. Get legal advice for your regions and configure notice and consent features accordingly; this page doesn't give legal conclusions.

What is the difference between conversation intelligence and a meeting notetaker?

Notetakers focus on transcripts, summaries and action items. Conversation intelligence adds team-level analysis, coaching scorecards and deal insights across many calls.

Do managers need a paid seat to review calls?

It depends on the vendor. Some offer free viewer or listener seats, while others license every user, so check seat types before you size the contract.

Which metrics are useful for coaching?

Use scorecard results on defined skills and outcome measures such as next-step rates. Treat talk ratio and similar metrics as prompts for review, not targets.

How long should call recordings be kept?

Set retention by policy and regulation, not by default settings. Shorter retention reduces risk; keep curated examples for training with appropriate consent.

What's the verdict?

Large teams that coach from deal outcomes should shortlist Gong and, if they use ZoomInfo, Chorus. Mid-sized teams get most of the coaching value from Avoma, Jiminny or Zoom Revenue Accelerator, and budget-first teams can start with Fathom or Fireflies and add structure later.
