Which white-label reporting platform should your agency choose?

Choose an all-in-one agency reporting platform if you report on several channels for many clients and want branding out of the box. Choose a build-your-own stack around Looker Studio if you have someone who enjoys data modelling and you want full control of layout.

  • Multi-channel agencies that want SEO, ads and social in one branded login: AgencyAnalytics.
  • Simple, template-led dashboards that a junior account manager can build: DashThis.
  • Agencies that need to blend sources into one cross-channel view: Whatagraph.
  • PPC-heavy agencies that also want account monitoring: Swydo.
  • KPI and goal tracking for clients who think in targets, not channels: Databox.
  • Agencies with in-house data skills and a tight budget: Looker Studio, usually fed by a connector such as Supermetrics.

Who needs a white-label reporting platform, and what problem does it solve?

It suits agencies that send recurring performance reports to several clients and currently assemble them by hand from screenshots and exports. The platform replaces that assembly with live connectors, branded templates and scheduled delivery.

The real problem is rarely the chart. It is the hours spent pulling numbers, the errors when a filter is wrong, and the client who reads a dashboard and still asks "so is this good?" A reporting platform fixes the first two directly. The third needs a written summary, which the better tools make easier through annotations, text blocks and goal markers.

What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

All-in-one platforms win on speed and branding, while build-your-own stacks win on flexibility. The trade-off is setup time against monthly cost and template rigidity.

AgencyAnalytics: best for multi-channel agencies at scale

AgencyAnalytics positions itself as reporting software built for marketing agencies, combining client dashboards and scheduled reports under your branding. Check how the AgencyAnalytics pricing page scales cost as you add clients, and whether a trial is offered, because small retainers can make per-account costs add up. Skip it if you mostly need one deeply customised dashboard rather than many standard ones.

DashThis: best for fast, template-led dashboards

DashThis focuses on getting a clean dashboard live quickly from preset templates and common marketing integrations. It suits agencies whose account managers build reports without a data specialist. Agencies that give each client several views should check how the pricing treats additional dashboards. Skip it if you need heavy data blending or calculated metrics across sources. Whatagraph positions itself around combining data from many marketing sources into a single report, with white-label options for agencies. Swydo: best for PPC-led agencies

Swydo positions itself around paid search and paid social reporting, with monitoring of account performance alongside the reports. Databox: best for goal and KPI tracking

Databox centres on metrics, goals and scorecards, with integrations that reach beyond marketing sources; confirm the ones you need. It suits clients who care about pipeline and revenue targets more than channel detail. Agency white-label and client management features vary by plan, so check those specifically. Looker Studio is Google's dashboarding tool and is built around Google's own marketing data sources. Agencies often feed it non-Google data through a third-party connector, which is where Supermetrics fits; check which sources each connector covers. The stack gives you total layout control, but someone has to build, maintain and fix it when a connector changes. Skip it if nobody on the team owns reporting. How do you protect data quality and freshness?

Decide one source of truth per metric, document each connector's refresh timing, and annotate anything that changes the numbers. Most "the report is wrong" conversations trace back to one of those three gaps.

  • Attribution and time zone: match the platform's reporting time zone and attribution window to how the client reads results.
  • Freshness: label each report with the date range and the last refresh time, so a client never compares a partial day.
  • Annotations: mark tracking changes, site launches and budget shifts on the chart where the line moves.
  • Permissions: give clients view-only access and check that one client can never see another's workspace.
  • Exports: confirm you can export raw data, not only PDFs, in case you need to reconcile or switch.

What should an executive client report include?

An executive report should fit on one page: three headline outcomes against target, what changed and why, and the decisions or approvals you need. Channel detail belongs in an appendix or the live dashboard.

  1. Summary in plain sentences: "Leads rose X% against target, driven by Y; organic traffic dipped after Z."
  2. Outcome KPIs: leads, revenue or pipeline, with the target beside each number.
  3. Channel snapshot: one line each for SEO, paid, social and email.
  4. Next month: the three actions you'll take and anything you need from the client.
  5. Source notes: the data source for each headline number, the date range, the attribution model and known gaps such as consent-mode modelling or a tracking outage.

Source notes protect you when a client's finance team compares your figures with theirs. Owners who want the same discipline applied to internal margins can compare agency profitability reporting tools. Delivery status, such as what shipped this month, usually comes from your project management tool for client delivery rather than the reporting platform.

Do clients actually read automated dashboards?

Many don't, which is why the written summary matters more than the number of widgets. Based on the product information reviewed, every platform here can schedule a report, but none can decide what the client should conclude from it.

What mistakes do agencies make when choosing a reporting platform?

The most common mistake is choosing on connector count instead of checking the specific connectors your clients use. The second is ignoring how pricing grows when you add clients or dashboards.

  • Promising a custom domain or full white-label before confirming the plan includes it.
  • Building a different template for every client, which makes maintenance unmanageable.
  • Sending dashboards with no commentary, then fielding the same questions every month.
  • Leaving reporting in a separate place from approvals and files; a client portal can keep them together.

What else do agencies ask about white-label reporting?

Most remaining questions are about data accuracy, branding depth and cost per client. The answers below are general, so confirm each vendor's current terms on its official pages.

Is white-label reporting the same as a custom domain?

No. White-labelling usually covers your logo, colours and sender name, while a custom domain puts reports on your own web address. Vendors often place the two on different plans, so check both before promising them to a client.

Why do report numbers differ from Google Ads or GA4?

Differences usually come from time zones, attribution settings, sampling, currency or a connector refreshing on a delay. Pick one source of truth per metric and note it in the report so the client knows which number to trust.

How often do reporting connectors refresh data?

It varies by vendor, plan and source, so there is no single standard schedule. Ask the vendor for the refresh schedule of each connector you rely on, not just the headline figure.

Should clients get dashboard logins or scheduled PDFs?

Executives tend to read a short scheduled summary, while marketing managers use a live dashboard. Offering both, with the summary as the default, covers most accounts.

Can I move reports from one platform to another?

Dashboards rarely export in a format another tool can import, so expect to rebuild templates. Export the historical data you need first, because some connectors only backfill a limited window.

What's the verdict?

AgencyAnalytics is the safest default for agencies reporting on several channels for many clients, and DashThis is the quicker, simpler option for template-led dashboards. Pick Whatagraph or Databox for blended or goal-led reporting, and Looker Studio with a connector only if someone owns the build.

Before you sign, connect two real client accounts during a trial, if the vendor offers one, and reconcile the numbers against the source platforms.
