Which webinar platform should you choose for B2B lead generation?

Choose by where your leads need to land and who will run the program. If sales lives in Salesforce or HubSpot and expects engagement data on the record, shortlist Goldcast, ON24 or Livestorm; if you run a handful of webinars a quarter and mostly need reliable delivery, Zoom Webinars, Demio or WebinarGeek will do the job with less operational overhead.

  • Enterprise programs that score engagement: ON24, for teams that want one engagement score passed to marketing automation and have someone to own the platform.
  • Video-first B2B marketing teams on HubSpot, Marketo or Salesforce: Goldcast, which also offers a free plan for teams that want to look before they talk to sales.
  • Lean teams that want live, on-demand and automated sessions in one browser tool: Livestorm.
  • Companies already standardized on Zoom: Zoom Webinars, with Zoom Events when you need multi-session formats.
  • Small marketing teams that want a simple attendee experience: Demio.
  • Teams that run webinars, summits and evergreen content from one vendor: BigMarker.
  • Teams that want published self-serve plans and euro billing: WebinarGeek.
  • Teams that also run hybrid or onsite events: RingCentral Events.

Do you need a webinar platform or a full virtual event platform?

You need a webinar platform if your format is one stage, one audience and one call to action. You need a virtual event platform once you add parallel tracks, sponsor booths, attendee networking or ticketing, because those features change the data model, the price and the staffing.

The distinction matters for lead generation because webinar tools are built around a short funnel: landing page, registration, reminder, live session, replay, follow-up. Event platforms are built around an attendee journey across many sessions, and the lead data is spread across session attendance, booth visits and meetings. Several vendors sell both, so check which product tier a feature actually sits in before you assume a webinar plan includes it.

Two adjacent formats blur the line. If you stream a stage to remote viewers while people also attend in person, the job moves into hybrid event management platforms, which carry a much heavier production load. If your program is mostly recorded sessions that run on a schedule, the job is closer to automation than hosting, and our comparison of webinar automation software for always-on programs covers replay timing, evergreen registration and honest labelling.

Which webinar platforms are worth shortlisting, and where do they fall short?

ON24, Goldcast and Livestorm are the strongest fits when the webinar exists to feed pipeline, because each is built to push engagement detail into the CRM rather than just attendance. The others are sound choices for narrower jobs, and each has a limitation that should decide whether it makes your list.

ON24: best for enterprise engagement scoring

ON24 is a long-standing enterprise webinar and digital-events platform. Its glossary describes an Engagement Score that combines viewer activities such as watch time, questions, polls and resources into one number, and its HubSpot integration updates contact records with registration and attendance activity. That is useful when sales wants a threshold for follow-up rather than a raw attendee list. The pricing page offers packages through a pricing request rather than published prices (checked 7 November 2026).

Strengths: mature engagement data, marketing-automation focus, broad content formats. Limitations: quote-based pricing and a platform that assumes someone owns it. Skip it if you run fewer than one webinar a month or lack an operations owner.

Goldcast: best for video-first B2B teams

Goldcast positions itself as an event and video platform for B2B marketers. Its integrations page lists HubSpot, Marketo, Eloqua and Salesforce. Its help center describes Starter, Pro, Premium and Enterprise tiers, with Starter limited to webinars and a 90-minute maximum event length, plus a free plan with limits such as one user per account (checked 7 November 2026). Paid prices come from sales.

Strengths: CRM-oriented engagement data and a clear path from webinars to multi-session events. Limitations: no public paid pricing, and tiers cap the number of included events. Skip it if you want to sign up with a card for a paid plan and launch this week.

Livestorm: best for lean teams that want every format

Livestorm runs in the browser and supports live, on-demand and pre-recorded sessions. Its HubSpot integration syncs registrants and attendees into HubSpot, which lets you build workflows around attendance, no-shows or replay views. Its pricing page describes a credit model in which credits are used only by attendees who actually join, not by hosts or team members (checked 7 November 2026); per-attendee figures differed across sources, so read the live page.

Strengths: one tool for live and evergreen sessions, no download for attendees. Limitations: usage-linked pricing needs an attendance forecast. Skip it if you need complex multi-track agendas.

Zoom Webinars: best if you already run on Zoom

Zoom's events and webinars pricing page lists Webinars at $89/month billed monthly or $66.67/month billed annually for a 500-attendee plan, with Webinars Plus and Events tiers above it for multi-session, networking and hybrid features (checked 7 November 2026). The vendor's support article compares its Zoom Events and Webinars Plus event types. Familiarity is the main advantage: presenters and attendees rarely need instructions.

Strengths: reliability, low training cost and published prices. Limitations: lead handling depends on integrations and your automation setup. Skip it if you want engagement scoring without building it yourself.

Demio: best for small teams that value simplicity

Demio is a browser-based webinar tool aimed at marketers. Its help center documents both standard (live) and automated events, and its host-based pricing article says Starter allows a single host while Growth and higher plans allow multiple hosts for simultaneous events (checked 7 November 2026). Price figures were inconsistent across Demio's own pages, so confirm them on the pricing page.

Strengths: a simple, marketing-led product that lets a small team reuse a strong session. Limitations: confirm enterprise controls and reporting depth with the vendor. Skip it if several presenters need to run sessions at the same time on an entry plan.

BigMarker: best for one vendor across formats

BigMarker markets webinars, summits and hybrid formats from one platform. Its pricing page places some formats, such as evergreen webinars, in higher packages, and its subscription policy describes a 7-day free trial and automatic renewal of monthly and annual plans (checked 7 November 2026).

Strengths: breadth of formats. Limitations: package structure needs careful reading, and published price figures conflicted across BigMarker pages. Skip it if you only need a straightforward live webinar.

WebinarGeek: best for teams that want self-serve

WebinarGeek's pricing page lists a Premium plan from €59/month billed yearly, with viewer tiers from 75 to 1,000, and an Enterprise plan at €349/month on an annual contract (checked 7 November 2026). The same page lists automatic replays and replay emails, which suits teams that want to start without a sales call.

Strengths: transparent plans, live and automated sessions in one dashboard. Limitations: extras such as additional users and branding profiles are priced as add-ons; check native integrations against your CRM. Skip it if you need deep Salesforce campaign handling the vendor cannot document.

RingCentral Events: best when webinars sit inside a wider event program

RingCentral acquired the Events and Session product lines from Hopin and now markets RingCentral Events for webinars alongside virtual, onsite and hybrid events. Its pricing page prices by organizer seat, starting at $99/organizer/month, with unlimited registrations and an annual commitment on every plan (checked 7 November 2026).

Strengths: one platform from webinar to hybrid, and pricing that doesn't penalize a big audience. Limitations: more platform than a webinar-only team needs. Skip it if webinars are your only format.

How are webinar platforms priced, and what drives the next tier?

Webinar platforms charge on one of five meters: attendee capacity, attendees who actually join, hosts, organizer seats or an allowance of events, with enterprise suites quoting privately. The meter decides what pushes you into the next tier, so match it to the number that grows fastest in your program.

  • Attendee capacity (Zoom Webinars, WebinarGeek): you pay for the largest live audience you might draw. It suits steady programs; it hurts when one session suddenly outgrows the cap.
  • Attendee credits (Livestorm): you pay for people who join, so registrant no-shows cost nothing. Forecast annual attendance, not peak attendance.
  • Hosts or organizers (Demio, RingCentral Events): cost grows with the people running sessions, not the audience. Good for large audiences with a small team.
  • Event allowances and tiers (Goldcast, BigMarker): plans include a number of events or formats, and evergreen or multi-session formats often sit higher up.
  • Quote-based (ON24): packages are tailored, so ask for renewal caps and what counts as an event in writing.

Annual billing usually lowers the monthly rate, as Zoom's page shows for Webinars, but it locks you in before you know whether the program works. Run two or three sessions on monthly billing or a trial, then commit. Production time, landing pages and follow-up labor often cost more than the license, so budget for them alongside it.

Where do webinar programs actually lose leads between registration and sales?

In our editorial assessment, the weak point is usually not the live session but the handoff, where registration data never reaches the CRM with its source, or engagement data arrives too late for sales to use. A platform with native CRM sync narrows that gap, but it doesn't define who follows up, how fast, or on which signal.

  5. Handoff: agree a rule, for example that anyone who asks a question or clicks a demo CTA is routed to a rep within a day.
  6. Follow-up: send the replay to no-shows and attendees with different messages.

What mistakes do B2B teams make when buying a webinar platform?

The most common mistake is buying for the live session and ignoring the data path afterwards. The second is choosing a pricing meter that punishes the metric your program will grow fastest, whether that's audience size, presenters or number of events.

  • Buying event software for webinar work. Multi-track features add cost and setup that a monthly webinar series doesn't need.
  • Treating attendance as the KPI. Measure meetings and opportunities influenced, not registrants.
  • Ignoring the replay. Some registrants will only ever watch on demand, so make sure replay views sync like live attendance.
  • Skipping a test registration. Register yourself with a tagged URL during the trial and confirm what reaches the CRM.
  • Committing annually before the format is proven. Monthly billing costs more per month but less than a year of an unused license.
  • Betting everything on one channel. Webinars reward teams with expertise to share; weigh them against other channels before scaling.

What else do buyers ask about webinar platforms for B2B lead generation?

Most remaining questions are about free plans, CRM sync, replays and when to add an event platform. The short answers are below; confirm plan details on each vendor's official pricing page.

Can a free webinar plan generate B2B leads?

It can produce registrations, but free plans usually restrict attendees, users, duration or integrations, which limits lead handling. Treat a free plan as a trial of the workflow, not a long-term program.

Which matters more, attendee capacity or CRM integration?

For lead generation, CRM integration usually matters more. Unless you expect a very large live audience, the value comes from getting engagement data to sales quickly.

Should replay viewers count as leads?

Yes, if they registered and watched a meaningful share of the session. Sync replay views the same way as live attendance and score them on the same signals.

How many registration fields should a B2B webinar form have?

Keep it to the fields sales needs for routing, often name, work email, company, role and one qualifying question. Enrichment tools can fill firmographic gaps later.

Is per-attendee or per-host pricing cheaper?

It depends on your ratio of audience to presenters. A small team reaching large audiences usually does better on host or organizer pricing, while a program with many presenters and modest audiences often does better on attendee-based pricing.

Do I need a separate virtual event platform as well?

Only if you run multi-track events with sponsors or networking. Many teams run webinars on one tool and use an event platform for one or two flagship events a year.
