Best Webinar Platforms for B2B Lead Generation

The best webinar platforms for B2B lead generation are the ones that turn a registration form into a scored, CRM-ready lead without a spreadsheet in the middle. For most mid-market teams that means Goldcast, Livestorm or ON24; smaller teams can start with Zoom Webinars or Demio and add process before adding software.

Which webinar platform should you choose for B2B lead generation?

Choose by where your leads need to land. If sales lives in Salesforce or HubSpot and expects engagement data on the record, shortlist Goldcast, ON24 or Livestorm; if you run a handful of webinars a quarter and mostly need reliable delivery, Zoom Webinars or Demio will do the job at lower operational overhead.

  • Enterprise programs with engagement scoring: ON24, for teams that want a single engagement score passed to marketing automation.
  • Video-first B2B marketing teams on HubSpot, Marketo or Salesforce: Goldcast.
  • Lean teams that want live, on-demand and automated sessions in one browser-based tool: Livestorm.
  • Companies already standardized on Zoom: Zoom Webinars or Zoom Sessions.
  • Small marketing teams that want a simple attendee experience: Demio.
  • Teams that run webinars, summits and evergreen content from one vendor: BigMarker.
  • Teams that want published self-serve plans: WebinarGeek.
  • Teams that also run hybrid or onsite events: RingCentral Events.

This page covers single-session lead-generation webinars. If you are planning multi-track conferences with sponsors and networking, start with our guide to virtual event platforms built for B2B marketing teams instead.

Do you need a webinar platform or a full virtual event platform?

You need a webinar platform if your format is one stage, one audience and one call to action. You need a virtual event platform once you add parallel tracks, sponsor booths, attendee networking or ticketing, because those features change the data model, the price and the staffing.

The distinction matters for lead generation because webinar tools are built around a short funnel: landing page, registration, reminder, live session, replay, follow-up. Event platforms are built around an attendee journey across many sessions, and the lead data is spread across session attendance, booth visits and meetings. Several vendors sell both, so check which product tier a feature actually sits in before you assume a webinar plan includes it.

If your program is mostly recorded sessions that run on a schedule, the job is closer to automation than hosting. Our comparison of webinar automation software for always-on programs covers replay timing, evergreen registration and honest labelling.

Which webinar platforms are worth shortlisting, and where do they fall short?

ON24, Goldcast and Livestorm are the strongest fits when the webinar exists to feed pipeline, because each pushes engagement detail into the CRM rather than just attendance. The others are sound choices for narrower jobs, and each has a limitation that should decide whether it makes your list.

ON24: best for enterprise engagement scoring

ON24 is a long-standing enterprise webinar and digital-events platform. Its glossary describes an Engagement Score that combines viewer activities such as watch time, questions, polls and resources into one number, and its HubSpot integration updates contact records with registration and attendance activity. That is useful when sales wants a threshold for follow-up rather than a raw attendee list.

Strengths: mature engagement data, marketing-automation focus, broad content formats. Limitations: pricing is quote-based and the platform assumes someone owns it. Skip it if you run fewer than one webinar a month or lack an operations owner.

Goldcast: best for video-first B2B teams

Goldcast positions itself as an event and video platform for B2B marketers. Its integrations page lists HubSpot, Marketo, Eloqua and Salesforce, and the vendor describes syncing event engagement activity into those systems. Pricing is through sales.

Strengths: CRM-oriented engagement data. Limitations: no public pricing, so budgeting needs a sales cycle. Skip it if you want to sign up with a card and launch this week.

Livestorm: best for lean teams that want every format

Livestorm runs in the browser and supports live, on-demand and pre-recorded sessions. Its HubSpot integration syncs registrants and attendees into HubSpot, which lets you build workflows around attendance, no-shows or replay views. Check how its pricing page counts attendees, and forecast your annual attendance before you choose a tier.

Strengths: one tool for live and evergreen sessions, no download for attendees. Limitations: usage-linked pricing needs an attendance forecast. Skip it if you need complex multi-track agendas.

Zoom Webinars and Zoom Sessions: best if you already run on Zoom

Zoom's events and webinars pricing page separates Zoom Webinars from its multi-session products, and the vendor's support article compares its Zoom Events and Webinars Plus event types. Familiarity is the main advantage: presenters and attendees rarely need instructions.

Strengths: reliability and low training cost. Limitations: lead handling depends on integrations and your automation setup. Skip it if you want engagement scoring without building it yourself.

Demio: best for small teams that value simplicity

Demio is a browser-based webinar tool aimed at marketers. Its help center documents both standard (live) and automated events, which lets a small team reuse a strong session without re-presenting it.

Strengths: a simple, marketing-led product. Limitations: confirm enterprise controls and reporting depth with the vendor. Skip it if procurement requires enterprise controls the vendor cannot document.

BigMarker: best for one vendor across formats

BigMarker markets webinars, summits and hybrid formats from one platform. Its pricing page places some formats, such as evergreen webinars, in higher packages.

Strengths: breadth of formats. Limitations: package structure needs careful reading. Skip it if you only need a straightforward live webinar.

WebinarGeek: best for teams that want self-serve

WebinarGeek publishes its plans on its pricing page, which also lists automatic replays and replay emails. It suits teams that want to start without a sales call.

Strengths: transparent plans, live and automated sessions in one dashboard. Limitations: check its native integrations against your CRM before you commit. Skip it if you need deep Salesforce campaign handling the vendor cannot document.

RingCentral Events: best when webinars sit inside a wider event program

RingCentral acquired the Events and Session product lines from Hopin, and now markets RingCentral Events for webinars alongside virtual, onsite and hybrid events.

Strengths: one platform from webinar to hybrid. Limitations: more platform than a webinar-only team needs. Skip it if webinars are your only format.

Where do webinar programs actually lose leads between registration and sales?

In our editorial assessment, the weak point is usually not the live session but the handoff, where registration data never reaches the CRM with its source, or engagement data arrives too late for sales to use. A platform with native CRM sync narrows that gap, but it does not define who follows up, how fast, or on which signal.

Map the path before you sign. A workable sequence looks like this:

  1. Landing page: capture UTM parameters in hidden fields so the source survives into the CRM. If you build pages outside the webinar tool, see our landing page builders for paid acquisition.
  2. Registration: ask only what sales needs to route the lead, typically company, role and one qualifying question.
  3. Sync: create or update the contact and add it to a campaign or marketing event at registration, not after the session.
  4. Engagement: pass attendance duration, poll answers, questions and CTA clicks as fields your lead scoring tool can read.
  5. Handoff: agree a rule, for example that anyone who asks a question or clicks a demo CTA is routed to a rep within a day.
  6. Follow-up: send the replay to no-shows and attendees with different messages.

What mistakes do B2B teams make when buying a webinar platform?

The most common mistake is buying for the live session and ignoring the data path afterwards. The second is underestimating attendance and choosing a plan that caps registrants or credits just as a program starts working.

  • Buying event software for webinar work. Multi-track features add cost and setup that a monthly webinar series does not need.
  • Treating attendance as the KPI. Measure meetings and opportunities influenced; our list of webinar analytics tools for pipeline reporting covers what to track.
  • Ignoring the replay. Some registrants will only ever watch on demand, so make sure replay views sync like live attendance.
  • Skipping a test registration. Register yourself with a tagged URL during the trial and confirm what reaches the CRM.
  • Overlooking interaction tools. Polls and Q&A are where intent shows up; compare options in our guide to webinar engagement tools.

What else do buyers ask about webinar platforms for B2B lead generation?

Most remaining questions are about free plans, CRM sync and replays. The short answers are below; confirm plan details on each vendor's official pricing page.

Can a free webinar plan generate B2B leads?

It can produce registrations, but free tiers usually restrict attendees, duration or integrations, which limits lead handling. Treat a free plan as a trial of the workflow, not a long-term program.

Which matters more, attendee capacity or CRM integration?

For lead generation, CRM integration usually matters more. Unless you expect a very large live audience, the value comes from getting engagement data to sales quickly.

Should replay viewers count as leads?

Yes, if they registered and watched a meaningful share of the session. Sync replay views the same way as live attendance and score them on the same signals.

How many registration fields should a B2B webinar form have?

Keep it to the fields sales needs for routing, often name, work email, company, role and one qualifying question. Enrichment tools can fill firmographic gaps later.

Do I need a separate virtual event platform as well?

Only if you run multi-track events with sponsors or networking. Many teams run webinars on one tool and use an event platform for one or two flagship events a year.

What's the verdict on webinar platforms for B2B lead generation?

Our editorial assessment: Goldcast, ON24 and Livestorm are the strongest shortlist when webinars are expected to produce pipeline, while Zoom Webinars and Demio suit teams that need dependable delivery and will handle lead routing in their marketing automation. Whichever you pick, the deciding test is whether a test registration arrives in your CRM with its source and engagement data intact.
