Which virtual event platform should a B2B marketing team choose?

Pick by event shape, not by feature count. A multi-track conference with exhibitors and paid sponsors needs Cvent, Bizzabo or Swapcard, while a 100-person customer summit with two stages and a networking lounge is usually better served by Airmeet, Zoom Events or RingCentral Events.

  • Large conference programs that also run in person: Cvent.
  • Marketing teams running a portfolio of branded events: Bizzabo.
  • Sponsor-heavy events where exhibitor ROI decides renewal: Swapcard.
  • Community-style summits that lean on networking: Airmeet.
  • Companies standardized on Zoom: Zoom Events.
  • Teams that want one product from webinar to multi-stage event: RingCentral Events.
  • Content-led B2B events tied tightly to marketing automation: ON24 or Goldcast.

If the event is really a single session with Q&A, a webinar tool is cheaper and simpler; see our guide to webinar platforms for B2B lead generation.

How does a 100-person summit differ from a multi-track event?

A 100-person summit is a produced show with a single agenda and light networking, so the platform mainly needs a good stage, a lobby and simple registration. A multi-track event adds parallel sessions, personal agendas, sponsor booths, matchmaking and ticket types, and each of those adds data, configuration and staff time.

Consider two hypothetical programs. A customer summit for 100 invited accounts needs a branded landing page, two or three sessions in sequence, a chat-based lounge and a clean list of who attended what. A partner conference for several thousand registrants needs track filtering, session capacity rules, sponsor lead retrieval, meeting scheduling and segmented post-event follow-up. The first can run on a lighter tool with a producer and one operations owner. The second usually needs a project manager, a production partner and a sponsor manager, whatever platform you choose.

The cost gap follows the same line. Lighter platforms tend to offer self-serve or per-event plans, while enterprise platforms are generally sold on annual contracts through sales, so get quotes early if you are planning the larger format.

Which virtual event platforms suit which B2B events, and what are their limits?

Cvent, Bizzabo and Swapcard handle the most complex multi-track programs, and they cost the most in money and setup. Airmeet, Zoom Events and RingCentral Events cover smaller multi-session events with less overhead, while ON24 is strongest when content and lead data matter more than networking.

Cvent: best for large programs that span virtual and in-person

Cvent is an event management suite with registration, onsite tools and an attendee app. Its support documentation describes the Attendee Hub event app, and its June 2026 release notes cover hybrid check-in and attendance counting. It suits teams that run many events a year and want one system of record.

Strengths: depth across registration, agenda and reporting. Limitations: admin complexity and quote-only pricing. Skip it if you run two or three virtual events a year.

Bizzabo: best for a portfolio of branded events

Bizzabo sells an event platform covering virtual, in-person and hybrid formats, with in-person tooling alongside its virtual experience. It fits marketing teams that want consistent branding and data across a calendar of events.

Strengths: portfolio management and branded experiences. Limitations: enterprise pricing and setup for small events. Skip it if you need a quick, self-serve summit.

Swapcard: best when sponsors and exhibitors fund the event

Swapcard focuses on matchmaking and exhibitor value. Its exhibitor and sponsor tools page describes exhibitor lead capture, and its hybrid event page describes AI-suggested meetings for both online and in-room attendees.

Strengths: exhibitor lead retrieval and meeting scheduling. Limitations: value depends on sponsors actually using it. Skip it if your event has no sponsors.

Airmeet: best for networking-led summits

Airmeet runs in the browser and offers webinar, multi-session and hybrid formats with booths and a social lounge. It suits community-style events where conversation matters as much as content.

Strengths: social and networking features, no attendee download. Limitations: Airmeet's own pages describe capacity differently, so confirm limits in writing. Skip it if you need deep onsite operations.

Zoom Events: best for companies already on Zoom

Zoom Events is Zoom's multi-session product, distinct from Zoom Webinars; the vendor's event type comparison sets out its multi-session and single-session formats. Familiarity lowers the support burden for speakers and attendees.

Strengths: recognizable experience and reliable streaming. Limitations: sponsor and exhibitor tooling is lighter than event-first suites. Skip it if sponsors are a major revenue line.

RingCentral Events: best for one product across formats

RingCentral Events came from the acquisition of Hopin's events business and is marketed for virtual, onsite and hybrid events. Confirm on its pricing page which plan includes the hybrid features you need.

Strengths: range from webinar to multi-stage events. Limitations: plan tiers gate hybrid features. Skip it if you only need a single-stage webinar.

ON24: best for content-led events that feed pipeline

ON24's virtual event capability extends its webinar platform into multi-session experiences, and its engagement data integrates with marketing automation.

Strengths: engagement data and content reuse. Limitations: networking is not its centre of gravity. Skip it if peer networking is the main reason people attend.

Will attendees actually network on a virtual event platform?

Based on the product information reviewed, every platform here offers networking features, but none can guarantee attendees will use them. Uptake usually depends on how the agenda makes time for it and whether someone hosts the conversations, which is an experience-design job rather than a software setting.

Design choices that matter more than the feature list:

  • Schedule networking blocks rather than leaving a lounge open all day.
  • Seed matchmaking with two or three profile questions at registration.
  • Give sponsors a defined slot and a reason for attendees to visit, such as a session follow-up resource.
  • Assign a host for each networking table or room.

What should you check on accessibility, integrations and analytics?

Check that captions, keyboard navigation and screen-reader support work in the attendee view, not just in a marketing claim, and ask each vendor for its accessibility documentation. On data, confirm which engagement events sync to your CRM at session level, because a single "attended" flag tells sales very little after a multi-track event.

  • Accessibility: live captions, caption languages, contrast, keyboard access and whether recordings keep captions.
  • Integrations: native CRM and marketing automation sync, plus whether session attendance and sponsor leads sync separately. Our guide to event marketing automation integrations goes deeper.
  • Sponsor data: how exhibitors export leads, and what consent attendees give. See event lead capture apps for exhibitors and sponsors.
  • Support: whether live production support is included or sold separately for event day.

What mistakes do B2B teams make when choosing a virtual event platform?

Teams most often buy for the biggest event they might run instead of the events they actually run. They also underestimate production: a multi-track virtual event needs rehearsals, a run-of-show and speaker support that no platform supplies by default.

  • Signing an annual contract before mapping the year's event calendar.
  • Assuming sponsors will self-serve booth setup without a sponsor manager.
  • Measuring registrations instead of session attendance, meetings booked and pipeline influenced.
  • Leaving post-event follow-up until the recordings are edited.

What else do buyers ask about virtual event platforms for B2B?

Common follow-ups cover contracts, attendee limits and recordings. The answers below are general; contract terms vary by vendor and deal.

Should we sign an annual contract or pay per event?

Pay per event if you run one or two virtual events a year. An annual contract usually only makes sense once you have a predictable calendar of several events.

Is a virtual event platform the same as a webinar platform?

No. Webinar platforms are built for single sessions and short funnels, while virtual event platforms add multiple tracks, networking, sponsors and ticketing.

Do attendees need to download anything?

Many platforms run in the browser for attendees, but some features such as mobile apps or desktop clients may be optional or required. Check the attendee requirements page for each vendor.

How do sponsors get their leads?

Most event-first platforms let exhibitors capture leads from booth visits, meetings or scans and export them. Confirm what attendees consent to share and how the export reaches sponsor CRMs.

Can we reuse session recordings after the event?

Usually yes, either on the platform as on-demand content or by downloading files. Check whether captions, chapters and engagement tracking carry over to the replay.
