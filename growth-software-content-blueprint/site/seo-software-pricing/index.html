SEO Software Pricing: What an Agency Pays for 5, 10, and 20 Clients

SEO software pricing for an agency is set less by the headline plan than by the limits around it: extra seats, project caps, tracked keywords, report allowances, local locations and add-ons. This guide prices those limits for three hypothetical agency sizes, using list prices from official vendor pages.

What actually drives an agency's SEO software bill?

Seats and capacity limits drive the bill more than the base plan does. An agency that adds clients and staff usually hits a user, project or keyword cap first, and the extra seats that follow can cost more than the plan itself.

Most stacks combine a tiered suite, a local tool priced by location band or scan credits, a per-user crawler licence and, at scale, a quote-only tier. For product fit rather than cost, start with our SEO platform shortlist by team type or the wider SEO software hub.

What do the main vendors charge?

Semrush One starts at $199 a month, Ahrefs Lite at $129 and SE Ranking Core at $129, all on monthly billing. What separates them for an agency is how many websites, keywords and users each tier includes, and what each extra user costs.

Semrush

Semrush now sells its main SEO plans as Semrush One. The SEO and AI Search pricing page lists Starter at $199 a month with 5 websites and 500 tracked keywords, and Pro+ at $299 with 15 websites and 1,500 keywords (checked 5 November 2026). Semrush's user management help page prices additional users at $45 a month on Starter and $80 on Pro+ (checked 5 November 2026). Semrush pages show Advanced both as a fixed price and as "Custom", and the standalone SEO Toolkit's prices also vary between pages, so check both on the pricing page.

Ahrefs

The Ahrefs pricing page lists Lite at $129 a month, Standard at $249, Advanced at $449 and Enterprise at $1,499 (checked 5 November 2026). Its plan comparison caps additional users at 2 on Lite, 5 on Standard and 10 on Advanced (checked 5 November 2026). On Lite, which is credit-based, an extra user costs $20 a month at casual usage and $40 at power usage (checked 5 November 2026). The per-user price on Standard and Advanced wasn't confirmed. Ahrefs' help center says tracked keywords update weekly by default on all plans (checked 5 November 2026), which matters if clients expect daily movement.

SE Ranking

SE Ranking's subscription page lists Core at $129 a month with 10 projects, 2,000 daily-tracked keywords and 1 manager seat, and Growth at $279 with 30 projects, 5,000 keywords and 3 seats (checked 5 November 2026). The same page lists the Agency Pack at $69 a month on annual billing only, adding white label and 30 client seats (checked 5 November 2026). Extra manager seats are sold separately, but SE Ranking's pages give different seat prices, so check the current one. The older Essential, Pro and Business plans are closed to new subscriptions (checked 5 November 2026).

Moz Pro and the tools outside the suite

Moz Pro uses tiered plans with campaign and tracked-keyword limits; its prices weren't confirmed for this draft, so check Moz Pro pricing. AgencyAnalytics prices client reporting by the number of clients, but its official pages show conflicting figures, so none is quoted here.

  • BrightLocal prices by location-count tier, and its help center lists Track at $62 a month for 2–5 locations and $83 for 6–10 on monthly billing (checked 5 November 2026). That price covers the whole band, not each location.
  • Local Falcon sells geo-grid scan credits; its pricing page lists Starter at $24.99 a month for 7,500 credits and Basic at $49.99 for 15,150 (checked 5 November 2026).
  • Screaming Frog SEO Spider is licensed per user per year; its pricing page lists $279 a year for one licence (checked 5 November 2026), with lower per-licence prices from five licences up.

What do 5, 10 and 20-client agencies need?

A five-client agency needs about two logins and 500 tracked keywords. A twenty-client agency needs about eight logins and 4,000 keywords, and that change in seats and capacity, not the client count itself, moves you between tiers.

The three profiles below are hypothetical. They assume about 100 tracked keywords and one monthly report per client, plus a share of clients with a physical location. Replace each figure with your own.

  • Profile 1: 5 clients, 2 people, about 500 keywords, 2 local clients.
  • Profile 2: 10 clients, 4 people, about 1,500 keywords, 4 local clients.
  • Profile 3: 20 clients, 8 people, about 4,000 keywords, 10 local clients.

The method is the same for every vendor. Pick the lowest tier where projects and keywords fit, add (logins − included users) × the extra-user price, then add white-label reporting, local tools, overage packs and any crawler licence. Add sales tax for your region, which list prices usually exclude.

What does each profile cost?

At five clients, the confirmed suite figures run from $149–$169 on Ahrefs Lite to $244 on Semrush One Starter before local tools, and the gap comes mostly from the second seat. At twenty clients, the question is whether a self-serve plan still fits at all, because project and user caps push you up a tier or into a quote.

Profile 1: five clients, two people

Every suite's entry tier fits the projects and keywords, so the second login decides the ranking. A Semrush One Starter stack with BrightLocal Track and one crawler licence comes to about $329 a month ($244 + $62 + $23.25). The same stack on Ahrefs Lite comes to about $234–$254, but with weekly rather than daily rank updates by default.

Profile 2: ten clients, four people

Seats are now the biggest line. On Semrush One Pro+, three extra users add $240, almost as much as the plan. On SE Ranking, compare three extra seats on Core with Growth plus one seat, because Growth also leaves room for prospect projects that Core's 10 doesn't.

Profile 3: twenty clients, eight people

Twenty projects outgrow Semrush One Pro+, and eight people outgrow Ahrefs' user caps on Lite and Standard, so the realistic options are Semrush Advanced, Ahrefs Advanced or SE Ranking Growth with extra seats. Get any Advanced or enterprise quote in writing, with renewal terms, before you compare it with a self-serve plan plus add-ons. Should you pay monthly or annually?

Pay monthly until your client count has been stable for a quarter, then move to annual billing if the discount is meaningful. Annual plans lock in capacity you may not use if a large client leaves.

  • Semrush says annual billing saves up to 17%, paid upfront; the pricing page lists Starter at $165.17 and Pro+ at $248.17 a month billed annually (both checked 5 November 2026).
  • SE Ranking lists Core at $103.20 and Growth at $223.20 a month billed annually, a 20% saving (both checked 5 November 2026). Because the Agency Pack is annual-only, white-label reporting on Core works out at about $172.20 a month ($103.20 + $69) before extra seats.
  • Ahrefs explains its yearly discount in a help article on how "2 months free" works on a yearly plan (checked 5 November 2026). Ahrefs pages disagree on whether Enterprise can be paid monthly, so confirm before you budget for it.
  • Local Falcon lists Starter at $299.88 a year for 90,000 credits (checked 5 November 2026), the same price and credit total as twelve monthly payments. The gain from annual billing is that the credits last the year rather than resetting monthly.

Before you switch, check whether add-on prices change on annual billing and whether you can upgrade mid-term without losing the discount.

What pushes you into the next tier?

The usual triggers are the project or website cap, the extra-user cap, the keyword allowance and the need for daily rather than weekly rank updates. Map your growth plan against those four limits before you sign.

  • Project caps: Semrush One Starter's 5 websites fit exactly five clients, so the first prospect or sixth client forces Pro+. Count prospect and competitor projects too.
  • User caps: Ahrefs' caps of 2, 5 and 10 extra users mean headcount alone can force a plan change.
  • Keyword allowances: at 100 keywords per client, Semrush One Starter's 500 keywords run out at five clients, while SE Ranking Core's 10 projects run out well before its 2,000 keywords do.
  • Update frequency: if clients expect daily movement and your tool updates weekly by default, that's a product gap as well as a cost issue.

Will your real invoice match the model?

Probably not exactly, because taxes, currency, promotional first-year pricing and mid-term upgrades all move the number. The model is most useful for spotting which limit you'll hit first, not for predicting the invoice to the dollar.

What isn't public, and what should you ask sales?

Enterprise tiers, volume discounts, renewal pricing and some per-user prices are quote-only or inconsistent across vendor pages. Ask for them in writing, because they decide what you'll pay in year two.

  • Which limits are per account, and which are per user or per project?
  • Are extra users and report add-ons priced the same on annual billing?
  • Can client viewers access reports without a paid seat?
  • Is our first-year price promotional, and what does renewal cost?

What should a budget planning checklist include?

One row per client, with sites, tracked keywords, update frequency, locations, competitors, report format and logins. Total each column, then compare the totals against every plan's caps before you look at price.

What else do agencies ask about SEO software pricing?

Most questions are about how current the figures are, seats and whether cheaper suites hold up at scale. The short answers are below.

How current are the prices on this page?

Each figure was matched against a search index of the vendor's official pricing or help page on 5 November 2026, because the pages themselves could not be fetched directly. Treat them as a starting point and confirm on the linked page before you buy.

Is the cheapest suite the best value for a small agency?

Not automatically. Once extra seats, branded reports and update frequency are included, the cheapest entry plan can cost more than a rival whose higher tier includes those items.

Do client viewers need paid seats?

It depends on the vendor. SE Ranking's Agency Pack adds view-only client seats, while other tools count every login, so ask before you budget for client access.

When does an enterprise quote make sense?

When your projects or users exceed the largest self-serve tier, or when you need contract terms such as invoicing, security reviews or guaranteed renewal pricing.

How often should you re-check these numbers?

Re-check every 60 to 90 days, and straight after any announced plan change. SEO vendors rename tiers and move limits often: Semrush moved to Semrush One and SE Ranking retired its Essential, Pro and Business plans, so an older budget may already be wrong.
