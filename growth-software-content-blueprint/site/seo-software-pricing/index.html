SEO Software Pricing: What an Agency Pays for 5, 10, and 20 Clients

SEO software pricing for an agency is set less by the headline plan than by the limits around it: extra seats, project caps, tracked keywords, report allowances, local locations and add-ons. This guide turns those limits into budget scenarios you can fill in with today's list prices.

What actually drives an agency's SEO software bill?

Seats and capacity limits drive the bill far more than the base plan does. An agency that adds clients and staff usually hits a user, project or report cap first, and the upgrade or add-on that follows can cost as much as the plan itself.

That's why this page doesn't copy headline monthly prices into a list. It models what three agency sizes consume, then shows where each consumption line meets a vendor limit. For product fit rather than cost, start with our SEO platform shortlist by team type or the wider SEO software hub.

How is SEO software priced?

Most agency SEO spend falls into five pricing models: tiered suites with usage caps, per-client reporting platforms, per-location local tools, per-licence desktop software and quote-only enterprise tiers. Your stack's total cost depends on how many of these models you combine.

  • Tiered suites. Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking and Moz Pro bundle projects, tracked keywords, crawl budgets and report allowances into plans, then charge for extra users or capacity.
  • Per-client reporting. Client reporting platforms such as AgencyAnalytics generally scale with the number of clients or campaigns connected.
  • Per-location local tools. Local platforms such as BrightLocal price by location or location band, while grid trackers such as Local Falcon tend to sell scan credits.
  • Per-licence desktop software. Crawlers such as Screaming Frog SEO Spider sell annual licences per user.
  • Quote-only enterprise tiers. Most suites have one, usually with an annual commitment.

How do the main vendors structure their plans?

The suites differ mainly in how they count projects, how they charge for extra users and how often they update rankings. Those three differences, not the entry price, decide which one is cheapest for your agency profile.

  • Semrush sells tiers defined by how many websites you can monitor and how many keywords you can track, with additional users and white-label report options sold as extras. The project step between tiers is the main upgrade trigger for agencies. See Semrush's plans page.
  • Ahrefs counts projects differently for sites you've verified and sites you haven't, which matters if you analyse prospects.   • SE Ranking positions itself on lower per-project cost, with extra seats, keyword packs, local locations and a white-label agency option sold as add-ons. Check whether the agency option requires annual billing. See SE Ranking's subscription page.

What do 5, 10 and 20-client agencies need?

A five-client agency typically needs two logins and a few hundred tracked keywords. A twenty-client agency needs eight or more logins and several thousand keywords, and that change in scale, not the client count, moves you between pricing tiers.

How do you calculate base and realistic monthly cost?

Base cost is the cheapest plan whose caps fit your projects and keywords. Realistic cost adds every extra user, report add-on, local location and keyword pack you need on top, then converts annual-only items to a monthly equivalent.

  1. Base: find the lowest tier where projects and tracked keywords both fit, at the update frequency your clients expect.
  2. Seats: (paid logins − users included) × the vendor's extra-user price for that tier. Check whether that tier caps how many users you can add.
  3. Reporting: add the white-label or report add-on if branded reports aren't included, or if your report count exceeds the allowance.
  4. Local: add per-location or credit costs for the clients that need map or grid data.
  5. Overages: add keyword packs or usage credits if a busy month would exceed the plan.
  6. Outside tools: add any reporting platform, local platform or crawler licence the suite doesn't replace.

Realistic monthly cost = base + seats + reporting + local + overages + outside tools. Then add tax for your region, which vendor pages usually exclude.

How do the three profiles play out?

At five clients, the cost difference between suites comes mostly from extra seats and white-label reporting. At twenty, it comes from whether a self-serve plan still fits at all, because project and user caps can push you into a quote.

Profile 1: five clients, two people

An entry tier usually fits the projects and keywords. The cost to check is the second login, plus whatever branded reporting costs on that tier. A plan that looks cheapest can lose that lead once a seat and a report add-on are included, so price all three lines before you choose.

Profile 2: ten clients, four people

This is where report allowances start to bite. Ten branded reports a month can exceed an included allowance, and three extra users can cost more than the plan itself. Compare a mid-tier plan with add-ons against the next tier up, which may include more of both.

Profile 3: twenty clients, eight people

Twenty projects and eight users can exceed both the project cap and the extra-user cap on self-serve tiers. Expect at least one vendor to route you to an enterprise or custom quote. Get that quote in writing, with renewal terms, before you compare it with a self-serve plan plus add-ons.

Should you pay monthly or annually?

Pay monthly until your client count has been stable for a quarter, then move to annual billing if the discount is meaningful. Annual plans lock in capacity you may not use if a large client leaves.

Most suites advertise a discount for annual billing, and some agency add-ons are sold on annual terms only. Check two things on each pricing page: whether add-on prices change on annual billing, and whether you can switch plans mid-term without losing the discount.

What pushes you into the next tier?

The usual triggers are the project or website cap, the extra-user cap, the report allowance and the need for daily rather than weekly rank updates. Map your growth plan against those four limits before you sign.

  • Project caps: the client that takes you one project past the limit forces an upgrade, so count prospect and competitor projects too.
  • User caps: some tiers limit how many extra users you can add, so headcount growth can force a plan change on its own.
  • Report allowances: branded reports often have a monthly ceiling.
  • Update frequency: if clients expect daily movement and your tier updates weekly, that's a product gap as well as a cost issue.

Will your real invoice match the model?

Probably not exactly, because taxes, currency, promotional first-year pricing and mid-term upgrades all move the number. The model is most useful for spotting which limit you'll hit first, not for predicting the invoice to the dollar.

What isn't public, and what should you ask sales?

Enterprise tiers, volume discounts, renewal pricing and some add-on terms are usually quote-only. Ask for them in writing, because they decide what you'll pay in year two.

  • Which limits are per account, and which are per user or per project?
  • Are extra users and report add-ons priced the same on annual billing?
  • What happens to rank history and reports if we downgrade or cancel?
  • Can client viewers access reports without a paid seat?
  • Are overages billed automatically, and can we cap them?
  • Is our first-year price promotional, and what does renewal cost?

What should a budget planning checklist include?

One row per client, with sites, tracked keywords, update frequency, locations, competitors, report format and logins. Total each column, then compare the totals against every plan's caps before you look at price.

What else do agencies ask about SEO software pricing?

Most questions are about seats, trials and whether cheaper suites hold up at scale. The short answers are below.

Why don't you list current prices on this page?

The vendors' pricing pages could not be fetched when this draft was prepared, and we only publish prices we have confirmed on the official page. Each vendor's pricing page is linked so you can check today's figures.

Is the cheapest suite the best value for a small agency?

Not automatically. Once extra seats, branded reports and update frequency are included, the cheapest entry plan can end up costing more than a rival whose higher tier includes those items.

Do client viewers need paid seats?

It depends on the vendor. Some allow shared or read-only report links, while others count every login, so ask before you budget for client access.

When does an enterprise quote make sense?

When your projects or users exceed the largest self-serve tier, or when you need contract terms such as invoicing, security reviews or guaranteed renewal pricing.

How often should you re-check these numbers?

Re-check every 60 to 90 days, and straight after any announced plan change. SEO vendors rename tiers and move limits often, so a budget built six months ago may already be wrong.
