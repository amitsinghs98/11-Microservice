How to Choose Email Marketing Software by List Size and Send Volume

To choose email marketing software, start with two numbers before you look at any feature list: how many active contacts you'll email, and how many emails you'll send each month. Those two figures decide which pricing model is cheaper, and your automation and integration needs then narrow the shortlist.

Why don't feature lists settle the decision?

Nearly every platform offers an editor, forms, segments and some automation, so feature grids rarely separate them. What does separate them is how the bill grows with your list and cadence, how deep automation goes before a tier wall, and how hard it is to leave.

A 2,000-contact list emailed once a month and the same list emailed three times a week are different workloads. They can belong on different platforms, or on different tiers of the same one.

What are the steps?

Count active contacts, estimate monthly sends, match them to a pricing model, rate your automation needs and list the integrations you can't live without. Then price the shortlist at today's size and at your 12-month forecast.

Step 1: count active contacts, not total records

Export your list and remove unsubscribed, hard-bounced and never-confirmed addresses. What's left is closer to what most vendors bill on, but definitions differ, so ask each vendor how it counts. Then forecast 12 months ahead: a list growing by 150 a month adds 1,800 contacts in a year, which is enough to cross a pricing tier or two.

Step 2: estimate monthly sends

Monthly sends = active contacts × campaigns per month + automated emails. Automated volume is easy to forget: a five-email welcome series for 200 new subscribers adds 1,000 sends a month on its own.

Step 3: match the pricing model

Use the two numbers to see which model favors you. The next section explains the trade-off with current examples.

Step 4: rate your automation complexity

Level 1 is broadcasts plus one welcome sequence. Level 2 adds several sequences with tags, simple branches and date triggers. Level 3 is event-driven journeys using purchase, product or CRM data. Level 1 fits almost any tool; Level 3 narrows you to the platforms in our lifecycle email automation shortlist, usually on a mid-tier plan.

Step 5: list integrations and migration effort

Write down every system that must sync, such as your store, CRM, forms, webinar tool and billing, with the direction of sync and the fields involved. Then list what you'd have to rebuild if you moved: automations, templates, segments and form embeds. Automations almost never transfer between platforms.

Is contact-based or volume-based pricing cheaper for you?

Contact-based pricing usually favors small lists you email often, and volume-based pricing favors large lists you email rarely. The crossover depends on the send allowance each contact tier includes.

Contact-based plans charge by list size and attach a send allowance. Mailchimp sets monthly sends at 10 times your contact limit on Essentials, 12 times on Standard and 15 times on Premium (checked 4 November 2026), and its pricing page notes that overages apply if you exceed limits. MailerLite's Comfort plan allows 10 times your subscriber tier each month, while its Power plan lists unlimited monthly emails under a fair-use policy (checked 4 November 2026). Kit prices paid plans by subscriber count and states no sending limits on any plan (checked 4 November 2026).

Volume-based plans charge mainly for emails sent. Brevo's Starter plan starts at $9 a month for 5,000 emails, and its free plan caps sending at 300 emails a day (checked 4 November 2026). This model rewards a large list that hears from you once a month.

Seats are the third cost axis. Agencies and teams with several editors should check whether extra users cost more. Our marketing automation pricing guide explains how contact, send, seat and event charges combine.

What does this look like for a real list?

In this hypothetical, a consultancy with 2,600 active contacts and a fortnightly newsletter needs about 5,500 sends a month, so the integration requirement, not price, decides the shortlist. The numbers below show how to get there.

The consultancy has 3,400 records; after removing unsubscribes and bounces, 2,600 are active. It sends two newsletters a month (5,200 sends) and a four-email onboarding series to about 80 new subscribers (320 sends), so it needs roughly 5,520 sends a month, rising to about 6,500 within a year. On a contact-based plan with a 10x multiplier, a 3,000-contact tier would allow 30,000 sends, far more than it needs. On a volume plan, it would buy around 6,000 to 7,000 emails a month.

Both models work at this size, so the real filter is a native two-way sync with its CRM. If it later wants CRM-driven journeys, the HubSpot Marketing Hub versus ActiveCampaign comparison is worth reading first.

Readers often doubt that a spreadsheet exercise predicts the bill well. The arithmetic shows the tier you'll start on, but not how quickly real engagement and list decay move you between tiers.

What usually goes wrong?

The common mistakes are pricing at today's list size, forgetting automated sends and paying to keep contacts who stopped engaging long ago. Underestimating migration is the most expensive one.

  • Pricing for this month's list instead of next year's.
  • Checking a send allowance against campaigns only.
  • Paying for contacts who haven't opened or clicked in a year, when a sunset policy would cost less than a tier upgrade.
  • Choosing a newsletter-first tool and later needing store events; ecommerce teams should start with our guide to ecommerce email platforms.
  • Budgeting no time to rebuild automations and re-test forms after a move.

What else do buyers ask about choosing a platform?

Most remaining questions are about list growth, forecasting and transactional email. The answers below are short.

Does a bigger list always mean a bigger bill?

On contact-based plans, yes. On volume-based plans, only if you email the bigger list more often. Either way, removing long-inactive contacts lowers cost and usually improves engagement.

How far ahead should I forecast?

Twelve months is a sensible horizon. It covers an annual contract and usually captures at least one tier change.

Should transactional email run through the same platform?

It can, but many teams separate it so receipts and password resets aren't affected by marketing reputation or marketing send limits.
