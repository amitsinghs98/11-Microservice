Which client onboarding software should an agency choose?

Pick the tool that removes your slowest onboarding step, which is usually chasing access or chasing files. Then make sure it hands off cleanly to the project tool where delivery actually happens.

  • Collecting brand assets, copy and documents without email chases: Content Snare.
  • Getting access to ad accounts, analytics and Search Console without passwords: Leadsie.
  • Small agencies wanting forms, contracts, invoices and workflows in one place: Dubsado or HoneyBook.
  • Structured, multi-step onboarding with approvals for larger accounts: Moxo.
  • A white-labelled portal plus CRM on a budget, if you'll invest in setup: SuiteDash.
  • Flexible intake forms that feed your existing tools: Jotform.

Why does agency onboarding break down?

Onboarding stalls when nobody owns it and when requests to the client arrive in pieces across several emails. Work starts late, the client feels the agency is disorganised, and the first month's results slip.

The typical gaps are predictable: the scope wasn't confirmed in writing, admin access to GA4 or the ad account took weeks, brand guidelines arrived after the first draft, and the kickoff agenda didn't settle who approves what. Software helps by turning those into a tracked checklist with reminders, but only if the checklist reflects your actual service.

What does each onboarding tool do well, and what does it miss?

Narrow tools do one job very well, and broad tools trade depth for coverage. Most agencies get the best result from one collector and one system of record.

Content Snare: best for collecting content and documents

Content Snare positions itself around requesting specific files and answers from clients, tracking what's missing and sending automatic reminders. That directly attacks the "we're still waiting for the logo" delay. It doesn't manage contracts, invoices or delivery tasks. Skip it if your onboarding needs very little from the client. See the Content Snare pricing page.

Leadsie: best for platform access without passwords

Leadsie positions itself around a single link through which a client grants access to platforms such as ad accounts and analytics properties. For paid media and SEO agencies, access is often the longest wait, so this is a high-leverage fix. It covers access only. Skip it if your services don't depend on client platform accounts. Confirm supported platforms on the Leadsie pricing page.

Dubsado: best for small agencies running the whole client flow

Dubsado is positioned as a client management tool spanning forms, proposals, contracts, invoices and automated workflows; confirm which features each plan includes. It suits solo consultants and small agencies who want one place from enquiry to kickoff. Workflows take time to design, and it isn't a delivery project tool. Skip it if you already run proposals and billing elsewhere. See the Dubsado pricing page.

HoneyBook: best for proposal-to-payment onboarding

HoneyBook is positioned across a similar span to Dubsado, framing proposals, contracts, invoices and automations as a client flow. It's oriented towards independent businesses and small teams. Larger agencies should check multi-user permissions and reporting carefully. Skip it if you need deep integration with a project management tool. Plans are on the HoneyBook pricing page.

Moxo: best for structured, multi-party onboarding

Moxo frames onboarding as a workflow of steps involving the client, such as document collection, forms, approvals and e-signature, inside a portal. That suits agencies onboarding larger clients where several stakeholders must sign off. Skip it if a checklist and a form would do. See the Moxo pricing page.

SuiteDash: best for an all-in-one white-label portal

SuiteDash is positioned as a branded client portal bundled with CRM and wider business tools; check the plan-level feature list. The breadth is the attraction and the risk: it can replace several tools, but expect a significant configuration effort. Skip it if you want something live this week. See the SuiteDash pricing page.

Jotform: best for flexible intake forms

Jotform is a general-purpose online form builder; confirm the logic, upload and integration options on your plan. It's an easy way to standardise an intake questionnaire that feeds your CRM or project tool. It won't track the wider onboarding checklist. Skip it if your existing tools already have adequate forms. See the Jotform pricing page.

How do you collect client access safely?

Request named user or partner access inside each platform rather than shared logins, and record who was granted what. That way you can remove access cleanly when a contract ends or a team member leaves.

  • Least privilege: ask for the lowest permission level that lets you do the work.
  • No passwords in forms or email: where a platform has no user roles, use a password manager's sharing; see our guide to password management tools for digital agencies.
  • Access register: log each platform, account ID, permission level, granting contact and date.
  • Two-factor authentication: confirm how codes will reach your team without depending on one person's phone.
  • Offboarding: schedule an access review at contract end.

What should the first 30 days look like?

Split the first month into collection, kickoff and first delivery, each with a named owner and a date. Clients judge the whole relationship on whether that first deliverable lands when promised.

  1. Days 1 to 5: signed scope confirmed, intake form sent, access and asset requests issued, welcome message with named contacts.
  2. Days 5 to 10: kickoff meeting covering goals, approval rules, reporting cadence and communication channels.
  3. Days 10 to 20: audits and baseline measurement; first plan shared for approval.
  4. Days 20 to 30: first deliverable shipped and a short review of what's still outstanding.

Turn this into a reusable template in your delivery tool; our guide to project management software for agencies compares tools with strong templates. If clients will work from a branded space afterwards, compare client portal software.

Does onboarding software actually speed up kickoff?

It speeds up the parts that are pure chasing, such as files and access, but it can't make a client prioritise you. Based on the product information reviewed, reminders and completion tracking are the features most likely to shorten the wait.

What mistakes do agencies make with onboarding tools?

The most common mistake is buying a broad platform and recreating a messy process inside it. The second is sending clients a long form with questions you'll never use.

  • Starting work before the scope is signed.
  • Asking for passwords instead of platform access.
  • Running onboarding outside the delivery tool, so tasks never get created.
  • No clear definition of when onboarding is finished.

What else do agencies ask about client onboarding software?

The recurring questions cover access security, tool overlap and how long onboarding should take. Treat these as general guidance and confirm current features on each vendor's site.

Should clients send passwords during onboarding?

No. Ask clients to grant your agency user or partner access inside each platform, or use a password manager's secure sharing if a platform has no user roles. Shared passwords in email or forms are hard to revoke and impossible to audit.

Do we need a separate onboarding tool if our CRM has forms?

Not always. If your CRM or project tool can send a form, track completion and remind the client, start there; add a dedicated collection tool when chasing files and access takes more than a few emails per client.

How long should agency onboarding take?

Aim to collect everything you need for kickoff within the first week, then run the first 30 days against a written plan. The exact timeline depends on scope, but a fixed checklist stops it drifting.

Can onboarding software handle contracts and the first invoice?

Some client management tools include proposals, contracts and invoicing in one flow. Others don't, so you'd pair them with dedicated e-signature and invoicing tools.

What should trigger the end of onboarding?

Define completion in writing: signed scope, every access request approved, assets received, kickoff held and the first deliverable date confirmed. Once all are ticked, the account moves to normal delivery.

What's the verdict?

Start with the bottleneck: Content Snare for files, Leadsie for platform access, and Dubsado or HoneyBook if you need a full enquiry-to-invoice flow. Choose Moxo or SuiteDash only when larger clients or a branded portal justify the setup.
