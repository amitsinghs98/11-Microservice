Which B2B intent data platform should you choose?

Start with first-party signals you already own, website visits and product or content engagement, then add one third-party source whose taxonomy matches your category: Bombora for broad topic research, G2 Buyer Intent if you sell software. Buy a predictive platform such as 6sense or Demandbase only when you have the ops capacity to configure and audit it.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • Clearly defined third-party topic source: Bombora.
  • Software vendors: G2 Buyer Intent.
  • Predictive prioritization at scale: 6sense.
  • Intent inside a full ABM platform: Demandbase.
  • Intent attached to contacts: ZoomInfo.
  • HubSpot-native: HubSpot buyer intent.
  • First-party website intent, European focus: Dealfront.

What is the difference between first-party and third-party intent?

First-party intent is behaviour on properties you control, such as your website, emails, webinars and product. Third-party intent is research observed elsewhere, such as publisher networks or review sites, and attributed to an account.

First-party signals are more reliable but only cover accounts that already know you. Third-party signals find accounts earlier but carry more noise. How reliable are intent signals?

Intent signals are probabilistic hints that an organisation is researching a topic, not evidence that a buyer is ready to purchase. Their value depends on recency, topic coverage and how confidently activity is resolved to the right account.

  • Recency: a surge from six weeks ago is history. Ask how often scores refresh and how long a signal stays active.
  • Topic coverage: if the taxonomy has no topic close to your product, you'll be matching on adjacent noise.
  • Identity confidence: activity is typically tied to companies via IP ranges, cookies or logged-in data, and remote work makes IP matching less certain. Ask whether the vendor exposes a confidence level.
  • False precision: a score of 87 versus 82 implies an accuracy no vendor can show. Treat scores as bands: hot, warm, ignore.

What does each intent platform do well, and where does it fall short?

Order follows our criteria for account prioritization: how transparent the signal source is, how it resolves to accounts, and how easily it activates in your CRM. No vendor paid for inclusion or position.

Bombora: a publisher co-op source for third-party topic surges

Bombora runs a data co-op of B2B publisher and media sites and reports when an account researches a topic more than its usual baseline (its Company Surge product).

Pricing model: licensed data, priced by quote. Confirm terms, contract length and minimums with Bombora.

Strengths: A defined taxonomy and a clear source model, which makes signals easier to explain to sales.

Limitations: Surge is account-level research behaviour, not a person and not a purchase decision, and coverage depends on which sites your buyers read.

Skip it if: you need a ready-made workflow; raw surge data needs a CRM, ABM or ad tool to act on it.

G2 Buyer Intent: software vendors whose buyers compare on G2

G2 Buyer Intent reports organisations viewing your G2 profile, your category, competitors and comparison pages. That is high-relevance intent, but only for software sold to buyers who use G2.

Pricing model: sold as part of G2's paid seller packages; quote-based.

Strengths: Signals sit close to vendor evaluation, so they're easier to act on than broad topic research.

Limitations: Only covers G2 activity, and only matters if you sell software listed on G2.

Skip it if: you don't sell software or your category gets little G2 traffic.

6sense: mid-market and enterprise teams wanting predictive account prioritization

6sense combines intent signals, firmographic and technographic data with predictive models that estimate buying stage, and pushes prioritised accounts into CRM, ads and sales workflows. Check its pricing page for current packages.

Pricing model: quote-based platform packages; ask about contract length and which packages include the intent and predictive features.

Strengths: Turns many signals into one prioritised list with buying-stage estimates.

Limitations: Model outputs are hard to audit, so false precision is a real risk; reps may trust a stage label more than it deserves.

Skip it if: you can't commit to a platform contract or don't have the ops capacity to configure it.

Demandbase: ABM programmes that want intent inside an ad and account platform

Demandbase bundles account data, intent signals, account-based advertising and sales insights in one platform, aimed at structured ABM programmes.

Pricing model: quote-based and packaged by module; confirm contract length.

Strengths: Intent, targeting and ads in one place reduces stitching work.

Limitations: A platform buy, not a data feed; small teams may pay for modules they won't use.

Skip it if: you only want a signal feed into your CRM; also read our ABM platforms for small B2B teams guide.

ZoomInfo: sales teams that already buy contact data from ZoomInfo

ZoomInfo offers intent signals alongside its contact and company database, so prioritised accounts come with contacts to reach. Pricing is quote-based on its pricing page.

Pricing model: quote-based; ask whether intent is included or sold separately.

Strengths: Signal and contact data in one tool shortens the path to outreach.

Limitations: Easy to turn intent straight into cold outreach without validation, which burns accounts.

Skip it if: you aren't already buying a large contact database.

HubSpot buyer intent: HubSpot teams wanting intent without another vendor

HubSpot's Breeze Intelligence adds enrichment and buyer-intent views inside the CRM, combining website visits with research signals to surface companies showing interest.

Pricing model: tied to HubSpot plans and credits; packaging has changed recently, so check current terms.

Strengths: No integration project; accounts appear where reps already work.

Limitations: Less control over taxonomy and source detail than a dedicated intent provider.

Skip it if: you're not on HubSpot or need broad third-party topic coverage.

Dealfront: European teams starting from first-party website intent

Dealfront, which includes the former Leadfeeder product, identifies companies visiting your website and adds firmographic data, with a European data focus. Plans are on its pricing page.

Pricing model: tiered subscription; check what each plan limits on the pricing page.

Strengths: First-party signals from your own site are the most explainable intent you can get.

Limitations: Only sees accounts that already visit you; it won't surface research happening elsewhere.

Skip it if: you need third-party topic research across the wider web.

Does intent data actually lead to more pipeline?

No vendor page can show that for your market; the only credible answer is a controlled pilot against your own pipeline. Expect intent to improve prioritization at the margin rather than create demand on its own.

How do you run a pipeline-outcome validation pilot?

Split a matched list of target accounts into a treated group that gets intent-driven outreach and a holdout that doesn't, then compare meetings and opportunities after a fixed window. Agree the success metric before you see any data.

  1. Pick the outcome: qualified meetings or opportunities created, not clicks or score changes.
  2. Build a matched list of target accounts of similar size, industry and region, and randomly split it into treated and holdout groups.
  3. Activate signals only for the treated group: alert reps, adjust ads or prioritise sequences when an account surges.
  4. Run for a fixed window long enough to cover your typical time from first touch to opportunity, often one or two quarters.
  5. Compare rates, not counts, and check whether surging accounts were already in active deals, which would inflate results.
  6. Review signal quality: sample a few surging accounts and ask reps whether the topic made sense.

A hypothetical illustration: if both groups produce similar opportunity rates, the signal isn't adding value at your current price, however compelling the dashboard looks.

How should intent data be activated in sales and marketing?

Route intent into the systems where work happens, the CRM, ad audiences and sales sequences, and attach a recommended action to each signal. A dashboard nobody checks is the most common failure.

Give reps context (topic and timing) rather than a bare score, and use warm signals for advertising and nurture rather than cold calls. For signal-driven targeting, compare account-based advertising platforms; to find contacts once an account qualifies, see B2B contact databases and sales intelligence tools. To keep those signals attached to one account record instead of scattered across tools, a B2B customer data platform can act as the join point.

What else do buyers ask about B2B intent data?

Most questions concern accuracy, privacy and cost. Short answers follow.

Is intent data the same as a lead?

No. Intent data usually describes an organisation's research activity, not a named person asking to be contacted, so treat it as a reason to prioritise an account rather than as permission to cold-call someone.

How do I test an intent provider's accuracy before buying?

Ask for signals on accounts you know well, including closed-won deals and current opportunities, and check whether the topics and timing match what actually happened.

Is third-party intent data compliant with privacy laws?

That depends on how the vendor collects and processes data and where your prospects are. Review the vendor's privacy documentation with your legal adviser, particularly for EU and UK prospects.

How many intent topics should we track?

Fewer than you think. A handful of topics tied closely to the problem your product solves gives cleaner signals than dozens of broad category terms.

Can a small team use intent data?

Yes, if it starts with first-party signals and a single affordable source, and if someone owns turning signals into actions. Without that owner, intent data rarely changes outcomes.

What's the verdict?

Intent data is worth buying when it changes which accounts your team works this week and a holdout pilot shows a pipeline difference. Start with first-party signals and one transparent third-party source, and move to a predictive platform only when you can audit its outputs.
