Which SEO reporting tool should your agency choose?

If SEO is most of your work and you want rank tracking, connectors and white-label reports in one place, start with AgencyAnalytics. If you already track rankings elsewhere, pick a reporting layer by pricing model: Swydo charges by data source, DashThis by dashboard, Whatagraph through sales, and Looker Studio costs nothing but your team's time.

  • SEO-led agencies wanting one platform: AgencyAnalytics.
  • Agencies with in-house data skills: Looker Studio.
  • Many clients with few sources each: Swydo.
  • A small number of rich dashboards: DashThis.
  • Larger agencies wanting managed onboarding: Whatagraph.
  • Agencies already on SE Ranking or Semrush: their built-in reports, before buying another tool.

What should SEO reporting software do for a client agency?

It should produce the same trustworthy report every month without copying and pasting: live GA4 and Search Console data, rankings, your branding and a scheduled send. Everything beyond that, such as annotations and stakeholder summaries, is about helping clients understand why numbers moved.

  • Data connectors: native GA4, Search Console and rank tracking connections, plus whatever else clients pay you for.
  • Report automation: templates you build once and clone per client.
  • White-label options: your logo, colors, domain and email sender, not the vendor's.
  • User seats: staff and client logins, and whether clients count as users.
  • Scheduled delivery: PDF or live link on a fixed date.
  • Data freshness: how often each connector refreshes, and how long history is kept.
  • Annotations and summaries: space to explain algorithm updates, site changes and next steps.

What does each reporting tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Each entry names the agency it suits, what stands out and a reason to skip it. Plan details change often, so confirm white-label, seat and connector rules on each official page.

AgencyAnalytics: best all-in-one for SEO-led agencies

AgencyAnalytics combines client dashboards, scheduled reports and its own SEO tools, including rank tracking and site audits, with integrations for GA4, Search Console and many marketing platforms. It prices per client on tiered plans (see AgencyAnalytics pricing). Skip it if you only need a reporting layer over rank data you already pay for.

Looker Studio: best free option for teams with data skills

Looker Studio is Google's free dashboard tool, with native connectors for GA4 and Search Console. Non-Google sources usually need partner connectors, which are often paid. Skip it if nobody owns template maintenance; broken charts after a connector change land on the account manager.

Swydo: best for many clients with few sources each

Swydo builds scheduled reports and dashboards from connected marketing sources and charges by the number of data sources you connect (see Swydo pricing). Skip it if each client has a long tail of platforms, because every source counts.

DashThis: best for a small number of rich dashboards

DashThis offers prebuilt templates and white-label dashboards, priced by how many dashboards you run (see DashThis pricing). Skip it if you plan separate dashboards per channel per client; the count climbs fast.

Whatagraph: best for larger agencies wanting guided setup

Whatagraph focuses on cross-channel reporting and data blending, with plans that start through a sales conversation (see Whatagraph pricing). Skip it if you want to self-serve a trial this afternoon.

SE Ranking: best for agencies tracking rankings in SE Ranking

SE Ranking pairs rank tracking and audits with a report builder and white-label options aimed at agencies. Skip it if most of your report comes from non-SEO channels. Our SE Ranking vs Semrush comparison covers agency limits in detail.

Semrush reports: best for agencies already on Semrush

Semrush's report builder can combine its own data with GA4 and Search Console, and branding options depend on your plan and add-ons (see Semrush pricing). Skip it if clients expect fully white-labeled portals rather than branded PDFs.

What should a monthly SEO client report include?

Open with a short written summary and the two or three outcomes the client cares about, then show the evidence behind them and close with next month's plan. A good report fits on a few screens and answers "is this working?" before it shows a single chart.

  1. Summary: three sentences on what changed, why and what's next, written by a person.
  2. Outcomes: organic leads, sales or goal completions from GA4, against the same month last year.
  3. Search demand and visibility: Search Console clicks, impressions and top gaining or losing queries.
  4. Rankings: priority keyword groups, not hundreds of individual terms; see our rank tracking software for multi-client SEO.
  5. Pages: top landing pages and any that dropped sharply.
  6. Work delivered: fixes, content and links shipped, with annotations on the charts.
  7. Next month: three priorities and anything you need from the client.

What does SEO reporting cost for a 10-client agency?

Cost depends on the pricing unit: multiply the per-client, per-source or per-dashboard rate by your real usage, then add seats, connector fees and the hours spent fixing reports. We couldn't verify current list prices on 4 November 2026, so use the model below with figures from each vendor's page.

A hypothetical agency with 10 clients, each reported on GA4, Search Console and rankings, has these units to price:

  • Per-client tools (AgencyAnalytics): 10 client slots on the tier that includes the white-label features you need.
  • Per-source tools (Swydo): 30 data sources (10 × 3), plus any ad or social accounts.
  • Per-dashboard tools (DashThis): 10 dashboards if one per client, 20 if SEO and local are split.
  • Looker Studio: no licence fee, but price any partner connectors for rank data and the staff hours to build and maintain 10 reports.
  • Everyone: extra staff seats, client logins and the rank tracker if it isn't included.

How do you keep vanity metrics out of SEO reports?

Report the metrics a client would pay for, such as leads, revenue and qualified traffic to money pages, and show rankings and traffic only as evidence of why those moved. If a metric can't change a decision, drop it.

  • Group rankings by intent or service line instead of listing every keyword.
  • Separate branded and non-branded Search Console clicks.
  • Don't count total backlinks; report links earned that matter.
  • Annotate algorithm updates and site changes, so dips aren't mistaken for failure.
  • Link SEO outcomes to wider performance in your marketing analytics software, without claiming more credit than the data supports.

What else do agencies ask about SEO reporting software?

The usual follow-ups concern free options, client logins and data freshness. Short answers follow.

Is Looker Studio good enough for client SEO reporting?

It can be, especially for GA4 and Search Console data, but you take on template building, connector costs for other sources and the maintenance when something breaks.

Do clients need their own logins to reporting tools?

Not always. A scheduled PDF or shared live link suits most clients, while logins help larger clients who want to explore data between reports.

How fresh is the data in SEO reporting tools?

It varies by connector and vendor, and Search Console data itself arrives with a delay, so check refresh schedules before promising clients live numbers.

Should rankings still appear in SEO reports?

Yes, as supporting evidence grouped by topic or service line, but lead with outcomes such as leads and revenue so rankings are read in context.
