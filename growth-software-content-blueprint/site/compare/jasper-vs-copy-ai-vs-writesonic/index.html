Quick verdict by buyer type

Based on the product information reviewed on 4 November 2026, these platforms no longer overlap as much as their shared history suggests. Each now leads with a different workflow.

  • Choose Jasper if your main problem is keeping many writers, campaigns or brands on-voice. Its pricing page centres on Brand Voices, Knowledge assets, Audiences and, on the Business plan, style guide rules and admin controls.
  • Choose Copy.ai if you want cheap shared chat access now and may later automate sales and marketing workflows such as prospect research or account-based content. It calls itself a GTM AI platform, and its higher tiers are priced around workflow credits.
  • Choose Writesonic if your real question is whether ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews mention your brand, and you want article generation and site audits attached to that tracking.
  • Choose none of them if your team mainly needs drafting, editing and research help. A team plan for a general assistant may cover that for less. The section on when a general assistant is enough explains the trade-off.

How each platform positions itself now

Jasper: brand-governed marketing content

Jasper's pricing page frames the product around "Jasper IQ", its layer for brand context. That layer covers Brand Voices, multi-modal Knowledge assets, Audiences, a Style Guide and Visual Guidelines. The page also lists more than 100 pre-built marketing agents, an image suite, a Chrome and Edge extension, and integrations such as Webflow, Google Docs and Slack. On the Business plan, it adds GEO, translation and deep-research agents, a no-code agent builder (Jasper Studio) and Jasper Grid for running content at scale.

Copy.ai: go-to-market workflows

Copy.ai's pricing page sells two different things. The low-cost Chat plan gives a small team multi-model chat. The larger plans sell automated "Workflows" for sales and marketing processes, metered in workflow credits. The Enterprise tier adds guided implementation, API access, bulk workflow runs and more than 20 tech integrations. The page's case studies cover sales collateral and product-catalogue copy at volume, which shows the buyer it is aiming at.

Writesonic: SEO plus AI search visibility

Writesonic's pricing page now leads with AI search visibility. It tracks prompts and answers across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews on self-serve plans, and across nine AI platforms on Enterprise. Every plan also includes AI article generation, site audits, Google Search Console and WordPress integrations, and a set number of brand-voice "writing styles". It reads as an SEO and GEO platform that still writes, not as a general copywriting tool.

Workflow-by-workflow analysis

Brand governance across many writers

Jasper is the only one of the three whose pricing structure is built around brand context. Brand Voices, Knowledge, Audiences and, on Business, Style Guide rules shown in the editor are the main levers. In our editorial assessment, this suits a content lead who needs freelancers, regional marketers and product marketers to start from the same approved positioning. The catch is that the stronger controls (unlimited voices, style guide, role-based permissions) are on the custom-priced Business plan, not on Pro.

Writesonic's writing styles are useful but narrower, scoped to its article and SEO workflows. The Copy.ai pricing page we reviewed did not set out brand-voice limits per plan, so ask the vendor how brand context is stored and shared before relying on it.

Short-form and campaign copy

All three can produce ad variants, email drafts and landing-page sections, but none of them prices for that job alone any more. Jasper's agents and Remix feature cover channel adaptation directly. Copy.ai's Chat plan gives five people unlimited chat words across OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini models, which is the cheapest route here for a team that mostly needs quick copy. If short copy is your main use, our roundup of AI copywriting software for ads, landing pages and product copy covers tools more focused on that output.

GTM automation

This is Copy.ai's territory. The Workflows product is meant to chain steps such as researching an account, drafting personalised outreach and producing sales collateral. Credits are the meter. Note that the Chat plan does not list workflow credits; the first tier that does is Growth, at $1,000 per month billed $12,000 per year with 20,000 workflow credits per month (checked 4 November 2026). That is a large jump, and it is aimed at revenue organisations, not a three-person marketing team.

SEO content and AI search visibility

Writesonic is the natural pick when the brief is "write articles that help us show up in search and in AI answers". Its self-serve plans combine prompt tracking, 15 to 50 AI articles a month, site audits and Search Console and WordPress connections. Two caveats from its own comparison table: content optimisation is marked as available only on Enterprise, and sentiment analysis starts on Growth. Jasper's Business plan also lists GEO agents, but as one capability inside a broader content platform. For dedicated tracking tools, see our guide to GEO and AI search visibility tools, which also explains why a visibility score is not a ranking factor.

Onboarding and integrations

Jasper Pro is self-serve with a 7-day trial and product training. Business adds a dedicated customer success manager. Copy.ai's Enterprise tier advertises a guided "Jumpstart" implementation and integrations with GTM systems, which signals that workflow value depends on setup work. Writesonic connects to Search Console and WordPress on every plan, but GA4 and Looker Studio only on Enterprise, according to its comparison table. Budget onboarding time as well as subscription cost for any of the three.

Limitations of each platform

  • Jasper: Pro is effectively a one-person plan, so a team that wants shared documents and permissions needs a Business quote. Knowledge and Brand Voice caps on Pro (5 and 2) are tight for an agency running several clients.
  • Copy.ai: the gap between a $29-a-month Chat plan and a $1,000-a-month Growth plan leaves no middle tier for a small team that wants a few automated workflows. The pricing page explains credits only through collapsed FAQs, so ask for a written estimate of credits per workflow run.
  • Writesonic: self-serve plans track three AI platforms; the other six named on the page need Enterprise. Content optimisation, GA4 and user access control are also Enterprise-only in its comparison table. Its pricing page notes that a fair usage policy applies.

When a general assistant is enough

If nobody on the team needs governed brand assets, automated GTM workflows or AI answer tracking, a general assistant may do the drafting job. Anthropic's pricing page lists Claude Team standard seats at $20 per seat per month billed annually, or $25 billed monthly, for teams of 2 to 150 (checked 4 November 2026). For three people on annual billing, that is $720 a year, with shared Projects for reference material.

The trade-off is structure. A general assistant will not enforce a style guide, schedule prompt tracking or run a CRM-connected workflow on its own. Our ChatGPT and Claude comparison for marketing work covers which assistant suits research, long-form drafting and file handling.

Switching considerations

  • Export brand context first. Copy Brand Voice descriptions, Knowledge files, audience definitions and saved prompts into a plain document before cancelling. Nothing on these pricing pages promises a one-click migration format.
  • Rebuild workflows by hand. Copy.ai workflows and Jasper agents are platform-specific. Document each step's inputs and outputs so you can recreate them elsewhere.
  • Keep visibility baselines. If you leave Writesonic, export your prompt lists and historical answer data, because a new tracker will sample answers differently.
  • Check renewal dates. Annual plans are billed up front, so time a switch for renewal.

FAQs

Which of the three is cheapest for a small team?

On list price, Copy.ai's Chat plan: $24 per month billed annually for up to five seats (checked 4 November 2026). It includes chat only, though, not the workflow credits that define Copy.ai's higher tiers.

Does Writesonic still work as a general AI writer?

It still generates articles, with 15 to 50 a month on self-serve plans. But its pricing now centres on AI search visibility, so a team that only wants drafting help is paying for tracking it may not use.

Can several people share one Jasper Pro subscription?

Jasper's comparison table lists Pro as "Single user" and puts document collaboration, sharing and user management on Business. Plan for one Pro seat per person, or ask for a Business quote.

Do any of these platforms guarantee accurate output?

No. All three generate text that needs fact-checking and editing. Our AI content workflow with human editorial checks sets out where review steps belong.

Verdict

Treat this as three separate purchases. Jasper is a brand-governance purchase whose full value sits in a quote-based Business plan. Copy.ai is either a very cheap shared chat seat bundle or a substantial GTM automation commitment, with little in between. Writesonic is an AI search visibility purchase that also writes.
