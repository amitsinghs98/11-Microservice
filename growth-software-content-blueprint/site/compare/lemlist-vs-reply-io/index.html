Lemlist vs Reply.io: Multichannel Outreach for Sales Teams

Both vendors split their range into an email-only plan and a per-user multichannel plan. The billing units differ: lemlist counts emails sent, while Reply.io counts active contacts. That changes which one is cheaper for your team.

Quick verdict by buyer

  • Email-only team of several people: lemlist's Email plan is priced on volume with unlimited users and senders, so a shared team of SDRs can work from one subscription. Reply.io's Email Volume plan is priced per user up to 5,000 active contacts and switches to unlimited users from 10,000.
  • SDR running email, LinkedIn and calls: the per-user prices are close. Reply.io's Multichannel plan lists LinkedIn and calls and SMS automation as included for each user. lemlist's Multichannel plan bundles LinkedIn, SMS and a dialer, and sells WhatsApp as an add-on.
  • Agency serving several clients: Reply.io publishes an Agency Core plan with multiple workspaces and unlimited clients. lemlist's pricing page has no agency tier, so you'd run separate workspaces or talk to sales.
  • Team that needs lead data in the same tool: lemlist sells enrichment and intent-signal credits as separate add-ons. Reply.io includes a small monthly live-data allowance and sells more credits on top.

Positioning

lemlist grew from personalised cold email, with custom text and images, Liquid syntax and its own lemwarm warm-up. It has since added a lead database, AI agents, LinkedIn and calling. Its pricing page groups these into Email, Multichannel and Enterprise plans, with data sold as add-ons.

Reply.io sells itself as a multichannel sales engagement platform with an AI SDR product called Jason. It offers an email-volume plan, a per-user Multichannel plan, AI SDR plans and dedicated agency plans. It sits close to the tools in our guide to sales engagement platforms for SDR teams. lemlist competes more often with the sending-first tools in our cold email software roundup for agencies.

Workflow-by-workflow analysis

Sequencing logic

Both tools support branching sequences that mix automated email steps with LinkedIn actions, calls and manual tasks. lemlist lists conditional next steps, auto-stop and campaign auto-launch on every plan. Manual steps and the unified inbox are Multichannel features. Reply.io's Multichannel plan adds team performance and channel efficiency reports, which helps a manager see which channel moves meetings.

Units of volume

This is the difference that matters most for cost. lemlist's Email plan is sized by emails sent per month. Reply.io's Email Volume plan is sized by "active contacts": unique people who get a first-step email in the month, with unlimited follow-ups. A four-step sequence to 2,500 new contacts uses 10,000 emails on lemlist, but only 2,500 active contacts on Reply.io. If you run long sequences, Reply.io's unit is more forgiving. If you send short sequences to many contacts, lemlist's unit may work out cheaper.

Mailboxes and deliverability

lemlist includes lemwarm and a deliverability hub on all plans, along with in-app domain and mailbox purchase, inbox rotation and a DNS setup test. Reply.io includes warm-up with each mailbox through its Mailtoaster service and sells new domains and mailboxes in-app. Domains start at $6 a year plus $5 per mailbox per month, and a pre-warmed package starts at $25 a year plus $15 per mailbox per month (checked 4 November 2026). For independent monitoring, see our email deliverability tools comparison.

Data credits and AI add-ons

lemlist says credits are charged only for successful results and roll over while your subscription stays active. Its enrichment pack equals about 400 emails or 100 phone numbers. Reply.io's included 50 live-data credits a month is a sampler, not a prospecting budget. Both vendors sell AI agents: lemAgent at lemlist, and Jason AI SDR at Reply.io from $500 a month on annual billing. Judge these on their own merits, not as part of the seat price. If data is your bottleneck, a dedicated provider from our lead enrichment software guide may give better coverage.

Team controls and CRM sync

lemlist's custom user roles, activity logs, SSO/SAML and "use lemlist from your CRM" sit in Enterprise. Self-serve plans list standard roles and permissions. Reply.io's Agency Core includes role-based access control and an agency dashboard. In either tool, decide which system owns the contact record and test that replies, meetings and opt-outs sync back to the CRM before you scale.

LinkedIn automation risk

Both vendors automate LinkedIn actions. LinkedIn's User Agreement restricts the use of bots and other automated methods on its service, and accounts can be restricted. Treat LinkedIn steps as a policy risk you accept knowingly. Keep the volume modest and never put a rep's only account at risk. Limitations of both

  • lemlist: SSO, custom roles and activity logs are Enterprise-only. There's no published agency plan. WhatsApp and lead data cost extra. Multichannel is priced per user, so occasional users still need a seat.
  • Reply.io: the active-contact unit takes some thought to forecast. The mailbox count shown for Multichannel varied between the monthly and annual views we reviewed. LinkedIn and calling cost extra on Email Volume. The included data credits are small.
  • Both: LinkedIn automation carries platform policy risk, telephony usage costs aren't clearly published, and neither tool's AI agent claims were independently checked for this draft.

Compliance still sits with the sender. B2B outreach has to respect opt-outs across every channel, identify the sender accurately and have a lawful basis for processing where the GDPR applies. Our walkthrough for building a respectful outreach stack covers suppression and data sourcing.

Best-for outcomes

lemlist is a good fit for teams that send personalised email at volume with several people on one account, value built-in warm-up and deliverability tooling, and want enrichment credits that roll over.

Reply.io is a good fit for teams that run long multichannel sequences where the active-contact unit stretches further, want LinkedIn and calling included per seat, or are agencies needing separate client workspaces on a published plan.

Consider another option if your outbound is pure high-volume email across dozens of mailboxes. Our comparison of Instantly and Smartlead mailbox economics covers that kind of setup. If your sales team needs pipeline management more than sequencing, start with a CRM suited to B2B SaaS sales teams.

Switching considerations

  1. Export the unsubscribe and do-not-contact lists first, and import them into the new tool before any sequence starts.
  2. Rebuild sequences by hand. Step types, conditions and personalisation variables don't map one-to-one between the two tools.
  3. Reconnect mailboxes and LinkedIn accounts, then restart at reduced volume.
  4. Recreate CRM field mappings and check that activity logging doesn't create duplicates.
  5. Finish or pause live sequences in the old tool so prospects don't get messages from both.

FAQs

Is lemlist or Reply.io cheaper for a single SDR?

On monthly billing, Reply.io's Multichannel seat ($99) listed below lemlist's ($109), checked 4 November 2026. On yearly billing they were $89 and $87. Data and extra mailboxes usually decide the real total.

What is an active contact on Reply.io?

Reply.io defines it as a unique contact who gets a first-step email in the month. Follow-ups to that contact don't count again.

Can a whole team share lemlist's Email plan?

The pricing page lists unlimited users and email senders on the Email plan, with price set by monthly email volume.

Do both tools sync with HubSpot and Salesforce?

lemlist lists HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive integrations on all plans. Reply.io offers CRM integrations too. Check field-level sync and activity logging during the trial.

Is LinkedIn automation allowed?

LinkedIn's terms restrict automated activity, so using automation risks account restrictions whichever vendor you choose. Read LinkedIn's current terms and decide your own risk tolerance.

Verdict

Our editorial assessment: lemlist is the stronger value for email-heavy teams that want many users on one volume-priced plan and solid deliverability tooling built in. Reply.io is the more economical per-seat choice for genuinely multichannel SDRs who run long sequences, and it's the only one of the two with a published agency structure. Run your own sequence length, seat count and mailbox needs through both pricing pages before you decide.
