Quick verdict by buyer type

There is no single winner here, and anyone telling you otherwise has usually picked one workflow and ignored the rest. Based on the product information reviewed, the split looks like this:

  • Agencies that report rankings to clients every week: Semrush. Its published plans list keywords tracked daily, while Ahrefs lists a weekly update frequency for Rank Tracker on every plan.
  • Link builders and digital PR teams: Ahrefs. Backlink research is the product's historical core, and features such as broken backlinks, linking authors and Content Explorer sit on its Standard plan and above.
  • Teams that mix SEO with paid search or market research: Semrush, which has a longer history of advertising and competitive research toolkits. Confirm which toolkit carries the reports you need under the current packaging.
  • Solo consultants on a tight budget: either can work at the entry tier, but read the limits first. Ahrefs Lite caps credits at 1,000 per user per month, and Semrush's SEO plan lists only basic keyword research.
  • Lean in-house teams with one site: Ahrefs counts verified projects as unlimited, which helps if you own the site, while its per-user charges add up fast on bigger teams.

How the two platforms are positioned

Semrush presents itself as a broad visibility platform. Its current SEO pricing page groups plans as SEO, Starter, Pro+ and Advanced, with AI search visibility features such as prompt tracking bundled into the higher plans. Around the SEO core sit add-ons for extra users, client reporting and agency lead generation.

Ahrefs positions itself around its own crawler and web index. Its plans run Lite, Standard, Advanced and Enterprise, with a lower-cost Starter plan and a limited Ahrefs Free account for site owners. Add-ons cover content tooling, extra reporting capacity, traffic analytics and audit boosts.

In our editorial assessment, the practical difference is emphasis. Semrush tends to suit teams who want many marketing questions answered in one login. Ahrefs tends to suit teams whose day revolves around links, competitor pages and keyword difficulty, and who are happy to add other tools for the rest. Workflow-by-workflow analysis

Keyword research

Both tools give search volume, difficulty, intent and SERP context. Ahrefs lists keyword clusters, search intents, AI suggestions and translations in Keywords Explorer from Standard upward, so Lite buyers get a thinner version. Semrush's SEO plan is described as "basic keyword research", with fuller research tools from Starter. If keyword discovery is the main job, our comparison of keyword research tools for agencies also covers dedicated options that cost less than either suite.

Backlink analysis

This is where Ahrefs has built its reputation, and its plan table puts broken backlinks, linking authors, outgoing links and batch analysis on Standard and above. Semrush has its own backlink database and analysis tools. Neither index sees the whole web, so treat counts as samples. Our roundup of backlink tools for link building teams explains why index size and link quality are different questions.

Content

Semrush lists content optimization on Pro+. Ahrefs sells AI Content Helper and AI Content Grader in its Content Kit add-on, from $99/mo (checked 4 November 2026), and includes Content Explorer for topic and link-prospect research from Standard. Teams writing at volume often pair either suite with a dedicated optimizer; see our notes on content scoring and brief tools.

PPC and market research

Semrush has a long track record of paid-search research, such as competitor ad copy and paid keyword data. Under the current packaging, check which toolkit includes those reports before assuming the SEO plan does. Ahrefs shows some paid-search signals, but it is not positioned as a PPC tool, and its Traffic Analytics add-on ($99/mo for traffic reports, checked 4 November 2026) is the route to non-organic channel estimates.

Technical audits

Both run site crawls. Ahrefs publishes crawl credits per month (100,000 on Lite, 500,000 on Standard, 1.5 million on Advanced) and maximum pages per project, which makes audit capacity easy to plan. Semrush lists Site Audit on its SEO plan and an "AI-ready" Site Audit from Starter. Check its page limits per audit against your largest client.

Rank tracking

This is the clearest operational difference on paper. Semrush's plan cards describe keywords "to track daily". The Ahrefs comparison table lists Rank Tracker update frequency as weekly on all four plans. For agencies that send weekly reports this may be fine. For teams that react to daily movement, or that run multi-client rank tracking with location detail, Semrush or a dedicated tracker is the safer fit. Semrush Pro+ also lists multi-location and device tracking.

Agency reporting

Semrush sells reporting as add-ons: a Base Report with GA and Search Console integrations and PDF export, and a Pro Report that adds external integrations, scheduled delivery, branding and white-labeling. Ahrefs includes Report Builder with a small report allowance, and adds a Looker Studio integration on Advanced. We could not confirm white-label options for Ahrefs Report Builder from its pricing page. If client reporting decides the deal, compare both against the dedicated tools in our SEO reporting software guide.

Database metrics and their caveats

Buyers often compare "Authority Score" with "Domain Rating", or traffic estimates side by side. These are proprietary models, not measurements, and each vendor calculates them differently. A domain can sit at 40 in one tool and 55 in the other without either being wrong.

  • Search volume is a modelled estimate. Expect different numbers for the same keyword, especially for low-volume and local terms.
  • Traffic estimates multiply ranking positions by assumed click-through rates. They are useful for comparing competitors in one tool and unreliable as absolute figures.
  • Backlink counts depend on what each crawler has found and kept. Use trends and referring domains rather than raw totals.
  • Difficulty scores use different formulas. Calibrate them against keywords you already rank for.

The practical rule: pick one tool as the reference for each metric, and do not mix sources inside a client report.

Pricing and total-cost scenarios

Headline prices hide most of the cost. The scenarios below use monthly-billing list prices checked 4 November 2026, and they leave out tax, overages and annual discounts.

Scenario A: in-house team, three users, one site

Assumptions: one owned website, 1,000 tracked keywords, three people who each need a login, no client reporting.

  • Semrush: 1,000 keywords exceeds the 500 on SEO and Starter, so Pro+ at $299. Two extra users at the add-on's starting price of $45 each brings the total to about $389/mo. Advanced lists "team users" as a feature, so confirm with Semrush that Pro+ accepts added seats.
  • Ahrefs: Lite's 750 keywords is too few, so Standard at $249. Two extra users at $60 each brings the total to $369/mo. Rankings refresh weekly under the listed plan terms.

Scenario B: 10-client agency, two users

Assumptions: 10 client sites (not ownership-verified in the tool), 100 keywords per client, one white-labelled monthly report per client.

  • Semrush: 10 sites needs Pro+ (15 websites) at $299, plus one user from $45, plus the Pro Report add-on at $20. Roughly $364/mo. Confirm whether the Pro Report price covers all client reports or is counted per report.
  • Ahrefs: 10 unverified projects needs Standard (20 projects) at $249, plus one user at $60. Standard includes 5 Report Builder reports, so 10 client reports needs the $99/mo Report Builder add-on. Roughly $408/mo. If clients verify their sites, the project limit stops binding.

Neither total is a quote. Ahrefs also notes that extra users and pay-as-you-go credits are charged automatically when enabled. For three agency sizes priced side by side, see our SEO software pricing guide for 5, 10 and 20 clients.

Limitations of both

Semrush

  • Rank tracking caps are tight on entry plans: SEO and Starter both list 500 tracked keywords and five websites.
  • Reporting, extra users and agency lead generation are separate line items, so the invoice grows with headcount and clients.
  • Packaging has changed recently. Older articles and screenshots may describe plans that no longer exist.

Ahrefs

  • Rank Tracker is listed as weekly, which some agencies will find too coarse.
  • Each plan caps how many extra users you can add (2, 5 or 10 below Enterprise), and the per-user price rises with the plan.
  • The FAQ states that Ahrefs never runs discounts and generally does not issue refunds, so test carefully on the cheaper tiers first.
  • Lite's 1,000 credits per user per month limits heavy research days.

Choose Semrush if, choose Ahrefs if

Choose Semrush if

  • You report rankings frequently and want daily tracking without a separate tracker.
  • Your team also works on paid search, competitor market research or AI visibility, and wants it in one account.
  • You need white-labelled PDF or link-shared client reports and are happy to pay for the reporting add-on.

Choose Ahrefs if

  • Link prospecting, competitor backlink gaps and content research are your main daily jobs.
  • You manage owned or verified sites, where unlimited verified projects make the plan go further.
  • You want predictable audit capacity through published crawl credits and page limits.

Consider another option if neither fits your budget or your workflow is narrow. Our pages on cheaper and simpler Semrush replacements and tools that cover individual Ahrefs jobs map those options, and the SE Ranking and Semrush agency comparison looks at a lower-cost suite in detail.

Switching considerations

  • Ranking history: export historical positions before you cancel. A new tool starts tracking from the day you add keywords.
  • Metric baselines: authority and traffic numbers will change with the tool. Tell clients before the first report, not after.
  • Report templates: rebuild these first, because they are what clients notice.
  • Overlap period: run both for one reporting cycle if your contract terms allow, then cancel. Ahrefs says downgrades and cancellations take effect at the end of the billing period.

FAQs

Is Semrush or Ahrefs cheaper?

At the entry tier Ahrefs Lite ($129/mo) lists lower than Semrush SEO ($139/mo), checked 4 November 2026. Once you add users, keywords and reports, the gap depends entirely on your workload, as the scenarios above show.

Do I need both tools?

Most small teams do not. Paying for both usually makes sense only when backlink research and daily, multi-location rank tracking are both heavy, client-billable workloads.

Which has more accurate data?

Neither vendor's estimates can be called accurate in an absolute sense, because both model search volume and traffic. Compare them against your own Search Console data for sites you control.

Can I try either before paying?

Semrush offers a free plan and a "try for free" option on paid plans. Ahrefs offers Ahrefs Free for site owners and a $29/mo Starter plan. Check the current trial terms on each pricing page.

Verdict

Semrush is the better fit for teams that treat SEO as one part of a wider marketing job and need daily ranking data and branded client reports. Ahrefs is the better fit for teams whose work centres on links and competitive content research, especially on owned sites. Model your own users, keywords and report count before you choose, because those three numbers move the bill more than the plan name.
