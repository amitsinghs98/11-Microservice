ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot: CRM and Marketing Automation Fit

Quick verdict by buyer type

  • Marketing-led SMB, small sales function: ActiveCampaign is usually the more economical way to get deep email automation with a workable deal pipeline.
  • B2B team where sales and marketing share one funnel: HubSpot is the stronger platform bet, because CRM, sequences, marketing automation and reporting sit on one data model, at a higher and more seat-sensitive cost.
  • Team that already runs a dedicated CRM: ActiveCampaign as the automation layer is often the lighter move. Adopting HubSpot Marketing Hub on its own means syncing two CRMs.
  • Team that expects heavy custom objects, permissions and multi-team reporting within two years: HubSpot, or a comparison with Salesforce, rather than ActiveCampaign.

Two different platform philosophies

HubSpot starts from the CRM. Its free CRM (marketed as Smart CRM) is the shared database, and Marketing Hub, Sales Hub, Service Hub and other hubs are products layered on top, each sold in Starter, Professional and Enterprise editions. You buy the hubs you need, and you buy seats for the people who use them.

ActiveCampaign starts from automation. The workflow builder, email, segmentation and site tracking are the core, and a deals pipeline with contact and deal records sits alongside. In our editorial assessment the CRM is designed to serve marketing automation and a straightforward sales process, not to be a company-wide system of record.

That difference decides most outcomes below. If the CRM is the centre of your revenue operation, HubSpot's model fits. If automated nurture is the centre and sales follows up on what it produces, ActiveCampaign's model fits.

Automation triggers and sales handoff

Both platforms trigger automation on form submissions, list or property changes, page visits (via their tracking scripts), email engagement and deal-stage changes. The difference is where the logic lives and who sees it.

ActiveCampaign

ActiveCampaign's builder is one contact-centred canvas: triggers, waits, if/else branches, goals that pull contacts forward when a condition is met, and actions that create or move deals. A typical handoff is a lead-score threshold or high-intent page visit that creates a deal, assigns an owner and notifies the rep, while the nurture branch pauses. It's flexible and fast to build. The trade-off is that sales-side activity, such as calls, sequences and forecasting, is lighter than in a dedicated sales CRM.

HubSpot

HubSpot splits automation across marketing emails, workflows and Sales Hub sequences, all reading the same contact, company and deal records. A handoff typically moves a lifecycle stage to sales-qualified, rotates ownership, creates a task and enrols the contact in a rep's sequence. Because both teams work on one record, attribution and lead status stay consistent without a sync. The catch is that the richer workflow and sequence features sit in paid editions, and each rep using them needs the right seat.

CRM depth

HubSpot's CRM covers contacts, companies, deals, tickets and associations between them, with custom objects and more granular permissions in higher editions. For a RevOps owner, that means territory rules, multiple pipelines and company-level reporting can live in one place.

ActiveCampaign's CRM handles contacts, accounts and deal pipelines well for a small team. Consider another option if you need complex associations, many pipelines with separate permissions, or a sales team that lives in the CRM all day. Landing pages and reporting

Landing pages and forms

Both offer forms and a drag-and-drop landing page builder, with the fuller page features on paid tiers. HubSpot's pages share its CMS components and connect directly to CRM properties and smart content. ActiveCampaign's pages are adequate for campaign and offer pages. Teams running serious conversion programmes often add a specialist builder from our landing page CRO tools list whichever platform they pick.

Reporting

HubSpot's advantage is cross-object reporting: campaign influence on deals, pipeline by source and rep activity in one dashboard, with custom report building and attribution concentrated in Professional and Enterprise. ActiveCampaign reports well on campaigns, automations and contact engagement, and offers attribution and deal reporting on higher plans. For a board-level pipeline view it usually needs exporting or a BI layer.

Cost by contacts and seats: two scenarios

Both scenarios are hypothetical. We list the cost lines each platform would generate so you can price them against the official pages on the day you buy.

Scenario A: three-person SMB

Assumptions: 2,500 contacts, all marketable; one marketer, one founder who sells, one assistant; needs email, forms, two landing pages, one pipeline and a five-step welcome automation.

  • ActiveCampaign: one plan priced at the 2,500-contact tier. Confirm the plan includes three users and the pipeline and landing page features you need, since those differ by tier.
  • HubSpot: free CRM plus Starter editions (individually or as the Starter bundle) priced by marketing contacts and seats. Check whether your welcome automation needs Professional-level workflows, because that edition jump is the single largest cost change in this scenario.

Our reading: at this size ActiveCampaign usually delivers more automation per dollar, while HubSpot Starter is attractive if the automation you need is simple.

Scenario B: ten-person B2B team

Assumptions: 15,000 contacts, of which 8,000 are marketable; three marketers, six sales reps, one ops lead; needs lead scoring, rep sequences, multiple pipelines and pipeline-by-source reporting.

  • HubSpot: Marketing Hub Professional at the 8,000 marketing-contact tier (HubSpot lets you mark contacts as non-marketing so they don't count toward the tier), Sales Hub seats for six reps, seats for marketers and ops, and any onboarding fee.
  • ActiveCampaign: a higher-tier plan at the 15,000-contact level (check whether unsubscribed or inactive contacts count), extra users beyond the plan allowance, and any sales add-ons. Many teams at this size also budget for a separate sales engagement tool.

Our reading: HubSpot's total is higher and grows with every rep, but it replaces a CRM-plus-sync arrangement. ActiveCampaign stays cheaper on contacts but may push you toward a second sales tool. Add-ons, onboarding and implementation effort

  • HubSpot: expect to budget for onboarding on Professional and Enterprise, where HubSpot has historically required a one-time fee (confirm it on the pricing page), plus extra marketing-contact tiers as the database grows. Data model design takes real time: lifecycle stages, deal stages, properties and ownership rules.
  • ActiveCampaign: setup is usually faster because there's less to model, but plan time for tag and custom-field hygiene. Automation-first accounts tend to accumulate overlapping tags that later make segmentation unreliable.
  • Both: integrations with billing, product or support data often need a connector. Our comparison of Zapier, Make and n8n covers that layer.

Long-term scaling

HubSpot scales by adding hubs, editions and seats, so the platform rarely becomes the ceiling, but the bill climbs in steps that are easy to underestimate at renewal. ActiveCampaign scales smoothly on contacts and automations. The pressure point is usually the sales side: once reps want forecasting, complex permissions or service ticketing, teams either add tools or migrate. Limitations of each

  • HubSpot: step-change pricing between editions, seat costs that scale with headcount, and key automation and reporting features held back from Starter. A team buying Marketing Hub only, without moving its sales team onto HubSpot's CRM, loses much of the platform's advantage.
  • ActiveCampaign: a lighter CRM, reporting that needs supplementing for revenue-level views, and tag sprawl risk. It is not a full service or support platform.

Switching considerations

Moving in either direction means rebuilding automations rather than importing them. Export contacts, deals, custom fields and consent status. Recreate lifecycle and deal stages before importing. Rebuild your top five automations first and run them in parallel with the old system. Keep the old account readable until reporting history has been exported, because engagement history rarely transfers in full.

FAQs

Can ActiveCampaign replace a CRM for a small sales team?

For a few reps working one or two pipelines, often yes. For larger teams that need sequences, forecasting and granular permissions, it is usually paired with or replaced by a dedicated CRM.

Do I need Sales Hub if I buy HubSpot Marketing Hub?

Not strictly, because the free CRM supports basic deal tracking. But rep-facing tools such as sequences and forecasting sit in Sales Hub, so a team with active sellers usually needs both.

Which is easier for one operator to administer?

ActiveCampaign, in our assessment, because there are fewer objects and editions to manage. HubSpot rewards a dedicated admin who will maintain properties, permissions and reporting.

How do contact counts differ between the two?

HubSpot bills Marketing Hub on marketing contacts, a status you assign. ActiveCampaign bills on its own contact definition. Ask each vendor how unsubscribed, bounced and inactive contacts are counted.

Fit-based verdict

Choose ActiveCampaign when automation is the engine, sales is small or simple, and you want strong nurture logic without paying for a seat-heavy platform. Choose HubSpot when the CRM is the operating system for revenue, several teams share the funnel, and you can fund the editions and seats that unlock its workflows and reporting. Before signing either, price your real contact count and seat list on the official pages, and ask about onboarding and renewal terms in writing.
