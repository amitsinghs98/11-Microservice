ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot: CRM and Marketing Automation Fit

For an ops or RevOps owner, ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot is a choice of operating model: HubSpot puts a shared CRM at the centre and sells Marketing Hub and Sales Hub on top, while ActiveCampaign puts automation at the centre with a lighter CRM beside it. ActiveCampaign is usually the leaner fit for marketing-led teams with simple sales; HubSpot is the stronger fit when several teams share one funnel and you can fund the seats.

Which should you choose: ActiveCampaign or HubSpot?

Choose ActiveCampaign if automated nurture is the engine of growth and sales is small or simple. Choose HubSpot if the CRM will be the system of record for marketing, sales and possibly service, and you expect more custom objects, permissions and cross-team reporting within two years.

  • Marketing-led SMB with one or two sellers: ActiveCampaign is usually the more economical route to deep automation with a workable deal pipeline.
  • B2B team where sales and marketing share one funnel: HubSpot, because Smart CRM, Sales Hub and Marketing Hub read and write the same records.
  • Team that already runs a dedicated sales CRM: ActiveCampaign as the automation layer is often the lighter move than adding a second CRM.
  • Team heading toward complex territories, many pipelines or enterprise permissions: HubSpot, or a wider comparison in our HubSpot vs Salesforce guide for growing businesses.

Is this a platform decision or an email-tool decision?

For whoever owns the stack, it's a platform decision, because the choice decides where contact, company and deal data lives and who administers it. If your real question is which tool sends better lifecycle email, read our email-focused HubSpot Marketing Hub vs ActiveCampaign comparison instead.

HubSpot starts from the CRM. Smart CRM is the shared database, and Marketing Hub, Sales Hub, Service Hub and other hubs are sold on top in Starter, Professional and Enterprise editions. You buy the hubs you need and the seats for the people who use them.

ActiveCampaign starts from automation. The workflow builder, email, segmentation and site tracking are the core, with contact, account and deal records alongside. In our editorial assessment, its CRM is designed to serve marketing automation and a straightforward sales process rather than to be a company-wide system of record.

How deep is each CRM for a RevOps owner?

HubSpot's CRM is the deeper system of record, with contacts, companies, deals and tickets linked by associations, and custom objects and finer permissions in higher editions. ActiveCampaign's CRM handles contacts, accounts and deal pipelines well for a small team, but it isn't built for complex associations or a sales floor that lives in the CRM all day.

For a RevOps owner, HubSpot's depth means territory rules, multiple pipelines, lifecycle stages and company-level reporting can all live in one place, without a sync between two databases. That only pays off if sales actually works in HubSpot. Buying Marketing Hub while sales stays in another CRM gives up most of the platform advantage.

ActiveCampaign is the more pragmatic choice when the CRM's main job is to catch what automation produces: a deal created at the right moment, an owner assigned and a task set. Consider another option if you need many pipelines with separate permissions. How do automation triggers and sales handoff differ?

Both trigger automation on form submissions, field or list changes, tracked page visits, email engagement and deal-stage changes. The difference is that ActiveCampaign keeps the logic on one contact-centred canvas, while HubSpot spreads it across workflows and Sales Hub sequences that all read the same CRM records.

ActiveCampaign: one canvas, fast to build

An ActiveCampaign automation combines triggers, waits, if/else branches, goals that pull contacts forward when a condition is met, and actions that create or move deals. A typical handoff fires on a lead-score threshold or a high-intent page visit, creates a deal, assigns an owner, notifies the rep and pauses the nurture branch. It's flexible and quick. The trade-off is that the sales side, such as call logging, sequences and forecasting, is lighter than in a dedicated sales CRM.

HubSpot: shared records, more seats

A HubSpot handoff typically moves the lifecycle stage to sales-qualified, rotates ownership, creates a task and enrols the contact in a rep's sequence. Because both teams work on one record, lead status and source stay consistent without a sync. The catch is that the richer workflow and sequence features sit in paid editions, and each rep who uses them needs the right seat.

Which reports better on pipeline and revenue?

HubSpot reports better across objects, such as campaign influence on deals, pipeline by source and rep activity in one dashboard, with custom reports and attribution concentrated in its higher editions. ActiveCampaign reports well on campaigns, automations and contact engagement, but a board-level pipeline view usually needs exporting or a BI layer.

The distinction matters for whoever answers "which channel produced this quarter's pipeline?". In HubSpot, that answer comes from the same records sales updates daily. With ActiveCampaign, it depends on how disciplined the deal data is and how much of the sales process happens inside it.

On landing pages and forms, both offer drag-and-drop builders, with fuller features on paid tiers. HubSpot's pages connect directly to CRM properties and smart content, while ActiveCampaign's pages are adequate for campaign and offer pages. What does each cost by contacts and seats?

ActiveCampaign's bill mainly follows plan tier and contact count, while HubSpot's follows hub edition, marketing-contact tier and the number of paid seats in each hub. In practice ActiveCampaign tends to stay cheaper as headcount grows, and HubSpot tends to cost more but can replace a separate CRM and the sync between them.

The two scenarios below are hypothetical and list cost lines only, so you can price them against the official pages on the day you buy.

Scenario A: three-person SMB

Assumptions: 2,500 contacts, all marketable; one marketer, one founder who sells and one assistant. The team needs email, forms, two landing pages, one pipeline and a five-step welcome automation.

  • ActiveCampaign: one plan at the 2,500-contact tier. Confirm it includes three users and the pipeline and landing page features you need, since these differ by plan.
  • HubSpot: free Smart CRM plus Starter editions of Marketing Hub and Sales Hub, individually or as a bundle, priced by marketing contacts and seats. Check whether your welcome automation needs Professional-level workflows, because that edition jump is the largest cost change in this scenario.

Scenario B: ten-person B2B team

Assumptions: 15,000 contacts, of which 8,000 are marketable; three marketers, six reps and one ops lead. The team needs lead scoring, rep sequences, multiple pipelines and pipeline-by-source reporting.

  • HubSpot: Marketing Hub Professional at the 8,000 marketing-contact tier (HubSpot lets you mark contacts as non-marketing so they don't count toward it), Sales Hub seats for six reps, seats for the marketers and ops lead, and any onboarding fee.
  • ActiveCampaign: a higher plan at the 15,000-contact level, extra users beyond the plan's allowance and any sales add-ons. Ask how unsubscribed and inactive contacts count. Many teams this size also budget for a separate sales engagement tool.

Our reading is that HubSpot's total grows with every rep but replaces a CRM-plus-sync arrangement, while ActiveCampaign stays cheaper on people but may push you toward a second sales tool. What do onboarding and implementation really involve?

HubSpot implementation is mostly data-model work, such as lifecycle stages, deal stages, properties and ownership rules, and Professional and Enterprise editions have historically carried a one-time onboarding fee that you should confirm on the pricing page. ActiveCampaign usually goes live faster because there's less to model, but it needs tag and custom-field discipline from day one.

How does each platform scale over two to three years?

HubSpot scales by adding hubs, editions and seats, so the platform rarely becomes the ceiling, but the bill rises in steps that are easy to underestimate at renewal. ActiveCampaign scales smoothly on contacts and automations, and its pressure point is usually the sales side.

Once reps want forecasting, complex permissions or service ticketing, ActiveCampaign teams tend either to add a sales CRM and sync it or to migrate. HubSpot teams more often hit budget questions: whether every new hire needs a paid seat, and whether an edition upgrade is worth it for one feature. How hard is switching in either direction?

Either way, you'll rebuild automations rather than import them, and engagement history rarely transfers in full. Plan the data model first, then migrate records, then rebuild automations in priority order.

  1. Export contacts, companies or accounts, deals, custom fields and consent status.
  2. Recreate lifecycle and deal stages in the destination before importing anything.
  3. Map tags in ActiveCampaign to properties or lists in HubSpot, or the reverse, and drop the ones nobody uses.
  4. Rebuild your five most important automations and run them in parallel with the old system.
  5. Keep the old account readable until reporting history is exported.

What else do buyers ask about ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot?

The questions that come up most are about CRM replacement, Sales Hub, administration and contact counting. Short answers follow.

Can ActiveCampaign replace a CRM for a small sales team?

For a few reps working one or two pipelines, often yes. Larger teams that need sequences, forecasting and granular permissions usually pair it with, or replace it by, a dedicated CRM.

Do I need Sales Hub if I buy HubSpot Marketing Hub?

Not strictly, because Smart CRM supports basic deal tracking. Rep-facing tools such as sequences and forecasting sit in Sales Hub, so a team with active sellers usually needs both.

Which is easier for one operator to administer?

ActiveCampaign, in our assessment, because there are fewer objects and editions to manage. HubSpot rewards a dedicated admin who maintains properties, permissions and reports.

How do contact counts differ between the two?

HubSpot bills Marketing Hub on marketing contacts, a status you assign to each record. ActiveCampaign bills on its own contact definition, so ask each vendor how unsubscribed, bounced and inactive contacts count.

What's the verdict?

Choose ActiveCampaign when automation drives growth, sales is small or simple, and you want strong nurture logic without a seat-heavy platform. Choose HubSpot when the CRM is the operating system for revenue, several teams share the funnel, and you can fund the editions and seats that unlock its workflows and reporting.
