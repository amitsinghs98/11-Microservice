Which should an agency choose?

Choose SE Ranking if your margin depends on tracking many client keywords and sending branded reports cheaply. Choose Semrush if your team sells research-heavy work, such as competitive analysis, paid search or AI visibility, and needs those tools in the same account as tracking.

  • Freelancers and agencies with up to 10 clients: SE Ranking Core, which fits 10 projects in one plan.
  • Agencies with 10 to 30 clients: SE Ranking Growth, unless research depth justifies Semrush Pro+.
  • Agencies selling competitive research or PPC: Semrush, with reporting add-ons budgeted per client.
  • Local-heavy agencies: compare the local add-ons below, then consider a local specialist.

How are SE Ranking and Semrush positioned?

SE Ranking positions itself as a capacity-rich SEO platform with an agency add-on for white-label and client access. Semrush positions itself as a broad visibility platform covering SEO, AI search, content, local and market research, sold as a core plan plus toolkits and add-ons.

In our editorial assessment, the difference shows up on the invoice. SE Ranking bundles more projects, keywords and seats into the base plan. Semrush sells breadth, then charges separately for users, reports and specialist toolkits. Neither approach is wrong; they suit different agency models.

How do their plan limits compare?

At similar monthly prices, SE Ranking Core lists twice the projects and four times the tracked keywords of Semrush's SEO plan. Semrush's advantage is in what each plan includes beyond tracking.

What does a 5-client agency pay?

On our matched 5-client workload, SE Ranking costs about $188 a month and Semrush about $262 a month on annual billing, once a second seat and white-label reports are included. Semrush's SEO plan also leaves no headroom: a sixth client forces an upgrade.

Assumptions: five client sites, 100 tracked keywords each (500 in total), two team members, one white-labelled monthly report per client, annual billing because SE Ranking's Agency Pack requires it. Prices checked 4 November 2026; tax excluded; add-ons at their listed rates.

  • SE Ranking: Core at $103.20 a month billed annually, plus one extra seat from $16, plus the Agency Pack at $69. About $188 a month, with 5 projects and 1,500 keywords of headroom.
  • Semrush: the SEO plan at $117.33 a month billed annually, plus one user at $45, plus five Pro Reports at $20. About $262 a month, at exactly its 5-website and 500-keyword cap.

Without white-label reports, the gap narrows to about $145 a month for SE Ranking (Core plus a seat, monthly billing) against $184 for Semrush (SEO plan plus a user, monthly billing).

What does a 15-client agency pay?

At 15 clients, SE Ranking Growth costs about $292 a month against roughly $638 for Semrush Pro+ on annual billing. The difference comes mostly from per-report white-label pricing and extra users on Semrush.

Assumptions: 15 client sites, 100 tracked keywords each (1,500 in total), three team members, one white-labelled monthly report per client, annual billing.

  • SE Ranking: Growth at $223.20 a month billed annually, with three seats, 30 projects and 5,000 keywords included, plus the $69 Agency Pack. About $292 a month.
  • Semrush: Pro+ at $248.17 a month billed annually (15 websites, 1,500 keywords), plus two users at $45 and 15 Pro Reports at $20. About $638 a month.

Semrush Pro+ again sits at its cap, so client 16 means custom Advanced pricing. SE Ranking Growth has room for twice the clients. What is included and what becomes an add-on?

SE Ranking includes more tracking capacity and seats in the base plan and sells white label, AI search tracking and API access as add-ons. Semrush includes AI visibility prompts from Starter upward but sells users, reports, local tools, content tools and agency lead generation separately.

  • SE Ranking Agency Pack: white-label interface, your own domain, reports sent from your own SMTP server, client view-only seats and unlimited scheduled reports (checked 4 November 2026).
  • SE Ranking AI Search add-on: its help center lists 200 checks a month for $89 on monthly billing (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Semrush AI visibility: Starter adds 50 tracked prompts and Pro+ 100 (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Semrush reports: a Base Report at $10 a month, and the white-label Pro Report at $20 a month, which its knowledge base says is bought per report (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Semrush agency lead generation: listed at $90 a month as a separate add-on (checked 4 November 2026).

If AI visibility is part of what you sell, Semrush's bundled prompts narrow the cost gap. If it is not, you are paying for capacity you will not use.

How do local SEO features compare?

SE Ranking includes a small local allowance in its core plans, while Semrush sells local tools per location. For agencies with many single-location clients, that difference can outweigh the core plan price.

SE Ranking's pricing page lists three locations included on Core and Growth, with extra locations from $18.40 (checked 4 November 2026); confirm exactly what each extra location covers. Semrush lists Local Base at $30 and Local Pro at $60 per location per month, billed annually, with listing management only on Pro (checked 4 November 2026).

Local-only agencies often get more from a specialist; our guide to rank tracking and reporting for a local SEO agency covers grid tracking and per-location pricing in depth.

How do onboarding and trials compare?

SE Ranking gives a longer trial, which helps when you want to load several client projects before deciding. Semrush offers a free plan and shorter toolkit trials, so plan your evaluation before you start the clock.

SE Ranking states that every plan starts with a 14-day free trial, while Semrush's knowledge base lists 7-day free trials for its toolkits (both checked 4 November 2026). Semrush also has a free plan with one demo project and 10 reports a day.

During either trial, import at least two real client projects, set up rank tracking and schedule one report. That shows more about fit than any feature list. Where does each fall short for agencies?

SE Ranking's main constraint is that white label is tied to annual billing, and its keyword and link databases differ from Semrush's, so test data in your niches. Semrush's main constraint is plan caps that sit exactly at common client counts, plus add-ons that scale with every new client and person.

  • SE Ranking: no white-label option on monthly billing; check that its research depth covers your markets.
  • SE Ranking: local, AI search and API features are capped or sold separately.
  • Semrush: 5 websites on the two lower plans and 15 on Pro+, then custom pricing.
  • Semrush: per-report white label makes reporting cost grow linearly with clients.

What else do agencies ask about SE Ranking and Semrush?

Questions usually cover data quality, annual commitments and running both tools. The short answers follow.

Is SE Ranking's data as good as Semrush's?

Each vendor runs its own databases and models, so results differ by niche and market. Compare both on clients whose real performance you know from Search Console before deciding.

Can I get white-label reports from SE Ranking on monthly billing?

No. SE Ranking lists its Agency Pack as available on annual billing only, checked 4 November 2026.

Should an agency run both tools?

Some do, using SE Ranking for tracking and reporting and Semrush for research. That only pays off if research is billable work every month.

How often should we recheck these prices?

Every 60 to 90 days, and before any renewal. Both vendors have changed packaging recently, so older reviews may describe plans that no longer exist.

Which fits your agency size?

For agencies up to about 30 clients that mainly sell tracking, audits and reporting, SE Ranking fits better on cost and headroom. For agencies that sell competitive research, paid search or AI visibility, Semrush's breadth can justify paying more, provided you budget reports and users per client.
