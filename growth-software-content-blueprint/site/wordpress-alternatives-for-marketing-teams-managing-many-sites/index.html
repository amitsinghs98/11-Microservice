WordPress Alternatives for Marketing Teams Managing Many Sites

The strongest WordPress alternatives for marketing teams running many sites are Duda and Wix Studio for portfolio management, Webflow for design-led brand sites, HubSpot Content Hub for CRM-led B2B sites, and Contentful, Storyblok or Sanity when one content hub feeds many front ends. Before you switch, price the plugin maintenance you're replacing, because that's usually the real cost.

Which WordPress alternative fits a multi-site marketing team?

If your pain is plugin updates and security across many sites, move to a hosted platform where the vendor owns the stack: Duda or Wix Studio for portfolios, Webflow or HubSpot Content Hub for a handful of brand sites. If your pain is duplicated content across sites, a headless CMS such as Contentful, Storyblok or Sanity lets one content model serve many front ends.

  • Dozens of templated sites (locations, franchises, clients): Duda.
  • Agency portfolio with varied designs: Wix Studio or Webflow.
  • A few CRM-connected B2B sites: HubSpot Content Hub.
  • Shared content across brands, regions and apps: Contentful, Storyblok or Sanity.
  • You need WordPress's ecosystem but less maintenance: stay on WordPress with a managed host or WordPress Multisite, which may be the cheapest fix.

Why do marketing teams with many WordPress sites look elsewhere?

The usual triggers are maintenance load, inconsistent governance and security exposure that grows with every site and plugin. Each site carries its own core, theme and plugin updates, and each plugin is a separate supplier you depend on.

Governance drifts too: different sites end up with different form plugins, SEO plugins and admin users, so audits take longer and brand consistency suffers. None of this means WordPress is a poor CMS. WordPress is open source, flexible and portable, and many teams fix these issues with standard builds and a managed host instead of replatforming. What does each type of alternative change for your team?

Hosted builders move security and updates to the vendor but reduce extensibility and portability; headless systems keep portability and reuse but move front-end work to developers. Pick the trade-off your team can staff.

Duda: best for many similar sites

Duda is built for agencies and platforms that publish many sites from templates; ask about client permissions, central management and white-labelling on the plan you'd buy. Check plans on the Duda pricing page. Limitations: check how extensions work on Duda, because in our editorial assessment no hosted builder matches the breadth of WordPress's plugin ecosystem. Skip it if each site needs bespoke functionality.

Wix Studio: best for varied agency portfolios

Wix Studio gives agencies a hosted platform; confirm its design, client handover and custom-code options. Limitations: confirm export options first, because leaving a hosted platform like this may mean a rebuild. Skip it if clients expect to own their codebase.

Webflow: best for a handful of design-led brand sites

Webflow offers visual design, CMS collections and managed hosting; confirm how team access and plans work on the Webflow pricing page. Limitations: check how licensing and content sharing work across several sites before you commit a portfolio. Skip it if you manage dozens of near-identical sites. The Webflow alternatives guide covers where it struggles.

HubSpot Content Hub: best for B2B sites that live in the CRM

HubSpot's CMS shares data with its CRM, so forms, contacts and attribution connect without plugins; see the Content Hub page. Limitations: its value depends on HubSpot being your system of record. Skip it if your CRM is elsewhere.

Contentful, Storyblok and Sanity: best for one content hub feeding many sites

Headless platforms store structured content once and publish it to many front ends, which suits brands with regional sites, product documentation and apps sharing content. Contentful leans enterprise, Storyblok gives marketers a visual editor, and Sanity lets developers shape the editing environment. Limitations: you need developers for front ends and a hosting platform. Skip them if you have no ongoing engineering budget. See our composable CMS guide for a fuller comparison.

Will leaving WordPress actually reduce maintenance work?

It reduces patching and plugin management, but it doesn't remove maintenance; it changes it into template governance, vendor management and, for headless, front-end engineering. In our editorial assessment, the savings depend on how many sites you run and how customised they are.

What does each option really cost once you include people?

Total cost is licence plus hosting plus the hours your team or contractors spend maintaining each site. Model the hours, because they usually outweigh licence differences across a portfolio.

A hypothetical worked example, with stated assumptions rather than vendor figures: a team runs 20 WordPress sites and spends 2 hours per site each month on core, theme and plugin updates, plus 1 hour per site on fixes. That's 60 hours a month. If a hosted builder cuts update work to zero but adds 0.5 hours per site for template and vendor admin, the team spends 10 hours a month plus whatever fixes remain. Multiply each figure by your blended hourly cost, add the licence quotes you receive and compare over three years, including a one-off migration estimate.

The calculation is illustrative only. Replace every number with your own time logs and the quotes on each vendor's pricing page. For CRM-side costs that often ride along, see our CRM pricing guide.

What should you check before migrating many sites?

Check exports, redirects, form and CRM integrations, user roles and contracts for every site before choosing a platform. Then migrate one representative site first and measure the effort.

  1. Inventory plugins per site and list what each does; many replace features you'll need to rebuild.
  2. Export content using an export route that keeps custom fields, such as a database export.
  3. Map URLs and redirects site by site; the replatforming guide covers this in detail.
  4. Rebuild forms and automations, and test each lead path into the CRM.
  5. Define roles centrally so governance improves rather than copying old admin sprawl.
  6. Check contracts for hosting renewals and plugin licences.

What else do buyers ask about WordPress alternatives for marketing teams?

Follow-up questions usually cover Multisite, lock-in and security. Brief answers are below.

Is WordPress Multisite a good alternative to switching?

It can be. Multisite is designed to run many sites from one installation, which can simplify updates, but a problem in shared code can affect every site in the network.

Which WordPress alternative has the least lock-in?

Headless CMS platforms usually have less lock-in than hosted builders, because content is available through APIs and you own the front end. Hosted builders that don't export sites have the most.

Are hosted builders more secure than WordPress?

They move patching and hosting security to the vendor, which removes a large share of your team's work. You still own user access, form data and integrations.

How long does migrating many WordPress sites take?

It depends on customisation and site count. Migrate one representative site first and use that effort to estimate the rest.

What's the verdict?

For many templated sites, Duda is the clearest WordPress alternative; for a few design-led brand sites, Webflow; for CRM-led B2B sites, HubSpot Content Hub; and for shared content across many properties, a headless CMS. If your problem is only maintenance, compare a managed WordPress setup first, because it keeps your ecosystem and exit options.
