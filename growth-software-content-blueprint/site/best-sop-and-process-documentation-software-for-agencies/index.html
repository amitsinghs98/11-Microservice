Best SOP and Process Documentation Software for Agencies

The right SOP software for agencies keeps procedures alive: each one has an owner, a review date and a checklist people actually run. Which SOP software should an agency choose?

Choose a checklist-execution tool if you need proof that a procedure was followed for each client. Choose a wiki-style tool if the main problem is that procedures are scattered and nobody can find the current version.

  • Running recurring client procedures as tracked checklists with approvals: Process Street.
  • Documenting and assigning procedures for small teams that want simplicity: SweetProcess.
  • Onboarding staff and contractors with role-based playbooks and quizzes: Trainual.
  • Turning screen clicks into step-by-step guides fast: Scribe.
  • Flexible wiki plus databases for teams that already use it: Notion.
  • Agencies inside the Atlassian ecosystem: Confluence.
  • A knowledge base that reminds owners to re-verify pages: Tettra.

What separates a living process from static documentation?

A living process has an owner, a review date, a version history and a way to run it, so it changes when the work changes. Static documentation is a file in a folder that was accurate the day it was written.

Agencies feel the gap when a new hire follows an SOP that refers to a menu that has since been renamed, or when two account managers run the same monthly report in different ways. The fix isn't more documents. It's assigning ownership, scheduling reviews and making the SOP the thing people work from, rather than something they read once.

What does each SOP tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Execution tools prove the work happened, training tools get people competent, and wikis make knowledge findable. Decide which of those you're missing most.

Process Street: best for running SOPs as tracked checklists

Process Street positions itself around turning procedures into checklist templates that you run for each client or instance, with assignments and approvals; confirm the logic and form features on your plan. That gives you a record of who did what and when, which is valuable for audits and QA. It's less natural for long reference material. Skip it if you mainly need a searchable wiki. See the Process Street pricing page.

SweetProcess: best for simple, assignable procedures

SweetProcess is positioned around documenting procedures, policies and processes and assigning them as tasks. It's straightforward for small agencies starting their first SOP library. Confirm how much branching it supports before relying on it for complex workflows. Skip it if you need heavily conditional checklists. See the SweetProcess pricing page.

Trainual: best for training-led documentation

Trainual is positioned around role-based playbooks and tracking who has completed which training; check its testing options on the official site. It suits agencies onboarding staff and contractors frequently. It's oriented to learning procedures rather than running them per client. Skip it if your team is stable and small. See the Trainual pricing page.

Scribe: best for capturing software steps

Scribe positions itself around capturing your on-screen steps and turning them into a step-by-step guide with screenshots. It removes most of the effort of writing software how-tos. It doesn't capture the reasoning behind each step, and guides go stale when interfaces change. Skip it if your SOPs are mostly judgement. See the Scribe pricing page.

Notion: best for a flexible wiki you already use

Notion is positioned as a workspace of pages and databases, so you can store SOPs with properties such as owner, status and review date. Its flexibility is the strength and the weakness: review discipline only exists if you build and enforce it. Skip it if you want structure imposed for you. See the Notion pricing page.

Confluence: best for Atlassian-based agencies

Confluence is Atlassian's team wiki, positioned around spaces, page templates, version history and permissions, with connections to Jira. It's a sensible choice if your delivery already runs on Atlassian. Without space owners, it accumulates outdated pages. Skip it if nobody else in the agency uses Atlassian. See the Confluence pricing page.

Tettra: best for keeping a knowledge base verified

Tettra positions itself as an internal knowledge base that prompts page owners to re-verify content on a schedule. That directly targets stale documentation. It stores procedures rather than running them. Skip it if you need per-client checklists. See the Tettra pricing page.

What does a healthy SOP lifecycle look like?

Every SOP moves through draft, review, publish, run, audit and revise, with a named owner and a dated review at each loop. If any stage is missing, the procedure drifts out of date.

  1. Draft: the person who does the work writes it, using a standard template with purpose, scope, steps and decision points.
  2. Review: a manager or peer checks it against how the work is actually done.
  3. Publish: versioned, with owner and next review date visible at the top.
  4. Run: people work from it, ideally as a checklist that records completion.
  5. Audit: sample completed runs to see where people deviate.
  6. Revise: update after each review date, tool change or incident.

What should an SEO audit SOP include?

An SEO audit SOP should define scope, tool settings, the checks to run, how findings are prioritised and how they're reported. The aim is that two people auditing the same site reach broadly the same priorities.

  • Inputs: client goals, access to Search Console and analytics, crawl limits and any staging URLs.
  • Crawl setup: user agent, JavaScript rendering, excluded paths and crawl speed agreed with the client.
  • Checks: indexability, canonicals, redirects, status codes, internal linking, page speed, structured data and duplicate content.
  • Prioritisation: impact versus effort, with a named owner for each fix (agency, client developer or CMS vendor).
  • Output: a findings sheet plus a short executive summary.
  • QA: a second person spot-checks the top findings before delivery.

Crawler choice affects the SOP's settings; compare technical SEO audit tools for large websites. Keep client logins out of the SOP itself and in a password manager instead.

Will your team actually keep SOPs up to date?

Only if updating them is somebody's job with a date attached. Based on the product information reviewed, review reminders and owner fields help, but no tool can make a busy account manager rewrite a procedure.

What's the verdict?

Choose Process Street or SweetProcess if you need SOPs run and recorded per client, Trainual if training is the bottleneck, and Scribe to speed up writing software steps. Use Notion, Confluence or Tettra when findability is the main problem, and add owners and review dates whichever you pick.

What else do agencies ask about SOP software?

Most remaining questions are about wiki versus checklist tools, review cadence and freelancer access. Use these as general guidance and confirm current features with each vendor.

Can we just keep SOPs in Google Docs?

You can, and many agencies start there. The problems appear later: no owner or review date, no way to see who followed the process, and search that mixes old drafts with current versions.

How often should SOPs be reviewed?

Set a review date on every SOP, with shorter cycles for procedures that depend on fast-changing tools such as ad platforms or analytics. Review immediately after any incident the SOP should have prevented.

Who should own an SOP?

The person who runs the process most often should draft and maintain it, with a manager approving changes. Ownership must be a named person, not a team.

Should freelancers have access to our SOPs?

Give them access to the procedures they need, with view-only permissions where possible. Keep anything containing client credentials or commercial terms out of SOPs entirely.

Do screen-capture guides replace written SOPs?

They're excellent for click-by-click software tasks but weak at explaining judgement, such as when to escalate. Combine captured steps with a short written section on purpose and decision points.
