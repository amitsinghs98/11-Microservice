Best Backlink Analysis Tools for Link Building Teams

A tool that reports more backlinks isn't automatically more useful; what a link team needs is clean referring-domain data, reliable new and lost alerts, and competitor gaps it can export into outreach. This guide compares the best backlink analysis tools on those jobs, plus the outreach tools that take over once prospects are found.

Which backlink analysis tool should you choose?

Choose Ahrefs if link research is the core of your work, Semrush if links are one job among several channels, and Majestic if you want a link-only specialist with its own trust metrics. Moz and SE Ranking are reasonable second opinions or budget options, and Search Console is the free check on your own site's links.

  • Link research as the main job: Ahrefs.
  • Links alongside keyword, PPC and audit work: Semrush.
  • A link-only specialist with trust and topic metrics: Majestic.
  • In-house teams that already use Moz metrics: Moz Link Explorer.
  • Agencies that want links, tracking and reports on one budget: SE Ranking.
  • Your own site's links, free: Google Search Console.
  • Managing outreach after prospects are found: BuzzStream or Pitchbox.

Which backlink metrics actually matter?

Referring domains matter more than total backlinks, and the relevance and traffic of those domains matter more than any vendor's authority score. New and lost links show momentum, and link intersect shows which sites link to competitors but not to you.

  • Referring domains: unique sites linking to you; a thousand links from one site is still one relationship.
  • Referring-domain quality: topical relevance, real organic traffic and editorial context, not just a score.
  • New and lost links: a dated feed of links gained and dropped, so you can react to lost links from important pages.
  • Link intersect or gap: domains linking to several competitors but not to you, which is the best-qualified prospect list.
  • Anchor text and target pages: where links point and what they say, which shows over-optimization and under-linked pages.
  • Link attributes: followed, nofollow, sponsored and user-generated links counted separately.

Why isn't a bigger link index the same as better data?

Index size counts how many links a vendor has crawled, including spam, scraped copies and pages nobody visits; it doesn't tell you whether the links that matter in your niche are found, or how quickly. For prospecting, freshness and accuracy on the sites you care about beat raw totals.

Every vendor crawls the web differently, so each will find links the others miss and keep some that no longer exist. That's why serious link teams check one tool's findings against another and against Search Console for their own site, rather than trusting a single headline number.

What does each tool do well, and when should you skip it?

Ahrefs and Semrush are the deepest general options, Majestic is the link-only specialist, and Moz and SE Ranking are best as second opinions or budget choices. The outreach tools only make sense once your analysis produces a prospect list worth managing.

Ahrefs: best when links are the core job

Site Explorer shows backlinks, referring domains, anchors and new and lost links, and Link Intersect finds sites linking to competitors but not to you. Lower tiers limit rows and some features, so compare them on the Ahrefs pricing page. Skip it if links are a minor part of your work and you'd rarely use the depth. Our Ahrefs alternatives by job list narrower options.

Semrush: best when links are one channel among several

Backlink Analytics covers referring domains, anchors and new and lost links, Backlink Gap compares competitors, and a backlink audit flags potentially toxic links. Plans are tiered on the Semrush plans page. Skip it if you want a link specialist and won't use the keyword, PPC or content modules. Our Semrush versus Ahrefs comparison covers the link workflow in detail.

Majestic: best link-only specialist

Majestic focuses entirely on links, with its own Trust Flow and Citation Flow metrics, topical categories for linking sites, and separate fresh and historic indexes. See the Majestic website for plans. Skip it if you need keyword research or audits in the same subscription.

Moz Link Explorer: best for teams already on Moz

Link Explorer reports linking domains, anchors, new and lost links and Moz's Domain Authority and Spam Score, which are Moz metrics, not Google signals. It's part of Moz Pro, priced on the Moz Pro pricing page. Skip it if your main need is high-volume prospect exports.

SE Ranking: best for budget link monitoring

SE Ranking includes backlink research and a monitor for links you've built, alongside tracking and reporting. Plans are on the SE Ranking subscription page. In our assessment it suits agencies monitoring client links more than teams doing heavy prospecting. Skip it if link building is your primary service.

Google Search Console: your own links, free

The Links report in Search Console lists top linking sites, linked pages and anchor text for a verified property. It shows nothing about competitors, so it's a check on your own profile rather than a research tool.

BuzzStream and Pitchbox: best for managing outreach

BuzzStream and Pitchbox manage prospect lists, contacts, outreach email and follow-up history for link campaigns. Neither has its own link index, so they depend on exports from the analysis tools above. Skip them if you send a handful of pitches a month; a spreadsheet will do.

Can an authority score tell you a link is worth having?

Not on its own: authority scores are each vendor's model of link popularity, and a high score can sit on a site with no relevant audience or a history of selling links. Use the score to filter a long list, then judge relevance, traffic and editorial context by looking at the page.

How do you run a competitor link-gap process?

Pick three to five true competitors, find domains that link to at least two of them but not to you, filter that list for relevance and quality, then export it with the linking page and context. That produces a prospect list based on proven willingness to link.

A hypothetical process for one client:

  1. Choose competitors: sites ranking for your target terms, not just business rivals.
  2. Run link intersect: list referring domains linking to two or more competitors but not to the client.
  3. Filter: remove spam, directories you'd never pursue, and sites with no relevant traffic.
  4. Classify: tag each prospect by link type, such as resource page, guest article, review or unlinked mention.
  5. Export: include the linking URL, the competitor page it points to and the anchor, so outreach can reference real context.
  6. Monitor: track new and lost links monthly and report wins through your SEO reporting software.

How does link analysis hand off to outreach?

The analysis tool finds and qualifies prospects; an outreach tool or email platform manages contacts, messages and follow-ups. The hand-off is an export, so check that the analysis tool exports linking URLs and context in a format your outreach tool imports.

What belongs on a backlink tool checklist?

Check referring-domain filters, new and lost history, link intersect, export limits and white-label reporting on the plan you'd actually buy. Then compare one client's profile across two tools and Search Console before you commit.

  • Can you filter by referring domain, link attribute, language and traffic?
  • How far back does new and lost history go, and how often does it update?
  • Is link intersect or backlink gap included on your tier?
  • How many rows can you export per report and per month?
  • How many users are included, and what does each extra seat cost?
  • Can you send branded link reports to clients?

What else do link builders ask?

The usual follow-ups are about toxic links, how often to check and whether one tool is enough. Short answers follow.

Should we disavow links that a tool flags as toxic?

Not automatically. Toxicity scores are vendor models; review flagged links by hand and consider disavowing only clear manipulative patterns, especially where a manual action is involved.

How often should we review a client's backlinks?

Monthly is enough for most clients, with alerts for lost links from important pages. During an active campaign, check new links weekly.

Is one backlink tool enough for an agency?

One paid tool plus Search Console is enough for most agencies. Link-building specialists often add a second index to catch links the first one misses.

What's the verdict?

Based on the product information reviewed, Ahrefs is the natural choice when links are the core job, Semrush when they're one channel among several, and Majestic when you want a link-only specialist with its own metrics. Moz and SE Ranking work as second opinions or budget monitors, and an outreach tool earns its place only once your gap process produces a real pipeline.

Judge every option by referring-domain quality and exports, not total link counts, and start with a trial on the vendor's official site using one client's competitor set.
