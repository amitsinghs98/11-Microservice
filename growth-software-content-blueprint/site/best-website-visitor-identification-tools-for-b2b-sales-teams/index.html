Which website visitor identification tool should a B2B sales team choose?

Choose a company-level tool with clear CRM sync, such as Dealfront, Snitcher or Leadinfo, unless you have a specific, legally reviewed reason to identify individuals. If your CRM is HubSpot or Pipedrive, try the built-in option first and upgrade only if it misses what you need.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • European buyers: Dealfront or Leadinfo.
  • Agencies and small teams wanting self-serve setup: Snitcher.
  • Sales-led teams wanting a managed service: Lead Forensics.
  • Identification tied to live chat and outreach: Warmly.
  • US-only person-level alerts, with legal sign-off: RB2B.
  • Already in the CRM: HubSpot or Pipedrive Web Visitors.

How does website visitor identification work, and where does it fail?

Most tools match a visitor's IP address, and sometimes cookies or other signals, to a company database, so they can name the organisation but rarely the person. Matches fail or mislead for home and mobile networks, VPNs, shared offices and ISPs.

That means a list of visiting companies is always partial and sometimes wrong: an IP range might belong to a co-working space, a parent company or a cloud provider. Ask each vendor how it assigns confidence, whether low-confidence matches are shown, and how it handles traffic from ISPs. Person-level identification goes further by inferring individuals, which is where accuracy and privacy risk both increase.

What are each tool's strengths and limitations?

Products are ordered by our criteria for a sales team: transparency about how companies are matched, CRM sync and alerting, then pricing accessibility. No vendor paid for inclusion or placement.

Dealfront: European B2B teams that want company identification plus firmographics

Dealfront, which absorbed the Leadfeeder product, identifies companies visiting your site, shows the pages they viewed, enriches them with company data, and syncs to CRMs. See plans on Dealfront’s pricing page.

Pricing model: tiered subscription; check what each plan limits on the pricing page.

Strengths: Filtering, page-level insight and CRM sync, with a European data focus.

Limitations: Company-level only by design, and match rates drop for remote and mobile traffic, as with every IP-based tool.

Skip it if: you specifically want individual-level identification.

Lead Forensics: sales-led teams that want a managed, contract-based service

Lead Forensics identifies businesses from website visits and provides contact details for people at those companies, along with alerts and CRM integration.

Pricing model: quote-based contracts; confirm term length and cancellation terms before signing.

Strengths: Packages identification with contact data in one service.

Limitations: Contract terms may be less flexible than self-serve tools, and contact suggestions are inferred from the company, not the visitor.

Skip it if: you want to try before committing to a contract.

Snitcher: agencies and small teams wanting simple, self-serve identification

Snitcher identifies companies behind website traffic, shows sessions and pages, and supports alerts, segments and CRM or Slack integration. Plans are on Snitcher’s pricing page.

Pricing model: self-serve tiers; check what each tier limits on the official page.

Strengths: Straightforward setup and a clear view of what each company looked at.

Limitations: Lighter on contact data and sales workflow than bundled platforms.

Skip it if: you need contacts and outreach in the same tool.

Leadinfo: European teams that want visitor ID inside their CRM

Leadinfo identifies companies visiting your website and pushes them into CRMs and inboxes with filters for fit. See Leadinfo pricing.

Pricing model: tiered subscription; check plan limits on the official page.

Strengths: A European focus and simple CRM workflows.

Limitations: Fewer advanced features than larger platforms.

Skip it if: most of your traffic and buyers are in North America.

RB2B: US-focused teams willing to manage person-level privacy risk

RB2B says it identifies individual US-based visitors and matches them to professional profiles, delivering alerts to Slack. Read its pricing and plan details and privacy documentation closely.

Pricing model: tiered plans; check current tiers and limits on the official page.

Strengths: Person-level alerts give reps a name rather than just a company.

Limitations: Person-level identification raises consent and legal questions, and matching confidence for an individual is lower than a dashboard name suggests. It is positioned for US traffic, not EU or UK visitors.

Skip it if: you sell into Europe or your legal team requires consent before identification.

Warmly: teams that want identification tied to chat and outreach

Warmly combines company and person-level visitor identification with intent signals, chat and automated outreach. Plans are listed on Warmly’s pricing page.

Pricing model: tiered plans; check which features each tier includes.

Strengths: Acts on visits in the moment, not just in a report.

Limitations: Automation makes it easy to contact people too early; validation steps are your responsibility.

Skip it if: you only want a list of companies for weekly review.

HubSpot: HubSpot portals that want visitor companies inside the CRM

HubSpot's Breeze Intelligence adds company identification and buyer-intent views inside HubSpot, alongside enrichment.

Pricing model: tied to HubSpot plans and credits; check current packaging.

Strengths: No extra integration, and visitor companies sit next to existing records.

Limitations: Less page-level and filtering depth than dedicated tools.

Skip it if: you're not on HubSpot.

Pipedrive Web Visitors: Pipedrive users who want a light, in-CRM option

Pipedrive has offered Web Visitors as a paid add-on rather than a core-plan feature; confirm current packaging on its pricing page, which we couldn't re-check for this draft. It surfaces companies visiting your site inside the Pipedrive workflow.

Pricing model: a paid add-on to Pipedrive plans; check the add-on price on the official page.

Strengths: Convenient for small Pipedrive teams.

Limitations: Only relevant if Pipedrive is your CRM, and less detailed than dedicated tools.

Skip it if: you use any other CRM.

What privacy and consent questions should you ask?

Ask what data the tool collects, on what legal basis, whether a cookie or tracking script requires consent under your banner, and whether it identifies individuals. Rules differ by region, so involve whoever owns privacy before installing a script.

In the EU and UK, tracking scripts and person-level identification usually need careful consent design; our roundup of consent and preference management platforms covers the banner side. Document which vendor processes what, and update your privacy notice. This page isn't legal advice.

How should sales validate a visit before reaching out?

Treat an identified visit as a reason to research, not a reason to email. A rep should confirm the company fits your target profile, check the pages viewed and existing CRM activity, and find a plausible contact before any outreach.

  1. Fit check: industry, size and region match your ideal customer profile.
  2. Signal check: pricing, product or comparison pages viewed, more than once, recently. A single blog visit isn't intent.
  3. Match check: is the IP plausibly the company's own network, not an ISP or co-working space?
  4. CRM check: is there an open deal, an existing customer or an owner? Route accordingly; see lead routing and scheduling software.
  5. Outreach: lead with relevance to the company's situation, never with "I saw you on our website".

What mistakes do teams make with visitor identification?

The commonest mistake is piping every identified company to sales, which floods reps with students, competitors, suppliers and existing customers. The second is ignoring privacy review because the tool is "only company-level".

Set exclusion lists for customers, competitors and ISPs, cap alerts to high-intent pages, and review match quality monthly.

What else do buyers ask about visitor identification?

Questions usually cover accuracy, legality and price. Short answers follow.

Can visitor identification tools tell me who a visitor is?

Most identify the company, not the person, because they match IP addresses to organisations. Tools that claim person-level identification rely on inferred data, which brings lower confidence and more privacy risk.

Is website visitor identification legal?

It depends on the method, the data collected and where visitors are located. Company-level IP matching and person-level identification are treated differently, so check with whoever owns privacy and consent at your company.

Why are some identified companies internet providers?

Remote workers and mobile users browse through ISP or carrier networks, so their IP addresses resolve to the provider rather than the employer. Good tools let you filter these out.

Should identified visitors go straight into sequences?

No. Validate fit, intent and CRM status first, and route only qualified accounts to a rep, who decides on outreach.

How do I compare match quality between vendors?

Install trials on the same site for the same period, then compare how many known customers and open opportunities each tool identifies correctly.

What's the verdict?

For most B2B sales teams, company-level identification with good CRM sync and strict validation rules is the sensible starting point, and the built-in HubSpot or Pipedrive option may be enough. Person-level tools can work in US-only contexts, but only with legal review and a rule that a human validates before outreach.
