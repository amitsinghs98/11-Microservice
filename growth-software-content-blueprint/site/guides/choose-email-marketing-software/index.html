How to Choose Email Marketing Software by List Size and Send Volume

Two numbers do most of the work in an email platform decision: how many people you can legitimately email, and how often you will email them. This guide turns those numbers, plus automation and integration needs, into a shortlist.

Why feature lists mislead

Almost every email platform offers a drag-and-drop editor, forms, segmentation and some automation. Comparing feature grids rarely separates them. What does separate them is how the bill grows as your list and cadence grow, how deep the automation goes before you hit a tier wall, and how painful it is to leave.

A 2,000-contact list sent to monthly and a 2,000-contact list sent to three times a week are different workloads. They can land on different platforms, or on different tiers of the same one.

The five-step method

Step 1: count active contacts, not total records

Export your current list and remove unsubscribed, hard-bounced and never-confirmed addresses. What remains is closer to what most vendors bill on. Definitions vary: MailerLite's pricing FAQ, checked 4 November 2026, says it bills on active subscribers and counts any address that was active at any point in the billing cycle, even if deleted later. Ask each vendor how they count.

Then forecast 12 months ahead. A list that grows by 150 a month crosses common tier boundaries within the first year.

Step 2: estimate monthly sends

Monthly sends = active contacts × campaigns per month + automated emails. Automated volume is easy to forget: a five-email welcome series for 200 new subscribers adds 1,000 sends a month on its own.

Step 3: match the pricing model

Use the two numbers to see which model is cheaper for you; the section below explains the trade-off.

Step 4: rate your automation complexity

  • Level 1: broadcasts plus a single welcome sequence.
  • Level 2: several sequences with tags, simple branching and date triggers.
  • Level 3: event-driven journeys using purchase, product or CRM data, with multi-branch logic and goals.

Level 1 fits nearly any tool. Level 3 narrows the field to platforms covered in our lifecycle email automation shortlist, and often to paid mid-tier plans.

Step 5: list required integrations and migration effort

Write down the systems that must sync: store, CRM, forms, webinar tool, billing. For each, note direction (one-way or two-way) and what data must flow. Then list what you would have to rebuild if you moved: automations, templates, segments and form embeds. Automations almost never transfer between platforms and must be rebuilt by hand.

Contact-based versus volume-based pricing

Contact-based pricing charges by the size of your list, with a send allowance attached. Mailchimp's pricing page, checked 4 November 2026, sets the monthly send limit on Standard plans at 12 times the maximum contact count of your tier and on Premium at 15 times. MailerLite's Comfort plan gives 10 times the ceiling of your subscriber tier each month, while its Power plan lists unlimited monthly emails under a fair-use policy (checked 4 November 2026).

Volume-based pricing charges by emails sent. Brevo's paid plans are sold by monthly email volume; its Starter plan was listed "from 5,000 emails per month" on the pricing page checked 4 November 2026. This favours large lists that are emailed infrequently.

Which pricing model tends to favour which workload
Workload Usually favours Why
Large list, monthly sends Volume-based You pay for the few emails sent, not for dormant contacts
Small list, frequent sends Contact-based Send multipliers usually cover several campaigns a week
Fast-growing list Either, with care Check whether you are auto-upgraded or paused at a tier boundary
Mixed marketing and transactional Volume-based or a separate transactional service Transactional sends can inflate a contact-based bill or limit

Seats are the third cost axis. Some platforms price additional users separately, which matters for agencies and teams with several editors. Our marketing automation pricing guide explains how contact, send, seat and event units combine.

Worked example

This is a hypothetical scenario. A B2B consultancy has 3,400 records; after removing unsubscribes and bounces, 2,600 are active. It sends a fortnightly newsletter (2 campaigns a month), runs a four-email onboarding sequence for about 80 new subscribers a month, and needs a two-way CRM sync.

  • Newsletter sends: 2,600 × 2 = 5,200
  • Onboarding sends: 80 × 4 = 320
  • Total: about 5,520 sends a month, rising to about 6,500 within a year as the list grows

Interpretation: the list is mid-sized and the cadence moderate, so either model could work. Automation is Level 2, but the CRM sync is the real filter. The consultancy should shortlist platforms with a native two-way integration for its CRM, price each at the 3,000- and 5,000-contact tiers, and compare those with volume-based plans at 6,000 to 7,000 sends. Because it may later want CRM-driven journeys, the HubSpot Marketing Hub versus ActiveCampaign analysis is worth reading before committing.

Platform selection checklist

  • Active contacts today and in 12 months
  • Monthly sends, including automated email
  • How the vendor counts contacts (active, total, or cumulative in a cycle)
  • What happens at the limit: automatic upgrade, overage charge or paused sending
  • Automation level needed (1, 2 or 3) and the cheapest tier that supports it
  • Required integrations, sync direction and data fields
  • Seats needed and their cost
  • Branding removal, dedicated IP or deliverability support, if needed
  • Export options for contacts, consent records and engagement history
  • Migration help offered and the rebuild work you will still do

Common mistakes

  • Pricing at today's list size instead of next year's.
  • Ignoring automated sends when checking against a send allowance.
  • Paying for contacts who have not opened or clicked in a year. A sunset policy is often cheaper than a tier upgrade.
  • Choosing a newsletter-first tool and later needing ecommerce event data; store owners should start with our guide to ecommerce email platforms.
  • Underestimating migration. Budget time to rebuild every automation and re-test every form.

FAQs

Does a bigger list always mean a bigger bill?

On contact-based plans, yes. On volume-based plans, only if you email the bigger list more. Either way, removing long-inactive contacts lowers cost and usually improves engagement metrics.

How far ahead should I forecast?

Twelve months is a sensible horizon. It covers an annual contract and captures at least one likely tier change.

Should transactional email run through the same platform?

It can, but many teams separate it so receipts and password resets are not affected by marketing reputation. The transactional email services guide compares dedicated options.
