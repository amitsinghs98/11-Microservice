Which B2B contact database should you choose?

Choose by the market you sell into and the number of contacts you'll really use each month: Apollo.io for small teams starting outbound on a budget, ZoomInfo for larger teams that want data, intent and enrichment from one contract, and Cognism or Dealfront when European coverage matters most. Lusha, Seamless.AI and UpLead suit lighter, credit-led use.

  • Large revenue teams buying a full platform: ZoomInfo.
  • Small teams that want data and sequencing together: Apollo.io.
  • Teams selling into Europe that prioritize compliance support: Cognism.
  • Reps who need quick contact lookups from LinkedIn or the CRM: Lusha.
  • Search-led prospecting on a self-serve plan: Seamless.AI.
  • Buyers who want simple credit-based list building: UpLead.
  • European company data plus website visitor identification: Dealfront.

What's the difference between contact data and account data?

Account data describes the company (industry, headcount, location, revenue band, technology used), while contact data describes the people in it (name, role, seniority, email, phone). You build target lists with account data and reach people with contact data, so check a vendor's strength on both.

The distinction matters when you compare plans. Some tools price account search generously and meter contact reveals tightly; others bundle both into one credit. Teams doing account-based selling often need deep account data and only a few contacts per account, while high-volume outbound needs the reverse. When records you already hold need filling in rather than new records found, our lead enrichment software shortlist is the closer fit.

How do vendors verify data, and how fresh is it?

Vendors combine public sources, contributed data, automated email checks and, in some cases, human research or phone verification, and each refreshes records on its own cycle. Because no buyer can audit that process directly, the only accuracy evidence that counts is a test on a sample of your own target accounts.

Ask each vendor three things: how a record's last-verified date is shown, how often emails are re-checked, and what happens to credits spent on a contact that bounces. Run every exported email through a separate checker before high-volume sends; our email verification and list cleaning tools page compares options.

What is each database good at, and where is it weak?

Each database earns its place for a specific buyer, and each has a weakness you should test before committing. Fit judgments are our editorial assessment based on official product information.

ZoomInfo: best for larger teams buying a full data platform

ZoomInfo combines contact and company data with intent signals, enrichment into the CRM and workflow features, sold through quotes starting from the ZoomInfo pricing page. It's built for teams that want one vendor for most data needs.

Limitation: annual commitments are a big step for small teams. Skip it if you have fewer than a handful of reps; our ZoomInfo alternatives for SMB sales teams covers lighter options.

Apollo.io: best for small teams starting outbound

Apollo.io pairs a searchable contact database with sequencing and a CRM sync, so one tool covers finding and contacting prospects. A free plan and credit-based paid seats are set out on the Apollo.io pricing page.

Limitation: coverage and accuracy vary by market, so sample-test your segments. Skip it if you only want data and already run sequencing elsewhere. Our Apollo.io alternatives page compares data-first options.

Cognism: best for teams selling into Europe

Cognism positions itself around European B2B coverage, phone data and compliance support, including checks against do-not-call registers. Pricing is quote-based via the Cognism pricing page.

Limitation: without a public price, you can't compare cost per contact until you're in a sales process. Skip it if you sell mainly into North America and want self-serve pricing.

Lusha: best for reps who need quick contact lookups

Lusha focuses on fast reveals of emails and phone numbers through a browser extension and CRM integrations, with a free plan and tiered credit plans on the Lusha pricing page.

Limitation: account-level research and intent are lighter than in the bigger platforms. Skip it if you need to build large, filtered target-account lists.

Seamless.AI: best for search-led prospecting on self-serve plans

Seamless.AI searches for contacts and companies on demand and exports them to your CRM or spreadsheets, with tiers on the Seamless.AI pricing page.

Limitation: as with any search-led tool, verify exported emails before sending at volume. Skip it if you need documented European compliance support.

UpLead: best for straightforward credit-based list building

UpLead offers filtered search across contact and company data and charges by credit, which keeps cost predictable for occasional list builds. Plans are on the UpLead pricing page.

Limitation: fewer sequencing and workflow features than Apollo.io. Skip it if you want prospecting and outreach in one tool.

Dealfront: best for European company data with visitor signals

Dealfront combines European B2B company and contact data with website visitor identification, so you can build lists from accounts already showing interest. Plans are on the Dealfront pricing page.

Limitation: its focus is outside North America. Skip it if your market is mainly the US.

How do credits change what a contact really costs?

The real price of a contact is the plan cost divided by the number of credits that produce a usable, verified, in-market contact, not the number of credits you buy. Unused credits that expire, reveals that bounce and duplicates of records you already own all raise that number.

Here's a hypothetical illustration, not any vendor's pricing. If a plan costs a fixed amount for 1,000 credits, you use 800 of them, and 75% of revealed emails pass verification, you've paid for 600 usable contacts, so each usable contact costs about two-thirds more than the sticker rate of plan price divided by 1,000. Ask each vendor:

  • Does a credit cover an email, a phone number or both?
  • Are credits refunded or replaced when a contact bounces?
  • Do unused credits roll over, and for how long?
  • Does exporting a record you already revealed cost a second credit?

What does a sound list-building workflow look like?

Build the account list first, pick two or three roles per account, reveal only those contacts, verify them, deduplicate against your CRM and suppression lists, then hand them to outreach. Revealing whole departments before you've qualified the account is where most credits are wasted.

  1. Define the ideal customer profile: industry, headcount band, region and any technology signals.
  2. Filter accounts and drop any already in your CRM as open deals or customers.
  3. Choose target roles and seniority for each account.
  4. Reveal contacts for those roles only.
  5. Verify emails and check phone numbers against applicable do-not-call lists.
  6. Deduplicate and apply your suppression list before import.
  7. Push to your sequencing tool with source and lawful-basis fields set; our sales engagement platform comparison covers that step.

Which data-protection and suppression checks should you run?

Confirm how each vendor sources and documents its data, honor opt-outs across every tool, and keep a central suppression list that blocks both exports and sends. If you contact people in the EU or UK, record your lawful basis and be ready to answer data-subject requests quickly.

  • Ask the vendor for its data-sourcing and privacy documentation.
  • Keep one suppression list and sync it to the database, CRM and sending tools.
  • Store source and date of collection on each contact you import.
  • Give every outbound email a clear way to opt out, and process it promptly.
  • Delete or refresh contacts you haven't engaged within a set period.
  • Check phone outreach against applicable do-not-call registers.

What else do buyers ask about B2B contact data?

The remaining questions are usually about free plans, accuracy claims and how many tools you need. Short answers follow.

Are free B2B contact database plans worth using?

They're useful for testing coverage in your market before you pay. Their credit limits are too low for steady outbound, so treat them as a sample, not a supply.

Should you trust a vendor's published accuracy rate?

Treat it as marketing until your own sample confirms it. Test a few hundred contacts from your real target segment and measure bounces and wrong numbers yourself.

Do you need more than one contact database?

Small teams usually don't. Larger teams sometimes add a second source for a region or role where the first is weak, often through an enrichment tool rather than a second full contract.

Is buying B2B contact data legal?

It depends on where your prospects are and how you use the data. Many teams rely on legitimate interest in the EU and UK, which still requires a documented assessment, transparency and an easy opt-out, so take local legal advice.
