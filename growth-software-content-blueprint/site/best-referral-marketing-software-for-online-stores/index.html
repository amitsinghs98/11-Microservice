Which referral platform should you choose?

If you want a standalone referral programme running quickly on Shopify, start with ReferralCandy. If loyalty and referrals should share one points balance, use Smile.io or Yotpo; if you need testing, attribution and fraud depth at scale, shortlist Friendbuy, Mention Me, Talkable or Extole.

  • Quick standalone programme for a small store: ReferralCandy.
  • Referrals inside a loyalty programme: Smile.io or Yotpo.
  • Growing DTC brands wanting referral and loyalty from a specialist: Friendbuy.
  • Brands that want to test offers and sharing methods: Mention Me.
  • Large retailers with complex campaigns: Talkable or Extole.

Who is this list for, and how do referrals differ from affiliates?

It's for direct-to-consumer and online store teams who want existing customers to bring in new ones in exchange for a reward. Referral programmes reward customers for personal recommendations; affiliate programmes pay publishers, creators and coupon sites a commission for traffic and sales.

The difference shapes the software. Referral tools focus on post-purchase prompts, share links, unique codes and double-sided rewards. Affiliate tools focus on partner onboarding, commission rules and payouts. If you mainly work with creators and publishers, read our guide to affiliate tracking software for ecommerce instead. What are the strengths and limits of each referral platform?

Store apps get a programme live quickly with sensible defaults; enterprise platforms add testing, attribution and governance. Pick the lightest tool that covers your fraud and reporting needs.

ReferralCandy: best for a quick standalone programme

ReferralCandy's pricing page lists plans that combine a monthly fee with a success fee on referred sales. Its help center explains that existing customers can't be referred, while suspicious patterns such as several referred purchases from one IP address are flagged for you to review, and the decision to withhold a reward stays with the merchant.

Strengths: documented, automatic fraud checks. Limitations: the success fee makes a successful programme more expensive. Skip it if referrals already drive a large share of revenue and a flat fee would cost less.

Smile.io: best for referrals inside a loyalty programme

Smile.io bundles referrals with points and VIP programmes, so referral rewards can be paid in points. That's simpler for customers and keeps one vendor. Limitations: check whether its referral analytics and fraud controls go far enough for your programme. Skip it if referrals are your main acquisition channel. See smile.io and our loyalty platform shortlist.

Yotpo: best alongside loyalty and reviews

Yotpo offers referrals within its loyalty product and alongside reviews and UGC. Limitations: it's most cost-effective when you use several Yotpo products. Skip it if you only want referrals. See yotpo.com.

Friendbuy: best for growing DTC brands

Friendbuy specialises in referral and loyalty programmes for consumer brands. Check its integrations against your store and email platform. Limitations: more configuration than a plug-and-play app. Skip it if you need a programme live today with defaults. See friendbuy.com.

Mention Me: best for testing offers and sharing methods

Mention Me focuses on referral experimentation, including testing reward offers and sharing methods. Limitations: it's aimed at established brands with steady order volume. Skip it if you have too few orders to run meaningful tests. See mention-me.com.

Talkable: best for large retail programmes

Talkable serves larger retailers that run several referral campaigns with different offers, placements and audiences. Limitations: enterprise pricing and onboarding. Skip it if you're a single small store. See talkable.com.

Extole: best for enterprise referral and advocacy programmes

Extole runs referral and advocacy programmes for large brands. Limitations: heavy for small teams. Skip it if one person will run the programme part-time. See extole.com.

How do links, codes and attribution windows affect results?

Links track clicks and cookies, codes survive when a friend buys on another device, and most programmes need both. The attribution window, meaning how long after a click a purchase still counts, decides how many referred orders you credit and pay for.

Ask each vendor what happens when a referred friend also uses an influencer or affiliate code, because two programmes may claim the same order. Our list of coupon and discount attribution tools covers that overlap. Trigger the share prompt on the order confirmation page and in a post-purchase email, when satisfaction is highest; for subscription products, consider waiting until after the second order.

Do referral programmes bring in new customers or reward existing ones?

Some referred orders come from people who'd have bought anyway, so platform-reported referral revenue overstates the effect. Fraud checks catch obvious self-referrals, but not every overlap with existing demand.

Which metrics should you track after launch?

Track participation, share rate, referred conversion, cost per referred customer and the quality of referred customers over time. Revenue alone hides whether you're paying rewards for orders you'd have won anyway.

  • Participation rate: customers who saw the offer and opened their share link.
  • Share rate: participants who actually shared.
  • Referred conversion: friends who clicked and bought.
  • Cost per referred customer: rewards plus software and success fees, divided by new customers.
  • Flagged-referral rate: share of referrals held for fraud review.
  • Referred repeat rate: do referred customers buy again as often as others?

What mistakes do stores make when buying referral software?

The common mistake is choosing on reward flexibility while ignoring fraud review and attribution overlap. The second is a reward so small nobody shares, or so large it attracts abuse.

  • Paying rewards before the friend's return window closes.
  • Running referral and affiliate programmes without a rule for orders both claim.
  • Hiding the programme so only a few customers ever see it.
  • Ignoring the success fee when forecasting cost at scale.

What else do store owners ask about referral software?

These questions come up when a store is designing its first programme. Short answers follow.

Should rewards be cash, discounts or points?

Discounts and points keep value in your store and cost you margin rather than cash. Cash or gift cards can motivate more sharing but cost more per referral.

Should both the referrer and the friend get a reward?

Double-sided rewards are common because the friend has a reason to buy and the referrer doesn't feel they're selling. Test the split against your margin.

How do I stop people referring themselves?

Use a platform that blocks matching emails, existing customers and repeated details automatically, and review flagged referrals before releasing rewards.

Can referral software replace an affiliate programme?

No. Referral tools reward customers for personal recommendations, while affiliate tools manage publishers and creators with commissions and payouts.

What's the verdict?

Small stores should start with ReferralCandy or the referral feature in their loyalty app, then move to Friendbuy or Mention Me once they want testing and deeper attribution. Large retailers with several brands or regions should compare Talkable and Extole on fraud controls, attribution rules and contract terms.
