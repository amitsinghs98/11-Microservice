Which pop-up and onsite engagement tool should you choose?

Choose by where the lead goes next: OptinMonster or Poptin if you run a content or B2B site and need many trigger types, Wisepops if you care most about frequency rules and controlled tests, and Privy or Klaviyo sign-up forms if the lead goes straight into a Shopify email or SMS program. If your form tool or CRM already has a decent pop-up, start there and only buy a specialist when its targeting runs out.

  • Most trigger and targeting options for content and B2B sites: OptinMonster.
  • Frequency control and campaign testing: Wisepops.
  • Shopify stores that want pop-ups, email and SMS in one app: Privy.
  • Brands already sending email through Klaviyo: Klaviyo sign-up forms.
  • Smaller sites wanting a straightforward builder: Poptin.

Do pop-ups capture more leads than they cost in annoyance?

Sometimes, and only when they're targeted, rare and relevant to the page. The vendors' own guidance stresses frequency rules for a reason: an untargeted pop-up on every page view trades short-term sign-ups for a worse experience, and nothing in the product documentation reviewed proves the trade pays off for your site.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

OptinMonster and Poptin win on breadth of triggers, Wisepops on governance of frequency and testing, and Privy and Klaviyo on keeping ecommerce leads in one system. The weak spots are mostly the reverse: breadth tools can tempt you into too many campaigns, and ecommerce suites are thinner outside their own channel.

OptinMonster: best for many triggers on content and B2B sites

OptinMonster builds pop-ups, slide-ins, floating bars and inline forms with rule-based display. Its documentation describes an exit-intent rule with adjustable sensitivity, and the product offers device-based targeting so you can show a lighter format on phones. That's useful for the brief's mobile-control requirement. Limitations: aggressive exit-intent settings can fire on accidental cursor movement, and the number of rules makes it easy to build overlapping campaigns. Skip it if you run Shopify and want pop-up sign-ups to land in the same tool that sends your email.

Wisepops: best for frequency control and controlled tests

Wisepops covers pop-ups, bars, embeds and notification feeds. Its FAQ covers display-frequency settings and how it handles several campaigns that match one page. Wisepops also promotes campaign testing; check whether a holdout group that never sees the campaign is available, because that's the cleanest way to answer whether a pop-up earns its place. Limitations: holdout tests need enough traffic to read, so low-traffic B2B sites may wait a long time for an answer. Skip it if you only need one newsletter pop-up.

Privy: best for Shopify stores combining pop-ups, email and SMS

Privy positions itself as an email, SMS and pop-up app for Shopify. Its displays page describes the pop-up and bar formats it offers, with sign-ups feeding the same app's email and SMS audience. Limitations: it's built around ecommerce, so B2B qualification logic and CRM routing are not its focus, and discount-led offers can attract low-intent sign-ups. Skip it if you sell services or software rather than products.

Klaviyo sign-up forms: best when you already send through Klaviyo

Klaviyo's built-in forms cover pop-up, flyout, full-page and embedded formats. Its help center documents targeting and behavior settings, such as display triggers, rules for when a form reappears and device targeting. A separate article covers optimizing pop-ups and flyouts for mobile. Limitations: it's a form layer for one email platform, so you won't get the campaign library or testing depth of a specialist. Skip it if your list lives in a CRM or a different email tool.

Poptin: best for smaller sites wanting a simple builder

Poptin offers pop-ups and embedded forms with a set of triggers and display rules. On its pop-ups page it promotes exit-intent and targeting options. Limitations: exit intent works differently on phones, so check its mobile options, and confirm which targeting rules your plan includes before you build campaigns around them. Skip it if you need enterprise governance or SSO.

What suppression rules stop repeat annoyance?

Suppress every campaign for visitors who already converted, back off for a set number of days after a dismissal, and never let two campaigns compete for the same visitor on the same page view. Those three rules prevent most of the damage a pop-up program can do.

A practical rule set, adapted to your traffic:

  1. Converted: hide all list-building campaigns from known subscribers, logged-in users and existing customers.
  2. Dismissed: wait several days before showing the same campaign again, and longer before showing any pop-up to someone who has dismissed two.
  3. One at a time: set a priority so only one interruptive format appears per page view.
  4. Context: exclude checkout, pricing-to-demo paths, support pages and legal pages, where an interruption costs more than it gains.
  5. Entry source: don't greet paid-ad visitors with an offer that contradicts the ad.

Document the rules alongside the campaigns so whoever adds the next pop-up inherits them. If you're also tailoring content by audience, our guide to B2B website personalization software covers non-interruptive alternatives.

What mobile, consent and performance trade-offs should you check?

On mobile, prefer small slide-ins or bars over full-screen overlays, and test exit intent carefully because phones have no cursor. For consent and speed, check that the tool's script loads only after the visitor's consent choices allow it, and that it doesn't shift the page layout when it appears.

Exit intent on phones is approximated with proxies such as scroll or back-button behavior, so read each vendor's mobile documentation and consider time or scroll triggers instead. Full-screen mobile interstitials that cover content can also frustrate visitors arriving from search, so keep mobile formats easy to dismiss.

Pop-up scripts are third-party JavaScript, so they belong in the same review as analytics tags. How should you measure whether a pop-up works?

Measure qualified leads and revenue per visitor against a holdout group, not the pop-up's own conversion rate. A pop-up that converts well but lowers order value or lead quality is a net loss.

Track impressions, dismissals, submissions and later unsubscribes for each campaign, then join submissions to CRM or ecommerce outcomes. What mistakes do buyers make with pop-up software?

The common mistakes are launching without suppression rules, judging success on submissions alone and running several tools that each show their own pop-up. Each one is avoidable at setup.

  • Installing a pop-up app, an email platform's form and a chat widget that all interrupt the same visit.
  • Using the same offer on every page instead of matching it to the content.
  • Collecting fields the sales team never uses; a short form plus a later qualification step often works better, as covered in our B2B lead capture form builders guide.
  • Forgetting accessibility: overlays need keyboard focus management and a clearly labelled close control.

What else do buyers ask about pop-up software?

These short answers cover the questions that come up most when teams plan an onsite capture program. Each one points back to the suppression and measurement rules above.

Is exit intent reliable on mobile?

Not fully. Phones have no cursor, so tools approximate exit intent with scroll or back-button behavior, so time or scroll triggers are often a safer choice on mobile.

How often should a pop-up appear to the same visitor?

Rarely. Hide it after a sign-up, wait several days after a dismissal and show only one interruptive campaign per page view, then adjust using a holdout test.

Do pop-ups hurt SEO?

They can hurt the experience of search visitors if they cover content on mobile. Keep mobile formats small and easy to dismiss, and avoid layout shift when they load.

Do I need a separate pop-up tool if my email platform has forms?

Not always. Start with your email platform's forms and buy a specialist only when you need more triggers, campaign priority or controlled experiments.

What's the verdict?

For most B2B and content sites, OptinMonster or Wisepops gives the most control, with Wisepops the better pick if testing and frequency governance matter most. Ecommerce teams should start with the forms in Privy or Klaviyo, because keeping capture and messaging in one system usually matters more than extra trigger types.
