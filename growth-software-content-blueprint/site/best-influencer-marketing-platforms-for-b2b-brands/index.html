Best Influencer Marketing Platforms for B2B Brands

B2B influencer marketing platforms should help you find people your buyers already trust, such as practitioners, analysts and niche newsletter writers, not just accounts with large followings. Onalytica and Favikon are the most B2B-oriented starting points, while Traackr and CreatorIQ suit brands running larger, governed creator programs.

Which B2B influencer platform should you choose?

Pick the tool that finds experts on the channels your buyers use, which for most B2B categories means LinkedIn, podcasts, newsletters and YouTube rather than Instagram or TikTok. Consumer-oriented discovery databases can still help with some categories, but they rank by reach, which is rarely what B2B campaigns need.

  • Finding recognized experts on a topic, including analysts and practitioners: Onalytica.
  • Finding and vetting LinkedIn creators: Favikon.
  • Global brands managing long-term relationships across markets: Traackr.
  • Enterprise teams that need governance, compliance and reporting across many campaigns: CreatorIQ.
  • Teams running outreach and payments for many mid-size creators: Upfluence.
  • Amplifying an expert's LinkedIn post to your target accounts: LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads.

Who is this list for, and how does B2B discovery differ from consumer reach buying?

This list is for B2B marketing teams that want credible outside voices to explain, review or co-create content about their category. Consumer influencer buying optimizes for reach and engagement at a cost per view, while B2B discovery optimizes for relevance and expertise, often with small audiences that include your exact buyers.

That shift changes the workflow. Instead of shortlisting dozens of creators by follower count, you research a few experts by topic authority, check who actually follows them, and build longer partnerships such as webinars, reports, podcast appearances or advisory roles. What are the strengths and limits of each platform?

For most B2B teams the realistic stack is a discovery tool (Onalytica or Favikon), a spreadsheet or CRM for relationships, and LinkedIn ads to amplify the content experts create. Broader creator platforms are worth their cost only when you run many creators across markets.

Onalytica: best for topic-based expert discovery

As publicly positioned (still to be confirmed on its official site), Onalytica specializes in B2B influencer marketing, finding experts by topic across social channels and other sources, with services to help run programs. It suits enterprise technology and professional services brands that want analysts, practitioners and executives. Skip it if you want a self-serve tool for quick creator campaigns.

Favikon: best for LinkedIn creator discovery

Favikon focuses on creator discovery and rankings with strong LinkedIn coverage, alongside other networks, plus audience and authenticity signals. It's a practical way to build a shortlist of LinkedIn voices in a niche. Skip it if you need contracting, payments and approvals inside the same tool.

Traackr: best for long-term programs across markets

Traackr is an influencer relationship and performance platform for brands managing creators over time and across regions, with discovery, relationship records and spend reporting. It's historically associated with consumer brands, so confirm coverage of the channels your B2B buyers use. Skip it if you'll only work with a handful of experts.

CreatorIQ: best for enterprise governance and reporting

CreatorIQ is an enterprise creator management platform with discovery, workflow, compliance controls and reporting across many teams and brands. It fits large companies with central governance needs. Skip it if your influencer budget is one line in a demand generation plan.

Upfluence: best for outreach and payments at volume

Upfluence combines creator search, outreach, campaign management and payments, with ecommerce integrations. B2B teams with large creator programs can use its workflow, but its data leans toward consumer creators. Skip it if your priority is finding niche experts.

Modash: a consumer discovery tool B2B teams should check carefully

Modash is positioned for creator discovery on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube with audience data. That helps for B2B categories with a strong YouTube presence, such as developer tools or creative software, but LinkedIn isn't among the networks it's known for, so confirm coverage before relying on it for LinkedIn experts. Skip it if LinkedIn is your main channel.

LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads: best for amplifying expert posts

LinkedIn's ad products include Thought Leader Ads, which promote posts from individual members as sponsored content, with the member's approval. That lets you put an expert's own words in front of your target accounts. Confirm current eligibility rules in Campaign Manager before planning around external experts. How should you score a B2B influencer for relevance and expertise?

Score each candidate on whether your buyers follow them and whether their expertise is real, then on safety and fit; reach comes last. A simple 1-to-5 score on each criterion, with written evidence, keeps the shortlist honest.

  1. Topic authority: do they publish original work on your category, such as research, talks, courses or a book?
  2. Audience match: are their followers in your target job titles, industries and regions? Ask for audience data where the platform doesn't show it.
  3. Engagement quality: are comments substantive questions from practitioners, or generic praise?
  4. Independence and credibility: how often do they promote vendors, and do they disclose it clearly?
  5. Brand safety: any past statements, conflicts or competitor ties that could create risk?
  6. Format fit: do they do what you need, such as webinars, reports, podcasts or LinkedIn posts?
  7. Reach: audience size, used only to break ties.

Do B2B influencer campaigns produce pipeline, or just awareness?

They can influence pipeline, but the effect is slow and hard to attribute, because buyers see an expert's content months before they talk to sales. Vendor dashboards report reach and engagement well and pipeline poorly.

What should a B2B influencer agreement cover?

Cover deliverables, disclosure, content rights and exclusivity in writing before any content is made. B2B experts often reuse their own work in talks and courses, so agree who can reuse what, for how long and in which channels.

  • Disclosure: paid relationships must be disclosed clearly, including on LinkedIn and in podcasts.
  • Usage rights: whether you can run their content as ads, quote it in sales decks or republish it, and for how long.
  • Review process: what you can check for accuracy without editing their opinion.
  • Exclusivity: a narrow, time-limited competitor restriction, if any.

What else do B2B marketers ask about influencer platforms?

These answers cover the practical questions B2B teams raise before their first expert campaign. Confirm data coverage with each vendor, because channel support varies.

Do B2B influencers need large followings?

No. An expert with a small audience made up of your buyers is usually worth more than a large general audience. Score relevance and expertise first.

Which channels matter most for B2B influencer marketing?

LinkedIn, podcasts, newsletters, YouTube and industry events matter most for most B2B categories. Check which of these a platform's data covers before buying.

Should we pay B2B experts or offer exposure?

Pay for work that takes their time, such as content, webinars or research, and disclose it. Exposure alone rarely sustains a relationship with a credible expert.

Can we run B2B influencer campaigns without a platform?

Yes, at small scale. A spreadsheet, LinkedIn search and a CRM can manage a handful of experts; a platform helps once discovery and reporting take real time.

What's the verdict?

Start with an expertise-first discovery tool, Onalytica for analyst and practitioner research or Favikon for LinkedIn creators, and keep relationship tracking simple until you work with more than a handful of experts. Move to Traackr or CreatorIQ only when the program spans many creators, markets and teams.
