Semrush Alternatives: SEO Platforms for Different Budgets

The right Semrush alternatives depend on which constraint is pushing you out: website caps and add-on costs point to SE Ranking, a lighter workflow points to Mangools or Ubersuggest, and local, link or reporting work points to specialists. This guide sorts the options by switch reason, so you replace only what you actually need to.

Which alternative fits your reason for switching?

Match the alternative to the single constraint that is costing you money or time, not to a general wish for something cheaper. Most teams leave a broad suite because one limit started to bite, and the best replacement is the one that removes that limit.

  • Cost and tracking capacity: SE Ranking, whose Core plan lists 10 projects and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords at a lower monthly price than Semrush's entry plan.
  • A lighter tool for one or two sites: Ubersuggest or Mangools.
  • A narrower, familiar suite: Moz Pro.
  • Local SEO depth: BrightLocal.
  • Backlink specialization: Ahrefs for a link-led suite, or Majestic for link data alone.
  • Agency reporting time: AgencyAnalytics, or SE Ranking's Agency Pack.

What does Semrush do well that you might lose?

You would lose breadth: keyword research, competitor analysis, audits, daily rank tracking, backlinks and AI search visibility in one login. If your team uses three or more of those every week, a single cheaper tool usually means rebuilding the gaps with several subscriptions.

Semrush's plans list keywords tracked daily on every self-serve tier, with multi-location and device tracking on Pro+ (checked 4 November 2026). It also sells white-labelled client reports and local tools as add-ons, and has a long record in paid-search research.

Before cancelling, read our workflow-by-workflow Semrush and Ahrefs comparison, which shows where that breadth earns its cost and where it does not.

Why do teams switch away from Semrush?

The legitimate triggers come from the plan structure itself: tight website and keyword caps on the lower plans, and add-ons that grow with headcount and client count. A third trigger is simple underuse, where a team pays for toolkits it rarely opens.

  • Website and keyword caps: the SEO plan ($139) and Starter ($199) both list 5 websites and 500 tracked keywords. A sixth client means Pro+ at $299, with 15 websites and 1,500 keywords, or custom Advanced pricing (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Add-on stacking: extra users are listed from $45 a month, and the white-label Pro Report at $20 a month, which Semrush's knowledge base says is bought per report (both checked 4 November 2026).
  • Unused features: teams that only need tracking and audits may not use the research and AI visibility tools they are paying for.
  • Specialist depth: local agencies may want more location and review tooling, and link builders may want a link-first index.

We have not relied on review-site complaints for any of these triggers.

Which alternatives cut cost or add capacity?

SE Ranking is the closest like-for-like replacement when the problem is project and keyword caps. Ubersuggest is the cheaper option for a solo operator who does not need agency features.

SE Ranking: best for agencies outgrowing the five-website cap

Best for: small and mid-sized agencies tracking many keywords across many projects. SE Ranking lists Core at $129 a month with 10 projects, one manager seat and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords, and Growth at $279 with 30 projects, three seats and 5,000 keywords (checked 4 November 2026). Extra manager seats are listed from $16 a month.

Trade-off: the white-label Agency Pack is listed at +$69 a month on annual billing only (checked 4 November 2026). Keyword and backlink databases differ between vendors, so compare results on your own niches during the 14-day trial.

Skip it if: you depend on Semrush's paid-search research. Our 5-client and 15-client SE Ranking and Semrush cost model runs the numbers.

Ubersuggest: best for founders on one or two sites

Best for: founders and solo marketers who need keyword ideas, a site audit and light rank tracking. Ubersuggest's pricing lists three monthly tiers from $29 to $99, with 1, 7 and 15 projects (checked 4 November 2026), and it also sells lifetime plans.

Trade-off: it does not try to match Semrush on competitive depth, multi-client management or reporting. Skip it if you run an agency with many markets.

Which alternatives offer a simpler workflow?

Mangools and Moz Pro both cover core SEO with fewer modules than Semrush, which shortens onboarding for small teams. The cost is less depth outside keywords, links and tracking.

Mangools: best for freelancers who find big suites overwhelming

Best for: freelancers, bloggers and small businesses. Mangools bundles KWFinder with SERP analysis, rank tracking and backlink lookups, and states that annual plans save 35% (checked 4 November 2026). Its pages showed inconsistent monthly prices, so confirm the current tier figures at checkout.

Trade-off: plans are built around lookup and tracking allowances that suit a small portfolio, not a 20-client agency. Skip it if you need PPC research or deep audits.

Moz Pro: best for in-house teams wanting a narrower suite

Best for: teams that want keyword research, link analysis, crawls and rank tracking without the wider marketing toolkits. Moz's Domain Authority is a metric many stakeholders already recognise.

Trade-off: it is narrower than Semrush in content, advertising and market research. We could not retrieve Moz's pricing page during this review, so compare tracked keywords and seats on the official Moz Pro page before deciding.

Which specialists beat Semrush on local, links or reporting?

BrightLocal, Ahrefs, Majestic and AgencyAnalytics each go deeper than Semrush on one job. None replaces the full suite, so most teams keep a research tool alongside them.

BrightLocal: best for local agencies

Best for: agencies and brands where locations, listings and reviews are the work. BrightLocal lists Track at $41 a month for one location on monthly billing, covering up to 100 keywords and four competitors per location, with a 7x7 search grid for up to five keywords (checked 4 November 2026). Higher tiers add listing sync and review tools. Our multi-location local SEO software guide compares it with other local platforms.

Skip it if: you need national keyword research or competitor gap analysis.

Ahrefs: best for link builders and digital PR teams

Best for: teams living in competitor link profiles. Ahrefs lists Lite at $129, Standard at $249 and Advanced at $449 a month, each with one user (checked 4 November 2026).

Trade-off: tracked keywords update weekly by default, and extra users cost $40 to $80 a month below Enterprise. It is a different emphasis, not usually a cheaper bill.

Majestic: best as a dedicated link index

Best for: specialists who already have keyword and audit tools and want Trust Flow and Citation Flow metrics. Majestic says its Historic Index is available on Pro and above (checked 4 November 2026).

Skip it if: you want one tool; it does not do keywords, audits or tracking. Our backlink analysis tool comparison explains how to judge link data.

AgencyAnalytics: best when reporting eats more hours than research

Best for: agencies assembling monthly reports from SEO, analytics, ads and social sources. AgencyAnalytics prices by the number of clients you manage and lists a 14-day trial (checked 4 November 2026).

Trade-off: it is a reporting layer, so most agencies keep a keyword and backlink tool underneath. Compare it in our SEO reporting software roundup.

Will a cheaper tool really cover your work?

Only if your work matches the tool's strength, and the plan pages alone cannot tell you how a different keyword database behaves in your niche. Run any trial on real client domains and compare results before you commit.

A practical decision rule from the evidence reviewed:

  • You hit the five-website cap and mainly need tracking, audits and reports: trial SE Ranking.
  • You open Semrush twice a month: try Ubersuggest or Mangools, and fill gaps with free tools such as Search Console.
  • Most clients are local businesses: BrightLocal plus a light research tool.
  • Links are the billable work: Ahrefs, or Majestic beside a cheaper suite.
  • You use research, PPC data and audits every week: stay on Semrush and prune your add-ons.

What should you check before switching?

Check data export, history import, report rebuild time and contract terms before you sign. The biggest hidden cost is lost ranking history, because a new tracker starts from the day you add keywords.

  1. Can you export tracked keywords, tags and ranking history from Semrush in a format the new tool imports?
  2. Does the new vendor import history, or does tracking restart from zero?
  3. Who rebuilds client report templates, and how long will it take?
  4. Which metrics will change? Tell clients that authority scores and traffic estimates differ between vendors.
  5. Are seats, client logins and white-label features included or sold as add-ons?
  6. When does your Semrush renewal fall, and what are the new vendor's cancellation terms?

What else do buyers ask about leaving Semrush?

The common questions are about the cheapest workable option, free tools and ranking history. Here are direct answers.

What is the cheapest real Semrush alternative for an agency?

Of the suites we could verify, SE Ranking Core lists 10 projects and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords at $129 a month, against 5 websites and 500 keywords on Semrush's $139 SEO plan, both checked 4 November 2026. Data depth and reporting add-ons still need comparing.

Is there a free alternative to Semrush?

Not a complete one. Google Search Console, Ahrefs' free tier for verified sites and Semrush's own free plan each cover part of the job, but none replaces a paid suite for multi-client work.

Will I lose my ranking history if I switch?

You keep it only if you export it before cancelling. Ask the new vendor whether it imports history and in which format.

Should I switch or downgrade?

If the problem is one add-on or a seat count, review your Semrush configuration first. Switching makes more sense when a structural limit, such as the website cap, keeps forcing a higher tier.

What's the verdict?

Semrush remains a strong default for teams that use its breadth. The case for leaving is clearest when one constraint dominates: capacity for agencies, simplicity for solo operators, location tooling for local work, link depth for link builders, or reporting time for client teams.

Pick the alternative that removes that constraint, trial it on real client data, and move your history before the renewal date.
