Short answer by switch reason

  • Marketing and sales need one database: HubSpot Sales Hub, or ActiveCampaign if email automation is the priority.
  • Reps live on the phone and in sequences: Close.
  • Reports and process rules have outgrown the defaults: Zoho CRM, or Salesforce Starter and Pro Suite if you expect to scale into a platform.
  • Your data model is not just "deals": Attio.
  • You simply want to pay less: Bigin by Zoho CRM, or a free tier compared in our free CRM limits guide.

What Pipedrive does well

Pipedrive is built around a visual deal pipeline and the next activity on each deal. For a small team selling a straightforward product, that focus is a strength: reps see what to do today, managers see where deals stall, and setup rarely needs a consultant.

Based on the product information reviewed, its core includes customisable pipelines and stages, email sync, activity scheduling, workflow automation and a marketplace of integrations. It sells tiered per-seat plans, and some capabilities, such as lead capture and email campaigns, have been offered as add-ons. Check the official Pipedrive pricing page for current tiers and add-ons; we could not verify them for this draft.

If those strengths still describe what your team needs, switching may cost more in disruption than it saves.

Why teams switch

We have not surveyed Pipedrive customers, so these are common decision patterns rather than complaint data. In our editorial assessment, teams usually start looking when one of five things happens:

  1. Automation needs grow beyond deal-stage triggers into nurture, lead scoring or multichannel sequences.
  2. Reporting questions get harder, such as forecasting by segment or measuring source-to-revenue.
  3. Marketing joins the CRM and wants campaigns, forms and lifecycle stages on the same records.
  4. The process stops fitting a single pipeline, for example partners, renewals or multi-product accounts.
  5. Total cost creeps up as seats and add-ons accumulate.

How we chose the alternatives

We selected products that small sales teams commonly evaluate as a direct replacement, and grouped them by the switch reason they address best. Criteria: pipeline usability, automation depth, email sync, reporting, customisation, published pricing model, and migration effort.

Evidence came from official pricing pages and product documentation. No hands-on testing was performed. For more automation and marketing alignment

HubSpot Sales Hub

Fit: teams where marketing generates most leads and wants campaigns, forms and deals on the same contact record.

Strengths: a shared database across sales and marketing hubs, a usable free tier, and clear tier limits. HubSpot's pricing page lists up to 50 restricted workflows on Starter, 300 on Professional and 1,000 on Enterprise. Sequences, forecasting and custom reporting appear from Professional, at $90 to $100 per seat per month depending on billing option, plus a required one-time $1,500 onboarding fee (checked 4 November 2026, HubSpot Sales Hub pricing).

Weaknesses: the jump from Starter to Professional is large, and marketing automation needs Marketing Hub, priced separately.

Migration notes: map Pipedrive stages to HubSpot deal stages, and decide early whether Pipedrive "organizations" become HubSpot companies with associations intact.

ActiveCampaign

Fit: small businesses whose main growth lever is email nurture, with a modest sales team working deals.

Strengths: ActiveCampaign is primarily a marketing automation platform with a visual automation builder, and its CRM and deal features let sales act on engagement data. Our ActiveCampaign and HubSpot fit comparison covers that trade-off in depth.

Weaknesses: pipeline management is less central than in Pipedrive, and pricing scales with contacts as well as plan. Check the ActiveCampaign pricing page for which plans include CRM features.

Migration notes: you will move contacts and deals, but also consent status and list membership. Audit those fields before import.

Close

Fit: inside-sales teams that make many calls and run email sequences from the CRM.

Strengths: Close emphasises built-in calling, SMS, email and sequences in a single workspace, which reduces the need for a separate engagement tool for small teams. If you are weighing a dedicated tool instead, see our sales engagement platform comparison.

Weaknesses: less suited to marketing-led teams, and calling costs and features vary by plan and country. See the Close pricing page.

Migration notes: Close organises around leads (companies) with contacts underneath, which maps reasonably well to Pipedrive organizations and persons.

For deeper reporting and customization

Zoho CRM

Fit: cost-conscious teams that need custom modules, process rules and more advanced reports without moving to an enterprise platform.

Strengths: broad customisation, process-management features such as Blueprint, and a wider Zoho suite for analytics, help desk and finance if you want it.

Weaknesses: the breadth makes setup heavier than Pipedrive, and the interface can feel busier. Features vary considerably by edition, so compare editions on the Zoho CRM pricing page.

Migration notes: Zoho offers import tools for common CRMs. Test custom-field mapping on a sample first.

Salesforce Starter Suite and Pro Suite

Fit: teams that expect to grow into complex processes and want a platform they will not need to leave.

Strengths: Starter Suite lists lead, account, contact and opportunity management, lead routing and email marketing at $25 per user per month. Pro Suite adds quoting, forecasting and more customisation at $100 per user per month billed annually (checked 4 November 2026, Salesforce Sales Cloud pricing).

Weaknesses: Pro Suite lists a cap of 5 flows per org and charges extra for API access. Beyond the suites, plan for admin capacity. Our HubSpot versus Salesforce analysis models that cost.

Migration notes: more mapping decisions (leads versus contacts, record types) than other options here.

Attio

Fit: startups and investor- or partner-driven businesses whose relationships do not fit a single linear pipeline.

Strengths: a flexible data model with custom objects and list views, and automatic enrichment of records from connected email and calendars, based on the vendor's product information.

Weaknesses: flexibility means you design the structure yourself, and the ecosystem is younger than the incumbents'. Check plan limits on the Attio pricing page.

Migration notes: plan your objects before importing; a straight copy of Pipedrive's structure wastes the reason for switching.

For a lower total cost

Bigin by Zoho CRM

Fit: very small businesses that want a Pipedrive-like pipeline at a lower price point.

Strengths: Bigin is Zoho's pipeline-centred CRM for small teams, with a simpler interface than Zoho CRM and a path to upgrade within the Zoho family.

Weaknesses: automation and reporting are intentionally lighter. Teams with growing process needs may move again within a year or two. See the official Bigin site for current plans.

Migration notes: typically the simplest move on this list because the concepts mirror Pipedrive's.

Cheaper licences do not always mean a lower total cost. Before you switch purely on price, model seats, add-ons and admin time using our breakdown of CRM seat and add-on costs.

Decision guide

  • If the trigger is marketing wanting in, shortlist HubSpot and ActiveCampaign.
  • If the trigger is call volume, trial Close against your busiest rep's day.
  • If the trigger is reporting, write down the five reports you cannot build today and test each in Zoho CRM and Salesforce.
  • If the trigger is structure, prototype your objects in Attio.
  • If the trigger is price, compare Bigin and free tiers, but check what you lose.

Who should stay on Pipedrive

Stay if your team is under roughly ten reps, sells a single product through one or two pipelines, and the main complaint is a missing feature that an integration or add-on can supply. A Marketplace app or an automation platform can often close a gap for less disruption than a migration.

Also stay if adoption is high. A CRM reps actually update is worth more than a more powerful one they avoid.

Switching checklist

  1. Write down the specific switch reason and the measurable outcome you expect.
  2. Export deals, persons, organizations, activities, notes, products and custom fields from Pipedrive, and archive the files.
  3. List every automation, integration and webhook, and decide which to rebuild.
  4. Check email sync: confirm how historical emails will be linked in the new system.
  5. Map stages, owners and custom fields; delete fields nobody uses.
  6. Run a test import of a few hundred records and have two reps work in it for a week.
  7. Set a cut-over date, freeze changes in Pipedrive, and keep read-only access until your renewal ends.

FAQs

What is the closest like-for-like alternative to Pipedrive?

In our assessment, Bigin by Zoho CRM and Close are closest in day-to-day feel, with Bigin simpler and Close more outbound-focused.

Is HubSpot more expensive than Pipedrive?

It depends on tier. HubSpot's free tools and Starter tier are inexpensive, but Professional is $90 to $100 per seat per month plus a $1,500 onboarding fee (checked 4 November 2026). Compare that with Pipedrive's current tier on its official pricing page.

Can I keep my email history when I switch?

Usually partially. Many CRMs re-sync recent email from the connected inbox, but notes and logged emails need exporting. Confirm with the new vendor before cut-over.

How long does a Pipedrive migration take?

For a small team with clean data, a few weeks including testing is realistic. Custom fields, automations and integrations add time.

Conclusion

Pipedrive remains a sound choice for focused pipeline management. Leave it when a specific need, such as marketing alignment, calling, reporting, data structure or cost, is consistently blocking the team, and choose the alternative built for that need.
