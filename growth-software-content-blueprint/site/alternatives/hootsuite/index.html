Hootsuite Alternatives for Agencies and Growing Brands

The best Hootsuite alternatives depend on who you are: agencies managing client approvals usually look at SocialPilot, Sendible, Agorapulse or Planable, while in-house brands weigh Sprout Social for depth, Buffer for simplicity and Later for visual planning. The deciding factor is often the pricing model, because per-seat, per-profile and per-channel plans scale very differently once you pass a handful of accounts.

Which Hootsuite alternative should you shortlist?

Shortlist by buyer type first and pricing model second. Agencies should start with SocialPilot, Sendible, Agorapulse or Planable for client workflows, and in-house teams with Sprout Social, Buffer or Later depending on how much inbox and reporting depth they need.

  • Agency on a tight budget with many client profiles: SocialPilot.
  • Agency that wants client-facing approvals and branded reporting: Sendible.
  • Team where community management and the inbox matter most: Agorapulse.
  • Team whose bottleneck is review and sign-off: Planable.
  • In-house brand that needs deep reporting and can pay per seat: Sprout Social.
  • Small brand that mostly schedules posts: Buffer.
  • Visual brand planning Instagram and TikTok content: Later.

What does Hootsuite do well?

Hootsuite covers scheduling, a social inbox, analytics and listening in one platform, with broad network support and enterprise governance. It remains a sensible default for organisations that need many networks, many teams and formal permissions in one contract.

Its streams view, which shows several feeds side by side, is still useful for monitoring. Larger plans add approval workflows, team permissions and more advanced analytics. If your team relies on those enterprise controls, an alternative has to match them, not just undercut the price.

Why do teams switch from Hootsuite?

The legitimate triggers are cost relative to team size, a pricing model that doesn't match how the team grows, and a need for client-facing agency features. Some teams also want a simpler tool because they only use scheduling.

  • Seat economics: when pricing follows users, adding a coordinator or a client approver can cost as much as adding a channel.
  • Feature mismatch: a team that only schedules posts may be paying for inbox, listening and analytics it never opens.
  • Agency workflows: client workspaces, external approvals and white-label reports matter more to agencies than to brands.
  • Reporting needs: some teams want cross-network reports they can send without rebuilding them each month.

None of these is a reason to switch on its own. Price the alternative against the features you actually use, not against Hootsuite's full list.

Which alternatives suit agencies with client accounts?

SocialPilot and Sendible are built around many client accounts, Agorapulse suits agencies doing community management, and Planable fits agencies whose biggest delay is client sign-off. Check client workspaces, external approvers and report branding before you compare prices.

SocialPilot: best for agencies scaling profile counts on a budget

SocialPilot focuses on bulk scheduling, client management and approvals at agency volume. In our editorial assessment its analytics are adequate for monthly client reports but lighter than Sprout Social's. Skip it if reporting is the product you sell to clients.

Sendible: best for client-facing approvals and branded reports

Sendible offers client approval flows and white-label options, which help agencies present reports under their own brand. Confirm which plan includes white-labelling before you budget. If branded reporting spans SEO and paid channels too, a dedicated white-label reporting platform may serve you better.

Agorapulse: best for inbox-heavy community management

Agorapulse is known for its unified social inbox, assignment and saved replies, with publishing and reporting alongside. It's a strong fit when replies, not posts, take most of your team's time. Consider another option if you need deep listening across the open web.

Planable: best for collaboration and approvals

Planable presents posts as previews that clients and colleagues comment on and approve. It's light on inbox and listening, so it suits teams whose pain is sign-off rather than engagement. Teams approving other creative alongside social posts should also look at creative proofing software for agency client approvals.

Which alternatives suit in-house and growing brands?

Sprout Social is the depth option for brands that can fund seats, Buffer is the simple, low-commitment option, and Later is the visual-planning option. The choice comes down to how much of your work is engagement and reporting rather than scheduling.

Sprout Social: best for reporting and inbox depth

Sprout Social combines publishing, a smart inbox, reporting and optional listening, with a reputation for polished analytics. The trade-off is per-seat pricing, which makes every new user a budget decision. Skip it if a team of five or more needs only scheduling.

Buffer: best for straightforward scheduling

Buffer prices by connected channel and keeps the interface simple, which makes costs easy to forecast for small teams. Its engagement and analytics features are basic next to Hootsuite's. For deeper measurement, pair it with a tool from our social media analytics roundup for multi-brand teams.

Later: best for visual content calendars

Later is built around a visual calendar and grid preview, with link-in-bio tools for Instagram and TikTok-led brands. It's weaker for community management, so brands with heavy reply volume should look at Agorapulse or Sprout Social instead.

How does cost scale for 10 social profiles?

At 10 profiles, per-channel pricing grows with every account you add, per-seat pricing grows with every person, and bundled plans jump in steps when you pass a profile or user allowance. Price your exact mix of profiles, editors and approvers on each vendor's page, because the cheapest model depends on that ratio.

The two scenarios below are hypothetical and contain no prices, because we couldn't confirm current tiers. They show which cost lines to price.

Scenario A: agency with two clients

Assumptions: 10 profiles (five networks for each of two clients), three agency staff who publish, and two client approvers who only review.

  • Per-seat tools: three paid seats, plus a check on whether client approvers need seats or can approve through a link.
  • Per-channel tools: ten channel charges; the number of people matters less.
  • Bundled plans: the first plan whose profile and user allowance both cover 10 profiles and three users, plus any add-on for white-label reports.

Scenario B: in-house brand

Assumptions: 10 profiles for one brand, four marketers who publish and reply, and one manager who views reports.

  • Per-seat tools: four or five seats, depending on whether a view-only user is free.
  • Per-channel tools: ten channel charges, but check whether the inbox and reporting your team needs are on the same plan.
  • Bundled plans: the plan that covers five users, and whether reply volume pushes you to a tier with inbox features.

In our assessment, per-channel models favour small teams with many profiles, and per-seat models favour large profile counts handled by few people. Which channel limits and export gaps should you check?

Check, network by network, whether each tool publishes directly or only sends a reminder for certain formats, and what you can export from Hootsuite before you cancel.   • Direct publishing versus notifications: some formats on some networks have needed a mobile notification and manual posting; confirm the current position for Stories, Reels, carousels and short video.
  • Profiles versus pages: check support for personal LinkedIn profiles as well as company pages, and for business versus creator accounts on Instagram and TikTok.

  • Content library and approvals: download stored assets and keep an export of approval records if clients ask for an audit trail.

How do you migrate from Hootsuite step by step?

Export first, connect profiles second, and cut over scheduled content last. Keep Hootsuite running until the new tool has published a full week of posts without problems.

  1. Inventory every connected profile, user, approval rule and saved report.
  2. Export analytics reports, scheduled-post lists and content library assets.
  3. Set up workspaces or client groups in the new tool, then reconnect profiles using an admin account for each network.
  4. Rebuild approval flows and invite external approvers.
  5. Recreate scheduled posts for the next two weeks, then pause the same posts in Hootsuite to avoid double publishing.
  6. Rebuild your monthly reports and compare one month's figures side by side.
  7. Disconnect profiles from Hootsuite and cancel at the end of the billing term.

What else do buyers ask about Hootsuite alternatives?

The common questions are about free plans, agency features and listening. Short answers follow.

Is there a free Hootsuite alternative?

Some tools, such as Buffer, have offered free plans with a small number of channels. Check the vendor's current plan page, because free allowances change often and rarely include an inbox or full analytics.

Which Hootsuite alternative is best for agencies?

It depends on the bottleneck. SocialPilot suits high profile counts on a budget, Sendible suits branded client reporting, Agorapulse suits inbox-heavy work and Planable suits approval-heavy work.

Do these tools include social listening?

Some offer listening as an add-on or on higher plans, but depth varies. If brand and competitor monitoring is a core job, compare dedicated social listening platforms separately.

Can I keep my Hootsuite analytics history after switching?

Usually only as exported reports. Export the date ranges you need before you cancel, because the new tool will normally start collecting from the day you connect each profile.

What's the verdict?

Switch from Hootsuite when its pricing model or feature mix no longer matches how your team works, not just to save money on paper. Agencies should compare SocialPilot, Sendible, Agorapulse and Planable on client workflows, and in-house brands should compare Sprout Social, Buffer and Later on inbox and reporting depth.
