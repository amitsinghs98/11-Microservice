Ahrefs Alternatives: Tools for Backlinks, Keywords, and Content

Ahrefs bundles several jobs into one subscription: link research, keyword discovery, rank tracking, audits and content research. Most teams looking elsewhere only need to replace one or two of those jobs, so this guide matches each alternative to a specific one.

Short answer by Ahrefs job

  • Backlink research is the job: Majestic for a link-only index, Moz Pro for links plus a lighter suite, Semrush for links inside a broad platform.
  • Budget keyword research is the job: Mangools (KWFinder) or Keywords Everywhere, with Search Console for your own sites.
  • You need an all-in-one agency suite: Semrush or SE Ranking, both of which list daily rank tracking on their plans (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Rank tracking is the job: AccuRanker, a dedicated tracker, if weekly updates are too slow.
  • Content research and optimization is the job: Clearscope, or one of the tools in our roundup of Surfer SEO alternatives.

What Ahrefs does well

Ahrefs runs its own crawler, which its pricing page describes as the world's second-most active. Link research is where its reputation sits: Site Explorer, link intersect-style competitor gaps, broken backlinks and linking-author views are core parts of the product, with several of those reports listed on Standard and above. Content Explorer helps find topics and link prospects, and Keywords Explorer adds clusters and intent labels from Standard.

It also publishes clear audit capacity. The plan table lists 100,000, 500,000 and 1.5 million crawl credits a month on Lite, Standard and Advanced, and unlimited projects for sites with verified ownership (checked 4 November 2026, Ahrefs plans and pricing). If link and competitor research are daily work, Ahrefs may still be the right core tool, and the alternatives below are complements rather than replacements. Our side-by-side of Semrush and Ahrefs workflows sets out that trade in detail.

Why teams look elsewhere

The reasons below follow from Ahrefs' published plan terms, checked 4 November 2026. We have not relied on review-site complaints.

  • Rank tracking frequency: the plan table lists Rank Tracker update frequency as weekly on Lite, Standard, Advanced and Enterprise.
  • Per-user pricing: each plan includes one user (Enterprise from three). Extra users cost $40/mo on Lite, $60 on Standard, $80 on Advanced and $100 on Enterprise, with caps on how many you can add below Enterprise.
  • Entry-tier credits: Lite includes 1,000 credits per user per month; Standard and above list unlimited credits.
  • Add-on costs: Content Kit is listed from $99/mo and the extra Report Builder capacity at $99/mo.
  • No discounts or general refunds: the FAQ says Ahrefs never runs discounts and does not issue refunds in general, though annual billing is listed at a saving of up to 17%.

Alternatives by job

Backlink-first: Majestic

Fit: link specialists who want a second link index to cross-check Ahrefs, or a cheaper link-only tool beside a lighter suite. Majestic is known for its Trust Flow and Citation Flow metrics and topical link classification.

Strengths: focused interface for link profiles, historic and fresh index views, and link-based metrics that differ from domain-rating style scores.

Weaknesses: no meaningful keyword research, auditing or rank tracking. You will pay for another tool for those jobs.

Migration notes: there is nothing to migrate for link research itself, but saved prospect lists and outreach tags must be exported from Ahrefs first. See the Majestic website for plans.

Backlink-first with a lighter suite: Moz Pro

Fit: in-house teams that want Link Explorer, Keyword Explorer, site crawls and rank tracking under one roof, with Domain Authority as a metric stakeholders already recognise.

Strengths: a narrower, more approachable suite, and an established link metric useful for client conversations.

Weaknesses: content research and competitive depth are thinner than Ahrefs. Compare tracked-keyword and seat limits plan by plan on the Moz Pro product page.

Migration notes: expect Domain Authority and Domain Rating to differ for the same site; reset client baselines rather than comparing them.

Budget keyword research: Mangools

Fit: freelancers and small-site owners who mostly used Ahrefs for Keywords Explorer. KWFinder is the centrepiece, with SERP analysis, a rank tracker and backlink lookup in the same subscription.

Strengths: clear difficulty and SERP views, and a lower barrier to entry than large suites.

Weaknesses: plans use daily lookup allowances that a busy agency will exhaust, and link data is not a match for a dedicated index. Confirm current allowances on the Mangools site.

Migration notes: rebuild keyword lists from Ahrefs exports and re-add tracked terms; ranking history will not carry over automatically.

Budget keyword research in the browser: Keywords Everywhere

Fit: content writers and solo marketers who want volume and competition data shown alongside Google results while they research.

Strengths: works inside the browser, so there is little to learn, and usage is bought as credits rather than a full suite subscription.

Weaknesses: it is a research aid, not a project tool. There is no audit, tracking or serious link analysis. Check the credit model on the Keywords Everywhere website.

Migration notes: pair it with Search Console data for your own sites; it does not store projects the way Ahrefs does.

All-in-one agency suite: Semrush

Fit: agencies that want daily rank tracking, client reports, research and AI visibility in one account. Semrush lists SEO at $139/mo, Starter at $199/mo and Pro+ at $299/mo, with 5, 5 and 15 websites and 500, 500 and 1,500 keywords tracked daily (checked 4 November 2026, Semrush pricing page).

Strengths: breadth, daily tracking, multi-location tracking on Pro+, and white-label reports through the Pro Report add-on ($20/mo).

Weaknesses: website caps are tight on the lower plans, and extra users start at $45/mo. It will not be cheaper than Ahrefs for every team.

Migration notes: new authority and traffic metrics mean new client baselines. Our guide to Semrush replacements covers the reverse move if you later outgrow it.

All-in-one on a tighter budget: SE Ranking

Fit: small agencies that need many projects and keywords per dollar. Core lists 10 projects, 1 manager seat and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords at $129/mo; Growth lists 30 projects, 3 seats and 5,000 keywords at $279/mo (checked 4 November 2026, SE Ranking pricing).

Strengths: generous tracking capacity, local and map rank tracking on both plans, and low-cost extra seats.

Weaknesses: white-label reporting needs the Agency Pack, listed from +$69/mo on annual billing only, and Core keeps six months of history. Compare backlink results against Ahrefs on a few of your own domains during the 14-day trial.

Migration notes: SE Ranking states it imports projects and ranking history, with free assisted migration on annual plans.

Rank tracking: AccuRanker

Fit: agencies that kept Ahrefs for research but need faster, more granular ranking data than a weekly refresh. AccuRanker is a dedicated rank tracker with on-demand refreshes and tagging for large keyword sets.

Strengths: focus. It does one job and is built for large keyword volumes and reporting segments.

Weaknesses: it adds a subscription rather than replacing one, and pricing scales with tracked keywords. Check the AccuRanker website. Our rank tracker comparison for multi-client SEO lists other options.

Migration notes: export historical positions from Ahrefs before you stop tracking there.

Content workflow: Clearscope

Fit: content teams who used Ahrefs for topic research and now need briefs and draft grading in the writing tool itself. Clearscope provides term recommendations and content grades, with integrations for common editors.

Strengths: a simple editorial workflow that writers adopt quickly.

Weaknesses: no link or rank data, and pricing is aimed at teams rather than hobbyists. See the Clearscope website, and our content optimization tools guide for alternatives.

Migration notes: keep a keyword source; Clearscope informs the content, not the keyword strategy.

Seat costs compared

Seats are often the hidden reason an Ahrefs bill grows. For a four-person team, using list prices checked 4 November 2026 at monthly billing:

  • Ahrefs Standard: $249 plus three extra users at $60 = $429/mo.
  • Semrush Pro+: $299 plus three users at the add-on's starting price of $45 = about $434/mo. Semrush lists "team users" as an Advanced feature, so confirm seat options on Pro+ with sales.
  • SE Ranking Growth: $279 with three manager seats included, plus one extra seat from $16 = about $295/mo.

These totals ignore differences in data and features, and they are not quotes. They show why seat terms deserve as much attention as plan prices. Our guide to building an SEO stack without duplicate features helps decide which people really need full seats.

Decision guide

  • Keep Ahrefs and add AccuRanker if research is fine and only ranking frequency is the problem.
  • Move to SE Ranking or Semrush if you need daily tracking, reports and more seats in one bill.
  • Pair Majestic with a cheaper suite if links are billable work but you rarely use the rest of Ahrefs.
  • Move to Mangools or Keywords Everywhere if you only ever opened Keywords Explorer.
  • Add a content optimizer if your bottleneck is draft quality rather than research.

Transition checklist

  1. List which Ahrefs reports each team member used in the last 90 days.
  2. Export tracked keywords, ranking history, saved keyword lists, portfolios and link prospect lists.
  3. Download recent Site Audit results so you have a baseline for the new crawler.
  4. Record current Domain Rating and traffic estimates for clients, and explain that new metrics will differ.
  5. Run the new tool alongside Ahrefs for one reporting cycle where your billing period allows.
  6. Rebuild client report templates before the first client-facing report.
  7. Cancel Ahrefs before renewal; its FAQ says cancellations take effect at the end of the billing period, and pay-as-you-go charges apply if enabled.

FAQs

Which tool has backlink data closest to Ahrefs?

Semrush and Majestic both maintain their own link indexes, and Moz runs Link Explorer. Every index finds a different subset of the web, so cross-check a handful of domains you know well before deciding.

Is there a cheaper Ahrefs option without leaving Ahrefs?

Yes. Ahrefs lists a Starter plan at $29/mo and an Ahrefs Free account with limited Site Explorer and Site Audit access for site owners (checked 4 November 2026). Both are much narrower than Lite.

Can one tool replace every Ahrefs job?

Semrush and SE Ranking come closest, but each has different strengths. Many teams end up with one suite plus one specialist tool.

Does weekly rank tracking matter?

It matters for agencies that react to daily volatility or promise frequent client updates. For monthly reporting on stable sites, weekly data is often enough.

Conclusion

Ahrefs is strongest as a link and competitive research tool. Replace it only for the jobs where its terms work against you, such as ranking frequency, seat costs or unused features, and keep the parts that still earn their place.
