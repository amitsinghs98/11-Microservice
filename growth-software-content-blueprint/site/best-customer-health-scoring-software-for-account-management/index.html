Which customer health scoring software should you choose?

Choose the tool that lets you see and change every signal and weight, and that shows account managers why a score moved, because an unexplained score gets ignored. For most small B2B teams that means a lighter CS platform or the scoring in your CRM; larger teams with several segments need the configurability of Gainsight, Totango or Planhat.

  • Enterprise CS with several segments and scorecards: Gainsight.
  • Mid-market SaaS scoring product engagement: ChurnZero.
  • Start with one scorecard, expand by module: Totango.
  • Product-led SaaS with warehouse data: Vitally.
  • Revenue and usage scored together: Planhat.
  • Small CS teams wanting a lighter platform: Custify.
  • Account data already in HubSpot: HubSpot Service Hub.

What signals belong in a customer health score?

A useful health score draws on five signal families: product usage, engagement with your team, support experience, customer sentiment and commercial status. Which ones matter, and how much, depends on your product and segment, so treat any off-the-shelf recipe as a hypothesis.

  • Usage: active users against licensed seats, adoption of the features tied to value, trend over recent weeks.
  • Engagement: attendance at business reviews, responsiveness, executive sponsor contact.
  • Support: open escalations, repeat issues, time to resolution.
  • Sentiment: survey responses and account manager judgement, recorded consistently. Our guides to feedback management software and NPS software cover collecting it.
  • Commercial: renewal date, payment issues, downgrade requests, contract changes.

A health score describes current condition. Predicting a specific outcome such as churn is a related but different job, covered in our guide to churn prediction software.

What does each health scoring tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The heavier platforms win on segmentation and governance, and the lighter ones win on speed to a working score. Ask every vendor to show a score change and the explanation an account manager would see.

Gainsight: best for enterprise scorecards

Gainsight is a customer success platform positioned for organizations with multiple segments and CS operations staff, with health scoring and playbooks among its documented themes. Ask for a demo of segment-specific scorecards and weighting. Skip it if you're a small team without someone to administer it; you'll pay for depth you won't use.

ChurnZero: best for engagement-led scoring

ChurnZero positions itself as customer success software with health scoring and automation themes. Confirm which usage, engagement and account inputs feed its scores and how scores trigger alerts or plays. Skip it if you can't send product usage data into it, because usage-based scores would then be thin.

Totango: best for starting small and expanding

Totango presents its customer success platform around journeys and modular building blocks, which can let you begin with one scorecard and add scope as the programme matures. Check which modules are in your package. Skip it if you want an opinionated, single default score.

Vitally: best for product-led SaaS

Vitally is a customer success platform aimed at B2B SaaS teams, with health scores built from customer data. Confirm which product, billing, CRM and warehouse sources it connects to. It fits teams with a data stack already in place. Skip it if your source data is unreliable, because the score will be too.

Planhat: best for revenue and usage together

Planhat emphasizes a flexible customer data model, so revenue, usage and relationship data can sit together in account views. That suits businesses whose accounts don't fit a standard template. Skip it if you need a score running next week with minimal design work.

Custify: best for smaller CS teams

Custify is a customer success platform aimed at SaaS companies, positioned as lighter than the enterprise platforms, which can suit a small team. Confirm its health scoring, lifecycle and automation features and current plans on its pricing page. Skip it if you need complex governance, many segments or deep enterprise controls.

HubSpot Service Hub: best when accounts live in HubSpot

HubSpot's Service Hub is its customer service product on the HubSpot CRM, so any health view would sit next to deals and contacts. Check whether health scoring is available, which tier includes it and how flexible its inputs are. Skip it if your CRM isn't HubSpot, or you need product usage at a level of detail HubSpot doesn't hold.

What does a simple health score model look like?

A simple model scores a few signals on a common scale, weights them and adds them up, with every weight written down and open to challenge. The example below is hypothetical and illustrates structure only; your weights should come from your own renewal outcomes.

  1. Usage (hypothetical weight 40%): share of licensed seats active over the last four weeks, scored 0 to 100.
  2. Value feature adoption (25%): whether the account uses the features most associated with renewal.
  3. Support (15%): deduct for open escalations or repeated issues.
  4. Engagement (10%): recent business review or sponsor contact.
  5. Commercial (10%): payment issues or downgrade requests pull the score down.

Show the trend, not just the number. An account sliding steadily over two months is often more urgent than one sitting slightly lower but stable.

Do health scores actually predict which accounts renew?

A health score only predicts renewals if its signals and weights have been checked against real outcomes, and vendor materials can't do that for your customer base. Expect the first version to be partly wrong.

How do you back-test a health score against renewals?

Score past accounts as of a date before their renewal, then compare the scores with which accounts renewed, expanded, contracted or churned. If low scores don't line up with worse outcomes, change the signals or weights before you roll the score out.

  • Use only data available at the time: otherwise the test looks better than reality.
  • Check each signal separately: a signal that doesn't separate renewals from churns is noise.
  • Look at disagreements: healthy-scored accounts that churned teach you the most.
  • Link to the CRM: keep renewal outcomes in the same record as the score; our guide to CRM for B2B SaaS teams covers that setup.

For usage signals at feature level, a feature adoption analytics tool can supply cleaner inputs than raw login counts.

Which health scoring approach fits your team?

Small teams should start with a transparent score in their CRM or a light CS platform, and move to a heavier platform only when segments and playbooks multiply. The tool matters less than having an owner who reviews the model with real outcomes.

  • One or two CSMs: CRM-based scoring or Custify.
  • A growing CS team with playbooks: ChurnZero, Vitally or Totango; compare them in our roundup of customer success software.
  • Several segments and a CS operations function: Gainsight or Planhat.

What else do buyers ask about customer health scoring software?

Questions cluster around how many signals to use, how to weight them and whether a CRM is enough. Treat the answers as starting points to check against your own renewal history.

How many signals should a health score include?

Fewer than most teams start with. A handful of signals you trust and can explain will beat a score built from every field available, because account managers need to know why a score moved.

Should health scores be the same for every customer segment?

Usually not. A self-serve customer and an enterprise account show health in different ways, so many teams keep a separate scorecard or different weights for each segment.

Can we run health scoring in our CRM instead of a CS platform?

Yes, for a simple score built from fields you already sync, such as usage summaries, ticket counts and renewal dates. A CS platform earns its cost when you need trends, playbooks and product usage at scale.

How often should we revise health score weights?

Review them at least every couple of quarters, and after any major product or pricing change. Use renewal and churn outcomes since the last review to decide what to change.

Is NPS a good health signal?

It's one useful signal, but survey responses are sparse and often come from a single contact. Combine it with behavioural and commercial signals rather than letting it dominate the score.

What's the verdict on customer health scoring software?

The best customer health score is one your account managers understand and your renewal history supports. Pick software that makes signals and weights visible and editable, then back-test before anyone relies on it.
