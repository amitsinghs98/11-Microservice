Semrush vs Ahrefs: Which SEO Workflow Fits Your Team?

Semrush vs Ahrefs has no universal winner: Semrush suits teams that want daily rank tracking, paid-search research and bolt-on client reports in one account, while Ahrefs suits teams whose work centres on links and competitor content on sites they own or verify. The bill is decided by users, tracked keywords and report count, not the plan name.

Which should you choose?

Choose Semrush if you report rankings to clients often, run paid search alongside SEO, or want branded reports without a separate reporting tool. Choose Ahrefs if link prospecting and competitor content research are your daily work and most of your projects are sites you can verify.

  • Agencies sending weekly or more frequent ranking updates: Semrush. Its plans list keywords tracked daily, while Ahrefs updates tracked keywords weekly by default.
  • Link builders and digital PR teams: Ahrefs, whose backlink tooling is the historical core of the product.
  • Teams mixing SEO with PPC and market research: Semrush, which has a longer history in advertising research.
  • In-house teams with one or two owned sites: Ahrefs goes further here, because verified projects are unlimited on every plan.
  • Solo consultants: compare Ahrefs' $29 Starter tier with Semrush's free plan before buying either full suite.

How are the two platforms positioned?

Semrush positions itself as a broad visibility platform that bundles SEO with AI search tracking and sells reporting, users and local tools as add-ons. Ahrefs positions itself around its own crawler and link index, with a plan ladder that scales mainly by users, projects and data history.

Semrush's current SEO pricing runs from an SEO plan through Starter and Pro+ to a custom Advanced tier, with AI visibility prompts added from Starter upward. Ahrefs runs Lite, Standard, Advanced and Enterprise, plus a lower-cost Starter plan and a free tier for site owners.

In our editorial assessment, the difference is one of emphasis rather than capability. Semrush answers more marketing questions in one login. Ahrefs goes deeper on links and competitive content, and expects you to add specialist tools for jobs it treats as secondary, such as daily rank tracking.

Which is stronger for keyword and backlink research?

For backlink research, Ahrefs is the more natural fit, because its plans and reputation are built around its link index and Site Explorer. For keyword research the gap is narrower: both give volume, difficulty, intent and SERP context, so data fit in your niche matters more than features.

Ahrefs' history allowance shapes link work more than most buyers expect. Its plan table lists six months of history on Lite, two years on Standard and five years on Advanced (checked 4 November 2026). If you audit link velocity or lost links over several years, Lite is too short.

Semrush keeps its own backlink database, and its Pro+ plan adds historical SEO data and a Compare Domains report, according to the Semrush pricing page (checked 4 November 2026). That makes Pro+ the realistic floor for competitive research on Semrush.

Neither index sees the whole web. Treat link counts as samples and compare referring-domain trends rather than totals. How do they handle content and PPC work?

Semrush covers both jobs more completely, because it sells a dedicated Content Toolkit and has long-standing paid-search research. Ahrefs supports content research well, but it is not positioned as a PPC tool.

Semrush's Content Toolkit is listed from $60 a month, with a Content Optimizer, SEO brief generator and topic finder (checked 4 November 2026). It is a separate subscription, so budget for it on top of the SEO plan. Teams writing at volume often add a dedicated optimizer instead; our content optimization tool guide compares them.

On paid search, check which Semrush toolkit carries the competitor ad and paid keyword reports you need before assuming the SEO plan includes them. How do audits and rank tracking differ?

Rank tracking is the clearest operational difference: Semrush plans list keywords tracked daily, while Ahrefs updates tracked keywords weekly by default on every plan. Both run site audits, so check crawl limits against your largest site rather than choosing on audit features alone.

Ahrefs' help center states that tracked keywords update weekly by default, and an Ahrefs blog post says daily updates come with the Rank Tracker Pro add-on (both checked 4 November 2026). We could not confirm that add-on's price, so ask Ahrefs before modelling it.

Semrush Pro+ adds multi-location and device tracking (checked 4 November 2026), which matters for agencies with local or mobile-heavy clients. If tracking is your main job, a dedicated tool may beat both; see our rank tracking software for multi-client SEO.

Which suits agency client reporting?

Semrush is easier to use for white-labelled client reports, because it sells a Pro Report add-on with branding and white-labeling. Ahrefs includes scheduled reports with an allowance that depends on plan, but we could not confirm white-label options from its official pages.

Semrush lists a Base Report at $10 a month and a Pro Report at $20 a month, and its knowledge base describes reports as purchased individually (both checked 4 November 2026). Read that as a per-report cost that grows with your client list. Ahrefs' help content says scheduled reports can go out as emailed PDFs and that the number of automated reports depends on your plan.

Can you trust their database metrics?

Trust them as consistent internal models, not as measurements. Authority Score and Domain Rating, traffic estimates and search volumes are calculated differently by each vendor, so the same domain can score very differently in each tool without either being wrong.

  • Search volume is modelled. Expect disagreement on low-volume and local terms.
  • Traffic estimates combine ranking positions with assumed click-through rates. They are useful for comparing competitors inside one tool and weak as absolute numbers.
  • Backlink counts reflect what each crawler found and kept. Track referring domains and trends.
  • Difficulty scores use different formulas. Calibrate them against keywords you already rank for.

The practical rule: pick one tool as the reference for each metric and never mix sources inside a client report. For sites you control, check estimates against Search Console.

What does each really cost for a real team?

For a three-person in-house team on one site, the two land within about $20 a month of each other. For a 10-client agency that wants white-labelled reports, Semrush costs more on these assumptions because reports are priced individually.

Both scenarios use monthly list prices checked 4 November 2026 and exclude tax, overages and annual discounts. They are illustrations, not quotes.

Scenario A: in-house team, three users, one owned site, 1,000 tracked keywords

  • Semrush: 1,000 keywords exceeds the 500 on SEO and Starter, so Pro+ at $299, plus two users at the $45 starting price. About $389 a month.
  • Ahrefs: Lite's 750 keywords is too few, so Standard at $249 plus two users at $60. About $369 a month, with weekly ranking updates unless you add Rank Tracker Pro.

Scenario B: 10-client agency, two users, 100 keywords per client

  • Semrush: 10 sites needs Pro+ (15 websites, 1,500 keywords) at $299, plus one user at $45, plus 10 Pro Reports at $20. About $544 a month.
  • Ahrefs: 10 unverified projects fits Standard (20 projects, 2,000 keywords) at $249, plus one user at $60. About $309 a month, before any reporting tool you add for white-label output.

Confirm with Semrush whether its extra-user price differs on Pro+. Ahrefs' pricing page states that it does not issue refunds in general (checked 4 November 2026), so test on the shortest commitment first. How hard is switching between them?

Switching is moderate work: the tools themselves are quick to set up, but ranking history, report templates and client metric baselines do not transfer. Plan one overlapping reporting cycle and export everything before you cancel.

  • Ranking history: export positions first, because a new tool starts tracking on the day you add keywords.
  • Metric baselines: tell clients that authority and traffic numbers will change with the tool before the first report, not after.
  • Report templates: rebuild these first, because they are what clients notice.
  • Overlap: run both for one cycle if your billing terms allow.

What else do buyers ask about Semrush and Ahrefs?

Most remaining questions are about entry cost, running both tools, and trials. The short answers follow.

Is Semrush or Ahrefs cheaper?

At the entry tier, Ahrefs Lite lists at $129 a month against $139 for Semrush's SEO plan, checked 4 November 2026. Once you add users, keywords and reports, the cheaper option depends on your workload.

Do I need both tools?

Most small teams do not. Paying for both makes sense mainly when link research and daily, multi-location rank tracking are both heavy, billable workloads.

Which has more accurate data?

Neither can be called accurate in an absolute sense, because both model search volume and traffic. Compare each against your own Search Console data for sites you control.

Can I try either before paying for a full plan?

Semrush lists a free plan with one demo project and 10 reports a day. Ahrefs offers a free tier for verified site owners and a $29 Starter plan (all checked 4 November 2026).

What's the verdict?

Semrush is the better fit for teams that treat SEO as one part of a wider marketing job and need daily rankings and branded client reports. Ahrefs is the better fit for link-led and competitive content work, especially on owned or verified sites.

Before you choose, write down three numbers: users, tracked keywords and client reports per month. Those move the bill more than any plan name.
