Semrush vs Ahrefs: Which SEO Workflow Fits Your Team?

Semrush vs Ahrefs has no universal winner: Semrush suits teams that want daily rank tracking, paid-search research and bolt-on client reports in one account, while Ahrefs suits teams whose work centres on links and competitor content on sites they own or verify. The bill is decided by users, tracked keywords and report count, not the plan name.

Which should you choose?

Choose Semrush if you report rankings to clients often, run paid search alongside SEO, or want branded reports without a separate reporting tool. Choose Ahrefs if link prospecting and competitor content research are your daily work and most of your projects are sites you can verify.

  • Agencies sending frequent ranking updates: Semrush, which tracks keywords daily.
  • Link builders and digital PR teams: Ahrefs, whose backlink tooling is its historical core.
  • Teams mixing SEO with PPC and market research: Semrush, which has a longer history in advertising research.
  • In-house teams with one or two owned sites: Ahrefs, because verified projects are unlimited on every paid plan.
  • Solo consultants: compare Ahrefs' $29 Starter tier with Semrush's free account and trial before buying either full suite.

If neither fits your budget, our shortlist of SEO platforms for agencies and in-house teams covers the wider field, and the SEO software category guide maps every related comparison.

How are the two platforms positioned?

Semrush positions itself as a broad visibility platform that bundles SEO with AI search tracking and sells reporting, users and other toolkits as add-ons. Ahrefs positions itself around its own crawler and link index, with a plan ladder that scales mainly by users, projects and data history.

Semrush now sells two SEO lines. Semrush One bundles the SEO Toolkit with the AI Visibility Toolkit in Starter, Pro+ and a custom Advanced tier, according to Semrush's plan comparison, while the standalone SEO Toolkit keeps the Pro, Guru and Business names (checked 5 November 2026). Ahrefs runs Starter, Lite, Standard, Advanced and Enterprise, plus a free tier for site owners.

In our editorial assessment, the difference is emphasis, not capability: Semrush answers more marketing questions in one login, while Ahrefs goes deeper on links and competitive content and leaves jobs such as daily rank tracking to add-ons.

How do the plans compare side by side?

At monthly billing, Ahrefs Lite is the cheaper paid entry point at $129, against $199 for Semrush One Starter or $139.95 for the standalone SEO Toolkit Pro. The two ladders scale on different limits: Semrush caps websites and tracked keywords, while Ahrefs caps unverified projects, users and data history.

Which is stronger for keyword and backlink research?

For backlink research, Ahrefs is the more natural fit, because its plans and reputation are built around its link index and Site Explorer. For keyword research the gap is narrower: both give volume, difficulty, intent and SERP context, so data fit in your niche matters more than features.

Ahrefs' history allowance shapes link work more than most buyers expect. Its help center lists 6 months of Site Explorer and Keywords Explorer history on Lite, 24 on Standard and 60 on Advanced (checked 5 November 2026). If you audit lost links over several years, Lite is too short.

Semrush keeps its own backlink database, and its Pro+ plan adds historical SEO data and a Compare Domains report (checked 5 November 2026). That makes Pro+ the realistic floor for competitive research on Semrush One.

Neither index sees the whole web, so compare referring-domain trends rather than totals. Our roundup of tools for link building teams explains why index size and link quality are separate questions. How do they handle content and PPC work?

Semrush covers both jobs more completely, because it sells a dedicated Content Toolkit and has long-standing paid-search research. Ahrefs supports content research well, but it is not positioned as a PPC tool.

Semrush's Content Toolkit is listed at $60 a month billed annually (checked 5 November 2026). It is a separate subscription, so budget for it on top of the SEO plan. Teams writing at volume may prefer a tool from our content optimization tool guide.

On paid search, confirm which Semrush toolkit carries the competitor ad reports you need. Teams running client PPC should also look at dedicated PPC management software for agencies, which handles bidding work neither suite attempts.

How do audits and rank tracking differ?

Rank tracking is the clearest operational difference: Semrush plans list keywords tracked daily, while Ahrefs updates tracked keywords weekly by default on every plan. Both run site audits, so check crawl limits against your largest site.

Ahrefs' help center states that tracked keywords update weekly by default, and an Ahrefs blog post says daily updates come with the Rank Tracker Pro add-on (both checked 5 November 2026). We could not find that add-on's price, so ask Ahrefs before modelling it.

Semrush Pro+ adds multi-targeting in Position Tracking (checked 5 November 2026), which matters for agencies tracking several locations or devices. If tracking is your main job, a dedicated tool may beat both; see our rank tracking software for multi-client SEO.

Which suits agency client reporting?

Semrush is the better fit for white-labelled client reports, because it sells a Pro Report add-on with branding and white-labeling. Ahrefs can email scheduled PDF reports, but its help center says it does not currently allow white-labelled reports.

Semrush's knowledge base lists a Base Report at $10 a month and a Pro Report at $20 a month, each purchased individually, with three Base reports included with a paid toolkit (checked 5 November 2026). That cost grows with your client list.

Ahrefs' Report Builder emails PDF reports to recipients without an Ahrefs login, while a help article states that it does not allow white-labelling its reports at present (both checked 5 November 2026). If reporting decides the deal, compare both with our SEO reporting software guide or the white-label reporting platforms for agencies.

Can you trust their database metrics?

Trust them as consistent internal models, not as measurements. Authority Score and Domain Rating, traffic estimates and search volumes are calculated differently by each vendor, so the same domain can score very differently in each tool without either being wrong.

  • Search volume is modelled; expect disagreement on low-volume and local terms.
  • Traffic estimates are useful for comparing competitors inside one tool, weak as absolute numbers.
  • Backlink counts reflect what each crawler kept; track referring-domain trends.
  • Difficulty scores use different formulas; calibrate them against keywords you already rank for.

Pick one tool as the reference for each metric, never mix sources inside a client report, and check estimates against Search Console for sites you control.

What does each really cost for a real team?

For a small in-house team on one site, Ahrefs Lite comes out roughly $75 to $95 a month cheaper than Semrush One Starter on these assumptions. For a 10-client agency that wants white-labelled reports, Semrush costs more, but Ahrefs needs a separate reporting tool to match it.

Both scenarios are hypothetical. They use monthly list prices checked 5 November 2026, assume each base plan includes one login, and exclude tax, overages and annual discounts. They are illustrations, not quotes.

Scenario A: in-house team, two users, one owned site, 500 tracked keywords

  • Semrush: Starter at $199 plus one user at $45. About $244 a month, with daily ranking updates.
  • Ahrefs: Lite at $129 plus one extra user billed at $20 (casual) or $40 (power). About $149 to $169 a month, with weekly ranking updates.

Scenario B: 10-client agency, three users, 100 keywords per client

  • Semrush: 10 sites and 1,000 keywords need Pro+ at $299, plus two users at $80 and 10 Pro Reports at $20. About $659 a month, or $459 without white-label reports.
  • Ahrefs: 10 unverified projects exceed Lite's five, so Standard at $249, plus two users at Standard's seat price, which you should confirm on the pricing page. Add a reporting tool if clients need white-label output.

Ahrefs' pricing page says it does not issue refunds in general (checked 5 November 2026), so start on the shortest commitment. For three agency sizes priced side by side, see the SEO software pricing guide for 5, 10 and 20 clients.

Where do both fall short in day-to-day use?

Based on the product information reviewed, Semrush's weak point is how quickly add-ons pile up, and Ahrefs' is weekly default tracking plus seat charges that rise with the plan. How much either matters depends on your reporting rhythm and team size, which only real use shows.

How hard is switching between them?

Switching is moderate work: the tools are quick to set up, but ranking history, report templates and client metric baselines do not transfer. Plan one overlapping reporting cycle and export everything before you cancel.

  • Ranking history: export positions first, because a new tool starts tracking on the day you add keywords.
  • Metric baselines: warn clients that authority and traffic numbers will change with the tool before the first report, not after.

What else do buyers ask about Semrush and Ahrefs?

Most remaining questions are about entry cost, running both tools, and trials. The short answers follow.

Is Semrush or Ahrefs cheaper?

At the entry tier, Ahrefs Lite lists at $129 a month against $139.95 for Semrush's SEO Toolkit Pro and $199 for Semrush One Starter, checked 5 November 2026. Once you add users, keywords and reports, the cheaper option depends on your workload.

Do I need both tools?

Most small teams do not. Paying for both makes sense mainly when link research and daily, multi-location rank tracking are both heavy, billable workloads.

Which has more accurate data?

Neither can be called accurate in an absolute sense, because both model search volume and traffic. Compare each against your own Search Console data for sites you control.

Can I try either before paying for a full plan?

Semrush offers a limited free account and lists a 7-day free trial on its SEO and AI search plans. Ahrefs offers Ahrefs Free for verified site owners and a $29 Starter plan (all checked 5 November 2026).

What's the verdict?

Semrush is the better fit for teams that treat SEO as one part of a wider marketing job and need daily rankings and branded client reports. Ahrefs is the better fit for link-led and competitive content work, especially on owned or verified sites, and it is cheaper to start.

Before you choose, write down three numbers: users, tracked keywords and client reports per month.
