Which freelancer management platform should an agency choose?

If you pay contractors in several countries, start with Deel or Remote, because cross-border agreements and payouts are where spreadsheets fail first. If your problem is finding talent rather than paying it, Upwork or Contra will help more, and if you're US-only with a handful of contractors, Gusto may be all you need.

  • International contractors with agreements and payouts in one place: Deel.
  • International contractors with an emphasis on compliance guidance: Remote.
  • Agencies managing a large, recurring freelancer pool with approval workflows: Worksome.
  • Sourcing specialists quickly from a large marketplace: Upwork.
  • Finding independent creative and technical talent through portfolios: Contra.
  • US agencies paying domestic contractors alongside employee payroll: Gusto.

What problem does freelancer management software solve for agencies?

It replaces the scattered trail of emails, PDFs, invoices and bank transfers that most agencies use to work with external contributors. In one system you can see who's under contract, what they're working on, what you owe them and what they can access.

Agencies lean on freelancers to absorb peaks, add specialist skills and cover leave. The admin load grows quietly: each new contributor needs an agreement, tax paperwork, a project brief, tool access, time or deliverable approval, an invoice, a payment and, eventually, offboarding. When that runs through an account manager's inbox, you lose track of which agreements are current and which former freelancers still have client logins.

No platform removes the legal questions. Whether someone is genuinely a contractor or should be treated as an employee depends on local law and the actual working relationship, and the rules differ by country. Treat any classification or tax feature as a support for expert advice, not a substitute.

What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

Deel and Remote are the most complete for agencies with international contributors; neither is a project tool, so tasking still happens elsewhere. Marketplaces solve sourcing but leave you with fees and a second system of record.

Deel: best for paying contractors across countries

Deel offers contractor agreements, invoice handling and payouts for international contractors, plus employer-of-record services if you later hire abroad. Its breadth suits agencies building a distributed bench. Limitations: it's a payments and compliance hub, not a place to assign or review work, and total cost depends on payout methods and currency conversion. Skip it if all your freelancers are local and invoice you directly. See Deel's pricing page.

Remote: best for compliance-led contractor management

Remote provides contractor management alongside employer-of-record services and emphasises compliance support. Limitations: as with Deel, tasking and approvals live in your project tool, and you should check on the official page which compliance protections are included and which cost extra. Skip it if you need sourcing help more than payments. See Remote's pricing page.

Worksome: best for managing a large freelancer pool

Worksome positions itself as a freelancer management system for organisations with many contingent workers, covering onboarding, classification support, approvals and payments. Limitations: it's aimed at larger programs, so a small agency may find the setup heavier than the problem. Skip it if you work with fewer than a handful of regular freelancers. See Worksome's site.

Upwork: best for sourcing specialists quickly

Upwork is a large freelance marketplace with contracts, milestones, time logging and payments handled inside the platform, plus business plans for teams. Limitations: fees apply to contracts (check the current fee schedule on the official page), and you'll still need to screen candidates yourself. Skip it if you already know who you want to hire. See Upwork pricing.

Contra: best for portfolio-led independent talent

Contra is a network for independent professionals built around portfolios, with tools for proposals, contracts and payments. It leans toward design, development and creative work that agencies buy often. Limitations: confirm current client-side terms and fees on Contra's own site before you commit. Skip it if you need high-volume hiring. See Contra's site.

Gusto: best for US contractors alongside payroll

Gusto runs payroll for US employees and can also pay contractors from the same account; check the official page for what contractor tax-form support each plan includes. That keeps everyone in one place for a US agency. Limitations: international contractor support is narrower than Deel's or Remote's, and there's no sourcing or tasking. Skip it if your bench is spread across countries. See Gusto pricing.

Does a platform really protect you from contractor classification risk?

Not on its own. Based on the product information reviewed, some platforms offer classification guidance or compliance add-ons, but the working relationship you create (fixed hours, your equipment, ongoing control) is what regulators look at, so take local legal and tax advice.

What should the freelancer lifecycle look like from onboarding to offboarding?

Run every contributor through the same six stages: approve, contract, set up, assign, pay and offboard. The platform handles some stages; your project, signature and password tools handle the rest, so map who owns each.

  1. Approve: record the need, budget and rate, and check the forecast; our capacity planning software guide covers spotting the gap.
  2. Contract: agreement, confidentiality, IP assignment and data-processing terms, signed electronically; compare e-signature tools for agency contracts.
  3. Set up: grant least-privilege access to your project tool and only the client vaults they need.
  4. Assign: brief and track work in your delivery system, and compare agency project tools and their guest-user rules.
  5. Pay: approve time or deliverables, then pay against invoices; agencies rebilling contractor time should align this with client invoicing software.
  6. Offboard: remove project, chat and client-platform access the day the engagement ends, rotate shared credentials and archive the agreement.

Offboarding is where agencies most often slip. Pair this with the handover checklist in our guide to password managers for agencies.

Which contractor and tax issues need expert review?

Classification, cross-border tax obligations and IP ownership are the three areas to put in front of a qualified adviser before you scale a freelancer bench. Rules differ by country and change, so don't rely on a platform's marketing or this page for a legal answer.

  • Classification: whether each contributor is legitimately independent under the law where they work and where you operate.
  • Tax paperwork: which forms you collect and file, and any withholding obligations.
  • Permanent establishment: whether long-term contractors in another country create obligations there.
  • IP and confidentiality: whether work created for clients is properly assigned to you, so you can assign it onward.
  • Client contracts: whether your client agreements permit subcontracting and how data can be shared.

What mistakes do agencies make with freelancers?

The most expensive mistake is leaving former freelancers with access to client platforms. Close behind is starting work before the agreement is signed, which leaves IP ownership unclear.

  • Paying from scattered invoices with no record of approved hours.
  • Treating a long-term, full-time freelancer exactly like an employee without advice.
  • Using a marketplace and a payments platform without deciding which is the system of record.
  • Rebilling contractor time without tracking the margin on it.

What's the verdict?

Deel or Remote is the right core for agencies paying contractors internationally, Gusto for US-only agencies, and Worksome for large freelancer programs. Use Upwork or Contra for sourcing, not as your system of record.

What else do agencies ask about managing freelancers?

Most follow-ups concern payments, access and agreements. These answers are general, so take professional advice on legal and tax questions.

Do we need a freelancer platform if we only use three contractors?

Probably not a dedicated one. A signed agreement template, your project tool, a password manager and your accounting software can cover a small bench, provided someone owns offboarding.

Can freelancers use our project management tool?

Yes, most agency project tools support guest or collaborator accounts. Limit them to the projects they work on and remove them when the engagement ends.

Who owns work a freelancer creates for a client?

That depends on your agreement and local law. Make sure your contract assigns IP to the agency so you can pass it to the client, and have a lawyer review the template.

Should we pay freelancers by the hour or by deliverable?

Hourly suits ongoing or uncertain work, and deliverable pricing suits well-defined pieces. Either way, approve the time or deliverable in writing before payment.

How do we compare platform fees fairly?

Model a realistic month with your actual contractors, countries and payout methods, including currency conversion. Headline per-contractor prices rarely tell the whole story.
