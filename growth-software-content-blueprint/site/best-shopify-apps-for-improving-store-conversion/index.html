Which Shopify conversion apps should you choose?

Start with Shopify's own Search & Discovery and Bundles apps, then add a specialist only where your data shows a leak they can't fix. For most stores that means one search or recommendation app, one reviews app and one capture tool, not one app per idea.

  • Free baseline for filters, synonyms and simple recommendations: Shopify Search & Discovery.
  • Large catalogs where shoppers can't find products: Boost AI Search & Discovery.
  • Upsells, cross-sells and cart offers in one place: Rebuy.
  • Personalized merchandising and content across the store: Nosto.
  • Product reviews on a budget: Judge.me.
  • Reviews, UGC and customer insight for brand-led stores: Okendo.
  • Fixed bundles without a third-party app: Shopify Bundles.
  • Email and SMS capture tied to flows you already run: Klaviyo sign-up forms.

Why group Shopify apps by conversion job instead of by popularity?

Because conversion problems are specific: shoppers can't find a product, don't trust it, don't see what goes with it, or leave without giving you a way to follow up. Grouping by job stops you buying three apps that all inject the same "you may also like" widget.

Overlap is the hidden cost. Search apps often include recommendations, recommendation apps often include upsell pop-ups, reviews apps add Q&A and loyalty, and email tools add on-site forms. Two apps doing the same job compete for the same page space, slow the page twice and split the credit, so neither one's reports can be trusted.

Which app fits each conversion job, and when should you skip it?

Each block names the job, the best-fit store and the main reason to skip. Treat every paid app as a recurring cost that must earn its place against the free option.

Shopify Search & Discovery: best free baseline for finding products

Search & Discovery is Shopify's own app for configuring storefront filters, search synonyms, boosted results and product recommendations. Because it's first-party, it adds no extra vendor to your data flow. It's the right first step for small and mid-size catalogs. Move past it when search terms with no results or poor filter coverage show up in your analytics and the app's settings can't fix them.

Boost AI Search & Discovery: best for large, filter-heavy catalogs

Boost focuses on product search and collection filtering for Shopify stores, with merchandising controls on top. It suits catalogs where attribute-based filters (size, fit, material, compatibility) decide the sale. It replaces native search and filter templates, so check theme compatibility and how you'd roll back. Our onsite search software guide compares it with platform-agnostic search engines.

Rebuy: best for cart and post-purchase offers

Rebuy centres on upsells, cross-sells, smart cart and post-purchase offers for Shopify brands. It suits stores where average order value is the lever and the catalog has natural add-ons. It overlaps with recommendation apps and cart-drawer apps, so remove those rather than running both. Skip it if your catalog has few complementary products.

Nosto: best for store-wide personalized merchandising

Nosto covers personalized recommendations, content and merchandising across the storefront, so it can replace several single-purpose apps. That's its appeal and its risk: it touches many templates, so it needs a clear owner. Our guide to ecommerce personalization software compares it with other personalization platforms.

Judge.me: best for budget-conscious review collection

Judge.me is a reviews app aimed at collecting and displaying product reviews with photos and Q&A. It's a sensible default for stores that need trust signals without a large monthly fee. Check that you can export reviews in a portable format before you collect thousands of them.

Okendo: best for brand-led stores that want UGC and insight

Okendo combines reviews with user-generated content and customer data capture, which suits brands that use attribute ratings (fit, skin type) to sell. It overlaps with loyalty, survey and quiz apps, so decide which tool owns each customer attribute. The wider category is covered in our reviews and UGC platforms guide.

Shopify Bundles: best for simple fixed bundles

Shopify Bundles is a first-party app for creating fixed bundles and multipacks that track inventory against the component products. It's the cleanest option when the bundle is defined in advance. Skip it if you need build-your-own boxes or tiered discounts, which need a specialist app.

Klaviyo sign-up forms: best for capture if Klaviyo already runs your email

Klaviyo includes on-site sign-up forms that feed straight into its lists and flows, so a separate pop-up app is often redundant. Klaviyo documents a free plan with profile and email-send limits; check the current limits on that page before relying on it. Skip it if you send email through another platform, because forms tied to the wrong tool create sync work.

What do conversion apps cost beyond the monthly fee?

Every storefront app can add scripts, data copies and theme changes that cost speed and flexibility. Those costs don't show on the invoice, which is why a store with ten modest apps can be harder to run than one with three expensive ones.

  • Performance: each app that loads on product or collection pages adds work for the browser. Track page speed before and after each install with one of the website performance monitoring tools.
  • Conflicts: two apps editing the same cart drawer or product template can break each other after a theme update.
  • Data access: read the permissions each app requests. A reviews app rarely needs order edit access.
  • Recurring fees: many apps price by order volume, so a good month can move you up a tier.
  • Lock-in: leftover code after uninstall, review data you can't export and customer attributes stored only in the app.

How do you audit your app stack before adding another tool?

List every installed app with its job, the pages it loads on, its monthly cost and its owner, then remove any app whose job is already covered. Only add a new app when a measured leak has no owner.

  1. Inventory: export the installed app list and note the job each one does in a single phrase.
  2. Map jobs: group by search, recommendations, reviews, bundles, capture, checkout and analytics. Two apps under one job is a flag.
  3. Check native features: compare each job against Shopify's own apps and your theme's built-in sections.
  4. Measure load: note which templates each app loads on and its effect on page speed.
  5. Review permissions and data: confirm what each app stores and whether you can export it.
  6. Decide: keep, replace or remove. Remove leftover theme code after uninstalling.
  7. Test additions: introduce one new app at a time with a holdout or before-and-after comparison, using an A/B testing tool where traffic allows.

Do conversion apps really lift sales, or just claim credit?

An app's own revenue report usually counts orders that touched its widget, which isn't the same as revenue it caused. Only a holdout test or a careful before-and-after comparison shows whether an app adds sales you wouldn't have made anyway.

What else do buyers ask about Shopify conversion apps?

Most questions are about how many apps are too many and what to try first. Short answers follow.

How many conversion apps should a Shopify store run?

There's no fixed number, but each conversion job should have one owner. If two apps do the same job, remove one.

Should I start with free Shopify apps?

Yes, for search, filters, simple recommendations and fixed bundles. Move to a paid app when your data shows a gap the free option can't close.

Do Shopify apps slow down my store?

They can, especially apps that load scripts on every page. Measure page speed before and after each install and remove leftover code after uninstalling.

How do I know if an app is actually improving conversion?

Compare against a holdout or a clean before-and-after period, and look at total revenue per visitor rather than the app's own attributed revenue.

What's the verdict on Shopify conversion apps?

Use Shopify's free apps as the baseline, then add one specialist per proven leak: Boost for search-heavy catalogs, Rebuy or Nosto for offers and personalization, Judge.me or Okendo for reviews, and Klaviyo forms if Klaviyo already runs your email. Audit before every install, and judge apps on incremental revenue, not their own dashboards.
