Best Project Management Software for Digital Marketing Agencies

Which project management software should a marketing agency choose?

Choose an agency-specific platform such as Teamwork.com or Productive if you bill retainers and need time, budgets and client access in one tool. Choose a general work management tool such as Asana, ClickUp, monday.com or Wrike if delivery is your main problem and billing already lives elsewhere.

By buyer type, based on our editorial assessment:

  • Retainer and client-work agencies that want budgets beside tasks: Teamwork.com.
  • Agencies that want projects, time, budgets and profitability in one system: Productive.
  • Teams that value clean templates and cross-team visibility: Asana.
  • Price-sensitive agencies willing to configure a flexible, feature-dense tool: ClickUp.
  • Teams that think in boards and dashboards and want quick setup: monday.com.
  • Larger creative teams that need proofing and request forms inside PM: Wrike.
  • Small studios that want calm client communication over detailed tracking: Basecamp.

What does an agency need that a generic task board misses?

An agency runs many clients in parallel, so it needs hard separation between client spaces, repeatable templates for recurring deliverables, and a link between hours and what the client pays. A generic board rarely answers "is this retainer still profitable?" on its own.

Think about the five kinds of work your team juggles: client projects with an end date, monthly retainers that repeat, internal tasks such as sales and hiring, creative jobs that need approvals, and reporting that has to go out on time. Software that treats all of them as identical tasks will work, but you'll rebuild the agency layer yourself with tags, custom fields and spreadsheets.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The two agency-specific platforms win on retainers and profitability; the general tools win on flexibility, familiarity and, often, a lower entry price. None is right for every agency, so read the skip-it-if lines as carefully as the strengths.

Teamwork.com: best for retainer-led client-work agencies

Teamwork.com positions itself around client work rather than generic tasks, so projects, time logging, budgets and client users sit in one product. Its support site lists the current plans as Free Forever, Basics, Accelerate, Optimize and Enterprise (checked 7 November 2026), with older Deliver and Grow plans now treated as legacy. Its strength is a project model that matches how agencies sell. Check which tier includes budgets and profitability reporting, because that decides the real cost for a ten-person team. Skip it if you only need a task list.

Productive: best for one system from deal to invoice

Productive goes beyond project management into budgets, time, resourcing and agency financial reporting. Its pricing page lists Essential, Professional and Ultimate plans priced per user, with Ultimate quoted on request (checked 7 November 2026); we've left out per-user figures because Productive's own pages showed different numbers. That breadth pays off only if you also move time and billing into it. Skip it if your finance team is happy with its current invoicing and only delivery needs fixing.

Asana: best for clean templates and visibility across teams

Asana is strong on project templates, timelines, portfolios and rules-based automation, which suits repeatable monthly deliverables. Its pricing page lists Starter at $10.99 and Advanced at $24.99 per user per month billed annually, or $13.49 and $30.49 billed monthly (checked 7 November 2026). The same page says guests can work on projects without adding to your user count, though that depends on your account being set up as an organization. It isn't built around agency billing, so retainer hours and profitability need a separate time or finance tool. Skip it if you want budgets and timesheets in the same screen as tasks.

ClickUp: best for agencies that want one heavily configurable tool

ClickUp combines tasks, docs, goals, time tracking and many views in one workspace, so it can stand in for several apps. Its FAQ lists Unlimited at $7 and Business at $12 per user per month billed yearly, alongside a Free Forever plan (checked 7 November 2026). For client access, ClickUp's help center says view-only guests carry no fee on paid plans, while guests with edit or comment rights count against a capped allowance (checked 7 November 2026). The trade-off is governance: without a clear structure for client spaces, it sprawls quickly. Skip it if nobody on your team will own the setup.

monday.com: best for board-first teams who want quick wins

monday.com's work management product is approachable, visual and quick to set up with dashboards and automations. Guest billing is the line to read: its support article says that on Standard, the first three guests are free and every four guests after that use one billed seat, Basic has no guests, and viewers don't count toward billing (checked 7 November 2026). Skip it if you need deep agency financials. Current seat prices are on the monday.com pricing page.

Wrike: best for creative teams that need proofing inside PM

Wrike offers request forms, workload views and proofing that suit agencies with steady creative output. Its pricing page lists a Free plan, Team at $10 and Business at $25 per user per month billed annually (checked 7 November 2026). Watch external access: Wrike's help center says the free Collaborator license is being discontinued and isn't available to new accounts from 20 January 2025 (checked 7 November 2026), so budget for paid seats for clients who need more than shared views. Skip it if you're under ten people and want something light.

Basecamp: best for small studios that value calm communication

Basecamp keeps messages, to-dos, files and schedules per project, and clients can be invited into projects. Its sign-up page says it bills only for employees and guests and clients are free to invite (checked 7 November 2026), which is unusually generous for client-heavy agencies. We haven't quoted a price because two official Basecamp pages showed different plan structures, so confirm the current one on the Basecamp pricing page. It's lighter on budgets and workload planning than the agency platforms. Skip it if you bill hourly against retainers.

What would a 10-person, 15-client agency actually pay for?

Cost comes down to paid internal seats, how clients are counted and which tier unlocks time, budgets and client permissions. For a ten-person agency with fifteen clients, the client-access rules can matter as much as the headline seat price.

Here's a hypothetical model built from stated assumptions and the list prices above. Assume ten internal users who all need full access, fifteen clients who each need one or two reviewers (fifteen to thirty guests), and annual billing.

  1. Internal seats on the published tiers: ten users on ClickUp Business at $12 come to $120 a month; on Wrike Team at $10, $100; on Wrike Business at $25, $250; on Asana Advanced at $24.99, about $250. All are annual-billing monthly equivalents before tax.
  2. Client users: on monday.com Standard, thirty collaborating guests would use roughly seven extra billed seats under the "first three free, then one seat per four" rule, while thirty view-only guests would add nothing. On Basecamp, the official page says clients are free; on Asana, guests in an organization don't add to the user count.
  3. Add-ons: time tracking, AI features or extra automations may sit on higher tiers.
  4. Billing term: monthly billing costs more where vendors publish both rates, as Asana does.

These figures are arithmetic on list prices, not quotes; add Teamwork.com and Productive once you've confirmed their current prices. If two tools land close together, pick the one that removes a separate time or billing tool, because that's where real savings appear.

Will a new project tool actually fix missed deadlines?

Software makes missed work visible, but it doesn't fix unclear scopes, too many clients per account manager or a team at full capacity. Based on the product information reviewed, the tools above help most when templates, ownership and weekly review habits are agreed first.

Which tool fits your agency's situation?

Match the tool to how you sell and staff the work. A retainer SEO agency, a campaign-led creative studio and a fast-growing performance shop need different things from project management software.

  • Retainer-based SEO or content agency, 5–15 people: recurring task templates and hours against monthly retainers matter most, so shortlist Teamwork.com and Productive, and compare ClickUp if budget is tight and someone will own the setup.
  • Creative studio with many approvals: Wrike, or a lighter tool plus proofing.
  • Agency with many client stakeholders who mostly watch progress: favor tools whose view-only or client access is free, such as Basecamp, ClickUp's view-only guests or monday.com viewers, and confirm the rules on each official page.
  • Agency automating handoffs between tools: see our roundup of workflow automation tools for marketing agencies.

What mistakes do agencies make when buying project management software?

The commonest mistake is buying for features instead of for the delivery process you already run. The second is inviting clients before permissions, guest billing and naming conventions are settled.

  • Building every client from scratch instead of from a tested template.
  • Letting each account manager invent their own statuses and fields.
  • Ignoring guest-user rules until the first invoice arrives, or assuming a free external license still exists.
  • Tracking time in a different tool without a reliable link to projects.
  • Skipping a pilot with one or two clients before migrating everyone.

What else do agencies ask about project management software?

Most remaining questions are about client access, seats and when to move off spreadsheets. The short answers follow; confirm plan details on each vendor's official pricing page.

Should clients have logins to our project management tool?

Give clients access only to the views they need, such as approvals, milestones and shared files. Full access to internal boards exposes estimates, internal notes and other clients' names if permissions are set up carelessly.

Do client guests count as paid seats?

It depends on the vendor, the plan and the guest's rights. Basecamp's official page says clients are free to invite, ClickUp doesn't charge for view-only guests on paid plans, and monday.com bills one seat per four guests after the first three on Standard, so check the rules before you invite fifteen clients.

Is a general work management tool enough for a small agency?

Often, yes, if you mainly need tasks, templates and a calendar. Once you need retainer budgets, billable time and profitability per client in one place, an agency-specific platform usually saves duplicate admin.

When should an agency replace spreadsheets with project management software?

Move when recurring work is missed, when nobody can see who is overloaded, or when you can't tell how many hours a retainer has used. Those are delivery risks, not tidiness problems.

How long does it take to move an agency onto a new project tool?

Plan for templates first, then one or two pilot clients, then the rest of the book. Rushing every client across in one week tends to recreate the old mess in the new tool.
