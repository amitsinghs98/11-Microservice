Which PPC management software should an agency choose?

Start with Google Ads manager accounts and the free desktop editors, then add one optimization tool that matches the job your team repeats most: Optmyzr for rules and broad automation, Adalysis for ad testing and audits, Opteo for a ranked to-do list, or Shape for budget pacing. Look at Search Ads 360 or Skai only when client budgets, channel count and governance needs justify an enterprise contract.

  • Agencies that want configurable rules, scripts and reporting across many accounts and channels: Optmyzr.
  • Teams whose weekly work is ad copy testing and account health checks: Adalysis.
  • Small teams and freelancers that want recommendations ranked for them: Opteo.
  • Agencies whose biggest risk is a client budget overspending or underspending by month-end: Shape.
  • Large search programs that need bidding and reporting across several engines: Search Ads 360.
  • Agencies running retail media, search and social for enterprise brands: Skai.

What's the difference between PPC management software, native consoles and dashboards?

Native consoles run campaigns, management software changes and checks many accounts at once, and dashboards only display results. An agency that buys a reporting dashboard expecting it to catch overspending will be disappointed, because most dashboards can't act on an account.

A management tool earns its fee by replacing manual review: spotting a broken landing page, an exhausted budget or an ad group with no active ads before the client does. A dashboard earns its fee by cutting report-building time, a separate problem covered in our guide to cross-channel paid media reporting tools.

Native tools cover more than people assume. Google describes a manager account as an umbrella account for viewing and managing linked accounts, with cross-account reporting, alerts, access levels and consolidated billing (checked 7 November 2026). Google Ads Editor is a free, downloadable application for downloading accounts, editing offline and posting changes (checked 7 November 2026). If your pain is only bulk editing, read our comparison of Google Ads Editor against third-party management tools first; our shortlist of Google Ads management tools for multi-account teams adds scripts and the API.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The third-party tools overlap more than their marketing suggests, so choose by the job your account managers repeat most. Each block names that job and who should look elsewhere.

Google Ads manager account, Google Ads Editor and Microsoft Advertising Editor: the baseline

Best for: every agency. A manager account gives one login across client accounts, and the two desktop editors handle bulk changes, find-and-replace and offline drafts. Microsoft says its editor lets you work offline and publish bulk changes across multiple campaigns (checked 7 November 2026). Limitations: little proactive monitoring, so a paused campaign or a budget running out early still needs a person to notice it; Google also notes that one email address can be associated with up to 20 Google Ads accounts, manager accounts included (checked 7 November 2026). Skip it if: you can't, really; everything else on this page sits on top of it.

Optmyzr: best for agencies that want configurable automation

Best for: agencies with enough accounts that rule-based checks and pre-built scripts replace hours of manual review. What it does: rule engines, optimization tools, budget monitoring, audits and reporting; its site lists support for Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Amazon Ads, Meta and LinkedIn, with the social networks handled through its social product line (checked 7 November 2026). Pricing: tiered by the ad spend you manage, with a 14-day free trial that needs no credit card (checked 7 November 2026). Optmyzr's own pages quoted different entry prices, so we've left the figure out. Limitations: the breadth is also the cost; someone has to decide which rules run where, or you get alert fatigue. Skip it if: you manage a handful of simple accounts.

Adalysis: best for ad testing and audits

Best for: teams whose weekly rhythm is testing ad copy and running health checks. What it does: structured ad testing, plus daily audits of Google Ads and Microsoft Ads accounts with alerts (checked 7 November 2026). Pricing: the pricing page says plans are based on ad spend and start from $149 a month, with unlimited accounts and no contract (checked 7 November 2026), and the docs describe a 30-day free trial without a card, plus discounts for six-month and annual terms (checked 7 November 2026). Limitations: it's narrower than a full automation suite, so pacing-heavy agencies may want more. Skip it if: your bottleneck is budget pacing rather than creative and account hygiene.

Opteo: best for small teams that want a to-do list

Best for: lean agencies and freelancers who want recommendations ranked for them. What it does: scans accounts and proposes improvements you approve, plus monitoring and reporting; its docs describe linking Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Meta, LinkedIn and TikTok accounts (checked 7 November 2026), though some features stay Google-only. Pricing: the pricing page lists Basic at $129 a month for 10 accounts and $25,000 in monthly spend, and Professional at $249 for 25 accounts and $100,000, with spend combined across connected accounts (checked 7 November 2026). Limitations: less room for custom logic than a rules-first tool, and the account caps matter as the book grows. Skip it if: your senior buyers prefer to write their own rules and scripts.

Shape: best for agencies pacing many client budgets

Best for: agencies whose clients set monthly budgets that must land on target across several platforms. What it does: budget tracking and pacing, including a Budget Pacer that recommends daily budgets (checked 7 November 2026). Pricing: the help center says one client can be set up free, with Premium tiers set by the total ad spend you manage (checked 7 November 2026); some help pages are old, so confirm the current tiers. Limitations: it paces; it doesn't test or audit. Skip it if: budgets are flexible and your pain is ad quality. Our list of budget pacing and spend forecasting tools compares it with the alternatives.

Search Ads 360: best for large multi-engine search programs

Best for: agencies running large search budgets across several engines for enterprise clients. What it does: Google's enterprise search platform; its help center lists Google Ads, Microsoft Advertising, Yahoo! JAPAN Ads and Baidu among supported platforms (checked 7 November 2026). Pricing: no public price; the route is Google's sales team or a partner. Limitations: onboarding and a contract make it rarely worth it for a small book. Skip it if: your clients spend modestly or run Google Ads only.

Skai: best for enterprise brands across search, social and retail media

Best for: agencies whose clients buy retail media alongside search and social. What it does: an enterprise omnichannel advertising platform. Pricing: Skai's pricing page describes flat annual pricing and lists a Standard tier at $114k a year for advertisers spending up to $4M annually (checked 7 November 2026). Limitations: an enterprise footprint and commitment. Skip it if: your work is mostly paid search for small and midsize businesses.

How is PPC management software priced?

Agency-focused tools mostly price by the monthly ad spend you manage through them, sometimes with an account cap on each tier, while enterprise platforms sell annual contracts. That means your bill rises with client growth even if your team doesn't, so model next year's managed spend, not this month's.

  • Spend-based tiers: Adalysis, Optmyzr and Shape all tie the tier to managed spend, and Adalysis says its system adjusts your subscription each month as spend changes (checked 7 November 2026). Ask how a seasonal spike is billed.
  • Spend plus account caps: Opteo's tiers combine both, so 12 small accounts can push you up a tier even at low spend.
  • Flat annual contracts: Skai publishes a flat annual model; Search Ads 360 is quote-only.

As a hypothetical example, an agency managing $80,000 a month across 20 accounts would sit above Opteo's Basic caps on both counts and inside Professional, at the $249 list price checked above. Use the same arithmetic on each vendor's current page before comparing quotes.

What does the workflow look like at 5, 20 and 50 accounts?

At five accounts, native tools plus a disciplined weekly checklist are usually enough. As the book grows toward 20 or 50 accounts, manual checks get harder to keep consistent, and a management tool earns its fee only if it replaces work rather than adding dashboards.

These are hypothetical planning scenarios, not measurements.

  • 5 accounts, one or two buyers: a manager account, the editors, a naming convention and a weekly checklist. Add Opteo or Adalysis only if one task clearly drags.
  • 20 accounts, three to five buyers: inconsistency becomes the risk. Standardize alerts for budget, disapprovals and conversion drops in one tool, Optmyzr or Adalysis, with Shape alongside if month-end variance keeps causing client calls.
  • 50 accounts, a team with leads: governance matters as much as optimization: role-based access, a reviewed change log, approval steps for big budget moves and a standard onboarding audit. Optmyzr suits most agencies; Search Ads 360 or Skai fit enterprise client mixes.

How should you handle client access and permissions?

Keep clients as owners of their own ad accounts and link them to your manager account, rather than creating accounts the agency owns. Then give each person in the agency the narrowest access level that lets them do their job.

  • Record who has admin, standard and read-only access for every client, and review it when someone leaves; a shared vault from our list of password managers for digital agencies keeps tool logins out of spreadsheets.
  • Connect third-party tools with a login that has only the access the tool needs, and decide in writing which changes it may make automatically.
  • Keep the change history exportable, so you can answer "who changed this, and why?" in a client review.

Will a PPC management tool actually save account-manager hours?

Only if it replaces a check someone already does by hand; a tool that adds new reports and alerts nobody owns usually costs time. The vendors document features, not the hours an agency recovers, so treat any time-saving claim as something to prove during a trial.

What mistakes do agencies make when buying PPC software?

The common mistakes are switching on too many rules at once, ignoring how pricing grows with the client book and confusing bidding with management. A short written trial plan avoids all three.

  • Automating before agreeing thresholds. A rule that pauses keywords on cost per acquisition needs an agreed target and minimum data per client.
  • Forgetting the pricing trigger. Ask what moves you to the next tier: managed spend, account count, users or features.
  • Treating bid automation as management. Platform bidding already does much of this; our roundup of PPC bid management and automation software explains where third-party bidding still adds value.

What else do agencies ask about PPC management software?

The follow-up questions are usually about cost, Microsoft Ads coverage, reporting and whether automation is safe to hand over. Short answers follow.

Do we need PPC management software if we already have a Google Ads manager account?

Not always. A manager account handles access, consolidated billing, alerts and cross-account reporting, so you need a third-party tool only when repetitive checks, budget pacing or audits are eating hours that a person would otherwise spend on strategy.

Is PPC management software priced per account or by ad spend?

Most agency-focused tools price by the monthly ad spend you manage through them, sometimes with an account cap on each tier, while enterprise platforms quote annual contracts. Model your total managed spend and account count before you compare, and confirm the current tiers on each official pricing page.

Can these tools manage Microsoft Advertising as well as Google Ads?

Optmyzr, Adalysis, Opteo and Search Ads 360 all document Microsoft Advertising support, but depth differs by channel and some features remain Google-only. Confirm which actions, not just which reports, are supported for each network you manage.

Is it safe to let a tool make automated changes to client accounts?

It's safe when every rule has a named owner, a cap on how much it can change and a log you review. Start with alerts and suggestions, then switch on automatic changes one rule at a time.

Does PPC management software replace a reporting tool?

Usually not. Management tools include reports, but agencies that send branded, multi-channel client reports often keep a separate reporting platform, so evaluate the two jobs separately.
