Quick answer

Budget cold email as a stack, not a subscription. A realistic monthly cost is the sending platform, plus each mailbox you pay for, plus amortised domain registration, plus verification credits for every new contact, plus any data or monitoring tools, plus the staff time to write, check lists and handle replies. For small teams, mailboxes and labour often outweigh the platform fee.

We could not re-verify vendor prices for this draft, so this page gives the structure of each vendor's pricing and a calculator you fill with current figures from the official pages linked below. It does not recommend high volumes: a smaller, well-targeted programme costs less and carries less reputational and legal risk.

The seven cost layers

  1. Sending platform. The sequencing tool, such as those compared in our roundup of cold email software for B2B agencies. Priced per seat, per active contact, per email volume or a mix.
  2. Domains. Many teams send outreach from secondary domains to shield the main brand domain. Each is an annual registrar fee plus DNS setup time.
  3. Mailboxes. Each sending address is usually a paid user in Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, or an account with a specialist mailbox provider. This is a recurring per-mailbox cost.
  4. Verification. Credits to check addresses before sending. Our email verification tool comparison explains per-credit pricing and minimum purchases.
  5. Inbox monitoring. Placement tests, blocklist checks and DMARC reporting. Some sending platforms bundle basic checks; dedicated tools are covered in the deliverability tools guide.
  6. Data. Contact database or enrichment credits, if you do not already have a researched list. See the B2B contact database comparison for how credits are consumed.
  7. Human operations. List research, copywriting, compliance review, reply handling and CRM hygiene. This is the line most budgets omit.

How sending platforms price

  • Per seat: each user pays, and mailboxes may be limited per seat. Predictable for small sales teams, expensive for agencies with many client mailboxes.
  • Per active contact or lead: you pay for the number of prospects in live sequences. Suits agencies running many mailboxes at modest volume.
  • Per email volume: tiers by emails sent per month. Rewards restraint and penalises spray-and-pray campaigns, which is no bad thing.
  • Per mailbox slot: pricing scales with connected sending accounts.
  • Bundled credits: data, enrichment or AI credits included with a plan, often resetting each billing period.

Several platforms advertise "unlimited mailboxes" on their sending plans while limiting active contacts or monthly emails. Unlimited mailboxes do not make unlimited mailboxes a good idea: every additional mailbox is another paid account and another reputation to look after.

A cost calculator framework

Fill in each unit cost from the vendor's current pricing page. Annualise anything billed yearly and divide by twelve.

Monthly cold email cost: inputs and formula
Input Symbol How to estimate
Platform fee P Plan that covers your active contacts and monthly emails, plus required seats
Mailboxes M × m Number of sending mailboxes times the per-mailbox monthly price
Domains D × d ÷ 12 Number of sending domains times annual registration
Verification N × v New contacts per month times cost per verification credit
Monitoring I Deliverability or DMARC tool subscription, if not bundled
Data R Database or enrichment plan, or credits used per month
Labour H × h Hours per month times loaded hourly cost

Monthly cost = P + (M × m) + (D × d ÷ 12) + (N × v) + I + R + (H × h). Divide by new prospects contacted to get cost per prospect, and by qualified conversations to get the number that actually matters.

Three outreach scenarios

These are hypothetical planning scenarios with stated assumptions, not recommendations of a volume. Each assumes researched, relevant prospects and a working opt-out process.

Scenario A: founder-led outreach

Assumptions: one person, about 200 new prospects a month, a three-step sequence (about 600 emails), 1 secondary domain, 2 mailboxes, 4 hours a week on research and replies.

Cost drivers: the smallest platform tier that fits 200 active contacts, 2 mailbox subscriptions, 1 domain, 200 verification credits a month and roughly 17 hours of founder time. At this size, a per-seat tool and a volume-tier tool may cost similar amounts; the founder's time is almost certainly the largest cost.

Scenario B: three-rep sales team

Assumptions: three reps, about 1,000 new prospects a month in total, four-step sequences (about 4,000 emails), 2 secondary domains, 6 mailboxes, data from a contact database, CRM sync required.

Cost drivers: per-seat pricing scales cleanly here, but check whether CRM sync sits on a higher tier. Add 6 mailboxes, 2 domains, 1,000 verification credits, a data plan and a monitoring tool. If reps also need calls and tasks, a sales engagement platform may replace both the sequencer and some manual work.

Scenario C: agency running five clients

Assumptions: five clients, each with their own domains and mailboxes (2 domains and 4 mailboxes per client, 10 domains and 20 mailboxes in all), about 300 new prospects per client a month, separate client workspaces and reporting.

Cost drivers: an active-lead or volume-tier platform avoids paying a seat per client mailbox, but check how client workspaces and white-label reporting are priced. Mailboxes and domains are 20 and 10 units respectively, so decide early whether the client or the agency owns and pays for them. Labour for reply triage across five inboxes is substantial and should be priced into the retainer.

Annual discounts, credit expiry and renewal

  • Annual billing: many vendors discount annual plans. Only commit once a pilot shows reply quality is acceptable, because a deliverability problem can stall a programme mid-contract.
  • Credit expiry: subscription credits for data, enrichment or verification commonly reset each period, while some pay-as-you-go verification credits are sold without expiry. Confirm which applies before buying in bulk.
  • Next-tier triggers: exceeding active contacts, monthly emails or mailbox slots. Ask whether the vendor upgrades you automatically or pauses sending.
  • Renewal: introductory pricing may not carry into the second year. Ask for renewal terms in writing.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • What counts as an active contact, and when does a contact stop counting?
  • Are warm-up, inbox rotation and deliverability checks included, or extra?
  • How are client workspaces, seats and white-label reports priced for agencies?
  • Do data credits roll over? What happens to unused credits on downgrade?
  • How does the platform handle unsubscribes and suppression across workspaces?
  • What does the acceptable-use policy prohibit, and what triggers account suspension?

The last two questions matter as much as price. Our guide to building a compliant B2B outreach stack covers the suppression and opt-out workflow every option must support.

FAQs

Why not send cold email from my main domain?

Complaints and spam reports from outreach can affect the reputation of the domain that also carries invoices, support and marketing. A secondary domain adds cost but limits that exposure. It does not remove your legal duties.

Is warm-up a substitute for list quality?

No. Warm-up may help a new mailbox build sending history, but relevant, verified recipients and low complaint rates do more for deliverability than any warm-up network.

What is a sensible first budget?

Price Scenario A with current vendor figures and run it for a month. Measure qualified replies before adding mailboxes or domains.

Do I need a separate CRM?

Usually yes once more than one person handles replies. See the small-business CRM shortlist for options that sync with outbound tools.
