Quick answer

For a team producing 10 articles a month, the AI subscription is likely to be a few percent of the real cost. Human research, editing and fact-checking dominate. In our hypothetical scenarios below, a $20 monthly assistant plan sits beside roughly $1,250 of editing time.

So compare tools on how much editing they save and what their usage limits allow, not on headline price or cost per word. Verified figures on this page come from Anthropic's Claude pricing page, checked 4 November 2026. Other vendors are described by pricing model only, with links to their official pages.

How AI content tools charge

Five pricing models dominate the category, and they are not interchangeable.

  • Per-user subscription with fair-use limits. General assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT charge per person and cap usage in time windows rather than by word count.
  • Per seat with feature tiers. Marketing platforms such as Jasper charge per seat and reserve brand governance or collaboration features for higher tiers. Our comparison of Jasper, Copy.ai and Writesonic explains how their focus differs.
  • Credits or generation quotas. Image tools and some writing tools sell a monthly pool of credits, with each output consuming a variable number.
  • Media minutes or exports. Video tools typically meter by minutes of video, exports or AI feature usage.
  • Seat plus usage. Enterprise plans increasingly combine a seat fee with metered usage at API rates.

Per-word pricing looks comparable but rarely is. A tool that produces 3,000 words you rewrite costs more than one that produces 1,200 words you lightly edit. Usage caps in general assistants also apply to research, revisions and analysis, not only to final output.

Monthly vs annual billing

On the verified Claude figures, annual billing saves 20% on Team seats ($20 against $25) and about 17% on Pro ($200 a year against $240 billed monthly). That is meaningful for a stable team and a trap for an agency whose client roster changes each quarter.

Our rule of thumb: pay monthly for the first two or three months while you learn which roles use the tool daily, then move only those seats to annual. Ask whether annual seats can be reassigned when people leave, because an unused annual seat erases the discount.

Limits and add-ons

The line items that most often surprise buyers are not on the headline plan card.

  • Usage windows. Assistants that reset limits every few hours or weekly can stall a deadline day even if monthly usage looks light.
  • Brand and knowledge features. Brand voice, style guides and shared knowledge bases are often reserved for higher tiers.
  • Image and video credits. Generation quotas are separate from writing seats. Our guides to AI image tools for ad creative and AI video editors for marketing compare these meters.
  • SEO data. Brief and optimisation tools usually sit outside the writing subscription. The SEO software pricing guide covers that budget line.
  • Admin and security. Single sign-on, audit logs and data controls tend to start at team or enterprise tiers.

Standard workloads and assumptions

To compare like with like, we use two standard units. An article is a 1,500 to 2,000-word researched blog post with sources. A marketing asset is a short piece: a social post, ad variant, email or product description.

All scenarios below are hypothetical. They assume a blended internal cost of $50 per hour for editing time, which is an illustrative figure, not market data. Substitute your own rate. Editing estimates are 2.5 hours per article (fact-check, edit, approval), 10 minutes per short asset, 1 hour per short video and 5 minutes per image.

Three cost scenarios

Scenario A: one writer, 10 articles a month

Tool invoice: one Claude Pro plan at $20 billed monthly (checked 4 November 2026). Editing: 10 articles at 2.5 hours equals 25 hours, or $1,250 at the assumed rate. Total: about $1,270 a month, or roughly $127 per article, before any SEO tool. The AI subscription is under 2% of that.

The lever that matters is editing time. A tool or workflow that cuts editing from 2.5 hours to 2 hours saves $250 a month here, far more than any difference between subscription prices. Our guide to long-form AI blog writing tools compares research and sourcing features that affect that number.

Scenario B: three people, 100 marketing assets a month

Tool invoice: three Claude Team standard seats at $25 each billed monthly, or $75 (checked 4 November 2026). Annual billing would be $60 a month. Add design seats for whichever tool you use. Editing: 100 assets at 10 minutes equals about 16.7 hours, or $833. Total: about $908 a month plus design seats, or roughly $9 per asset.

Short-form work is where specialised copywriting tools try to win. Before paying for one, check whether its templates genuinely cut review time. The AI copywriting software roundup compares those features.

Scenario C: five people, 10 articles, 100 assets, 8 videos and 40 images

Tool invoice: four Claude Team standard seats and one premium seat. Billed monthly, that is $225 (4 x $25 plus $125); billed annually, $180 a month (checked 4 November 2026). Add separate video, image and design subscriptions, which we leave as variables because their pricing was not verified for this draft.

Editing: 25 hours for articles, 16.7 for assets, 8 for videos and 3.3 for images, about 53 hours or $2,650. Total: roughly $2,875 a month on monthly billing, plus media tools.

Free plans vs trials

Free plans are useful for testing prompts and workflows, but their usage limits make them unreliable for deadline work. Claude's free plan is listed as "limited usage" with limits reset every five hours and weekly (checked 4 November 2026). Trials on paid marketing platforms are better for testing collaboration and brand features, because those are rarely on free tiers. Our roundup of free AI writing tools and their real limits covers this in detail.

Renewal and next-tier triggers

  • Someone hits usage limits more than once a week: price a higher-usage seat for that person only.
  • A second writer joins: compare a team plan against two individual plans, since shared projects and admin controls usually justify the switch.
  • Clients require data controls, single sign-on or audit trails: that usually means an enterprise conversation and a seat minimum.
  • Media volume doubles: re-check credit and minute quotas before renewal, not after an overage.

At renewal, audit actual logins. Our marketing stack audit guide shows how to spot seats and overlapping tools nobody uses.

Questions to ask sales

  • How are usage limits defined, and what happens when we reach them mid-month?
  • Which brand, collaboration and security features are on which tier?
  • Can annual seats be reassigned or reduced at renewal?
  • Are image, video or API usage billed separately from seats?
  • Is our content used to train models, and can that be switched off on our plan?
  • What is the renewal price, and is any discount limited to the first term?

FAQs

What is a fair cost per AI-assisted article?

There is no universal figure. In our hypothetical Scenario A it is about $127 including editing, almost all of it human time. Your number depends on editing hours, internal rates and SEO tooling.

Is a general assistant cheaper than a marketing platform?

On the invoice, often yes. Whether it is cheaper overall depends on whether the platform's brand and workflow features cut enough editing time to cover the difference. Our guide to AI writing tools by team type compares those trade-offs.

Should we budget for API usage instead of seats?

Only if someone will build and maintain the integration. API pricing can be cheaper at scale but moves engineering and monitoring work onto your team.
