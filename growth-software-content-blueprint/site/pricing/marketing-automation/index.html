Quick answer

For a solo marketer with a few hundred subscribers, the figures we confirmed put email-led automation at roughly $97 to $240 a year. For a three-person B2B team that needs CRM-native workflows and scoring, HubSpot Marketing Hub Professional costs at least $12,600 in year one once its required onboarding fee is included. Integration platforms such as Zapier add a separate bill that scales with usage, not contacts.

The biggest cost driver is rarely the headline price. It is the unit you will grow fastest in: contacts, sends, tasks or seats. If you are still choosing a platform, start with our comparison of marketing automation software for small teams and return here to price the shortlist.

The four pricing units

Marketing automation sits across several product types covered in our automation and analytics category overview, and each type tends to bill differently.

Contact-based

Email and marketing automation platforms such as MailerLite, ActiveCampaign and HubSpot price mainly on how many contacts or subscribers you store or market to. Costs rise with list growth even if you send less. Ask whether unsubscribed or inactive contacts count.

Send-volume-based

Brevo prices its marketing plans mainly on emails sent per month. That rewards large lists that send infrequently and penalises high-frequency senders.

Task-, operation- or execution-based

Workflow tools meter activity. Zapier counts tasks, Make meters operations or credits, and n8n Cloud meters workflow executions. A single lead can consume one unit or ten depending on how many steps your workflow has, so the same automation can cost very differently on each. Our side-by-side look at Zapier, Make and n8n usage models walks through that maths.

Seat-based

Some plans charge per user. HubSpot's Starter tier is priced per seat, and Professional and Enterprise include a number of core seats with extra seats charged on top. Seat pricing hurts agencies and teams where many people need occasional access.

Event- or profile-based

Product-led messaging platforms often bill on tracked profiles or events rather than marketing contacts. Customer.io is one example of this style; check its official pricing page for current units. These suit SaaS lifecycle messaging more than newsletter marketing.

Monthly vs annual billing

MailerLite and Brevo both show roughly a 10% saving for annual billing: MailerLite Growing Business drops from $12 to $10.80 a month ($129.60 a year), and Brevo Standard from $18 to $16.17 a month (checked 4 November 2026). HubSpot frames the difference as monthly versus annual commitment.

Annual billing makes sense once your list size and workflow needs have been stable for a quarter. Before that, the 10% saving is smaller than the cost of being locked into the wrong tier. Also check whether annual plans let you move up mid-term and whether moving down waits until renewal.

Limits, onboarding and add-ons

The figures most often missed in a budget:

  • Required onboarding: HubSpot lists a one-time Professional Onboarding fee of $3,000 and an Enterprise Onboarding fee of $7,000 (checked 4 November 2026). For many small teams this is the single largest first-year line item after the subscription.
  • Extra seats: HubSpot lists additional core seats from $45 a month on Professional and from $75 a month on Enterprise (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Contact tier jumps: contact-based plans step up in bands. Adding 50 contacts can move you into the next band for the whole month.
  • Usage overages: Zapier says paid plans switch to pay-as-you-go when you reach your task limit unless you turn that off; with it off, Zaps pause until the next usage period (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Premium support and services: phone support, dedicated managers and migration help are often tied to higher tiers or sold separately. Ask for this in writing.
  • Channel add-ons: SMS, WhatsApp, extra landing pages or dedicated IPs are usually priced separately from email.

CRM costs often sit alongside automation costs. If your automation tool and CRM are separate, our breakdown of CRM seat, onboarding and admin costs covers the other half of the bill.

Scenario B: three-person B2B marketing team

Assumptions: three users, 2,000 marketing contacts, lead scoring and multi-step nurture with handoff to sales in a shared CRM.

  • HubSpot Marketing Hub Professional: 3 core seats and 2,000 contacts are included. Year one is $800 x 12 + $3,000 onboarding = $12,600, or $13,680 at the $890 figure. Year two, without onboarding, is $9,600 to $10,680.
  • Adding a fourth user: add at least $45 a month, or $540 a year.
  • Lower-cost alternatives: ActiveCampaign and Brevo can be far cheaper at this list size, but scoring, CRM depth and reporting differ by tier. Price them on their own pages and compare features line by line; our ActiveCampaign versus HubSpot analysis covers where the extra cost does and does not buy capability.

Scenario C: small agency automation layer

Assumptions: five staff build and monitor client automations, about 2,000 tasks a month across lead routing and reporting notifications.

  • Zapier Team: listed from $69 a month for up to 25 users (checked 4 November 2026), so at least $828 a year. The task tier needed for 2,000 tasks is selected on the pricing page and may cost more; price it there.
  • Zapier Professional: from $19.99 a month, but limited to 1 user, which rarely suits an agency.
  • Make or n8n: often cheaper for high-step workflows, with n8n self-hosting trading licence cost for server and maintenance time. The workflow automation guide for agencies covers multi-client set-ups.

How tasks accumulate: a lead-routing Zap with four action steps run for 300 leads uses roughly 1,200 tasks if each successful action counts as a task. Confirm Zapier's current counting rules in its help documentation before sizing a tier.

Free plan vs trial

A free plan is a permanent tier with caps; a trial is temporary access to paid features. HubSpot lists a free tier with 2,000 email sends a month and 1 automated action, MailerLite lists a free tier with 250 subscribers, and Zapier lists 100 free tasks a month (all checked 4 November 2026). Trials are useful for testing a paid workflow, but do not launch production automations on one.

Renewal and next-tier triggers

Track these monthly so the next invoice is not a surprise:

  • Contacts within 10% of the next band.
  • A needed feature, such as lead scoring, A/B testing or custom reporting, that sits one tier up.
  • Seat requests from sales or client-services staff.
  • Task or operation usage trending above 80% of the allowance.
  • Renewal date, notice period and any expiry of introductory discounts.

Before renewing, prune unengaged contacts, archive dead workflows and check whether another tool in your stack now duplicates the feature you are paying for.

Questions to ask sales

  • Which contacts count toward billing: all, marketing only, or active only?
  • What happens if we exceed the contact or send limit mid-term?
  • Is onboarding mandatory, and can it be waived or credited?
  • Is the quoted price introductory, and what will the renewal price be?
  • Which support channels are included at this tier?
  • Can we move down a tier at renewal without losing data or workflows?
  • Are SMS, extra seats, sandboxes or API access billed separately?

The same questions apply to email-first platforms; our email marketing software shortlist and the guide to lifecycle email automation tools show how packaging differs there.

FAQs

Why can't I compare automation tools on price per contact alone?

Because some charge by sends, tasks or seats instead. Convert each to your actual workload first: contacts in six months, emails per month, workflow steps per lead and number of users.

Is HubSpot's onboarding fee optional?

HubSpot's pricing page describes Professional and Enterprise onboarding as required one-time fees (checked 4 November 2026). Ask sales directly whether any exceptions apply to your account.

Does annual billing always save money?

Only if your tier fits for the whole year. A roughly 10% saving is easily wiped out by being locked into a tier that is too large or too small.
