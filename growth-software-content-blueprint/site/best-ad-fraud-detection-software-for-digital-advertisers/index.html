Which ad fraud detection tool should you choose?

Search and lead-gen advertisers worried about bots and repeat clicks should shortlist a click-fraud tool such as CHEQ, Lunio, ClickGuard or Fraud Blocker. Brands buying programmatic display and video at scale need a verification vendor such as HUMAN, DoubleVerify or Integral Ad Science instead.

  • Search and social advertisers wanting click protection within a broader suite: CHEQ, including ClickCease.
  • Paid media teams focused on wasted spend across many networks: Lunio.
  • Google Ads accounts wanting granular rules and evidence: ClickGuard.
  • Small businesses wanting straightforward click-fraud blocking: Fraud Blocker.
  • Advertisers wanting another click-fraud option to compare: Spider AF.
  • App install and multi-channel performance campaigns: TrafficGuard.
  • Programmatic buyers and platforms facing sophisticated bots: HUMAN.
  • Brands needing verification, viewability and brand safety: DoubleVerify or Integral Ad Science.

What counts as invalid traffic, and what doesn't?

Invalid traffic is any click or impression that doesn't come from a real person with genuine interest, from simple crawlers to bots built to mimic people, click farms and publishers inflating their own numbers. An unusual click isn't automatically fraud: repeat visits from a real buyer, shared office networks and VPN users all look odd and can be legitimate.

The industry distinguishes general invalid traffic, such as known crawlers that list-based filters catch, from sophisticated invalid traffic that needs behavioural analysis to detect. The two call for different tools. Most search advertisers' risk sits in competitors' or bots' clicks on high-cost keywords and fake form fills. Programmatic buyers face hidden ads, spoofed domains and bot impressions at scale. Affiliate programmes face their own patterns, which our list of affiliate fraud prevention tools covers.

Treating every anomaly as fraud has a cost. Block a corporate network or an entire region and you may cut off real customers, then blame the campaign when leads fall.

What do the ad platforms already filter for you?

The major ad platforms filter some invalid activity automatically, and Google credits advertisers for clicks it identifies as invalid. Third-party tools exist because advertisers want visibility into what's caught, protection against patterns the platforms don't credit, and controls such as exclusions.

Google, for example, explains how it detects and credits invalid clicks in Google Ads Help, and lets advertisers exclude IP addresses at campaign level. Paid social platforms offer far fewer exclusion levers, so third-party tools there lean on audience exclusions and reporting rather than blocking. Check each platform's current invalid-traffic policy before assuming a tool can block on every channel.

What is each tool best for, and when should you skip it?

Pick the tool that acts on the channels where most of your spend sits, and insist on seeing the evidence behind each block. A tool that blocks without explanation is hard to tune and harder to defend.

CHEQ (including ClickCease): best for click protection inside a wider security suite

What it does: CHEQ markets ClickCease as part of its range, offering click-fraud protection for paid search and social alongside broader protection against fake traffic in forms and analytics. See CHEQ's site for current product names and tiers.

Strengths: covers fake leads and polluted analytics, not just paid clicks. Limitations: product naming and packaging have changed over time, so confirm exactly which tier covers your channels. Skip it if you only need basic Google Ads IP blocking.

Lunio: best for wasted spend across many networks

What it does: detects invalid traffic across search, social and other networks and feeds exclusions back to supported platforms, with reporting on wasted spend. See Lunio's site.

Strengths: a multi-network view suits paid media teams with a broad mix. Limitations: what it can block versus only report varies by network. Skip it if you spend almost entirely on one search engine.

ClickGuard: best for granular Google Ads rules

What it does: monitors paid clicks, applies customisable blocking rules and provides click-level data you can use when requesting refunds. See ClickGuard's site.

Strengths: fine control for specialists who want to set their own thresholds. Limitations: granular rules need attention, or they'll over-block. Skip it if nobody will review the rules regularly.

Fraud Blocker: best for simple protection on small accounts

What it does: click-fraud detection and blocking aimed at small and mid-sized advertisers, with fraud scoring and automated exclusions. See Fraud Blocker's site.

Strengths: a focused product for advertisers who don't need enterprise controls. Limitations: check its controls and integrations against enterprise tools if your needs grow. Skip it if you need programmatic coverage.

Spider AF: an alternative click-fraud tool to compare

What it does: ad fraud detection and click protection covering search and other campaign types. See Spider AF's site.

Strengths: another option to set against the larger click-fraud tools on evidence and controls. Limitations: confirm channel support, regional coverage and language for your markets. Skip it if it doesn't support the networks and regions where you spend.

TrafficGuard: best for app and multi-channel campaigns

What it does: invalid-traffic prevention for performance campaigns, including mobile app installs as well as search and social. See TrafficGuard's site.

Strengths: suits teams buying installs and clicks from several sources. Limitations: more than a search-only advertiser needs. Skip it if you don't run app campaigns.

HUMAN: best for sophisticated bots in programmatic

What it does: bot detection and fraud prevention for advertisers, platforms and publishers across programmatic channels. See HUMAN's site.

Strengths: focused on sophisticated invalid traffic at scale. Limitations: enterprise sales and integration. Skip it if you're a search-led small business.

DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science: best for verification and brand safety

What they do: measure and filter invalid traffic, viewability and brand-safety risk for display, video, social and CTV buys. See DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science.

Strengths: established verification vendors for large media buys. Limitations: they're not search click-fraud tools. Skip them if your spend is mostly paid search.

How often are "fraud" alerts really false positives?

No vendor-neutral benchmark exists that we could verify, so treat any tool's fraud rate as a claim to test against your own data. The safest assumption is that some flagged clicks are real people until evidence says otherwise.

What should you verify before blocking traffic?

Before blocking, confirm that flagged traffic both looks invalid and fails to convert, and check it isn't a known legitimate source. Then block narrowly, monitor results, and keep evidence for any refund claim.

  1. Check conversions first: compare flagged traffic's leads and sales against your CRM, not just clicks. Phone leads count too, so connect call tracking for paid campaigns.
  2. Rule out known sources: your own office, agency staff, customers' corporate networks, partners and QA tools.
  3. Look for patterns, not single events: many clicks from one source in a short window, impossible geographies or zero engagement time.
  4. Check the placement: for display and video, find which sites or apps the traffic came from, and exclude placements before blocking people.
  5. Block narrowly: a specific IP or placement, not a whole country or network range, unless the evidence is strong.
  6. Monitor after blocking: watch lead volume and quality for two or three weeks, and roll back if real leads fall.
  7. Keep evidence: export click-level data and timestamps to support refund requests to the ad platform.

What else do advertisers ask about ad fraud detection?

Advertisers most often ask about refunds, competitors and whether small accounts need a tool at all. Short answers follow.

Do small advertisers need ad fraud detection software?

Not always. If leads are tracked properly and conversion quality is stable, native filtering and occasional reviews may be enough; consider a tool when you see repeated suspicious clicks on expensive keywords or a rise in fake form fills.

Will Google refund fraudulent clicks?

Google credits clicks it identifies as invalid, and you can request an investigation for clicks you believe were missed. Evidence such as click-level logs from a detection tool helps when you ask.

Can I stop competitors from clicking my ads?

You can exclude specific IP addresses on Google and Microsoft campaigns, and detection tools automate this. Competitors on changing networks or mobile connections are harder to block reliably.

Does ad fraud software stop fake leads?

Some tools also protect forms by screening bot submissions before they reach your CRM. Check whether that's included or a separate product.
