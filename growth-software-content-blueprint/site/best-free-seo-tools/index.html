The free SEO tools worth building on are the ones that read a client's own data: Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, GA4, PageSpeed Insights and Google Business Profile. Which free SEO tools should a small agency set up first?

Start with Google Search Console, then add GA4, Google Business Profile for local clients, and Bing Webmaster Tools as a second index view. Those four are free from the search engines themselves, and every later step in your workflow depends on the first-party data they hold.

  • First-party search data: Google Search Console, with Bing Webmaster Tools alongside it.
  • Crawling small brochure sites: the free version of Screaming Frog SEO Spider.
  • Backlink and site-health data on sites you can verify: Ahrefs' free access for site owners.
  • A handful of competitor lookups for a pitch: a free Semrush account, which is capped.
  • Client dashboards at no cost: Looker Studio connected to Search Console and GA4.
  • Local presence: Google Business Profile itself.

Who is a free stack realistic for?

It suits freelancers and agencies of one to three people with a few retainers, where you already have access to every client site. Once you're pitching prospects you can't access or reporting on many accounts, the hours spent on workarounds start to cost more than a subscription.

What does each tool do, and where does it stop?

Each free tool does one job well and stops before the next one, which is why a free stack needs several of them. Each block gives the job, the limit that matters and when to skip it.

Google Search Console: best for every client from day one

It shows how Google crawls, indexes and displays a verified site, including the queries and pages that earn impressions and clicks, plus URL inspection, sitemaps and a field-data Core Web Vitals report. It's free, and it's Google's own data, which no third-party estimate replaces. Limitations: no competitor data, a limited history window and capped interface exports, so larger pulls need the API or a connector. Skip it if: never. Get Owner or Full access during onboarding.

Bing Webmaster Tools: best as a second index view

Bing's free counterpart to Search Console adds a site scan for on-page issues, and sites can be imported from Search Console. Limitations: its keyword data reflects Bing searchers, so don't present it as Google demand. Skip it if: the client gets negligible Bing traffic.

Google Analytics 4: best for tying organic visits to leads

The standard product is free, and most clients already have it connected to Looker Studio. Limitations: setup takes real effort, some reports apply data thresholds, and exploration retention depends on a setting many properties leave at its default. Skip it if: the client has moved to another platform, the situation our guide to GA4 alternatives and complementary analytics covers.

PageSpeed Insights: best for explaining slow templates

It pairs a Lighthouse lab test with real-user field data where Google has enough, and its recommendations name specific resources a developer can fix. Limitations: one URL per run in the interface, and lab scores move between runs. Skip it if: you need ongoing monitoring across many pages, a job for dedicated website performance monitoring.

Google Business Profile: best for any client with a location

It manages the listing behind Maps and the local pack: categories, hours, photos, posts, reviews and performance insights. Limitations: it covers your own listing only, with no rank grids, citation audits or competitor views. Skip it if: the client has no location or service area. Multi-location clients soon need the tools in our local SEO software guide.

Looker Studio: best for templated monthly reports

Google's free dashboarding tool connects natively to Search Console and GA4, and one template can be copied per client. Limitations: non-Google connectors are often paid third-party add-ons, and a broken source can go unnoticed until a client asks. Skip it if: clients expect white-label reports blending SEO, paid and social data.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider (free version): best for small-site audits

This desktop crawler finds broken links, redirect chains, duplicate titles and missing metadata. The free version is capped at a set number of URLs per crawl, and a paid annual licence removes the cap. Neither figure was re-confirmed for this draft, so check the SEO Spider product page. Skip it if: client sites regularly exceed the cap. Our technical SEO audit tools for large websites cover what comes next.

Ahrefs free access: best for backlink checks on sites you control

Ahrefs gives verified site owners limited free access to site-level audit and backlink data, without a desktop app. Limitations: it's built around sites you own, so it won't support prospect or competitor research. Skip it if: that research is your main need. Current tiers are on Ahrefs' pricing page.

Semrush free account: best for a few lookups during a pitch

It opens Semrush's research tools for competitor visibility that first-party tools can't give you, capped by daily requests and project count. Limitations: the caps run out fast in real research. Skip it if: you need monthly competitor tracking for several clients. Limits are on Semrush's plans page.

Google Keyword Planner: best for commercial keyword themes

It's free inside a Google Ads account and gives keyword ideas with volume and bid estimates. Limitations: built for advertisers, it groups close variants, can show broad ranges and has no organic difficulty score. Skip it if: you need precise organic estimates. Our keyword research tools comparison explains what paid databases add.

What does a zero-budget client workflow look like?

It runs in four passes: onboarding access, a baseline audit, keyword discovery from real queries, and a monthly templated report. Rankings come from Search Console average position, explained honestly, rather than from a daily rank checker.

  1. Onboarding, week one: request access to Search Console, GA4 and Business Profile, then verify the site in Bing Webmaster Tools and Ahrefs.
  2. Baseline audit: crawl with the Screaming Frog free version, or Ahrefs' cloud audit if the site exceeds the cap. Then run the five most important templates through PageSpeed Insights.
  3. Keyword discovery: export Search Console queries with high impressions and a low click-through rate, expand them in Keyword Planner, and spend a few Semrush free reports on competitor gaps.
  4. Monthly reporting: a Looker Studio template with Search Console clicks and impressions, GA4 organic key events and three sentences of commentary.

Which paid tasks have a no-cost substitute?

Rank tracking, content gap research, schema validation and brief writing all have workable free substitutes. Each one is slower or less precise than the paid version, so use them while client count is low.

  • Rank tracking: Search Console's query and page filters, compared month on month. It's an average across impressions, not a daily position check, but it reflects real searches.
  • Content gap research: the live results page, People Also Ask boxes and Google Trends, reviewed by hand.
  • Schema validation: Google's Rich Results Test.
  • Content briefs: a free AI assistant with careful editing. Our roundup of free AI writing tools and their limits shows where they fall short.

When does a paid plan become necessary?

Pay when a client needs daily rank tracking for a fixed keyword set, when you pitch prospects whose sites you can't access, or when building reports by hand eats billable hours. In our assessment, many small agencies reach that point at around four or five active retainers, though your service mix moves the line.

What mistakes do agencies make with free tools?

The common ones are using personal Google accounts for client properties, treating a trial as a free tool, and presenting estimates as exact traffic. Each is cheap to avoid and painful to unwind later.

  • Adding client properties to a personal Google account instead of a documented agency account.
  • Building a workflow on a trial without exporting before it ends.
  • Reporting Keyword Planner ranges or third-party estimates as precise figures.
  • Never asking whether a free tier permits client work and what you can export.

What else do agencies ask about free SEO tools?

Most questions are about whether free tiers allow client work and how they differ from trials. The short answers are below.

Can an agency run client SEO entirely on free tools?

Yes, for a small number of clients whose sites you can access. The gaps are competitor research, reliable daily rank tracking and polished white-label reporting.

Is Ahrefs' free access the same as a trial?

No. It is limited, ongoing access to site-level data for sites you verify, not a time-limited version of the paid plans. Check the current scope on Ahrefs' own pages.

Which free tool should a new agency set up first?

Google Search Console. Keyword discovery, reporting and technical checks all draw on the query and indexing data it provides.

What's the verdict?

The free stack is genuinely useful because it's built on first-party data, but it breaks on competitor research and scale. Use it to prove value on early retainers, then upgrade when workarounds cost more hours than a subscription costs money.
