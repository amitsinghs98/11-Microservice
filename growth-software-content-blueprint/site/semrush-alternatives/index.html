Semrush Alternatives: SEO Platforms for Different Budgets

The right Semrush alternatives depend on which constraint is pushing you out: website caps and add-on costs point to SE Ranking, a lighter workflow points to Mangools or Ubersuggest, and local, link or reporting work points to specialists. This guide sorts the options by switch reason, so you replace only what you actually need to.

Which alternative fits your reason for switching?

Match the alternative to the single constraint that is costing you money or time, not to a general wish for something cheaper. Most teams leave a broad suite because one limit started to bite, and the best replacement is the one that removes that limit.

  • Cost and capacity: SE Ranking.
  • A lighter tool for one or two sites: Ubersuggest or Mangools.
  • A narrower, familiar suite: Moz Pro.
  • Local SEO: BrightLocal.
  • Backlinks: Ahrefs, or Majestic for link data alone.
  • Agency reporting: AgencyAnalytics, or SE Ranking's Agency Pack.
  • Weekly use of research, ad data and audits: stay on Semrush and prune add-ons.

What does Semrush do well that you might lose?

You would lose breadth: keyword and competitor research, audits, daily rank tracking, backlinks, advertising research and AI search visibility in one login. If your team uses three or more of those weekly, a cheaper tool usually means rebuilding the gaps with extra subscriptions.

Semrush's pricing page says Semrush One Pro+ adds multi-location and device tracking plus SEO historical data back to 2012, neither of which Starter includes (checked 5 November 2026). The SEO Toolkit is also sold on its own, but its listed prices differed between Semrush pages.

Before cancelling, read our workflow-by-workflow Semrush and Ahrefs comparison, which shows where that breadth earns its cost and where it does not.

Why do teams switch away from Semrush?

The legitimate triggers come from the plan structure itself: tight website and keyword caps on the entry plan, and add-ons that grow with headcount and client count. A third trigger is simple underuse, where a team pays for toolkits it rarely opens.

  • Website and keyword caps: Semrush One Starter is $199 a month for 5 websites and 500 tracked keywords; a sixth client means Pro+ at $299 for 15 websites and 1,500 keywords. Annual billing brings those to $165.17 and $248.17 a month (both checked 5 November 2026).
  • Seat costs: Semrush's user-management page lists additional users at $45 a month on Starter and $80 on Pro+ (checked 5 November 2026).
  • Report costs: client reports are bought per report, at $10 a month for a Base Report or $20 for a Pro Report, and white-labeling needs Pro (checked 5 November 2026).
  • Specialist depth: local agencies may want more location and review tooling, and link builders a link-first index.

How did we choose these alternatives?

Each tool had to solve at least one switch reason better than Semrush and publish enough official product information to describe it responsibly. The order follows the switch reasons, not a ranking, and no vendor paid to appear or influenced placement.

We weighed job coverage, limits, seat and client billing, reporting and data portability. Vendor sites could not be loaded directly, so each price was matched against a search index of the vendor's own page on 5 November 2026; where official pages disagreed, we left the number out.

Which alternatives cut cost or add capacity?

SE Ranking is the closest like-for-like replacement when the problem is project and keyword caps. Ubersuggest is the cheaper option for a solo operator who does not need agency features.

SE Ranking: best for agencies outgrowing the five-website cap

Best for: small and mid-sized agencies tracking many keywords across many projects. SE Ranking lists Core at $129 a month with 10 projects, one manager seat and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords, and Growth at $279 with 30 projects, three seats and 5,000 keywords, or $103.20 and $223.20 a month billed annually (checked 5 November 2026). It offers a 14-day free trial.

Trade-off: the white-label Agency Pack costs $69 a month and is sold on annual billing only (checked 5 November 2026). Compare its keyword data on your own niches during the trial.

Skip it if: you depend on Semrush's advertising research. Our 5-client and 15-client SE Ranking and Semrush cost model runs the numbers.

Ubersuggest: best for founders on one or two sites

Best for: solo marketers who need keyword ideas, an audit and light tracking. Ubersuggest's pricing page lists three monthly tiers from $29 to $99, with 1, 7 and 15 projects, plus one-time lifetime plans (checked 5 November 2026).

Trade-off: far less competitive depth and multi-client tooling than Semrush.

Skip it if: you run an agency across many markets or need branded client reports.

Which alternatives offer a simpler workflow?

Mangools and Moz Pro cover core SEO with fewer modules than Semrush, which shortens onboarding. The cost is less depth outside keywords, links and tracking.

Mangools: best for freelancers who find big suites overwhelming

Best for: freelancers, bloggers and small businesses. Mangools bundles KWFinder with SERP analysis, rank tracking and backlink lookups, and its FAQ says annual billing saves up to 35% against monthly (checked 5 November 2026). Its pages showed inconsistent monthly prices, so confirm the current figures at checkout.

Trade-off: daily lookup and tracking allowances suit a small portfolio, not a 20-client agency.

Skip it if: you need advertising research or deep technical audits.

Moz Pro: best for in-house teams wanting a narrower suite

Best for: teams that want keyword research, link analysis, crawls and rank tracking without wider marketing toolkits, plus the Domain Authority metric many stakeholders recognise.

Trade-off: no Moz page could be retrieved for this review, so compare tracked keywords and seats on the official Moz Pro page.

Skip it if: you need content, advertising or market research in the same tool.

Which specialists beat Semrush on local, links or reporting?

BrightLocal, Ahrefs, Majestic and AgencyAnalytics each go deeper than Semrush on one job. None replaces the full suite, so most teams keep a research tool beside them.

BrightLocal: best for local agencies

Best for: agencies and brands where locations, listings and reviews are the work. BrightLocal lists Track at $41 a month for one location on monthly billing, covering up to 100 keywords and four competitors per location, with a 7x7 search grid for up to five keywords and a 14-day free trial (checked 5 November 2026). Our multi-location local SEO software guide compares it with other local platforms.

Trade-off: prices rise by location band, so a growing local roster changes the bill.

Skip it if: you need national keyword research or competitor gap analysis.

Ahrefs: best for link builders and digital PR teams

Best for: teams living in competitor link profiles. Ahrefs lists Lite at $129, Standard at $249 and Advanced at $449 a month (checked 5 November 2026).

Trade-off: its help center says tracked keywords update weekly by default on all plans, and on Lite extra users are billed by usage, at $20 or $40 a month (both checked 5 November 2026). It is a different emphasis, not usually a cheaper bill.

Skip it if: daily rank movement is what your clients pay to see.

Majestic: best as a dedicated link index

Best for: specialists who already have keyword and audit tools and want Trust Flow and Citation Flow metrics. Majestic says its Historic Index is available on Pro plans and above, with no contract (checked 5 November 2026).

Trade-off and skip it if: it does not do keywords, audits or tracking, so skip it if you want one tool. Listed prices varied by currency and page date. Our backlink analysis tool comparison explains how to judge link data.

AgencyAnalytics: best when reporting eats more hours than research

Best for: agencies building monthly reports from SEO, analytics, ads and social data. AgencyAnalytics prices by the number of clients you manage and offers a 14-day free trial without a card (checked 5 November 2026). Its per-client figures differed between official pages, so confirm the rate there.

Trade-off: it is a reporting layer, so most agencies keep a keyword and backlink tool underneath.

Skip it if: you report on a handful of clients and Semrush's per-report fee is cheaper. Compare it in our SEO reporting software roundup.

Will a cheaper tool really cover your work?

Only if your work matches the tool's strength, and the plan pages alone cannot tell you how a different keyword database behaves in your niche. Run any trial on real client domains and compare results before you commit.

What should you check before switching?

Check history import, report rebuild time and contract terms before you sign. The biggest hidden cost is lost ranking history, because a new tracker starts from the day you add keywords unless it can import the old data.

SE Ranking's help center describes exporting Semrush Position Tracking data in its extended format and importing it with position history, one search engine at a time (checked 5 November 2026).

  1. Does the new vendor import Semrush ranking history and tags, or does tracking restart from zero?
  2. Who rebuilds client report templates, and how long will it take?
  3. Which metrics will change? Warn clients that authority scores and traffic estimates differ between vendors.
  4. Are seats, client logins and white label included or sold as add-ons?
  5. When does your Semrush renewal fall, and what are the new vendor's cancellation terms?

What else do buyers ask about leaving Semrush?

Most questions concern the cheapest workable option, free tools and ranking history. Here are direct answers.

What is the cheapest real Semrush alternative for an agency?

Of the full suites reviewed, SE Ranking Core lists 10 projects and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords at $129 a month, against 5 websites and 500 keywords on Semrush One Starter at $199, both checked 5 November 2026. Data depth and reporting add-ons still need comparing on your own niches.

Is there a free alternative to Semrush?

Not a complete one. Google Search Console, Ahrefs' free tools for verified sites and Semrush's own free plan each cover part of the job, but none replaces a paid suite for multi-client work.

Will I lose my ranking history if I switch?

Not if you export it before cancelling and the new tool can import it. SE Ranking documents an import for Semrush Position Tracking exports; for any other vendor, ask which formats it accepts.

Should I switch or downgrade?

If the problem is one add-on or a seat count, review your Semrush configuration first. Switching makes more sense when a structural limit, such as the website cap, keeps forcing a higher tier.

What's the verdict?

Semrush remains a strong default for teams that use its breadth. Leave when one constraint dominates, pick the alternative that removes it, trial it on real client data, and move your history before the renewal date.
