Which SEO software should you choose?

Most teams should buy one all-in-one suite, keep Google Search Console connected, and add a specialist only when a single job outgrows the suite. Semrush suits generalist agencies, Ahrefs suits link-led research, SE Ranking suits agencies watching per-client cost, and Moz Pro suits in-house generalists.

Specialists earn a place for narrower reasons:

  • Deep technical crawls you configure yourself: Screaming Frog SEO Spider.
  • Very large daily keyword sets: AccuRanker.
  • Multi-location and local service businesses: BrightLocal.
  • First-party search data for any site you own, at no cost: Google Search Console.

Which SEO jobs does one platform need to cover?

Five jobs carry most of the weight: keyword research, rank tracking, technical audits, backlink analysis and client or stakeholder reporting. A suite that does all five adequately usually beats a stack of five specialists until one job grows large enough to need its own tool.

  • Keyword research: depth, intent labels and export caps (keyword research tools for agencies).
  • Rank tracking: quotas, update frequency and client workspaces (rank trackers for multi-client work).
  • Technical audits: crawl limits and JavaScript rendering (audit tools for large websites).
  • Backlinks: referring-domain quality and competitor gaps (backlink analysis tools).
  • Reporting: scheduled, branded reports agencies often buy separately (SEO reporting software).

What does each platform do well, and who should not buy it?

Semrush and Ahrefs are the broadest suites, while SE Ranking and Moz Pro trade some depth for easier economics or a gentler learning curve. The four specialists each do one job well, and each block names who should look elsewhere.

Semrush: best for generalist agencies

What it does: keyword, competitor, backlink, audit, tracking, local and advertising research in one login, which suits agencies selling several channels. SEO is now sold as the SEO Toolkit; Semrush One bundles it with AI visibility tracking.

Pricing: Semrush's knowledge base lists SEO Toolkit Pro at $139.95, Guru at $249.95 and Business at $499.95 a month on monthly billing (checked 5 November 2026). Position Tracking allows 500 keywords across 5 projects on Pro, 1,500 across 15 on Guru and 5,000 across 40 on Business (checked 5 November 2026). Semrush One starts at $199 a month (checked 5 November 2026), and the plans page lists a 7-day free trial (checked 5 November 2026). Extra users are billed on top.

Limitations: five projects run out fast, so most agencies land on Guru, and seats push the bill up again.

Skip it if: you'll never open the PPC or content modules, or you only need one job such as tracking. For agency economics, see our SE Ranking versus Semrush breakdown.

Ahrefs: best for link-led research

What it does: Site Explorer for backlink and competitor research, plus Keywords Explorer, Site Audit, Rank Tracker and Content Explorer. Link research is its center of gravity. The former Webmaster Tools now run as Ahrefs Free, with Site Audit and Site Explorer for sites you verify (checked 5 November 2026).

Pricing: the pricing page lists Lite at $129, Standard at $249, Advanced at $449 and Enterprise at $1,499 a month, plus a $29 Starter plan (checked 5 November 2026). Lite includes 750 Rank Tracker keywords and 5 unverified projects (checked 5 November 2026). Extra users cost more on each tier.

Limitations: Ahrefs' help center says tracked keywords update weekly by default on every plan (checked 5 November 2026), so daily movement is an extra cost, not a plan feature.

Skip it if: client reports depend on daily rankings, or several people need logins on a tight budget. Our Ahrefs alternatives by job map each function to a narrower tool.

SE Ranking: best for cost-conscious agencies

What it does: rank tracking, keyword and competitor research, backlinks, audits, local tools and a report builder.

Pricing: the old Essential, Pro and Business plans are closed to new buyers. The subscription page lists Core at $129 a month with 10 projects and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords, and Growth at $279 with 30 projects and 5,000 keywords, with Enterprise quoted (checked 5 November 2026). White label comes from the Agency Pack add-on, which SE Ranking's FAQ prices from $69 a month on annual billing only (checked 5 November 2026).

Skip it if: you run a link-building shop that needs the deepest link index, or need enterprise governance on a self-serve plan. Growth's allowances suit many small clients, but no single dataset is its strongest.

Moz Pro: best for in-house generalists

What it does: Keyword Explorer, Link Explorer, site crawls and rank tracking. Domain Authority is Moz's own metric, not a Google signal.

Pricing model: tiered plans with query, tracking and seat limits. We couldn't confirm current figures, so check the Moz Pro pricing page.

Skip it if: you run PPC alongside SEO, or you're an agency with dozens of clients that needs white-label portals.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider: best for hands-on technical audits

What it does: a desktop crawler for broken links, redirects, duplicate content, metadata, hreflang and structured-data errors.

Pricing: the free version crawls up to 500 URLs, and the paid license is £199 per user per year, unlocking JavaScript rendering, saved crawls and the Google Analytics and Search Console integrations (both checked 5 November 2026).

Skip it if: you want a prioritized to-do list rather than raw crawl data. Configuration is deep and cheap per seat, but there's no keyword or link data, and desktop crawls are awkward to share.

AccuRanker: best for large daily keyword sets

What it does: dedicated rank tracking with tagging, filtering, Search Console and Analytics connections and exports.

Pricing: the pricing page lists Professional at €224 a month and Expert at €764, with daily updates and a 10% annual discount (checked 5 November 2026). Confirm each tier's keyword allowance and how deep daily checks go, since neither was clear in what we checked.

Skip it if: you track a few hundred keywords; a suite's built-in tracker is cheaper, and you'd still need other tools for research, links and audits.

BrightLocal: best for multi-location and local work

What it does: local rank and grid tracking, citation building and auditing, review monitoring and Google Business Profile reporting.

Pricing: the pricing page lists Track at $41, Manage at $54 and Grow at $65 a month for one location, with a 14-day free trial (checked 5 November 2026); prices step up by location band.

Skip it if: you're an ecommerce or SaaS team without physical locations; it's thin for national research, links and large audits. Our local SEO software guide for multi-location businesses compares it with other local options.

Google Search Console: the free baseline

What it does: reports queries, clicks, impressions and indexing for verified properties. It's free from Google Search Console, and Google's documentation says the performance report goes back 16 months (checked 5 November 2026), so export data if you need longer history.

Skip it if: never, but it covers only sites you verify and offers no competitor research. Our free SEO tools roundup covers what else costs nothing.

What does SEO software really cost a team?

The real cost is the base plan plus extra seats, reporting or white-label add-ons, and any overage on keywords, projects or locations. For agencies, those extras often decide the comparison more than the headline price.

Take a hypothetical agency: three logins, 12 client sites, about 1,800 keywords tracked daily and branded monthly reports. Against the allowances quoted above, Semrush Guru's 15 projects fit but its 1,500 tracked keywords don't, SE Ranking Core's 10 projects fall short while Growth covers both, and Ahrefs Lite's 750 keywords would need extra blocks plus a daily-update cost. That's our reading of the stated limits, not a quote, so price each vendor on five lines:

  1. The lowest plan whose project cap covers 12 clients.
  2. Included seats, and the price of each extra one.
  3. Whether 1,800 daily keywords fit, and the cost of each extra block.
  4. Whether white-label reports are included or an annual-only add-on.
  5. The annual discount against the flexibility of monthly billing.

Which setup fits your kind of team?

Small teams need one suite plus Search Console, and growing agencies need to price seats and keyword quotas side by side. Local and ecommerce teams usually add one specialist for the job a suite handles thinly.

Solo consultant or two-person agency

SE Ranking Core or Ahrefs Lite plus Search Console is usually enough. Add a Screaming Frog license when an audit outgrows the suite's crawler.

Agency with 10 to 30 clients

Seat and project limits now drive cost. Price SE Ranking Growth against Semrush Guru for the full team, and consider AccuRanker once keyword counts pass the suite's allowance. At this size you also need a client pipeline, which our CRM options for marketing agencies cover.

In-house marketing team

Pick the suite your team will actually open weekly, such as Moz Pro or Semrush, and pair it with a reporting layer from our marketing analytics software guide so organic results sit beside other channels.

Local business or multi-location brand

Lead with BrightLocal, and keep Google Business Profile work in the same workflow.

Ecommerce store

Faceted catalogs need a crawler that copes with scale, so budget for Screaming Frog beside Semrush or Ahrefs. Copy teams may also want a content optimization tool for product and category pages.

Specialist workflows

Link builders should start from Ahrefs. Teams tracking brand mentions in AI answers should read our AI search visibility tools comparison before paying for a bundle such as Semrush One.

What mistakes do SEO software buyers make?

The most expensive mistake is paying for two all-in-one suites that duplicate each other's core jobs. The second is comparing headline plans instead of the full team bill.

Map jobs to tools first with our guide to building an SEO stack without duplicate features, then ask each vendor:

  • Do verified and unverified client sites count differently against the project cap?
  • Are tracked keywords updated daily or weekly, and do mobile or extra locations count separately?
  • What does each extra user cost, and is there a cap?
  • Is white-label reporting included, and is it tied to annual billing?

What else do buyers ask?

These questions come up once the shortlist exists. Each answer settles the point briefly.

Do I need an all-in-one suite or separate SEO tools?

Most small teams get more value from one suite plus free Google tools. Separate tools make sense once a single job, such as tracking thousands of keywords daily, outgrows what the suite includes.

Is Semrush or Ahrefs better for an agency?

It depends on the workflow you sell. Semrush covers more channels, including paid search and local, while Ahrefs centers on link and competitor content research and tracks rankings weekly by default.

Are third-party traffic and authority scores accurate?

They're estimates built from each vendor's own data and models. Use them to compare sites inside one tool, and use Search Console for your own sites' actual performance.

How often should we review our SEO subscriptions?

Check plan limits and prices every quarter and before every annual renewal. Vendors rename tiers and move allowances often enough that last year's best fit can drift: SE Ranking and Semrush have both restructured their plan lineups.

What's the verdict?

Based on the product information reviewed, Semrush is the broadest single suite, Ahrefs is the natural home for link-led research, and SE Ranking is the one to price first if per-client cost matters most.
