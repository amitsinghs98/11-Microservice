Which referral program software should a B2B SaaS company choose?

Match the tool to where conversion happens: in-product tools suit self-serve signups, CRM-synced tools suit deals closed by sales, and lightweight link tools suit early programmes. If most of your referrers are agencies and consultants rather than customers, you need partner or affiliate software instead.

  • Product-led SaaS with referrals inside the app: Cello.
  • Sales-led SaaS rewarding on closed-won deals: Referral Rock.
  • Lean teams wanting a self-serve programme fast: GrowSurf.
  • Pre-launch waitlists and milestone rewards: ReferralHero or Viral Loops.
  • Customer referrals plus a wider partner programme: PartnerStack.
  • SaaS teams mixing referrals and affiliates: FirstPromoter.

How is a customer referral program different from an affiliate program?

A customer referral program rewards existing users for recommending you to peers, usually with credits, upgrades or modest cash. An affiliate program pays publishers, creators and consultants a commission for driving sales, often recurring.

The software overlaps, but the priorities differ. Referral tools optimise for in-product prompts, double-sided rewards and frictionless sharing; affiliate tools optimise for partner onboarding, commission tiers, tax forms and payouts. What does each referral tool do well, and where does it fall short?

In-product and CRM-synced tools give the cleanest attribution for B2B, while lighter tools trade attribution depth for speed. Pick the tool whose reward trigger matches the moment a customer becomes valuable to you.

Cello: best for in-product referrals

Cello's setup guide describes an embeddable referral component that sits inside your app. Cello also writes about billing and CRM integrations; confirm which ones it supports for your stack so rewards can follow paid conversions.

Strengths: referral prompts where users already work. Limitations: it needs developer time to embed. Skip it if most deals close through a sales team rather than in the product.

Referral Rock: best for sales-led programmes

Referral Rock lists CRM integrations, and its B2B SaaS page pitches the product at sales-led programmes; ask for a demo of a reward tied to a deal stage in your CRM. Strengths: account-level attribution that sales teams trust. Limitations: less focus on in-app sharing. Skip it if your product is fully self-serve.

GrowSurf: best for lean self-serve programmes

GrowSurf's referral pricing page bases plans on programme participants rather than your whole user base. Strengths: quick setup and a pricing unit that's easy to forecast. Limitations: CRM deal-stage rewards need more configuration than CRM-native tools. Skip it if rewards must wait for a sales-closed contract.

ReferralHero: best for waitlists and milestone rewards

ReferralHero positions itself around pre-launch waitlists and referral campaigns with configurable reward triggers. Check which triggers and anti-fraud options come with your plan. Skip it if you need deep CRM-driven B2B attribution.

Viral Loops: best for template-based campaigns

Viral Loops offers ready-made referral and waitlist templates that marketers can launch without much engineering. Limitations: templates are built around individuals rather than multi-user accounts. Skip it if one referral can involve several seats and a sales cycle. See viral-loops.com.

PartnerStack: best when referrals sit inside a partner programme

PartnerStack offers a referral widget you can embed in your product alongside its affiliate and reseller tooling. Strengths: one system for customers, affiliates and resellers. Limitations: it's priced and scoped as a partner platform. Skip it if you only want a customer referral programme.

FirstPromoter: best for SaaS mixing referrals and affiliates

FirstPromoter tracks referrals and affiliates for SaaS, with commissions tied to billing data. Limitations: it's designed affiliate-first, so customer referral prompts are simpler. Skip it if in-product sharing is your main lever. See firstpromoter.com.

How should you qualify a referral before paying a reward?

Pay only when the referred account meets a rule you'd be happy to defend: a paid plan, a minimum contract value, or a set number of days without a refund. Write the rule down before launch and put it in the programme terms.

  • New account only: the company must not already be a customer, trial or open opportunity in your CRM.
  • Different organisation: block referrals where referrer and referee share an email domain, unless you deliberately reward internal expansion.
  • Payment cleared: wait until the first invoice is paid and the refund window has passed.
  • One reward per account: decide who wins when two users refer the same company.
  • Manual review over a threshold: large rewards get a human check.

How much can you afford to pay per referral?

Work backwards from what a new customer is worth and what you already pay to acquire one through other channels. A referral reward should cost less than your blended acquisition cost, while still being worth a customer's effort.

Hypothetical example, calculated from stated assumptions rather than any vendor's data: suppose a new account pays 100 a month in your currency, gross margin is 80% and you'd accept a payback of six months on acquisition. That account earns 80 a month in margin, so six months gives an acquisition budget of 480. If your referral software and admin come to an assumed 20 per referred account, you could fund a double-sided reward of up to about 460, for example a credit to the referrer and a discount to the new account.

Credits and upgrades usually cost you less than their face value, because they're delivered in your own product. Cash or gift cards cost face value but may motivate customers who don't need more of your product. Track whether referred accounts retain as well as other customers before you raise rewards.

Do B2B customers actually refer other businesses for rewards?

Some do, but in B2B the reward is rarely the main motive: customers refer because the product makes them look good to peers, and the reward removes friction. Based on the product information reviewed, programmes that ask at a moment of success tend to be designed more carefully than programmes that rely on a large incentive.

Which referral metrics should you report?

Report referred accounts, qualified accounts, revenue from referred accounts, cost per qualified referral and the share of active customers who referred someone. Those five numbers show whether the program pays back.

Add retention of referred accounts compared with other channels after a few quarters. Make sure your attribution rules agree with the rest of your partner reporting, which our guide to building a B2B partner and affiliate program covers.

What else do SaaS teams ask about referral software?

Most questions are about rewards, attribution and running referrals alongside affiliates. Short answers follow.

Should SaaS referral rewards be cash or account credit?

Credit and upgrades usually cost less than their face value and keep customers engaged. Cash can motivate referrers who don't need more of your product, but it costs face value.

Can one tool run both customer referrals and affiliates?

Several can, including PartnerStack and FirstPromoter. Keep separate reward rules and reporting, because the two groups behave differently.

How do referral tools attribute a referral to an account?

Most use a referral link or code, then match the signup or CRM record. B2B teams should check that attribution survives a long sales cycle and several contacts at one company.

Do I need a developer to launch a SaaS referral program?

For in-product prompts and billing-based rewards, usually yes. Hosted pages and CRM integrations can reduce the work for a first version.
