Which sales forecasting software should a growing B2B team choose?

If your CRM is clean and your team is under a few dozen reps, start with the forecasting built into Salesforce or HubSpot's paid tiers, then add a dedicated layer only when managers spend more time arguing about the number than inspecting deals. Clari, Gong Forecast and Aviso suit teams that need AI risk signals and rollups across several managers; Forecastio is the lighter option for HubSpot shops.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • Salesforce orgs with disciplined stages: Salesforce Collaborative Forecasts.
  • HubSpot teams on Professional or Enterprise: HubSpot's forecasting tool, then Forecastio if you outgrow it.
  • Multi-layer rollups and a weekly forecast call: Clari Forecast.
  • Teams already recording calls in Gong: Gong Forecast.
  • Teams that want AI forecasting bundled with a wider revenue platform: Aviso.
  • Outreach customers: Outreach's forecasting features, before buying a separate tool.

How is sales forecasting software different from a CRM dashboard?

A CRM dashboard shows what's in the pipeline; forecasting software asks each layer of management to commit to a number, records how that number changes, and flags deals whose activity doesn't support the stage they're in. The output is a call you can defend, not a chart of weighted pipeline.

The features that separate the two are forecast categories (commit, best case, pipeline), rollups where a manager can adjust a rep's call without editing the rep's deals, history that shows slippage week over week, and risk signals from activity data. A dashboard can approximate some of this with custom reports, but it rarely keeps an audit trail of who changed the call and why.

Forecasting also depends on what feeds it. If stages, close dates and amounts are unreliable, start with CRM data quality and deduplication tools before spending on a forecast layer. If you're still choosing the CRM itself, our guide to the best CRM for B2B SaaS sales teams covers that decision.

What does each forecasting tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The order follows our criteria for a growing B2B team: start with what your CRM already includes, then dedicated tools ranked by how much forecasting workflow they add for the contract you take on. Payment doesn't influence inclusion or order.

Salesforce Collaborative Forecasts: Salesforce orgs with disciplined stages

Salesforce maps opportunity stages to standard forecast categories (Pipeline, Best Case, Commit, Omitted and Closed), and admins can add a Most Likely category and rename categories. You can run several forecast types side by side, for example by product line or opportunity splits.

Pricing model: included in eligible Sales Cloud editions; check edition availability for splits and custom-field forecasts.

Strengths: No new vendor, native rollups and manager adjustments, and the data never leaves the CRM.

Limitations: It forecasts what reps enter. Activity-based risk signals and slippage analysis are thinner than in dedicated tools unless you add Salesforce's own revenue intelligence products.

Skip it if: your stage definitions are loose or reps rarely update close dates; fix the process first.

HubSpot forecasting: HubSpot teams on paid Sales Hub tiers

HubSpot's knowledge base lists the forecast tool for Sales Hub Professional and Enterprise, and its product and services catalog doesn't list forecasting for Starter. HubSpot's own pages describe the difference between Professional and Enterprise forecasting inconsistently, so confirm what your tier includes.

Pricing model: per-seat Sales Hub tiers; forecasting arrives at Professional.

Strengths: Forecast categories and team rollups inside the CRM your reps already use.

Limitations: Depth depends on tier, and teams wanting AI deal-risk explanations usually add a layer on top.

Skip it if: you're on Starter and don't plan to upgrade; a dedicated tool may cost less than the tier jump.

Clari Forecast: multi-layer rollups and the weekly forecast call

Clari Forecast is built around submitting, rolling up and inspecting the forecast across managers, with AI projections alongside the human call. Clari has merged with Salesloft, and the forecasting product keeps the Clari name inside the combined company.

Pricing model: quote-based; request a proposal and ask how packaging has changed since the merger.

Strengths: Purpose-built for the forecast call, with history that shows how each layer's number moved.

Limitations: Contract size and implementation effort are heavy for a team with a single sales manager.

Skip it if: you have one manager and a handful of reps; native CRM forecasting covers you.

Gong Forecast: teams already recording calls in Gong

Gong Forecast adds forecast boards and submissions on top of Gong's deal data. Gong's help center explains that its AI deal predictor draws on signals from calls, emails and CRM history, and frames its deal score as a relative ranking rather than a win probability.

Pricing model: add-on licensed with the Gong platform; quote-based.

Strengths: Risk signals come from real buyer conversations, not just CRM fields.

Limitations: Most of the value depends on Gong already capturing your calls and email, so it's a poor standalone buy.

Skip it if: you don't use Gong for conversation capture; compare revenue intelligence platforms first.

Forecastio: forecasting for HubSpot-centered teams

Forecastio publishes its plans with a set number of seats included and extra seats priced separately, and offers a pilot period. It's built around forecasting from HubSpot data.

Pricing model: published monthly plans, billed annually, with included seats.

Strengths: Transparent pricing and a narrow focus that suits a HubSpot team without a RevOps hire.

Limitations: Its strength is HubSpot data; Salesforce shops have better-integrated options.

Skip it if: your forecast needs conversation or email activity signals.

Aviso: AI forecasting inside a broader revenue platform

Aviso combines forecasting with deal intelligence, conversation intelligence and AI agents. Its own pages give different pricing signals, from per-seat figures to custom bundles, so treat any number you see as a starting point for negotiation.

Pricing model: mix of per-seat and custom pricing, with some usage-based AI charges described; confirm in a quote.

Strengths: Broad scope in one contract, which can replace two or three point tools.

Limitations: Inconsistent public pricing, and broad platforms take longer to adopt than a single-purpose tool.

Skip it if: you only need a forecast rollup and already have conversation capture elsewhere.

BoostUp, now presented as Terret: forecasting plus deal inspection

BoostUp combines forecast rollups, projections from historical conversion rates and deal inspection. Its site now presents the product under the Terret name, so confirm the company and contract entity before you buy.

Pricing model: quote-based; no public price list.

Strengths: A forecasting-first product, so the evaluation stays focused on the rollup and deal inspection.

Limitations: A rebrand mid-evaluation means checking roadmap and support continuity.

Skip it if: you want a long-established, widely staffed vendor ecosystem.

Outreach: forecasting for existing Outreach customers

Outreach has added forecasting and deal management features to its sales engagement platform, including forecast inspection and deal-health signals drawn from buyer engagement.

Pricing model: part of an Outreach contract; pricing depends on the products bundled.

Strengths: If reps already live in Outreach, the engagement data feeding the forecast is already there.

Limitations: Little reason to adopt it for forecasting alone.

Skip it if: you don't use Outreach; see our sales engagement platforms roundup for that decision.

Do AI forecasts beat a disciplined manager's call?

Vendors publish accuracy figures from their own customer bases, which aren't comparable across tools or transferable to your team. The defensible test is to run the tool's projection alongside your managers' calls for a full quarter and compare both against the result.

What weekly forecast hygiene cadence should you run?

A forecast is only as good as last week's data, so set a fixed weekly rhythm: reps update deals by a set day, managers inspect and adjust the next day, and leadership reviews the rolled-up number after that. Software makes the cadence visible; it doesn't replace it.

  1. Rep update: every open deal in the current period has a current stage, close date, amount and next step.
  2. Exception review: the manager reviews deals that slipped a close date, skipped a stage, or show no buyer activity for two weeks.
  3. Manager call: commit and best case are submitted with a one-line reason for every change from last week.
  4. Leadership rollup: compare the call with the AI or weighted projection and discuss the gap, not the total.
  5. Monthly retro: review which categories were most often wrong and tighten stage exit criteria.

The same cadence feeds compensation and capacity planning, so agree definitions with finance. Our guide to sales compensation management software covers that hand-off, and RevOps analytics tools cover reporting beyond the forecast.

What mistakes do teams make when buying forecasting software?

The commonest mistake is buying a forecasting layer to fix a data problem. The second is letting the AI projection replace the conversation instead of informing it.

  • Undefined stages: if stage exit criteria aren't written down, categories mean different things to each rep.
  • Too many categories: keep commit, best case and pipeline until the team uses them consistently.
  • No history: make sure the tool snapshots the forecast weekly so you can measure slippage.
  • Ignoring adoption: a tool managers don't open on forecast day is shelfware.
  • Buying overlapping tools: check whether your conversation intelligence or engagement platform already includes forecasting.

What else do buyers ask about sales forecasting software?

Buyers mostly ask when forecasting software becomes worth it and what data it needs. Short answers follow.

Can a spreadsheet replace sales forecasting software?

For a small team with one manager, often yes, if the spreadsheet is snapshotted weekly. It breaks down when several managers adjust calls and you need history of who changed what.

How many reps do you need before forecasting software pays off?

There's no fixed threshold. The trigger is when managers spend more time reconciling the number than inspecting deals, which usually arrives with a second layer of management.

What data does forecasting software need?

At minimum, clean stages, close dates and amounts on every open deal. Activity data from email, calendar and calls improves risk signals but can't fix missing basics.

What are forecast categories?

Labels such as commit, best case and pipeline that describe how confident a rep is in a deal closing this period. They're separate from stages and should have written definitions.

Should AI projections replace the manager's forecast?

No. Treat the projection as a second opinion and discuss the gap between it and the manager's call each week.

What's the verdict?

Use your CRM's native forecasting until your weekly cadence is stable, because a dedicated tool amplifies good process and exposes bad data. When rollups across several managers and evidence-based deal risk become the bottleneck, shortlist Clari, Gong Forecast or Aviso, and Forecastio if you're on HubSpot.
