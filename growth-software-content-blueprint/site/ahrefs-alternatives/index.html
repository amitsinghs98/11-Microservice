Ahrefs Alternatives: Tools for Backlinks, Keywords, and Content

Most teams searching for Ahrefs alternatives only need to replace one Ahrefs job, such as weekly rank tracking or keyword lookups, rather than the whole suite. Which Ahrefs alternative fits the job you need?

Start by naming the Ahrefs job you want to replace, because no single alternative is better at all of them. Link work, keyword lookups, rank tracking and content research each have a different best replacement.

  • Backlink research: Majestic for a link-only index, Moz Pro for links inside a lighter suite.
  • Budget keyword research: Mangools or Keywords Everywhere, with Search Console for your own sites.
  • An all-in-one agency suite: Semrush or SE Ranking, both of which list daily rank tracking.
  • Rank tracking only: AccuRanker, if weekly updates are too slow.
  • Content briefs and grading: Clearscope, or one of the content optimizers covered in our Surfer SEO alternatives guide.

What does Ahrefs still do well?

Ahrefs remains a strong link and competitive research tool, built on its own crawler and link index. If links and competitor pages are daily, billable work, it may still be your right core tool, with the alternatives below acting as complements.

Its plans are generous for owned sites: Ahrefs lists unlimited verified projects on every plan, with 5, 20 and 50 unverified projects on Lite, Standard and Advanced (checked 4 November 2026). It also offers a $29 Starter plan and a free tier for verified site owners. Our Semrush vs Ahrefs workflow comparison sets out where each suite is stronger.

Why do teams look beyond Ahrefs?

The documented triggers are weekly default rank tracking, per-user pricing that rises with the plan, and short data history on the entry tier. Each comes from Ahrefs' own published terms, not from review-site complaints.

  • Rank tracking frequency: Ahrefs' help center says tracked keywords update weekly by default on every plan, with daily updates sold through a Rank Tracker Pro add-on (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Per-user pricing: extra users are listed at $40 a month on Lite (up to 2), $60 on Standard (up to 5) and $80 on Advanced (up to 10) (checked 4 November 2026).
  • History limits: Lite keeps six months of history, Standard two years and Advanced five.
  • No promotions: the pricing page says Ahrefs never runs discounts and does not issue refunds in general, although annual billing gives 12 months for the price of 10.

Which tools replace Ahrefs for backlink research?

Majestic is the closest link-first replacement, and Moz Pro suits teams that want links inside a lighter suite. Both need cross-checking against domains you know, because every index sees a different slice of the web.

Majestic: best for link specialists wanting a second index

Best for: link builders who cross-check Ahrefs, or who want a cheaper link tool beside a lighter suite. Majestic runs a Fresh Index for everyday work and says its Historic Index is available on Pro and above (checked 4 November 2026). Its Trust Flow and Citation Flow metrics differ from domain-rating style scores.

Skip it if: you also need keywords, audits or tracking from the same tool. Plans are on the Majestic pricing page, which quotes local currencies.

Migration note: export saved prospect lists and outreach tags from Ahrefs first.

Moz Pro: best for in-house teams wanting links plus a lighter suite

Best for: teams that want Link Explorer, Keyword Explorer, crawls and rank tracking under one roof, with Domain Authority as a metric stakeholders already know.

Skip it if: competitive content research is central. We could not retrieve Moz's pricing during this review, so compare tracked keywords and seats on the Moz Pro product page.

Migration note: Domain Authority and Domain Rating will differ for the same site, so reset client baselines. Our guide to backlink analysis tools covers how to judge link data quality.

Which tools replace Keywords Explorer on a budget?

Mangools suits small-site owners who want a simple keyword tool with tracking included, and Keywords Everywhere suits writers who want volume data inside their browser. Neither is a full Ahrefs replacement for an agency.

Mangools: best for freelancers and small sites

Best for: people who mostly used Ahrefs for keyword discovery. Mangools centres on KWFinder, with SERP analysis, rank tracking and backlink lookups in the same subscription, and says annual plans save 35% (checked 4 November 2026). Its pages showed inconsistent monthly figures, so confirm prices at checkout.

Skip it if: you run a busy agency; daily lookup allowances suit small portfolios.

Keywords Everywhere: best for writers researching in the browser

Best for: content writers and solo marketers who want metrics beside search results. Its plans are annual only, starting with Bronze at $7 a month ($84 a year) for 100,000 credits (checked 4 November 2026).

Skip it if: you need projects, audits or tracking. It is a research aid, so pair it with Search Console. For more choices, see the keyword research tool shortlist for agencies.

Which all-in-one suites suit agencies leaving Ahrefs?

Semrush suits agencies that want breadth, daily tracking and white-label report add-ons. SE Ranking suits agencies that want the most projects, keywords and seats for the money.

Semrush: best for breadth and daily tracking

Best for: agencies that want research, daily tracking, reports and AI visibility in one account. Semrush lists SEO at $139, Starter at $199 and Pro+ at $299 a month, with 5, 5 and 15 websites and 500, 500 and 1,500 keywords tracked daily (checked 4 November 2026).

Skip it if: you manage many small client sites on a tight budget; the website caps bite quickly and extra users start at $45 a month. Our Semrush alternatives guide covers the reverse move.

SE Ranking: best for capacity per dollar

Best for: small agencies. SE Ranking lists Core at $129 a month with 10 projects, one manager seat and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords, and Growth at $279 with 30 projects, three seats and 5,000 keywords, with extra seats from $16 a month (checked 4 November 2026).

Skip it if: you need white-label reports on monthly billing; the Agency Pack is annual only. Compare backlink results against Ahrefs on a few of your own domains during the 14-day trial.

What if you only need rank tracking or content help?

Keep Ahrefs for research and add a specialist: AccuRanker for faster rankings, or Clearscope for briefs and draft grading. That adds a subscription rather than replacing one, so it only makes sense if the gap is costing you client time.

AccuRanker: best for agencies needing daily and on-demand rankings

Best for: teams that find a weekly refresh too slow. AccuRanker's help center says tracked keywords refresh automatically every 24 hours and can also be refreshed on demand, and its pricing page lists Professional at €224 a month (both checked 4 November 2026).

Skip it if: monthly client reporting on stable sites is your rhythm. Our rank tracker comparison for multi-client SEO lists other options.

Clearscope: best for content teams grading drafts

Best for: teams that used Ahrefs for topic research and now need briefs and grades in the writing workflow. Clearscope lists Essentials at $129 a month with unlimited users and 20 monthly drafts (checked 4 November 2026).

Skip it if: you need link or rank data. See our content optimization tools guide for alternatives.

How do seat costs compare for a four-person team?

For four people, SE Ranking Growth comes in well below both Ahrefs Standard and Semrush Pro+, because three seats are included. Ahrefs and Semrush land within a few dollars of each other on these assumptions.

  • Ahrefs Standard: $249 plus three extra users at $60 is $429 a month.
  • Semrush Pro+: $299 plus three users at the $45 starting price is about $434 a month. Confirm the Pro+ user price with Semrush.
  • SE Ranking Growth: $279 with three seats, plus one seat from $16, is about $295 a month.

These are monthly list prices checked 4 November 2026. They ignore data and feature differences and are not quotes. Our guide to building an SEO stack without duplicate features helps decide who really needs a full seat.

Is another link index as good as Ahrefs' data?

Not identical, and the vendor pages cannot settle that question: each crawler finds and keeps a different set of links. The only fair comparison is on domains whose link profiles you already know well.

What should a transition checklist include?

Export everything Ahrefs holds that you cannot regenerate, then run the new tool alongside for one reporting cycle. Ranking history and link prospect lists are the two things teams most often lose.

  1. List which Ahrefs reports each team member used in the last 90 days.
  2. Export tracked keywords, ranking history, keyword lists and link prospect lists.
  3. Download recent site audit results as a baseline for the new crawler.
  4. Record current client metrics and explain that new metrics will differ.
  5. Rebuild client report templates before the first client-facing report.
  6. Cancel before renewal; the pricing page says refunds are not generally issued.

Link builders moving prospect lists into outreach should keep consent rules in view; our compliant B2B outreach stack guide covers that side.

What else do buyers ask about replacing Ahrefs?

Buyers mostly ask about link data, cheaper Ahrefs tiers and whether one tool can do everything. Short answers follow.

Which tool has backlink data closest to Ahrefs?

Semrush and Majestic both maintain their own link indexes, and Moz runs Link Explorer. Every index finds a different subset of the web, so cross-check domains you know before deciding.

Is there a cheaper option without leaving Ahrefs?

Yes. Ahrefs lists a $29 Starter plan and a free tier for verified site owners, checked 4 November 2026. Both are much narrower than Lite.

Can one tool replace every Ahrefs job?

Semrush and SE Ranking come closest, with different strengths. Many teams end up with one suite plus one specialist.

Does weekly rank tracking matter?

It matters for agencies that react to daily volatility or promise frequent updates. For monthly reporting on stable sites, weekly data is often enough.

What's the verdict?

Replace Ahrefs only for the jobs where its terms work against you, such as tracking frequency, seat costs or history limits, and keep it where it still earns its place. For many teams the answer is Ahrefs plus one specialist rather than a full switch.
