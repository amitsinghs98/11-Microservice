Which Ahrefs alternative fits the job you need?

Start by naming the Ahrefs job you want to replace, because no single alternative is better at all of them. Link work, keyword lookups, rank tracking and content research each have a different best replacement.

  • Backlink research: Majestic for a link-only index, Moz Pro for links inside a lighter suite.
  • Budget keyword research: Mangools or Keywords Everywhere, with Search Console for your own sites.
  • An all-in-one agency suite: Semrush or SE Ranking, both of which list daily rank tracking.
  • Rank tracking only: AccuRanker, if weekly updates are too slow.
  • Content briefs and grading: Clearscope, or one of the content optimizers covered in our Surfer SEO alternatives guide.

What does Ahrefs still do well?

Ahrefs remains a strong link and competitive research tool, built on its own crawler and link index. If link work is daily, billable work, it may stay your core tool, with the alternatives below as complements.

Its limits are generous for owned sites: the help center lists unlimited verified projects on any plan, 5, 20 and 50 unverified projects on Lite, Standard and Advanced, and 750, 2,000 and 5,000 Rank Tracker keywords (checked 5 November 2026). Site owners can also use Ahrefs Free on verified websites, and the $29-a-month Starter plan adds Keywords Explorer and Rank Tracker, renewable monthly only (both checked 5 November 2026). Our Semrush vs Ahrefs workflow comparison sets out where each suite is stronger.

Why do teams look beyond Ahrefs?

The documented triggers are weekly default rank tracking, per-user costs, user caps and short data history on the entry tier. Each comes from Ahrefs' own published terms.

  • Rank tracking frequency: the help center says tracked keywords update weekly by default on all plans (checked 5 November 2026). Daily updates cost extra.
  • Users: Ahrefs caps additional users at 2, 5 and 10 on Lite, Standard and Advanced, and on credit-based Lite it bills extra users by usage: $20 a month for a casual user, $40 for a power user (checked 5 November 2026). Higher-tier seat prices conflicted across Ahrefs pages; confirm them on the Ahrefs pricing page.
  • History limits: the same plan comparison lists 6 months of history on Lite, 24 on Standard and 60 on Advanced.
  • No promotions: Ahrefs says it runs no one-off or seasonal discounts and offers no promo codes, describes yearly billing as "2 months free", and its terms say refunds are not issued in general (all checked 5 November 2026).

Which tools replace Ahrefs for backlink research?

Majestic is the closest link-first replacement, and Moz Pro suits teams that want links inside a lighter suite. Both need cross-checking against domains you know, because every index sees a different slice of the web.

Majestic: best for link specialists wanting a second index

Best for: link builders who cross-check Ahrefs, or who want a cheaper link tool beside a lighter suite. Majestic runs a Fresh Index for everyday work and says its Historic Index is available on Pro plans and above (checked 5 November 2026).

Skip it if: you also need keywords, audits or tracking from the same tool. Its pages showed prices in mixed currencies and ages, so check the Majestic plans page directly.

Moz Pro: best for in-house teams wanting links plus a lighter suite

Best for: teams that want Link Explorer, Keyword Explorer, crawls and rank tracking under one roof.

Skip it if: competitive content research is central. We could not confirm Moz's current plans, so compare tracked keywords and seats on the Moz Pro product page.

Which tools replace Keywords Explorer on a budget?

Mangools suits small-site owners who want a simple keyword tool with tracking included, and Keywords Everywhere suits writers who want volume data inside their browser. Neither is a full Ahrefs replacement for an agency.

Mangools: best for freelancers and small sites

Best for: people who mostly used Ahrefs for keyword discovery. Mangools centres on KWFinder, with SERP analysis, rank tracking and backlink lookups in the same subscription, and its FAQ says annual plans save up to 35% and sub-users are available on Premium and higher (both checked 5 November 2026). Monthly figures differed across its pages, so confirm at checkout.

Skip it if: you run a busy agency; sub-users share the parent account's lookup limits, which suits small portfolios.

Keywords Everywhere: best for writers researching in the browser

Best for: content writers and solo marketers who want metrics beside search results. Its plans are annual only: Bronze at $84 a year for 100,000 credits and one seat, Silver at $168 for 400,000 credits and three seats (checked 5 November 2026).

Skip it if: you need projects, audits or tracking. For more choices, see the keyword research tool shortlist for agencies.

Which all-in-one suites suit agencies leaving Ahrefs?

Semrush suits agencies that want breadth, daily tracking and AI visibility data in one account. SE Ranking suits agencies that want the most projects, keywords and seats for the money.

Semrush: best for breadth and daily tracking

Best for: agencies that want research, daily tracking and reports together. Semrush lists Semrush One Starter at $199 and Pro+ at $299 a month, with 5 and 15 websites and 500 and 1,500 tracked keywords, and its help center prices additional users at $45 a month on Starter and $80 on Pro+ (both checked 5 November 2026).

Skip it if: you manage many small client sites on a tight budget; the website caps and per-user fees add up quickly. Our Semrush alternatives guide covers the reverse move.

SE Ranking: best for capacity per dollar

Best for: small agencies. SE Ranking lists Core at $129 a month with 10 projects, one manager seat and 2,000 daily-tracked keywords, and Growth at $279 with 30 projects, three seats and 5,000 keywords (checked 5 November 2026). The extra-seat price differed across its pages, so confirm it before budgeting.

Skip it if: you need white-label reports on monthly billing; the Agency Pack starts at $69 a month and is billed annually only (checked 5 November 2026). Compare backlink results against Ahrefs on your own domains during the 14-day trial, which needs no credit card (checked 5 November 2026).

What if you only need rank tracking or content help?

Keep Ahrefs for research and add a specialist: AccuRanker for faster rankings, or Clearscope for briefs and draft grading. That adds a subscription, so it only pays if the gap costs you client time.

AccuRanker: best for agencies needing daily and on-demand rankings

Best for: teams that find a weekly refresh too slow. AccuRanker's help center says keywords update automatically every 24 hours and can also be refreshed on demand, and its pricing page lists Professional at €224 a month, with 10% off annual payment (both checked 5 November 2026). Ask about keyword allowances, which were not stated.

Skip it if: monthly client reporting on stable sites is your rhythm. Our rank tracker comparison for multi-client SEO lists other options.

Clearscope: best for content teams grading drafts

Best for: teams that need briefs and draft grades in the writing workflow. Clearscope lists Essentials at $129 a month with unlimited users and 20 monthly drafts (checked 5 November 2026).

Skip it if: you need link or rank data. See our content optimization tools guide for alternatives.

How do seat costs compare for a three-person team?

For a hypothetical three-person team on monthly billing, Ahrefs Lite costs $169 to $209, SE Ranking Growth $279 and Semrush One Starter $289 a month. The plans differ sharply in projects and keywords, so the cheapest seat count is not automatically the cheapest fit.

Assumptions: monthly list prices checked 5 November 2026, one login included in each base plan (three on SE Ranking Growth), no add-ons, tax excluded.

  • Ahrefs Lite: $129 plus two usage-billed users at $20 to $40 each is $169 to $209, but only 5 unverified projects.
  • SE Ranking Growth: $279 with three seats included and 30 projects.
  • Semrush One Starter: $199 plus two users at $45 is $289, with 5 websites. On Pro+, $299 plus two users at $80 is $459.

What should a transition checklist include?

Export everything Ahrefs holds that you cannot regenerate, then run the new tool alongside for one reporting cycle. Ranking history and link prospect lists are the two things teams most often lose.

  1. List which Ahrefs reports each team member used in the last 90 days.
  2. Export tracked keywords, ranking history, keyword lists and link prospect lists.
  3. Download recent site audit results as a baseline for the new crawler.
  4. Record current client metrics; new metrics will differ.
  5. Rebuild client report templates before the first client-facing report.
  6. Cancel before renewal, because Ahrefs' terms say refunds are not issued in general.

What else do buyers ask about replacing Ahrefs?

Buyers mostly ask about link data, cheaper Ahrefs tiers and whether one tool can do everything. Short answers follow.

Which tool has backlink data closest to Ahrefs?

Semrush and Majestic both maintain their own link indexes, and Moz runs Link Explorer. Every index finds a different subset of the web, so cross-check domains you know before deciding.

Is there a cheaper option without leaving Ahrefs?

Yes. Ahrefs lists a $29-a-month Starter plan and a free tier, Ahrefs Free, for verified site owners, both checked 5 November 2026. Both are much narrower than Lite.

Can one tool replace every Ahrefs job?

Semrush and SE Ranking come closest, with different strengths. Many teams end up with one suite plus one specialist.

Does weekly rank tracking matter?

It matters for agencies that react to daily volatility or promise frequent updates. For monthly reporting on stable sites, weekly data is often enough.

What's the verdict?

Replace Ahrefs only for the jobs where its terms work against you, such as tracking frequency, user caps or history limits, and keep it where it still earns its place. For many teams the answer is Ahrefs plus one specialist rather than a full switch.
