Which e-signature tool should an agency choose?

If enterprise clients send you their own paper and expect a familiar signing name, DocuSign or Adobe Acrobat Sign is the safe choice. If you mainly send your own MSAs, SOWs and change orders, a lighter tool such as Dropbox Sign, signNow, Zoho Sign or Yousign usually covers the job for less effort.

  • Agencies serving enterprise and regulated clients: DocuSign.
  • Agencies standardised on Adobe and PDF workflows: Adobe Acrobat Sign.
  • Small agencies that want a clean sender experience and templates: Dropbox Sign.
  • Teams that send many routine documents and want bulk send: signNow.
  • Agencies already using Zoho apps: Zoho Sign.
  • European agencies that want EU-focused signing options: Yousign.
  • Agencies that want signing built into the proposal itself: PandaDoc.

What does an agency actually need to sign?

Most agencies sign four kinds of document: a proposal or quote, a master services agreement, a statement of work per engagement and change orders when scope moves. Each has a different signer, frequency and risk, so the tool must handle templates and reminders well rather than one-off uploads.

The MSA is signed once and sets liability, IP ownership, payment terms and termination. SOWs are signed per project or per retainer period and carry the deliverables and price. Change orders are small but frequent, and they're where unsigned work leaks into the margin. Contractor agreements and NDAs flow in the other direction, from you to freelancers, which our guide to freelancer and contractor management platforms covers.

If your proposals already include a signature block, you may only need a standalone signing tool for the MSA and change orders. Our guide to proposal software for marketing agencies explains which proposal tools include signing.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

DocuSign and Adobe Acrobat Sign win on recognition, identity options and enterprise controls; the lighter tools aim for simplicity. PandaDoc is different again, because signing is one step inside a proposal and contract workflow.

DocuSign: best for agencies selling to enterprise clients

DocuSign is the name many procurement and legal teams already know, with templates, signing order, reminders, audit certificates, identity verification options and a wide integration catalog. For an agency, that familiarity removes friction when a client's legal team asks how you sign. The trade-off is plan complexity, so check whether envelope allowances or add-ons make the cheapest tier a poor guide to real cost. Skip it if you send a handful of contracts a month to small clients. Compare plans on the DocuSign products and pricing page.

Adobe Acrobat Sign: best for teams already working in Acrobat

Adobe Acrobat Sign ties signing to Acrobat's PDF editing, so a team that already edits and comments on contracts in Acrobat can send them for signature without switching tools. It's a good fit for agencies whose contracts arrive as client-supplied PDFs. The limitation is packaging: signing features vary across Acrobat bundles, so confirm what's included. Consider another option if your team doesn't use Adobe tools. Details are on the Adobe Acrobat Sign page.

Dropbox Sign: best for small agencies sending their own templates

Dropbox Sign, formerly HelloSign, offers a straightforward sender experience, reusable templates, reminders, audit trails and an API. For a small agency with a standard MSA and SOW, that simplicity is the point. In our assessment, its advanced identity and workflow options are thinner than DocuSign's. Skip it if enterprise clients insist on a specific provider. Plans are on the Dropbox Sign pricing page.

signNow: best for routine, high-volume sending

signNow, part of airSlate, emphasises templates, bulk send, signing links and workflow options. That suits agencies that send many similar documents, such as contractor agreements or renewal SOWs. Its interface and integrations are less familiar to large clients. Consider another option if brand recognition matters to your clients' legal teams. See the signNow pricing page.

Zoho Sign: best for agencies already on Zoho

Zoho Sign connects with Zoho CRM, Zoho Books and the wider Zoho suite, so a signed SOW can sit next to the deal and the invoice. That integration is its strongest argument. Outside the Zoho ecosystem, the advantage narrows. Skip it if your CRM and accounting live elsewhere. Plans are on the Zoho Sign pricing page.

Yousign: best for EU-based agencies

Yousign is a European e-signature provider that positions itself around EU regulations and signature levels. That's useful for agencies whose clients ask which eIDAS signature level a document uses. It has less presence with North American enterprise buyers. Consider another option if most clients are outside Europe. Check which signature levels each plan includes on the Yousign pricing page.

PandaDoc: best when signing belongs inside the proposal

PandaDoc includes e-signature in its proposal, quote and contract documents, so a client can review scope, choose options and sign in one place. That's efficient for agencies whose proposal effectively is the contract. It overlaps with a standalone signing tool, so don't pay for both without a reason. Skip it if you only need to sign client-supplied PDFs. See the PandaDoc pricing page.

How do envelope limits affect what you pay?

An envelope is usually one sending event, which can contain several documents and signers, and some plans may cap how many you can send, so check each vendor's terms. If you send a separate envelope for every change order, a low cap can push you to a higher tier faster than seat count does.

To estimate, count last year's signed documents by type: MSAs, SOWs, change orders, renewals and contractor agreements. Then check whether each vendor caps envelopes, whether bulk send counts each recipient separately and whether identity verification costs extra per use. Bundling an SOW and its first change order in one envelope can reduce volume, but only if your process allows it.

How should you handle contract documents from draft to archive?

Use one approved template per document type, send from a shared account, file the signed copy and audit certificate automatically, and record key dates in your CRM. The list below turns that into a routine any account manager can follow.

  • Template control: lock legal clauses in the MSA and SOW templates; only scope, price and dates are editable.
  • Signing order: set the client signer first and your director second, or as your process requires, with a named fallback.
  • Reminders and expiry: turn on automatic reminders and set an expiry so stale offers don't get signed months later.
  • Identity: decide which documents need stronger signer verification, and confirm the cost before you switch it on.
  • Filing: save the signed PDF and audit certificate to a client folder and link them to the CRM deal.
  • Hand-off: trigger onboarding and first invoice from the signed SOW, not from an email.
  • Renewals: record term, notice period and renewal date where someone will see them in time.

Will clients' procurement teams accept your signing tool?

Most smaller clients will sign with whatever tool you send, but larger organisations sometimes require their own provider or insist on signing their own paper. Ask during the sales process, before the contract is ready, so the signature step doesn't delay the start date.

What else do agencies ask about e-signature software?

Common questions cover legality, signer identity and how many tools you need. These answers are general information, not legal advice.

Are e-signatures valid for agency contracts?

Many jurisdictions recognise electronic signatures for ordinary commercial contracts, but the rules and exceptions differ by country and document type. Ask a qualified lawyer if a contract is high value, unusual or cross-border.

What is an audit certificate?

An audit certificate is a record the signing tool produces showing who viewed and signed a document, when and from where. Keep it with the signed contract, because it is your evidence of the signing process.

Do we need a separate e-signature tool if our proposal software has signing?

Often not, if your proposals carry the scope and terms clients sign. A standalone tool still helps for client-supplied PDFs, contractor agreements and documents your proposal tool doesn't handle.

Should change orders be signed or just approved by email?

A signed change order gives both sides a clear record of new scope and price. Email approval may be quicker, but it is easier to dispute later, so many agencies require a signature above a set value.

Who should own the e-signature account at an agency?

An operations or finance lead should own the account, templates and user list. Shared ownership by account managers tends to create duplicate templates and lost signed copies.

What's the verdict?

Choose DocuSign or Adobe Acrobat Sign if your clients are large and expect a recognised provider, and choose Dropbox Sign, signNow, Zoho Sign or Yousign if you mainly send your own templates. Use PandaDoc if the proposal is where clients sign.
