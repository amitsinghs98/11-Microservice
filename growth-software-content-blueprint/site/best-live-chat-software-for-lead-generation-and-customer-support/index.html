Which live chat tool should you choose for sales and support?

Choose Qualified or HubSpot if live chat is mainly a pipeline tool that books demos and syncs to the CRM, and LiveChat or Intercom if it's mainly a support channel staffed by agents. Tidio and Crisp are candidates for small businesses that want chat, a basic bot and a shared inbox without a long setup.

  • B2B demo booking for Salesforce-centered sales teams: Qualified.
  • Chat, bots and lead capture on the same CRM record: HubSpot.
  • Agent-staffed chat for support and sales teams: LiveChat.
  • SaaS companies supporting customers in-app and on site: Intercom.
  • Small businesses and ecommerce stores wanting chat plus an AI bot: Tidio.
  • Lean teams wanting chat and a multi-channel inbox: Crisp.
  • Simple, human-first chat without heavy automation: Olark.

What's the difference between live chat, chatbots and a shared inbox?

Live chat is a real-time conversation between a visitor and a person; a chatbot is software that answers or routes without a person; a shared inbox is where a team manages conversations asynchronously. Most live chat software for businesses now bundles all three, but you should decide which job each does before you compare features.

For lead generation, the job is to identify high-intent visitors, qualify them fast and get them to a rep or a calendar while they're still on the pricing page. For support, the job is to answer customers quickly, hand complex issues to the right agent, and keep a transcript attached to the customer's history.

Those jobs pull in different directions. Sales chat values speed and routing to an owner; support chat values context and a clean handoff to tickets. Qualified and HubSpot are the strongest for turning chats into pipeline, LiveChat and Intercom are the strongest for staffed support, and Tidio, Crisp and Olark trade depth for simplicity. Choose by the job your chat does most often, not by the longest feature list.

Qualified: best for B2B demo booking on Salesforce

What does a B2B demo setup look like compared with a SaaS support setup?

A B2B demo setup runs chat only on high-intent pages, qualifies in two or three questions and books a meeting with the account owner. A SaaS support setup runs chat in the product for logged-in users, passes identity and plan data to the agent and turns unresolved chats into tickets.

B2B demo setup (hypothetical): show the widget on pricing, comparison and demo pages. Ask company size and use case, look up the account, and route to the owner if one exists, otherwise to a round-robin pool. Offer a calendar link when no rep is free, sync the transcript and source page to the CRM, and alert the rep. Qualified or HubSpot fit; our lead routing and scheduling guide covers the routing layer if your chat tool can't do it.

SaaS support setup (hypothetical): install the messenger in-app with secure identity verification so agents see who's asking. Offer help-center search first, let a bot answer common how-to questions with sources, and hand off to a person when the customer asks or the bot isn't confident. Outside hours, collect the question and convert it into a ticket. Intercom or LiveChat plus a help desk fit; see our help desk guide for B2B SaaS for the ticketing side.

Which live chat features matter most when you shortlist?

Routing, CRM sync and bot-to-human handoff matter most, because they decide whether a conversation reaches the right person with context. Widget design and emoji support barely matter.

  • Routing: by page, language, account owner, team and availability, with a fallback when nobody's free.
  • Hours and offline mode: business hours per team and a clear offline experience.
  • Visitor context: current page, pages viewed, identified company and, for logged-in users, plan and account.
  • CRM sync and transcripts: two-way, with the transcript and source on the contact record.
  • Bot handoff: the bot passes the conversation and what it learned, and the visitor can always ask for a person.
  • Mobile apps: agents and reps can answer from a phone with notifications they'll actually see.
  • Privacy: consent integration, data retention settings, masking of sensitive fields and data processing terms.
  • Seats: how the vendor counts agents, and whether occasional responders need a full seat.

What else do buyers ask about live chat software?

Questions cluster around staffing, after-hours coverage, bots and privacy. Short answers follow; check vendor specifics on their official pages.

Should sales and support share one live chat widget?

They can share a widget if the first question or the page context routes visitors to the right team. Separate widgets on marketing and in-app pages are often cleaner, because the audiences and response expectations differ.

What happens to chats that arrive outside business hours?

Set the widget to switch to an offline form or a bot that collects the question and an email address, and say plainly when someone will reply. Promising live help and leaving the visitor waiting does more harm than showing no chat at all.

Do we need a chatbot if we already have live chat?

Not always. A simple qualifying bot that asks one or two questions before handoff helps busy teams, but a full automated agent only pays off once your knowledge base can answer common questions reliably.

Does live chat create privacy obligations?

Usually. Chat widgets can set cookies and collect personal data, so cover them in your privacy notice and consent setup, and check each vendor's data processing terms and hosting options.

How many agents does a small team need to staff live chat?

It depends on volume and the response time you promise, so start by staffing chat on your highest-intent pages during core hours. Measure first-response time for a month before widening coverage.
