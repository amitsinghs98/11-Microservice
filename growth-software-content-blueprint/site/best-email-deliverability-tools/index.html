Best Email Deliverability Tools for Marketing Teams

Email deliverability tools fall into four jobs: proving your domain is authenticated, testing where a message lands, watching sender reputation, and alerting you to blocklist listings. Most marketing teams need the free mailbox-provider dashboards, one DMARC reporting tool and, if campaigns carry real revenue, a seed-testing or monitoring platform.

Which deliverability tool should you start with?

Start with Google Postmaster Tools and Microsoft SNDS, which are free and show how two of the largest mailbox providers see your domain or IPs, then add a DMARC reporting tool such as dmarcian or EasyDMARC. Add GlockApps for affordable seed tests, Validity Everest for enterprise-grade monitoring, MxToolbox for blocklist alerts, or Mailgun Optimize if you already send through Mailgun.

  • Free reputation signals: Google Postmaster Tools and Microsoft SNDS.
  • DMARC reports made readable: dmarcian or EasyDMARC.
  • Pre-send inbox placement tests: GlockApps.
  • High-volume programs with revenue at stake: Validity Everest.
  • Blocklist and DNS monitoring: MxToolbox.
  • Mailgun senders wanting one vendor: Mailgun Optimize.

What do SPF, DKIM and DMARC actually prove?

SPF lists the servers allowed to send for your domain, DKIM signs each message so receivers can check it wasn't altered, and DMARC tells receivers what to do when a message fails and sends you reports about it. Together they prove a message really came from you; they don't prove anyone wants it.

The DMARC piece matters most for monitoring. Its aggregate reports reveal every service sending as your domain, including forgotten tools and spoofers, and DMARC reporting tools turn those raw XML files into something a marketer can read. Move from a monitoring-only policy toward enforcement only once every legitimate sender passes.

Mailbox providers increasingly expect this setup from anyone sending at volume; Google's email sender guidelines describe its expectations for authentication and unsubscribe handling.

Is a deliverability tool the same as an email validation tool?

No. Validation checks whether an address exists and is safe to mail before you send, while deliverability tools check how mailbox providers treat the mail you do send.

You usually need both: validation reduces bounces that damage reputation, and deliverability monitoring shows the result. Our comparison of email verification and list cleaning tools covers the first job; this page covers the second.

What does each tool monitor, and where does it stop?

The free provider dashboards and DMARC tools tell you whether you're trusted; the seed-testing and monitoring platforms tell you where mail lands. None of them tells you whether recipients wanted the email, which is still the biggest reputation input.

Google Postmaster Tools: best free view of how Gmail sees you

After you verify your domain, Google Postmaster Tools shows Gmail-side signals such as spam complaints, authentication results and delivery errors, and Google has been extending it with sender compliance status.

Limitations: it covers Gmail only, and low-volume domains may see little or no data. Skip it if you never send to consumer Gmail addresses, though few marketers can say that.

Microsoft SNDS: best free check on Outlook.com treatment

Smart Network Data Services reports how Outlook.com sees traffic from IP addresses you control, including complaint and trap indicators.

Limitations: it's IP-based, so if your email platform sends from shared IPs, the data reflects the pool, not you. Skip it if you don't have dedicated IPs.

dmarcian: best for rolling out DMARC policy safely

dmarcian processes DMARC aggregate reports into views of who sends as your domain and whether they pass, with plans on the dmarcian pricing page.

Limitations: it proves authentication, not inbox placement. Skip it if you already have DMARC at enforcement and stable senders.

EasyDMARC: best for managing authentication records in one place

EasyDMARC combines DMARC reporting with tools for building and checking SPF, DKIM and DMARC records; plans are on the EasyDMARC pricing page.

Limitations: like dmarcian, it doesn't test placement or reputation. Skip it if one of your existing tools already parses DMARC reports.

GlockApps: best for affordable pre-send seed tests

GlockApps sends your message to a panel of seed mailboxes and reports inbox, tab and spam placement by provider, alongside authentication and blocklist checks, on credit-based plans listed on the GlockApps pricing page.

Limitations: seed accounts don't behave like your real subscribers, so results are a sample. Skip it if you send too rarely to act on test results.

Validity Everest: best for high-volume programs with revenue at stake

Validity Everest combines seed-based placement testing, reputation monitoring, campaign analytics and validation, sold through sales.

Limitations: it's more platform than most small teams need, and pricing isn't self-serve. Skip it if email is a minor channel for you.

MxToolbox: best for blocklist and DNS alerts

MxToolbox offers free lookups for DNS records and blocklists, with paid monitoring that alerts you when a domain or IP is listed.

Limitations: it won't tell you where mail lands. Skip it if you only send from shared IPs you can't influence.

Mailgun Optimize: best for teams already on Mailgun

Mailgun Optimize bundles placement testing, reputation and blocklist monitoring and validation for Mailgun senders.

Limitations: its value is highest inside Mailgun. Skip it if your marketing email runs on another platform and you want a neutral tool. Product teams sending app mail should also read our guide to transactional email services for SaaS.

Do seed tests show where your email really lands?

Seed tests are a useful early warning, but they show how a set of test mailboxes treated one message, not how your engaged and unengaged subscribers will. Engagement-based filtering means real placement can differ from seed results in either direction.

How do you diagnose a sudden deliverability drop?

Check authentication first, then blocklists, then provider dashboards, then what changed in your sending. Most incidents trace back to a broken DNS record, a new or poorly sourced list, or a jump in volume.

  1. Confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC still pass; a DNS change or new sending tool often breaks one.
  2. Look up your domain and IPs on major blocklists.
  3. Read Postmaster Tools and SNDS for complaint spikes or delivery errors.
  4. Compare bounce codes by provider to see whether the problem is one mailbox provider or all of them.
  5. Review recent changes: list imports, volume, content, links or a new subdomain.
  6. Pause the riskiest segment, fix the cause, then resume gradually to engaged contacts.

What should a monthly deliverability routine include?

Once a month, review authentication and DMARC reports, check provider dashboards and blocklists, run a seed test on a representative campaign, and prune contacts who haven't engaged. It takes an hour or two and catches most problems before revenue does.

  • Authentication: new senders in DMARC reports, any failing sources, and whether you can tighten policy.
  • Reputation: complaint and error trends in Postmaster Tools and SNDS.
  • Blocklists: confirm no listings, or open a delisting request with the cause fixed.
  • Placement: one seed test on a typical campaign, plus a rendering and link check with an email testing tool.
  • List hygiene: sunset long-unengaged contacts and validate new imports.

What else do marketers ask about deliverability tools?

Most questions concern whether paid tools are necessary, how often to test, and what to do about shared IPs. The answers below are general guidance.

Do small marketing teams need a paid deliverability tool?

Not always. Free provider dashboards plus a DMARC reporting tool cover the basics, and a paid seed-testing or monitoring tool earns its cost once email revenue is significant.

How often should you run inbox placement tests?

Run one before major campaigns and after any change to domains, sending platform or templates. A monthly test on a typical campaign is a sensible baseline.

Can you fix deliverability on a shared IP?

You can control your domain reputation, authentication and list quality, which matter a great deal. IP reputation on a shared pool is managed by your email platform, so raise persistent problems with its support team.
