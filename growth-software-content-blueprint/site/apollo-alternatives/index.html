Which Apollo alternative fits what you're replacing?

If the data is the problem, look at ZoomInfo, Cognism or Lusha. If you need flexible enrichment, look at Clay; if you need stronger sequencing, look at Outreach or Salesloft for SDR teams, or lemlist and Instantly for leaner cold email.

  • You need data with better coverage in your market: ZoomInfo for enterprise-scale coverage, Cognism if Europe matters, Lusha for a lighter, self-serve start.
  • You need enrichment from several sources: Clay, which chains data providers in a spreadsheet-style workflow.
  • You need engagement more than data: Outreach or Salesloft for managed SDR teams; lemlist or Instantly for small teams and agencies.
  • You want to stay all-in-one: lemlist now pairs sending with a lead database and data add-ons, so it's the nearest single-tool swap here.

For the full field of data vendors, our guide to B2B contact databases and sales intelligence tools goes wider than this page.

What does Apollo do well?

Apollo bundles a large B2B contact database, enrichment, sequences and CRM sync in one product with self-serve plans. For a small team that wants to search, reveal contacts and send its first sequences without buying three tools, that bundle is hard to beat on simplicity.

Plans are credit-based and tiered, and Apollo has offered a free entry point. Check credit allowances, rollover rules and seat pricing on the official Apollo pricing page; we didn't confirm the current figures for this draft.

Why do prospecting teams look beyond Apollo?

In our editorial assessment, teams usually look elsewhere when data coverage is thin in their target segment or region, when credits run out before the month does, or when they need deeper sequencing or enrichment than a bundle provides. These are decision patterns, not complaint data.

  • Coverage: every database is stronger in some countries, industries and seniority levels than others.
  • Verified data: bounce rates or wrong numbers make reps stop trusting the tool.
  • Credits: how many credits a reveal costs, and whether unused credits roll over, decide the real monthly cost.
  • Enrichment depth: teams want firmographic, technographic or custom research fields a single database doesn't hold.
  • Engagement: managers want call workflows, coaching or deliverability controls that dedicated platforms offer.

Which alternatives offer better contact data?

ZoomInfo is the broadest option for teams that can sign an enterprise contract, and Cognism is the one to test if European coverage and phone data matter. Lusha is the easiest to start with on a self-serve budget.

ZoomInfo: best for coverage at enterprise scale

Best for: funded teams that need broad company and contact coverage plus intent and buying signals. Limitations: pricing is quote-based and contracts tend to be annual, which is heavy for a three-person team. Skip it if you want month-to-month terms. Our ZoomInfo alternatives for SMB teams covers that trade-off.

Cognism: best when Europe is in your territory

Best for: teams selling into the UK and EU that need phone numbers as well as emails. Limitations: quote-based pricing, and coverage should be sampled in any region outside its focus. Skip it if your list is mostly North American SMBs and email-only outreach is enough.

Lusha: best for a quick self-serve start

Best for: small teams that reveal contacts one at a time from LinkedIn or a CRM. Limitations: lighter on sequencing and list building than Apollo, so you'll probably pair it with a sending tool. Check how many credits an email reveal costs versus a phone reveal on the pricing page linked above.

Which alternatives are better for enrichment?

Clay is the main alternative when you need enrichment from several sources rather than one database. It lets you chain providers, so if the first one can't find an email or a field, the next one is tried.

Best for: RevOps-minded teams and agencies building custom research fields, such as a hiring signal or a tech-stack check, before outreach. Limitations: it takes real setup and maintenance, and credits are consumed per enrichment step, so a long chain costs more per row than a single lookup. Skip it if nobody on the team enjoys building workflows. We model the credit maths in Clay vs Apollo for enrichment workflows, and our lead enrichment software shortlist covers simpler options.

Which alternatives are better for sequences and sending?

Outreach and Salesloft suit managed SDR teams that need call workflows, governance and coaching. lemlist and Instantly suit small teams and agencies that mainly need reliable cold email, with lemlist also covering LinkedIn steps and lead data.

Outreach and Salesloft: best for managed SDR teams

Strengths: mature sequencing, calling and manager oversight. Limitations: quote-based, built for larger teams, and you still need a data source. Skip them if you have fewer than five reps.

lemlist: best single-tool swap for small teams

Strengths: personalized multichannel sequences with a lead database and data sold as add-ons. Limitations: data and sending are priced separately, so compare the combined cost with Apollo's bundle.

Instantly: best for volume cold email

Strengths: built around many sending mailboxes and warm-up. Limitations: email-first, with less depth for calling or LinkedIn. Our cold email software roundup compares it with similar tools.

What does a three-seat prospecting workflow really cost?

Cost is seats, plus the credits your reveals consume, plus mailboxes and domains, plus whatever CRM tier the integration needs. The credit line is the one that surprises teams, because phone reveals usually cost more than email reveals.

Hypothetical assumptions: three SDRs, each adding 400 new contacts a month, with phone numbers wanted for 20% of them. That's 1,200 email reveals and 240 phone reveals a month, before any re-enrichment of old records.

  • All-in-one (Apollo or lemlist): three seats on the plan that includes sequences, plus enough credits for 1,440 reveals. Check whether unused credits roll over.
  • Data vendor plus sender (Lusha plus Instantly, for example): credit plans sized to 1,440 reveals, plus a sending plan and mailboxes. Two invoices, but each one is sized to its own job.
  • Enrichment-led (Clay plus a sender): credits per enrichment step multiplied by 1,200 rows. A two-step waterfall can cost up to twice as much per row as a single lookup.

Plug each vendor's current credit cost per reveal into that arithmetic. The cheapest stack is the one with the lowest cost per usable contact, not the lowest sticker price.

How accurate is any of this contact data in practice?

Accuracy varies by region, industry and seniority for every vendor, so a vendor's own accuracy claim tells you little about your list. The only reliable test is to run the same sample of target accounts through two or three tools and measure bounces and wrong numbers yourself.

What compliance checks apply whichever tool you pick?

You remain responsible for having a lawful basis to contact each person, honoring opt-outs and following the email rules in every country you send to. Ask each vendor how it sources data, how it handles removal requests and which regions it treats differently.

Verify addresses before sending, keep suppression lists synced across tools, and document your basis for contacting people in Europe and the UK. Our guide to a compliant B2B outreach stack sets out the controls step by step.

What should you check before leaving Apollo?

Check what you can export, how revealed contacts and sequence history will move to your CRM, and when your current credits and contract expire. Then test the new tool's coverage on your own accounts before cancelling.

  1. Export saved lists, revealed contacts and sequence performance.
  2. Confirm that your CRM, not Apollo, is the system of record for contacts and activity. If you don't have one yet, start with our small-business CRM picks.
  3. Rebuild suppression and do-not-contact lists in the new tool first.
  4. Run a 200-account coverage test against your ideal customer profile.
  5. Use up or document remaining credits before the renewal date.

What else do buyers ask about Apollo alternatives?

The common questions are about cheaper all-in-ones, pairing tools and credit rules. Confirm plan details on each vendor's official pricing page.

Is there a cheaper all-in-one alternative to Apollo?

Few tools match Apollo's mix of database, enrichment and sequences in one plan. lemlist is the closest single-tool option, but because its data is an add-on, compare the combined cost for your volume.

Can I use Clay and Apollo together?

Yes, and many teams do. Apollo can act as one data source or the sending layer, while Clay handles multi-source enrichment and research fields.

Do unused prospecting credits roll over?

It depends on the vendor and plan. Some roll credits over while you stay subscribed and others reset them monthly, so check the pricing page and terms before you size a plan.

Which Apollo alternative is best for European prospects?

Cognism is the one to test first if Europe is your main market. Run a sample of your own target accounts before you sign, because coverage varies by country and role.

What's the verdict?

Stay on Apollo if its bundle covers your market well and your credit budget lasts the month. Otherwise, replace only the part that's failing: a data vendor for coverage, Clay for enrichment, or a dedicated sender for engagement.
