Best Email Marketing Software for Small Businesses

The best email marketing software for a small business is the one whose billing unit matches how you send: subscriber-priced tools suit small lists mailed often, while send-priced tools suit large lists mailed rarely. Below, eight platforms are matched to creators, local businesses, online stores and B2B teams.

Which email platform should you choose?

Pick MailerLite or Brevo if cost control matters most, Kit if you're a creator, Klaviyo if you run an online store, and ActiveCampaign or HubSpot Marketing Hub if email has to feed a sales pipeline. Mailchimp and Constant Contact remain sensible for local businesses that value familiarity, as long as you price them at next year's list size rather than today's.

Who is this guide for?

It's for owners and small marketing teams choosing a permission-based email platform: newsletters, promotions and automated sequences sent to people who opted in. It's the main buying guide in our email marketing and outreach software hub, so it stays broad.

Cold outreach to prospected lists is a different category; start with our shortlist of cold email platforms for B2B agencies. Stores should also read our look at email platforms built for ecommerce brands.

How did we choose these platforms?

We assessed each platform on eight criteria: everyday usability, list-size economics, automation depth, landing pages, forms, segmentation, deliverability support and price behavior at realistic contact counts. The evidence is official pricing pages and help-center documentation; no hands-on testing was done, and no sends or inbox placement were measured.

Vendor sites couldn't be fetched directly for this draft, so the figures below were matched against search-engine copies of official pages on 5 November 2026 and should be re-checked on the live pages before you commit. Where sources conflicted, looked dated or showed only promotions, we left the number out. No vendor paid to influence the order.

What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

Each platform below is strong for one buyer type and a poor fit for at least one other. Read the "skip it if" line first; it rules tools out fastest.

MailerLite: best for lean teams that want everything in one place

What it does: campaigns, visual automation, forms, pop-ups, landing pages, a simple website builder and digital product sales.

Pricing model: subscriber-based paid tiers (Comfort, Power and Enterprise). The free plan was cut on 16 June 2026 and now allows 250 active subscribers, 2,500 emails a month and two seats (checked 5 November 2026), down from 500.

Strengths: an approachable editor and a wide bundled toolset for a one-person marketing function.

Limitations: new accounts must complete a profile and be approved before sending (checked 5 November 2026), so imported lists of uncertain origin draw questions. Ecommerce features are thinner than store specialists'.

Skip it if: you need lead scoring and sales handoff, or your list came from anywhere other than your own opt-in forms.

Mailchimp: best for local businesses that want a familiar tool

What it does: campaigns, automation flows, forms, landing pages, segmentation and a large integration library.

Pricing model: Free, Essentials, Standard and Premium, priced by contact tier. The Free plan covers 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with a daily cap of 250; Essentials stores up to 50,000 contacts with a monthly send limit of 10 times your contact tier, and Standard allows 12 times (checked 5 November 2026). Listed prices were promotional, so we don't quote them.

Strengths: breadth of integrations and templates, and a short learning curve.

Limitations: unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts count toward your billed total, while archived and cleaned contacts don't (checked 5 November 2026), so an unpruned list costs more than its reachable audience.

Skip it if: your list will pass 10,000 contacts within a year and you mail it infrequently. Our roundup of Mailchimp alternatives for growing businesses covers where teams usually go next.

Brevo: best for big lists mailed a few times a month

What it does: email campaigns, automation, SMS and WhatsApp messaging, a lightweight CRM and transactional email.

Pricing model: plans are sized by monthly email volume. The pricing page lists a free plan of 300 emails a day and Starter from $9 a month for 5,000 emails (checked 5 November 2026). Brevo's help center adds that Starter tiers also set a contact limit, and passing it upgrades you to the next tier automatically (checked 5 November 2026).

Strengths: volume pricing can cost far less than contact pricing for a large, lightly mailed list.

Limitations: the advantage shrinks if you send daily, the contact caps mean Brevo isn't purely send-priced, and sending starts only after account approval.

Skip it if: you mail a mid-sized list several times a week.

Kit: best for creators, coaches and consultants

What it does: tag-based subscribers, sequences, visual automations, landing pages, paid newsletters and creator recommendations.

Pricing model: subscriber-based Creator and Pro tiers. The Free plan costs nothing while you stay under 10,000 subscribers, but automations and sequences need Creator or Pro (checked 5 November 2026). Paid prices conflicted across Kit's own pages, so we don't quote them.

Strengths: simple tagging for audiences spanning several lead magnets, and a free allowance that lets a newsletter grow first.

Limitations: lighter design controls and store-event depth than ecommerce tools.

Skip it if: you need CRM-style scoring. B2B consultants should compare our guide to newsletter platforms for B2B creators.

Klaviyo: best for stores where order data should drive email

What it does: segmentation on store events, pre-built ecommerce flows, predictive analytics, SMS and revenue reporting.

Pricing model: billed on active profiles, with mobile messaging priced separately. The free plan allows 250 active profiles and 500 email sends a month, and paid accounts that exceed their profile limit move up a tier at the next billing cycle; suppressed profiles don't count (both checked 5 November 2026).

Strengths: store integrations and attribution reporting that general tools struggle to match.

Limitations: without a store, most of the value goes unused while the profile-based bill still grows.

Skip it if: you're a service business. Stores watching cost can compare our Klaviyo alternatives for email and SMS.

ActiveCampaign: best for B2B firms that need branching automation

What it does: email, a visual automation builder with conditional logic, site tracking, lead scoring and CRM features on higher tiers.

Pricing model: the pricing page lists Starter, Plus, Pro and Enterprise, priced by contact count, with one seat on Starter and Plus, three on Pro and five on Enterprise (checked 5 November 2026). There's no permanent free plan; the 14-day trial is limited to one user and 100 email sends (checked 5 November 2026).

Strengths: automation depth for the money, which our HubSpot Marketing Hub vs ActiveCampaign comparison weighs against CRM alignment.

Limitations: the builder rewards teams with time to design journeys, lead scoring depends on tier, and a 100-send trial can't show real campaign behavior.

Skip it if: you send one newsletter a month and nothing else.

Constant Contact: best for owners who want guided tools

What it does: email campaigns, event registration, social posting and basic automation.

Pricing model: Lite, Standard and Premium plans priced by contact count, with a trial rather than a free plan. The official pricing copy we found looked dated, so confirm prices and trial terms on the Constant Contact pricing page.

Strengths: a workflow aimed at non-marketers, plus event features that suit nonprofits and local organizers.

Limitations: segmentation and behavioral automation are lighter than ActiveCampaign or Klaviyo, and with no free plan you're paying from the end of the trial.

Skip it if: you plan multi-step journeys triggered by website behavior.

HubSpot Marketing Hub: best for teams already on HubSpot CRM

What it does: email, forms, landing pages, workflows on paid tiers and reporting on the same record your sales team uses.

Pricing model: free tools plus paid tiers billed by seats and marketing contacts. HubSpot's product catalog lists Starter at $20 a month per core seat with 1,000 marketing contacts, and the pricing page lists Professional at $890 a month billed monthly ($800 a month on annual billing) with three core seats, plus a required one-time $3,000 onboarding fee (both checked 5 November 2026). Starter was also discounted for a limited time.

Strengths: one contact record for marketing and sales simplifies handoff and attribution.

Limitations: the step from Starter to Professional is large, and the advanced workflows most B2B teams want sit on the higher tier.

Skip it if: you have no CRM plans and simply need a newsletter.

What does email software really cost at 2k, 10k and 50k contacts?

At 2,000 contacts the gap between vendors is usually small in absolute terms; by 50,000 contacts, the billing unit decides whether you pay a modest or a painful monthly bill. The scenarios below are hypothetical: they apply only the limits confirmed above and price no tier, so run your own numbers on each vendor's pricing page.

Scenario 1: 2,000 contacts, four campaigns a month

That's about 8,000 sends a month. MailerLite, Mailchimp and Klaviyo free plans stop at 250 contacts or profiles, and Brevo's 300-a-day free cap means one campaign to 2,000 people would take seven days to go out. Kit's free plan holds the list, but a welcome sequence needs a paid tier. Mailchimp Essentials' 10-times rule gives a 2,000-contact tier about 20,000 sends, well above this cadence.

Scenario 2: 10,000 contacts, weekly sends plus a welcome flow

Weekly campaigns plus automation come to roughly 45,000 to 50,000 sends a month. That fits inside Mailchimp Essentials' 100,000-send allowance at this tier, but it's past Kit's free threshold and well past HubSpot Starter's 1,000 included marketing contacts. On Klaviyo, suppressed profiles leave the active count, but you must pick the lower tier yourself.

Scenario 3: 50,000 contacts, twice-monthly sends

That's about 100,000 sends a month from a big but lightly mailed list. It sits at the 50,000-contact ceiling of Mailchimp Essentials, so any growth forces a plan change, while a send-volume model like Brevo's deserves a serious quote. On contact-priced tools, archiving unsubscribed contacts may cut the bill more than switching vendors would. Where do free plans and cheap tiers stop working?

Based on the official limits reviewed, most free plans here stop at 250 contacts or a few hundred sends, which suits testing rather than an established customer list; Kit is the exception on list size but withholds automation. The cheaper paid tiers usually limit sends, seats or automation as well as contacts, so the constraint you hit first may not be the one on the pricing headline.

Which platform fits your type of business?

Match the platform to the data that should trigger your emails: subscriber tags for creators, simple segments for local firms, store events for ecommerce, and CRM fields for B2B. That usually narrows the shortlist to two or three tools.

  • Creator with a growing newsletter: Kit or MailerLite; check export options before adding paid subscriptions.
  • Local business with occasional promotions: Mailchimp, Constant Contact or MailerLite, priced at next year's list size.
  • Ecommerce store: Klaviyo first, with Brevo or MailerLite as cheaper options if you don't need predictive features.

What should you ask before migrating?

Ask whether contacts export with tags, custom fields, consent records and unsubscribe status intact, and whether automations must be rebuilt by hand. Those two answers decide how long the move really takes.

  • Does the new platform approve accounts or review imported lists before sending? MailerLite and Brevo both do.
  • What sending ramp does the vendor recommend for a new domain or IP?
  • Which reports and historical engagement data will you lose on export?

Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your sending domain before the first campaign. Our guide to deliverability monitoring tools for marketing teams explains how to watch the results after you switch.

What mistakes do small businesses make when buying?

The most common mistake is pricing today's list instead of next year's. The second is buying automation nobody has time to build.

  • Ignoring automatic upgrades. Brevo and Klaviyo move paid accounts up a tier when you pass a contact or profile limit, so growth raises the bill without asking.
  • Trusting open rates. Privacy features distort opens; measure clicks, conversions and revenue, as our guide to measuring email ROI alongside deliverability explains.
  • Paying twice for AI writing. Many platforms bundle writing assistants; compare them with dedicated AI email copywriting tools before upgrading for that feature alone.

What else do buyers ask about email marketing software?

Most remaining questions concern free plans, billing units and CRMs. The answers are general; confirm any limit on the vendor's pricing page.

Is a free email marketing plan enough for a small business?

Usually only while the list is very small, because most free plans reviewed here stop at 250 contacts. Kit's free plan covers a larger list but excludes automations and sequences, so most established businesses still need a paid tier.

Should I choose a platform priced by contacts or by sends?

Choose the unit that matches your behavior. Large lists mailed rarely favor send-based pricing, while smaller lists mailed often favor contact-based pricing with generous send allowances.

Do I need a separate CRM as well as an email platform?

B2B teams with a sales pipeline usually do. Creators and most local businesses can manage with tags and segments inside the email platform.

Which email marketing platform has the best deliverability?

No independent comparison was made for this page, and vendor deliverability claims aren't comparable. Inbox placement depends more on your authentication, list quality and sending habits than on the platform you pick.

What's the verdict?

Our editorial assessment: start with MailerLite or Brevo if cost control comes first, Kit if you're a creator, Klaviyo if you run a store, and ActiveCampaign or HubSpot if email must feed a sales pipeline. Whatever you shortlist, confirm the current limits on the vendor's pricing page, model next year's list, and start a trial from the vendor's official site before you migrate anything.
