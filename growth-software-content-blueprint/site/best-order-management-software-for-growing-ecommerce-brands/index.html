Which order management system should a growing brand choose?

Multichannel sellers with marketplace volume should shortlist Linnworks or Extensiv Order Manager, brands that need accounting-grade inventory should look at Brightpearl or Cin7, and smaller teams should start with Ordoro, Zoho Inventory or Veeqo. Fluent Commerce only makes sense once you route orders across stores and warehouses at enterprise scale.

  • High-volume marketplace and webstore sellers: Linnworks.
  • Retail and wholesale brands that want orders, stock and accounting together: Brightpearl.
  • Brands mixing B2B, DTC and manufacturing or assemblies: Cin7.
  • Multi-warehouse brands that want OMS plus warehouse tooling from one vendor: Extensiv Order Manager.
  • Amazon-heavy sellers that mainly need shipping and stock sync: Veeqo.
  • Small teams already on Zoho apps: Zoho Inventory.
  • Small, shipping-led teams that want simplicity: Ordoro.
  • Enterprise retailers with stores and distributed fulfilment: Fluent Commerce.

When does a brand actually need an order management system?

You need one when you sell through more than one channel or ship from more than one location and can no longer keep stock and order status accurate by hand. Before that point, your commerce platform's built-in order and inventory tools are usually enough.

Common triggers: oversells after a marketplace launch, a second warehouse or 3PL, bundles and kits that break stock counts, wholesale orders entered separately, and returns that never make it back into sellable stock. An OMS sits between your sales channels and your fulfilment, holding one version of each order and each SKU's availability.

The boundary with neighbouring tools matters. Listing products on marketplaces is the job of marketplace management software, keeping product data clean for shopping channels belongs to product feed management software, and the shopper-facing returns flow is covered by returns and exchanges software. Several OMS tools overlap with all three, which is where duplicate spend creeps in. The ecommerce growth hub maps the full category.

What is each order management system good at, and when should you skip it?

Every system here can sync orders and stock; they differ in how they model inventory, how much accounting they include and how far they scale across locations. Weigh the limitation against your next 18 months, not your current month.

Linnworks: best for high-volume multichannel sellers

Linnworks is built for sellers listing across many marketplaces and webstores, with stock sync, order rules, shipping integrations and listing tools in one place. It suits teams whose main pain is keeping marketplace stock accurate at volume. The rules engine is powerful, but it takes setup time and someone who owns it. Check the current pricing model and request a quote with your real order counts on the official pricing page. Skip it if you sell mainly through one webstore.

Brightpearl: best for retail and wholesale brands that want accounting built in

Brightpearl, now part of Sage, combines orders, inventory, purchasing and accounting-grade stock records for brands selling retail and wholesale. Its strength is a single financial view of stock and orders. The trade-off is a heavier onboarding than lighter OMS tools need. Skip it if you need a quick install or already run inventory in an ERP.

Cin7: best for brands mixing B2B, DTC and assemblies

Cin7 sells inventory and order management with B2B portals and bill-of-materials features, which suits brands that kit, assemble or wholesale. It offers more than one product line, so confirm which edition you're being quoted for and what each includes on the pricing page. Skip it if you're a pure marketplace seller that needs listing tools more than manufacturing features.

Extensiv Order Manager: best for brands running several warehouses or 3PLs

Extensiv Order Manager (formerly Skubana) focuses on multichannel orders, inventory and routing across warehouses. Its value grows if you or your 3PL also use Extensiv's warehouse software. Check current pricing on the official site. Skip it if you ship from one location and don't plan to add more.

Veeqo: best for Amazon-heavy sellers

Veeqo is owned by Amazon and centres on shipping, stock sync and order handling for marketplace sellers. Confirm its current commercial terms on the official site before you plan around it. It's lighter on accounting, wholesale and complex routing. Skip it if marketplaces are a small share of your orders.

Zoho Inventory: best for small teams already using Zoho

Zoho Inventory covers orders, stock, warehouses and shipping, and connects naturally with Zoho Books and CRM. Plans are tiered and listed on the official pricing page. It's a sensible first OMS for a small team. Skip it if you need deep marketplace automation or high-volume routing.

Ordoro: best for small, shipping-led teams

Ordoro combines shipping labels, inventory sync, dropshipping and kitting in a simpler interface. It fits teams of a few people who want fewer moving parts. Skip it once you need multi-location routing logic or accounting-grade inventory.

Fluent Commerce: best for enterprise distributed order management

Fluent Commerce is a distributed OMS for retailers routing orders across stores, warehouses and drop-ship partners, with ship-from-store and click-and-collect logic. It's sold and implemented at enterprise scale. Skip it unless you have physical stores and a team to run the implementation.

Is your brand ready to migrate to an order management system?

You're ready when your SKU data is clean, your fulfilment rules are written down and someone owns the system after launch. If any of those is missing, fix it first, because an OMS automates whatever mess it inherits.

  • SKU hygiene: one unique SKU per sellable variant, with bundles and kits defined as components.
  • Stock baseline: a recent count per location you trust.
  • Routing rules: which location ships which orders, when to split shipments, and backorder handling.
  • Channel list: every storefront, marketplace and wholesale route, with its connector confirmed on the vendor's integrations page.
  • Returns flow: how returned items are graded and restocked.
  • Accounting link: whether orders and stock values post to your accounting system, and who reconciles.
  • Cut-over plan: a quiet sales period, parallel running and a rollback option.

If you're also reconsidering your storefront, settle that first; our guide to Shopify alternatives for more store control covers platform-level trade-offs.

Where do order management rollouts usually go wrong?

Data preparation and connector setup are the parts of onboarding most likely to take longer than planned, because they depend on your data rather than the software. That's the area to stress-test in a demo with your own SKUs and order types.

What does ecommerce order management really cost?

Pricing in this category commonly scales with order volume, with users, locations, channels or SKUs as possible secondary levers, so ask each vendor which applies and whether onboarding is charged separately. Budget for implementation and connector maintenance as well as the subscription.

Ask each vendor what happens when you exceed an order band, whether peak months count, which connectors cost extra, and whether onboarding is fixed-fee. A brand that doubles order volume in Q4 can land in a higher tier for the whole year if the contract measures peak rather than average. What else do buyers ask about ecommerce order management software?

Most questions are about when an OMS replaces platform tools, ERPs or 3PL software. Short answers follow.

Is an OMS the same as an inventory management system?

They overlap. An inventory system tracks stock; an OMS also captures orders from every channel, routes them to a location and tracks them to delivery, and most products here do both.

Do I need an OMS if my 3PL has its own software?

Often yes, if you sell through several channels or use more than one 3PL. The 3PL system manages its warehouse; the OMS decides which location gets each order.

Can an OMS replace an ERP?

For smaller brands, a system with accounting-grade inventory can delay an ERP. Larger brands usually run both, with the OMS feeding orders to the ERP.

How do bundles affect stock accuracy?

If bundles aren't modelled as components, selling a bundle doesn't reduce the stock of its parts, which causes oversells. Check how each system handles kits before you migrate.

How long does an OMS migration take?

It depends on SKU count, channels and data quality. Ask each vendor for a typical onboarding plan for a business of your size, and add time for parallel running.

Choose for your channel mix and fulfilment footprint: Linnworks or Extensiv for multichannel and multi-warehouse volume, Brightpearl or Cin7 for inventory with accounting depth, and Ordoro, Zoho Inventory or Veeqo for smaller teams. Clean your SKU data before you sign.
