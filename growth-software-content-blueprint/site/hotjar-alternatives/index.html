Which Hotjar alternative fits your primary need?

If cost is the trigger, start with Microsoft Clarity; if you need funnels, form analytics and feedback in one budget tool, look at Mouseflow or Lucky Orange. If you need product analytics or engineering context next to recordings, PostHog or LogRocket fit better, and FullStory or Contentsquare suit large teams with dedicated analysts.

  • Free heatmaps and recordings for a marketing site: Microsoft Clarity.
  • Conversion research with funnels and form analytics: Mouseflow or Lucky Orange.
  • Heatmaps plus simple A/B tests for landing pages: Crazy Egg.
  • Enterprise digital experience analytics: FullStory or Contentsquare.
  • Replay linked to product events, flags and surveys: PostHog.
  • Replay linked to front-end errors and performance: LogRocket.

What does Hotjar do well, and why do teams switch?

Hotjar bundles heatmaps, session recordings, on-site surveys and feedback widgets in an interface that marketers and designers can use without an analyst. Teams usually switch because of session-based pricing, a need for deeper funnels or product analytics, or a wish for a free tool for low-stakes sites.

Its strength is breadth for qualitative research: you can watch a recording, see where clicks cluster and ask visitors why, all in one place. Hotjar is now part of Contentsquare, which also sells its own enterprise platform.

The legitimate switching triggers we see in buyer questions are structural. Plans are priced by session volume, so traffic growth raises cost even when research needs don't change. Recording can be sampled once traffic exceeds the plan's allowance, so check how that works on your tier. And when the questions shift from "why did this page fail?" to "which users retain?", a behaviour tool alone runs out of road.

How is session replay different from product analytics?

Session replay shows you what individual visitors did on a page; product analytics counts what groups of users do across sessions and over time. Replay explains a problem you've already spotted, while product analytics tells you where the problem is in the first place.

Heatmap and replay tools are page-centred. They're ideal for diagnosing a landing page, a checkout step or a confusing form. Product analytics tools are user-centred: they track named events, build funnels and cohorts, and measure retention and feature adoption.

Many teams need both, and the line is blurring. PostHog and Amplitude now offer replay next to analytics, while some replay tools add funnels. Which tools cover heatmaps and replay on a budget?

Microsoft Clarity is the obvious first stop because Microsoft offers it free, but it covers recordings and heatmaps only. Mouseflow, Lucky Orange and Crazy Egg cost money and add funnels, forms, feedback or testing on top.

Microsoft Clarity: best for free heatmaps and recordings

Clarity offers heatmaps, session recordings and behaviour insights such as rage and dead clicks, with an integration for Google Analytics. Its limitation is scope: there are no on-site surveys or feedback widgets, and enterprise support and contractual controls are lighter than paid tools offer. Skip it if your research depends on asking visitors questions.

Mouseflow: best for funnels and form analytics

Mouseflow pairs recordings and heatmaps with conversion funnels, form analytics and feedback surveys, which makes it the closest budget match for Hotjar's mix. Pricing scales with recordings, so check how sampling works once you hit the plan's monthly allowance.

Lucky Orange: best for small teams that want chat in the same tool

Lucky Orange combines recordings, heatmaps, form analytics, surveys and live chat. That breadth suits a small ecommerce or services site with one person running the research. Consider another option if you need deep segmentation or a large analyst team.

Crazy Egg: best for landing-page heatmaps and quick tests

Crazy Egg focuses on heatmaps, scroll maps and recordings, with built-in A/B testing for simple page changes. It suits marketers optimising landing pages; for a dedicated testing programme, compare the options in our A/B testing software shortlist.

Which tools go deeper for enterprise experience teams?

FullStory and Contentsquare are built for organisations with analysts, governance requirements and high traffic. Both are sales-led, quote-based and slower to implement, which is the price of their depth.

FullStory: best for cross-team digital experience analytics

FullStory captures user interactions for search and analysis across product, support and engineering, with session replay at the centre. Skip it if a marketing team of two needs a quick answer on one landing page.

Contentsquare: best for large ecommerce and experience programmes

Contentsquare offers zone-based heatmaps, journey analysis and replay for large sites. Because it owns Hotjar, moving "up" to Contentsquare is a different buying conversation from moving away; ask what carries over from your Hotjar account before you negotiate.

Which tools tie replay to product analytics?

PostHog links replay to product events, feature flags and surveys, while LogRocket links replay to front-end errors and performance data. Both suit SaaS teams more than marketing-site owners.

PostHog: best for engineering-led SaaS teams

PostHog bundles product analytics, session replay, surveys, feature flags and experiments, with usage-based pricing and a free monthly allowance per product. Its limitation for marketers is the setup, which assumes someone can instrument events. Our product analytics shortlist for SaaS startups compares it with Mixpanel and Amplitude.

LogRocket: best for debugging what users hit

LogRocket records sessions alongside console logs, network requests and errors, so engineers can reproduce what a user saw. It's the wrong tool if your goal is landing-page conversion research.

What if surveys were the main reason you used Hotjar?

Then a replay tool alone won't replace Hotjar, and you'll need either an all-in-one such as Mouseflow, Lucky Orange or PostHog, or a dedicated survey tool. Decide whether the surveys need to trigger on behaviour, such as exit intent or a specific page, because that narrows the field quickly.

Dedicated voice-of-customer platforms go further on question logic, panels and analysis. Our voice of customer software shortlist covers them. For lead-capture forms rather than research, look at a form builder instead.

Which privacy controls and session caps should you compare?

Compare default masking of text and form inputs, page exclusion, consent integration, data location and retention first, then the session or recording cap and what happens when you exceed it. Those details decide both compliance risk and how representative your recordings are.

How do you move off Hotjar without losing research?

Export what you need, run the new tool in parallel and rebuild surveys before you remove the Hotjar script. Recordings and heatmaps rarely transfer between vendors, so plan on starting fresh.

  1. List what you actually use: heatmaps, recordings, surveys, feedback widgets, funnels or interviews.
  2. Export survey responses and feedback, and save screenshots of key heatmaps with their date ranges.
  3. Note which pages are excluded from recording and replicate those exclusions first.
  4. Install the new script through your tag manager and confirm consent behaviour.
  5. Run both tools for two to four weeks on your highest-traffic pages.
  6. Rebuild active surveys and their targeting rules, then retire the old ones.
  7. Remove the Hotjar script and cancel at the end of the billing term.

What else do buyers ask about Hotjar alternatives?

Buyers mostly ask about free options, privacy and whether replay can stand in for analytics. The answers below stay within what official product information supports.

Is Microsoft Clarity a full Hotjar replacement?

For heatmaps and session recordings, it covers the core job for free. It doesn't replace Hotjar's on-site surveys or feedback widgets, so pair it with a survey tool if you rely on those.

Can session replay replace product analytics?

No. Replay shows individual sessions, while product analytics measures funnels, cohorts and retention across many users. Most SaaS teams use replay to explain what analytics has already flagged.

Do heatmap tools slow down a website?

Any third-party script adds some load. Load it through your tag manager, limit recording to the pages you study, and check your site's performance before and after installation.

Can I move my Hotjar recordings to another tool?

Generally not. Plan to export survey responses and save key heatmap views, then start collecting fresh recordings in the new tool.
