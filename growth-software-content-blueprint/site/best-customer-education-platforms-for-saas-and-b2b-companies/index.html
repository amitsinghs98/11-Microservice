Best Customer Education Platforms for SaaS and B2B Companies

Customer education platforms run your product academy: courses, learning paths, certifications and tutorials for customers and partners, connected to the systems that tell you whether trained accounts behave differently. Which customer education platform should you choose?

Choose by the scale of your program and how tightly education must connect to your CRM and product data. A team certifying thousands of partners needs enterprise permissions, SSO and commerce, while a team publishing its first ten courses mostly needs fast authoring and a branded front door.

  • Established SaaS academies that want ties to customer success data: Skilljar.
  • Large programs spanning customers, partners and employees: Docebo.
  • Branded learning businesses, including paid training: Thought Industries.
  • Enterprise education teams focused on engagement and analytics: Intellum.
  • Mid-sized programs that want a straightforward LMS with portals: LearnUpon.
  • Teams running customer and internal enablement on one platform: WorkRamp.
  • A first academy on a modest budget: Thinkific.

What does a customer education platform add beyond a knowledge base?

A knowledge base answers a specific question when someone searches for it, while an education platform teaches a sequence of skills and records who completed them. That record is the point: it lets you segment customers by training status, issue certifications and compare trained and untrained accounts.

Most SaaS companies need both, and they should link to each other. Help articles live in a customer knowledge base and self-service portal; structured courses, learning paths and exams live in the academy. Discussion and peer help belong in a customer community platform, which some education vendors now bundle or integrate.

Use the academy for what articles do badly: onboarding new admins in order, certifying partners, teaching best practice beyond button-clicking, and re-training users after a major release.

What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

The first four platforms are built for dedicated education teams and enterprise requirements, the next two sit in the middle, and Thinkific is a budget starting point. In our assessment, integration with your CRM and CS platform is the feature that most separates them for SaaS use.

Skilljar: best for SaaS academies tied to customer success

Skilljar is a customer and partner education platform, with learning paths, assessments, certificates, branded academies and integrations with CRMs and analytics tools. Its positioning around customer education makes it a natural shortlist candidate for teams that want completions to reach customer success data.

Limitations: ask how completions reach your CRM and CS platform, and which integrations are native rather than built on the API. Skip it if you need a single platform for employee compliance training too. See Skilljar's official site.

Docebo: best for large, multi-audience programs

Docebo is an enterprise learning platform used for customer, partner and employee training, with AI-assisted authoring, multiple branded portals, ecommerce and extensive integrations. It fits organizations that want one learning system across audiences.

Limitations: breadth and configurability lengthen implementation. Skip it if you're launching a small customer academy with one admin. See Docebo's official site.

Thought Industries: best for branded and paid training

Thought Industries focuses on external learning businesses, with strong control over branded learner experiences and support for selling training, subscriptions and certifications. It suits companies treating education as a revenue line or a premium service.

Limitations: its commerce and design depth may be more than a free onboarding academy needs. Skip it if you'll never charge for training. See Thought Industries' official site.

Intellum: best for enterprise education teams

Intellum targets large organizations running customer and partner education at scale, with an emphasis on learner engagement and analytics. It's worth a look when education is a department, not a side project.

Limitations: an enterprise focus means a sales-led process and implementation. Skip it if you need to launch within weeks with a small budget. See Intellum's official site.

LearnUpon: best for straightforward mid-sized programs

LearnUpon is an LMS that supports separate portals for different audiences, which lets one admin team run customer, partner and internal training without mixing them. Its appeal is a manageable admin experience.

Limitations: community and advanced content design are less central than in academy-first tools. Skip it if peer discussion is core to your program. See LearnUpon's official site.

WorkRamp: best for customer and employee learning together

WorkRamp positions itself as a learning platform for both employee enablement and customer education. That suits companies where the same team trains sales, support and customers on the same product.

Limitations: a dual focus can mean customer-facing features trail specialists. Skip it if your academy is purely external and large. See WorkRamp's official site.

Thinkific: best for a first academy on a budget

Thinkific is a course creation platform aimed at creators and small businesses, and it can host a simple branded product academy with quizzes and certificates. It's a pragmatic way to prove demand before buying an enterprise platform.

Limitations: it's built for creators, so CRM syncing, SSO and B2B reporting are lighter. Skip it if you need learner data in your CS platform from day one. Check plans on Thinkific's pricing page.

How should you map curricula for admins and end users?

Build separate learning paths for the people who configure your product and the people who use it every day, because their jobs and patience differ. Admins need depth and certification, while end users need short, task-based lessons they can finish in minutes.

A starting curriculum for a B2B SaaS product might look like this:

  • Admin path: account setup and permissions; integrations and data import; configuration for the main use case; reporting; troubleshooting and support escalation; an admin certification exam.
  • End-user path: a short orientation; the three tasks users do most; tips that save time; what's new after each major release.
  • Partner or consultant path: everything in the admin path plus implementation methodology and a practical assessment.

Tie each path to a lifecycle moment. The admin path belongs inside your onboarding journey, end-user lessons can be surfaced through in-app prompts, and release courses go out with product announcements. Send completions to the CS platform your account team works in so CSMs can see which accounts have untrained admins.

Check SSO and localization before you commit: if customers sign in through your product, learners shouldn't need a separate password, and international accounts will expect courses in their language.

Does customer education really reduce support tickets and churn?

It can, but treat reduced tickets and better retention as hypotheses to measure, not outcomes to promise. Trained customers may differ from untrained ones simply because engaged accounts both train more and churn less, so compare like-for-like accounts before and after training.

What mistakes do teams make when launching an academy?

The commonest mistake is launching a big catalog with no owner to update it, so courses go stale after the first redesign. The second is measuring course completions instead of customer behavior.

  • Recording long webinars as courses. Break content into short lessons tied to tasks.
  • No integration plan. If completions never reach the CRM, the academy can't inform renewals.
  • Ignoring partners. Partners often need certification more than customers do.
  • Overlooking events. Live training can draw on community platforms built for B2B brands rather than another tool.

What else do buyers ask about customer education platforms?

Remaining questions usually cover pricing, overlap with internal LMSs and when to charge for training. Short answers follow.

Can we use our employee LMS for customer education?

Sometimes, if it supports external users, branding and separate portals. Many internal systems are built around HR records and compliance, which makes external sign-up, SSO from your product and commerce awkward.

Should we charge for customer training?

Charge for advanced certifications or services-style training if customers see clear career or business value, and keep onboarding courses free. Charging for basic onboarding tends to slow adoption.

How do customer education platforms price?

Pricing models vary by vendor and may depend on learner counts, active users or modules, so check each official pricing page. Ask which learners count toward the limit and what happens when you exceed it.

What should we measure first?

Start with enrollment and completion by account, then link completions to product usage and ticket volume. Treat any link to churn as a hypothesis until you've compared similar accounts.

What's the verdict?

Choose Skilljar if education must connect closely to customer success, Docebo or Intellum for enterprise scale, Thought Industries if training is a revenue line, LearnUpon or WorkRamp for mid-sized programs, and Thinkific to prove demand cheaply. Plan the admin and end-user paths before you compare authoring tools.
