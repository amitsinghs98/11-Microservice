HubSpot Marketing Hub vs ActiveCampaign: Automation and CRM Fit

For the person who owns email, HubSpot Marketing Hub vs ActiveCampaign is a choice between a marketing tool that sits on a shared CRM and an automation-first email platform with a lighter CRM attached. HubSpot wins when sales already lives in HubSpot; ActiveCampaign wins when deep automation per dollar matters more than one shared record.

Should an email marketer choose HubSpot Marketing Hub or ActiveCampaign?

Choose HubSpot Marketing Hub if your sales team already works in HubSpot and you need marketing and sales to read the same contact record. Choose ActiveCampaign if email and automation are the main job, your list is large relative to your budget, and sales can live with a lighter deals pipeline or a separate CRM.

  • Small B2B team where marketing feeds three to five reps: HubSpot is the safer fit, because forms, scoring, lifecycle stages and deals share one database without a sync.
  • Marketing-led SMB with a big newsletter and few salespeople: ActiveCampaign usually stretches further, because its automation builder is central to every plan and pricing is driven by contacts rather than marketing seats.
  • Team that just needs campaigns and a welcome series: neither may be necessary. Our email marketing software picks for small businesses include simpler tools.

How do the email and automation builders differ?

ActiveCampaign's automation builder is the product's center of gravity, so branching, waits, goals and conditional paths are part of everyday use from the entry tier. HubSpot's workflows can be as capable, but the multi-step versions an email marketer actually wants have generally sat in Professional, which changes the budget conversation.

In ActiveCampaign, a typical nurture runs in one canvas: a form submission starts the automation, tags and custom fields record behavior, and goals pull contacts out when they convert. Marketers who think in triggers and branches tend to find this model direct. The trade-off is governance. Tags multiply quickly, and without a naming convention a two-year-old account becomes hard to audit.

In HubSpot, the workflow tool works on CRM objects, so a single workflow can update a contact's lifecycle stage, create a task for the owner and enroll a company in a sequence of actions. That's powerful for B2B, where the account matters as much as the person. It also means the email marketer is editing shared CRM data, so permissions and property hygiene need an owner.

Both offer drag-and-drop email editors, templates, personalization tokens and A/B testing of some form. The real differentiator is not the editor but whether automation sits inside your price point. Our lifecycle email automation software guide compares other builders if neither feels right.

How do forms and lead scoring compare?

Both include native forms that write straight to the contact record, so you don't need a separate form tool for standard lead capture. Lead scoring exists in both, but on each platform it sits above the entry tier, so check the plan before you design a scoring model.

HubSpot's forms are part of the free tools and feed directly into CRM properties, lists and workflows. Because the data lands in the same database sales uses, a form fill can update a lifecycle stage and notify an owner without any integration. ActiveCampaign's forms add contacts to lists, apply tags and start automations, which suits newsletter and content-led capture well.

For scoring, the question is less "does it exist?" than "who acts on the score?" In HubSpot, a score can drive lifecycle stage and owner assignment in the same system reps work in. In ActiveCampaign, scoring is strong for segmenting and triggering marketing automations, but if your reps live in Salesforce or Pipedrive, the score has to sync across before it changes their day. If your forms need conditional logic, multi-step qualification or routing to a calendar, both platforms' native forms may feel basic. A specialist from our B2B form builders roundup can sit in front of either.

What does each really cost for a small B2B team and a marketing-led SMB?

HubSpot usually costs more once you need Professional-level workflows and scoring, because the edition price, seats and any required onboarding stack together. ActiveCampaign's cost grows mainly with contact count, so a large but low-value list hurts it more than seat count does.

Both vendors change list prices and promotions often, so this section shows the cost lines to put into each vendor's own calculator rather than quoting figures that would date quickly. The two scenarios are hypothetical.

Scenario A: small B2B team

Assumptions: two marketers, four sales reps, 8,000 contacts of which 5,000 receive marketing email, a nurture with branching, lead scoring and automatic owner assignment.

  • HubSpot cost lines: the Marketing Hub edition that includes workflows and lead scoring; marketing seats for two users; the marketing-contact tier for 5,000 contacts; any one-time onboarding the edition requires; and Sales Hub seats if reps need sequences or forecasting beyond the free CRM.
  • ActiveCampaign cost lines: the tier that includes lead scoring and the users you need; the 8,000-contact band; and, if reps work in another CRM, the integration and any sync tool between them.

HubSpot's advantage is that only contacts you mark as marketing contacts count toward the marketing tier, so the 3,000 sales-only contacts don't add to the email bill. HubSpot explains the distinction on its marketing contacts page.

Scenario B: marketing-led SMB

Assumptions: one marketer, an owner who closes deals, 20,000 newsletter subscribers, weekly campaigns, a welcome series and a few behavior-triggered automations.

  • HubSpot cost lines: a 20,000 marketing-contact tier is the dominant line, and the Starter-to-Professional jump arrives as soon as you want branching automations.
  • ActiveCampaign cost lines: the 20,000-contact band on the lowest tier with the automations you need; extra users are rarely needed.

In our editorial assessment, scenario B favors ActiveCampaign on most configurations, and scenario A narrows or reverses once you count the integration work ActiveCampaign needs. Our marketing automation pricing guide explains how contacts, seats and tasks interact across vendors.

How does the handoff from marketing to sales work in each?

In HubSpot, the handoff is a change of lifecycle stage and owner on a record sales already sees, so there's nothing to sync. In ActiveCampaign, the handoff is clean if sales uses its deals pipeline, and an integration project if sales uses another CRM.

Whichever you pick, define the handoff in writing before you build it: the score or behavior that makes a lead sales-ready, who owns it, how fast they must respond and what happens if they don't. Then build the alert, the task and the recycling path back to nurture. Teams that skip the recycling step end up with leads that sales rejected sitting in no automation at all.

Where do HubSpot Marketing Hub and ActiveCampaign fall short for email teams?

HubSpot's main weakness for an email-first buyer is cost concentration: the features that justify it cluster in Professional, so a small team can pay for far more platform than it uses. ActiveCampaign's main weakness is the sales side, where a B2B team with real pipeline reporting needs often ends up maintaining a second system and a sync.

How hard is switching between them?

Moving contacts and templates is straightforward; moving automation logic is not, because HubSpot workflows act on CRM objects while ActiveCampaign automations lean on tags and lists. Budget time to redesign the logic rather than copy it.

  1. Export contacts with consent status, unsubscribes and bounce history, and import suppression data first.
  2. Map HubSpot properties to ActiveCampaign custom fields and tags, or the reverse, and remove fields nobody uses.
  3. Rebuild the top three automations, then retire the rest unless they earn their place.
  4. Recreate forms and replace embed codes on every page, including old landing pages.
  5. Warm sending gradually from the new platform and watch deliverability for the first month.

What else do email marketers ask about HubSpot Marketing Hub vs ActiveCampaign?

Most follow-up questions are about contact counting, free plans and whether one tool can replace the other's CRM. Short answers follow.

Does HubSpot charge for every contact in the CRM?

No. Marketing Hub pricing is based on marketing contacts, the contacts you choose to market to. Contacts you don't mark as marketing contacts don't count toward that tier.

Is ActiveCampaign's CRM enough for a B2B sales team?

For a small team with a simple pipeline, often yes. Teams that need forecasting, complex permissions or multiple pipelines with detailed reporting usually pair ActiveCampaign with a dedicated CRM.

Which is easier for a single marketer to run?

ActiveCampaign is usually quicker for one person building email automations. HubSpot asks for more setup but pays it back when several teams share the data.

Can I start on HubSpot's free tools and upgrade later?

Yes. HubSpot's free tools include forms and basic email, and contacts stay in the same CRM when you upgrade, which makes later migration within HubSpot simple.
