Which link-in-bio or campaign page tool should you choose?

Pick a link-in-bio tool when the page is a menu for an audience you already have, and pick a landing-page builder when the page has one job, such as capturing a lead or selling one offer, and you need to measure it. Brand teams running creator campaigns usually need both: creators keep their own bio links, and the brand sends traffic to a page it controls.

  • Creators who want the most familiar option: Linktree.
  • Creators selling products, services or brand deals from one hub: Beacons.
  • Creators selling digital products and coaching: Stan.
  • Teams already planning Instagram and TikTok in one scheduler: Later's link-in-bio feature.
  • Marketers who need short links and a simple page in one account: Bitly.
  • Cheap one-page sites on your own domain: Carrd.
  • Brand-run campaign pages with testing: Unbounce or Leadpages.

What's the difference between a link-in-bio page and a landing page?

A link-in-bio page lists several destinations for an audience arriving from a social profile; a landing page asks one audience to take one action. The first optimises for choice and speed of editing, the second for conversion and measurement.

That difference shows up in features. Link-in-bio tools give you templates, social icons, commerce blocks and simple click analytics, edited from a phone. Landing-page builders give you layout control, forms, A/B testing, custom domains and tracking scripts. What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Linktree, Beacons and Stan are the strongest picks for a creator's own audience, while Unbounce and Leadpages are the right picks when a brand pays for the traffic. Later, Bitly and Carrd fill gaps between the two.

Linktree: best for creators who want a familiar link hub

Linktree is a widely used link-in-bio tool with commerce and email-collection blocks and click analytics. Strengths: the format is familiar to social audiences, and editing is fast. Limitations: design control is limited, and you should check which plan includes deeper analytics and tracking options. Skip it if the page is a paid-traffic campaign that needs testing. See linktr.ee.

Beacons: best for creators selling and pitching brands

Beacons combines a link-in-bio page with a creator store, email tools and a media kit for brand deals. Strengths: one place for selling and brand outreach. Limitations: it's built for the creator, so a brand gets little control over the page. Skip it if your brand team needs to own the destination. See beacons.ai.

Stan: best for digital products and coaching

Stan is a creator storefront for selling digital downloads, courses, memberships and booked calls from a bio link. Strengths: checkout built for creators. Limitations: narrow scope; check the official site for current plans. Skip it if you only need a list of links. See stan.store.

Later: best for teams already scheduling posts

Later's link-in-bio feature turns an Instagram or TikTok grid into a clickable page tied to scheduled posts. Strengths: no extra tool if you already plan content in Later. Limitations: it's most useful inside Later's workflow. Skip it if you don't use Later for scheduling. See later.com.

Bitly: best for short links plus a simple page

Bitly is primarily a link shortener and QR code tool that also offers simple link-in-bio pages. Strengths: one account for every short link and its click data, which helps with campaign governance. Limitations: the page builder is basic. Skip it if the page itself must persuade. See bitly.com.

Carrd: best for simple one-page sites

Carrd builds simple, responsive one-page sites, with forms and custom-domain support; check which plan includes them. Strengths: more layout freedom than a bio tool. Limitations: no built-in A/B testing or deep analytics. Skip it if you need to compare page variants. See carrd.co.

Unbounce: best for brand campaign pages with testing

Unbounce is a landing-page builder with A/B testing, forms, custom domains and tracking-script support for paid campaigns. Strengths: the measurement controls a brand needs when it pays creators. Limitations: it takes more setup than a bio page. Skip it if the page is for a creator's own audience. See unbounce.com.

Leadpages: best for small teams capturing leads

Leadpages offers landing pages, pop-ups and forms aimed at small businesses. Strengths: straightforward lead capture. Limitations: fewer advanced controls than enterprise builders. Skip it if you need complex personalisation. See leadpages.com.

How do you measure traffic from a link-in-bio page?

Tag every outbound link with UTM parameters and read results in your own analytics, not just in the bio tool's click counter. Click counts show interest; only your site analytics or store data show what that interest became.

  • Use one naming convention for source, medium and campaign across every creator. Our list of UTM governance tools helps teams enforce it.
  • Give each creator a distinct campaign or content value, so you can compare them.
  • Check whether pixels are allowed on the plan you choose before you promise retargeting audiences to anyone.
  • Pair links with codes where viewers may not click, and decide which wins if both appear.
  • Test commission tracking through the page if creators are paid per sale; the tools in our affiliate software guide for SaaS brands should credit a click that passes through a bio link.

Is a link-in-bio page enough for a brand-run creator campaign?

Usually not: the creator owns the page, the brand can't test it, and measurement depends on what the creator's plan includes. Based on the product information reviewed, link-in-bio tools work best as the bridge from a profile to a brand-owned page, not as the campaign page itself.

Should creators and brands pick different tools?

Yes. Creators should optimise for ease, selling and their own audience relationship, while brands should optimise for control, testing and data ownership.

For creators: choose Linktree for simplicity, Beacons if you also pitch brands, or Stan if you sell your own products. Put email capture on the page, because your follower count belongs to the platform but your list belongs to you. For brands: build campaign pages in Unbounce, Leadpages or your own CMS, and give each creator a tagged link and, where relevant, a unique code. If you manage creators through an influencer marketing platform, check whether it already generates tracked links before adding another tool.

What mistakes do teams make with bio links and campaign pages?

The usual mistake is treating clicks on a bio page as campaign results. The other is changing links mid-campaign without updating tags, which breaks reporting.

  • Too many links, which dilutes the one action you care about.
  • No custom domain on brand pages, which weakens trust and makes links fragile.
  • Untagged links, so traffic lands in your analytics as direct or referral.
  • Forgetting attribution limits: many purchases happen later on another device. Our guide to measuring influencer campaign ROI covers how to combine signals.

What else do people ask about link-in-bio tools?

Most questions are about free plans, domains and tracking. Short answers follow.

Is a free link-in-bio plan enough?

For a personal creator page, often yes. Custom domains, pixels and deeper analytics may be limited to paid tiers, so check the plan before you rely on them.

Can I use my own domain for a link-in-bio page?

Several tools support custom domains; check which plan includes them. A brand-owned domain also makes links easier to keep if you switch tools.

Do link-in-bio tools support UTM parameters?

You can add UTM parameters to any destination URL by hand, and some tools may add them for you. Always confirm the tags arrive in your analytics.

Should brands ask creators to use a brand landing page?

For paid campaigns, yes. The creator's bio link can point to it, and the brand keeps control of the offer, the form and the tracking.

What's the verdict?

Creators get the most from Linktree, Beacons or Stan, depending on whether they need simplicity, brand deals or a storefront. Brands running paid creator campaigns should send traffic to an Unbounce, Leadpages or CMS page they control, with Bitly or Carrd as lighter options.
