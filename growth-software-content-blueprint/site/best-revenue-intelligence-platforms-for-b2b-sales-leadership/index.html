Which revenue intelligence platform should B2B sales leadership choose?

Choose by the signal you trust least today: if you can't see what happens on calls, start with Gong; if the forecast call is the pain, start with Clari inside Salesloft; if reps don't log activity, look at Backstory (formerly People.ai). Teams already standardized on Outreach should evaluate its built-in intelligence before adding a second platform.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • Conversation-led deal inspection at scale: Gong.
  • Forecast-led leadership with engagement in the same suite: Clari and Salesloft.
  • Outreach customers: Outreach's deal and forecast intelligence.
  • Automatic activity capture into the CRM: Backstory.
  • A broad platform with AI forecasting and conversation capture: Aviso.
  • Mid-sized teams that want revenue intelligence as an add-on to meeting capture: Avoma.

What does revenue intelligence cover, and where does it overlap with other tools?

Revenue intelligence combines four signals: pipeline data from the CRM, activity data from email and calendars, conversation data from calls, and forecast submissions from managers. The category exists because each signal alone misleads; a deal can look healthy in the CRM while the buyer has gone quiet on every channel.

The overlap is real. Conversation intelligence software covers the call layer and coaching; sales engagement platforms run sequences and capture activity; sales forecasting software owns the rollup. Most vendors here started in one of those and expanded, so ask which layer is native and which was bolted on.

For leadership, the useful output is a short list of deals at risk with the evidence attached, not another dashboard. Those signals are only as reliable as the account records underneath, which is why some teams unify data first with a customer data platform suited to B2B.

What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

The order follows our criteria for B2B sales leadership: breadth of native signals, then the quality of deal inspection and explanation, then the weight of the contract and rollout. Payment doesn't affect inclusion or order.

Gong: conversation-led deal inspection

Gong records and analyzes calls, emails and meetings, then rolls them into deal and account views with risk warnings. Its pricing page describes per-user licenses plus a platform fee that scales with users, and its plans and seats documentation explains how core and add-on products are licensed. Some automated agent work draws on Gong Credits included with seats.

Pricing model: quote-based per-user licenses, a platform fee and credits for some AI features.

Strengths: Conversation data is the core of the product, which makes deal inspection evidence-based.

Limitations: Platform fee plus seats makes it expensive for small teams, and credits add a usage variable to model.

Skip it if: you have fewer than a handful of quota-carrying reps or don't hold recorded calls.

Clari and Salesloft: forecast-led leadership with engagement in one suite

Clari and Salesloft have combined into one company operating as Salesloft, with Clari Forecast keeping its name. The pairing puts forecast rollups, pipeline inspection and sales engagement in one vendor relationship.

Pricing model: quote-based; ask how packaging and contracts have changed since the merger.

Strengths: Strong forecast workflow, now alongside cadences and conversation capture in the same suite.

Limitations: Post-merger packaging is still settling, so roadmap and integration claims need to be confirmed in writing.

Skip it if: you're happy with your current engagement platform and only want conversation insight.

Outreach: intelligence for teams already running Outreach

Outreach has added AI deal and conversation intelligence to its engagement platform, including call analysis, deal-health signals and forecast inspection. Its support glossary notes subscriptions are billed monthly against an annual term, with pricing set by the products in the contract.

Pricing model: annual contract priced by bundled products.

Strengths: Engagement data is native, so activity gaps show up without another integration.

Limitations: Little reason to adopt for intelligence alone, and conversation depth is worth comparing with Gong.

Skip it if: your reps don't run sequences in Outreach.

Backstory (formerly People.ai): automatic activity capture

People.ai now operates as Backstory. It captures email, meeting and call activity and matches it to CRM opportunities without rep logging, and its help center introduces the platform under the new name. Its pages position pricing around usage rather than every seat.

Pricing model: quote-based, with usage-oriented positioning.

Strengths: Addresses a familiar revenue-intelligence weakness: missing activity data in the CRM.

Limitations: Captured activity needs governance (which contacts, which opportunities) before leaders trust it.

Skip it if: your CRM activity data is already complete because another tool captures it.

Aviso: broad AI revenue platform

Aviso combines AI forecasting, deal intelligence, conversation capture and agents. Its own pages give differing pricing signals, from per-seat figures to custom bundles and AI usage charges.

Pricing model: mixed per-seat and custom pricing; confirm every component in a quote.

Strengths: Broad scope that can consolidate several point tools under one contract.

Limitations: Inconsistent public pricing and a wide surface area that takes time to adopt.

Skip it if: you need one capability done deeply rather than many done adequately.

Avoma: revenue intelligence as an add-on to meeting capture

Avoma sells meeting recording and notes as base plans per recorder seat, with revenue intelligence (deal risk, forecasting and win-loss) as a published per-seat add-on.

Pricing model: published per-seat base plan plus per-seat add-ons; viewers free.

Strengths: Transparent pricing and a gentle entry point for mid-sized teams.

Limitations: Forecasting and pipeline depth are lighter than the forecast-first platforms.

Skip it if: you need multi-layer forecast rollups across several sales managers.

Why do revenue intelligence rollouts stall after the first quarter?

The usual failure point is adoption, not features: managers keep running the forecast call from the CRM, reps ignore risk alerts, and the platform becomes a call library. Plan the change in the meeting rhythm before you sign the contract.

What does implementation and adoption really involve?

Expect to connect CRM, email, calendar and calling systems, agree which users and opportunities are captured, and rewrite the weekly pipeline review around the new deal views. The technical setup is usually faster than the behavioral change.

  1. Data scope: decide which mailboxes, calendars and call sources are captured, and exclude personal or sensitive categories.
  2. CRM mapping: agree how activity is matched to accounts and opportunities, and who fixes mismatches.
  3. Explainability check: for every risk score, confirm a manager can see why it fired.
  4. Meeting redesign: run one pipeline review per week from the platform, not the CRM report.
  5. Adoption metrics: track manager logins on review day and deals inspected, not just licenses assigned.

What mistakes do sales leaders make with revenue intelligence?

The biggest mistake is buying the platform with the most features instead of the one that fixes your weakest signal. The second is paying for overlap with tools you already own.

  • Duplicate capture: two tools recording the same calls doubles cost and confuses the source of truth.
  • Opaque scores: a risk score without visible reasons won't change a manager's call.
  • Ignoring usage components: ask how credits, platform fees and seat types change at renewal.
  • Skipping consent and policy work: recording and capture rules need legal and HR input in each region you sell into.
  • No owner: someone in RevOps must own configuration after go-live.

What else do buyers ask about revenue intelligence platforms?

Leaders mostly ask how this category differs from tools they already own and how long adoption takes. Short answers follow.

What is the difference between revenue intelligence and conversation intelligence?

Conversation intelligence analyzes calls and meetings. Revenue intelligence combines that with CRM pipeline, email and calendar activity and forecasts to judge deal and pipeline health.

Do we need revenue intelligence if we already have a CRM?

Not always. If your CRM holds complete activity data and managers inspect deals weekly, native reports may be enough; the platform adds value when activity and conversation signals are missing.

How long does a revenue intelligence rollout take?

Technical connection is usually the quick part. Plan a full quarter for managers to change how they run pipeline reviews.

Are revenue intelligence accuracy claims reliable?

Treat them as vendor claims. Ask for the method and run your own comparison against manager calls over a full quarter.

Who should own a revenue intelligence platform?

Revenue operations should own configuration and data rules, with sales leadership owning how it's used in pipeline reviews.

What's the verdict?

Pick the platform whose native signal fixes your biggest blind spot: Gong for conversations, Clari with Salesloft for the forecast call, Backstory for missing activity, Outreach if you already run it. Aviso and Avoma are worth a look when budget or consolidation matters more than depth in one layer.
