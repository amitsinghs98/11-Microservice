Which should an agency choose?

Choose SE Ranking if your margin depends on tracking many client keywords and sending branded reports cheaply. Choose Semrush if your team sells research-heavy work, such as competitive analysis, paid search or AI visibility, and needs those tools in the same account as tracking.

  • Freelancers and agencies with up to 10 clients: SE Ranking Core, which fits 10 projects in one plan.
  • Agencies with 10 to 30 clients and a small team: SE Ranking Growth, unless research depth justifies Semrush One Pro+.
  • Agencies selling competitive research, PPC or AI visibility: Semrush One, with reports and users budgeted per client and per person.
  • Local-heavy agencies: compare the local options below, then consider a local specialist.

How are SE Ranking and Semrush positioned?

SE Ranking positions itself as a capacity-rich SEO platform with an Agency Pack add-on for white label and client access. Semrush positions itself as a broad visibility platform, sold as Semrush One (SEO plus AI visibility), standalone toolkits and per-item add-ons.

Semrush's knowledge base explains that Semrush One bundles the SEO Toolkit with the AI Visibility Toolkit, while the SEO Toolkit is still sold on its own plan ladder (checked 5 November 2026). This page compares Semrush One, the ladder on Semrush's main SEO pricing page, so don't mix in standalone-toolkit prices from older reviews.

What does a 5-client agency pay?

On our hypothetical 5-client workload, SE Ranking Core with the Agency Pack costs about $172 a month against about $265 for Semrush One Starter with white-label reports, billed annually. Starter leaves no headroom for a sixth client.

Hypothetical assumptions: five client sites, 100 tracked keywords each (500 in total), one person logging in, one white-labelled monthly report per client, annual billing because SE Ranking's Agency Pack requires it, one login included with each base plan, tax excluded. Our arithmetic, from listed prices.

  • SE Ranking: Core at $103.20 a month billed annually plus the $69 Agency Pack, about $172.20 a month, with 5 projects and 1,500 keywords of headroom.
  • Semrush: Starter at $165.17 a month billed annually plus five Pro Reports at $20, about $265.17 a month, at exactly its 5-website and 500-keyword cap.

Without white-label reports and on monthly billing, it's $129 for SE Ranking Core against $199 for Semrush One Starter. A second person adds $45 a month on Semrush; SE Ranking's extra-seat price varies across its pages, so confirm it or compare Growth, which includes three seats.

What does a 15-client agency pay?

At 15 clients, SE Ranking Growth with the Agency Pack costs about $292 a month against about $708 for Semrush One Pro+ with two extra users and 15 white-label reports, on annual billing. Most of the gap is Semrush's per-report and per-user pricing.

Hypothetical assumptions: 15 client sites, 100 tracked keywords each (1,500 in total), three team members, one white-labelled monthly report per client, annual billing, one login included with each base plan, tax excluded.

  • SE Ranking: Growth at $223.20 a month billed annually, with three seats, 30 projects and 5,000 keywords included, plus the $69 Agency Pack. About $292.20 a month.
  • Semrush: Pro+ at $248.17 a month billed annually (15 websites, 1,500 keywords), plus two users at $80 and 15 Pro Reports at $20. About $708.17 a month, or $408.17 without the reports.

Semrush Pro+ again sits at its cap, so client 16 means the next tier, whose price differs across Semrush's own pages. These totals are illustrations, not quotes. Our SEO software pricing guide for 5, 10 and 20 clients adds other suites to the same model.

What is included and what becomes an add-on?

SE Ranking includes more tracking capacity and seats in the base plan, and sells white label and AI search tracking as add-ons. Semrush One includes AI visibility prompts but sells extra users, white-label reports, local tools and agency lead generation separately.

  • SE Ranking Agency Pack: its FAQ describes a white-label interface on your own domain, reports sent from your own SMTP server and view-only client seats (checked 5 November 2026). SE Ranking pages give different client-seat counts, so confirm the number before you promise client logins.
  • SE Ranking AI Search add-on: its help center lists 200 checks a month for $89 on monthly billing, where one check is one prompt on one AI engine (checked 5 November 2026).
  • Semrush AI visibility: its knowledge base lists 50 prompts tracked daily on Starter and 100 on Pro+ (checked 5 November 2026).
  • Semrush reports: a Base Report at $10 a month and the white-label Pro Report at $20 a month, each purchased individually (checked 5 November 2026).
  • Semrush agency lead generation: the pricing page lists Lead Generation at $90 a month as a separate add-on (checked 5 November 2026).

If you sell AI visibility, Semrush's bundled prompts narrow the cost gap; if you don't, you're paying for capacity you won't use.

How do local SEO features compare?

SE Ranking includes a small local allowance in its core plans, while Semrush sells local tools per location. With many single-location clients, that can outweigh the core plan price.

SE Ranking's help center says Core, Growth and Enterprise each include three Local Marketing locations, with more available to buy (checked 5 November 2026); the extra-location price differs between SE Ranking pages, so check the subscription page. Semrush lists Local Base at $30 and Local Pro at $60 per location per month, billed annually, with listing management only on Pro (checked 5 November 2026).

Which produces better client-facing reports?

On paper, SE Ranking offers more white-label control for a flat fee, including a custom domain and sending reports from your own mail server, while Semrush brands reports one at a time. Whether clients find either report clearer, plan pages can't say.

How do onboarding and trials compare?

SE Ranking gives a longer trial, which helps when you want to load several client projects before deciding. Semrush offers a shorter trial plus a limited free account, so plan your evaluation before you start the clock.

SE Ranking states that every plan starts with a 14-day free trial, while Semrush's knowledge base lists a 7-day free trial on its SEO and AI search plans (checked 5 November 2026). SE Ranking's trial has lower limits than its paid plans, so treat it as a workflow test: import two real client projects, set up tracking and schedule one report. Where does each fall short for agencies?

SE Ranking's main constraints are that white label is tied to annual billing and that its keyword and link databases differ from Semrush's, so test data in your niches. Semrush's main constraint is plan caps that sit exactly at common client counts, plus add-ons that scale with every new client and person.

  • SE Ranking: AI search tracking and extra local locations cost extra, and its extra-seat price isn't consistent across its own pages.
  • Semrush: Pro+ users cost more than Starter users, and the tier above Pro+ has no consistent public price.

What else do agencies ask about SE Ranking and Semrush?

Questions usually cover data quality, billing terms, plan names and running both tools. Short answers follow.

Is SE Ranking's data as good as Semrush's?

Each vendor runs its own databases and models, so results differ by niche and market. Compare both on clients whose real performance you know from Search Console before deciding.

Can I get white-label reports from SE Ranking on monthly billing?

Not through the Agency Pack. SE Ranking lists it as an add-on for annual billing only, so a monthly subscriber gets standard reports without full white label.

Is the Semrush SEO Toolkit the same as Semrush One?

No. Semrush One bundles the SEO Toolkit with the AI Visibility Toolkit, while the standalone SEO Toolkit is sold on its own plan ladder, so check which one a quote or review refers to.

Should an agency run both tools?

Some do, using SE Ranking for tracking and reporting and Semrush for research. That only pays off if research is billable work every month.

How often should we recheck these prices?

Every 60 to 90 days, and before any renewal. Both vendors have changed packaging recently, so older reviews may describe plans that no longer exist.

Which fits your agency size?

For agencies up to about 30 clients that mainly sell tracking, audits and reporting, SE Ranking fits better on cost and headroom. For agencies that sell competitive research, paid search or AI visibility, Semrush's breadth can justify paying more, provided you budget reports and users per client.
