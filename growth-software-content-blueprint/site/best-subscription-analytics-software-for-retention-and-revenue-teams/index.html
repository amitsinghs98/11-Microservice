Which subscription analytics software should you choose?

If you bill through one platform and your plans are simple, start with its built-in analytics and add a dedicated tool only when you need cohorts, CRM segmentation or several revenue sources in one view. Choose ChartMogul or Baremetrics for dedicated SaaS metrics, and a finance-oriented platform such as Maxio when finance needs metrics that reconcile with recognised revenue.

  • Dedicated metrics across one or more billing systems: ChartMogul.
  • Smaller SaaS teams that also want to work on failed-payment recovery: Baremetrics.
  • A low-commitment baseline: ProfitWell Metrics from Paddle.
  • Stripe-only billing with simple plans: Stripe Billing's built-in analytics.
  • Billing and analytics from one vendor: Chargebee or Recurly.
  • B2B finance teams with contracts and revenue schedules: Maxio.

How is subscription analytics different from billing infrastructure?

Billing infrastructure creates subscriptions, invoices and payments; subscription analytics reads those events and turns them into consistent recurring-revenue metrics. One moves money, the other explains what the money is doing.

The distinction matters when you buy. A billing platform's reports describe its own data. A dedicated analytics tool can combine several billing sources, normalise annual and monthly plans into MRR, and segment by fields from your CRM. If you're still choosing how to bill, see our guide to subscription commerce software for the infrastructure side.

Retention teams also need the "why" behind the numbers. Subscription analytics shows where churn and contraction happen; churn prediction software tries to show which accounts are next.

What do the core subscription metrics mean?

Agree on these definitions before you compare tools, because each vendor documents its own calculation rules. The terms below describe the common meaning; confirm the exact rules in each product's documentation.

MRR (monthly recurring revenue)
Normalised monthly value of active subscriptions, usually excluding one-off charges and taxes.
ARR (annual recurring revenue)
Recurring revenue expressed annually, commonly MRR multiplied by twelve.
Expansion and contraction
MRR gained or lost from existing customers through upgrades, seats or downgrades.
Logo churn and revenue churn
The share of customers lost, and the share of recurring revenue lost, in a period.
Involuntary churn
Cancellations caused by failed payments rather than a decision to leave.
Gross and net revenue retention
Revenue kept from a starting cohort, excluding (gross) or including (net) expansion.
Reactivation
A churned customer returning to a paid subscription.

What does each subscription analytics tool do well, and where does it fall short?

The dedicated tools are best at consistent metrics across sources, and the billing platforms are best at zero-setup reporting on their own data. Maxio sits between them, serving finance as much as retention teams.

ChartMogul: best for dedicated SaaS metrics

ChartMogul is a dedicated subscription analytics product that calculates recurring-revenue metrics such as MRR, churn and retention from your billing data. It's a candidate for teams that bill through more than one system or want metrics independent of their billing vendor; confirm supported sources and how tiers are set on its pricing page. Skip it if your billing data is messy and nobody will fix plan mappings.

Baremetrics: best for smaller teams that want recovery too

Baremetrics offers subscription metrics dashboards and markets tools for retention and failed-payment recovery. Confirm which recovery and cancellation-insight features are available, and on which plans, on its pricing page. Skip it if your billing system isn't among its supported connectors.

ProfitWell Metrics from Paddle: best for a baseline

ProfitWell Metrics is Paddle's subscription metrics product. It's worth a look for teams that want standard metrics before committing to a larger analytics tool; check current pricing and supported billing sources on Paddle's page. Skip it if you need deep custom segmentation, CRM fields or finance-grade reconciliation.

Stripe Billing analytics: best for Stripe-only businesses

Stripe Billing is Stripe's subscription billing product, and its reporting describes the subscription data held in Stripe. For a single-product business with straightforward plans, that may be all you need. Skip it if you also bill through app stores, invoices or another processor; check whether its reports can include that revenue.

Chargebee: best for billing and analytics in one vendor

Chargebee is a subscription billing platform with reporting built on its own billing data. Keeping both in one system removes a sync and a reconciliation step. Check which reporting and analytics features each plan includes on the Chargebee pricing page. Skip it if you're happy with your billing platform and only want better metrics.

Recurly: best for brands already on Recurly billing

Recurly is a subscription billing platform with reporting on its own subscriber data. It suits teams that want retention reporting close to payment data; confirm which analytics and recovery features your plan includes. Skip it if you need analytics across multiple billing systems.

Maxio: best for B2B SaaS finance teams

Maxio positions itself as a billing and financial operations platform for B2B SaaS companies, which matters when finance needs subscription metrics to reconcile with recognised revenue. Confirm its revenue recognition and metrics scope with Maxio, and expect it to be heavier to adopt than a dashboard tool. Skip it if you only need retention charts for the CS team.

Why do subscription metrics disagree between tools?

Different tools handle discounts, trials, refunds, pauses, annual plans and currency in different ways, so the same billing data can produce different MRR and churn figures. Vendor documentation explains each tool's rules, but reconciling them in practice is where trust is won or lost.

How should finance and customer success share subscription data?

Finance should own metric definitions and reconciliation, and customer success should own the account-level response to churn and contraction. Write the definitions down once and make every dashboard reference them.

  • Monthly: finance closes the period and publishes reconciled MRR, churn and retention.
  • Weekly: CS reviews accounts with contraction, failed payments or downgrade requests.
  • Shared fields: account ID, plan, renewal date and owner match across billing, CRM and analytics.
  • Escalation path: a metric that moves sharply gets checked for data errors before anyone presents it.

Which subscription analytics setup fits your stage?

Early teams should start with what their billing platform or a free tool provides, and move to a dedicated tool when segmentation or multiple sources demand it. Later-stage B2B teams usually need finance-grade reconciliation, which favours a platform that finance will accept.

  • Early-stage, one processor, simple plans: billing reports or ProfitWell Metrics.
  • Several revenue sources or CRM segmentation: ChartMogul or Baremetrics.
  • Finance-led B2B with contracts: Maxio, or your billing platform plus a warehouse.
  • CS teams acting on retention signals: feed metrics into your customer success platform.

What else do buyers ask about subscription analytics software?

Most questions are about where numbers come from and why tools disagree. The answers below assume you'll confirm connector and plan details on each vendor's site.

Why does our MRR differ between the billing system and the analytics tool?

Tools treat discounts, taxes, trials, one-off charges, annual plans and currency conversion differently. Document the rules each system uses, then pick one as the source for reporting and reconcile the other to it.

Do we need subscription analytics if our billing platform has reports?

Not always. Billing reports may be enough for a single billing system and simple plans; a dedicated tool earns its place when you need cohorts, segmentation by CRM fields or several revenue sources combined.

Can subscription analytics replace our accounting system?

No. Subscription metrics such as MRR are management figures, not recognised revenue, so your accounting or revenue recognition system remains the record for financial statements.

How often should subscription metrics refresh?

Daily is plenty for most teams, because retention and MRR trends move over weeks. Ask vendors about sync latency anyway, so nobody misreads a delay as a drop.

Which subscription metric should a small SaaS team watch first?

Net revenue retention by cohort is a good first choice, because it combines churn, contraction and expansion. Pair it with logo churn so a few large expansions don't hide a steady loss of customers.

What's the verdict on subscription analytics software?

Pick the tool whose metric definitions your finance team will sign off, then use it consistently. Clean, agreed numbers that both finance and CS trust are worth more than a dashboard with every chart.
