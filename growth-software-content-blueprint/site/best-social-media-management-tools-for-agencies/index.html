Best Social Media Management Software for Agencies

The best social media management tools for agencies live or die on client separation, approvals and reporting, not on how many networks they schedule to. The right pick depends on how the vendor counts seats, profiles and client workspaces once you pass a handful of accounts.

Which social media tool should your agency choose?

Choose by the job that takes most of your team's week: approvals, community management, reporting or sheer posting volume. A tool that excels at one of these is usually average at another, and the pricing unit decides how painful growth will be.

  • Large agency with heavy inbox and reporting needs: Sprout Social or Hootsuite.
  • Community management and inbox first: Agorapulse.
  • White-label client experience at small-agency scale: Sendible.
  • Many clients on a tight budget: SocialPilot.
  • Newer all-in-one built around agencies: Vista Social.
  • Approvals are the bottleneck: Planable.
  • Calendar-led planning with simple approvals: Loomly.

What does an agency need that a brand team doesn't?

An agency needs hard separation between clients, external approvers who don't consume paid seats, and reports it can send under its own name. A brand team can live without all three, which is why brand-first tools often feel expensive or clumsy in agency hands.

  • Multi-brand workspaces: each client's profiles, assets, drafts and permissions sit apart, so nothing posts to the wrong account.
  • Approvals: internal review, then client sign-off with comments and version history.
  • Scheduling at volume: bulk upload, queues and per-network previews.
  • Social inbox: comments and messages across profiles, with assignment and tagging.
  • Reporting: scheduled, branded exports per client, ideally without manual rework.
  • Client access: a view or portal clients can use without training.

Content creation is a separate question; if your bottleneck is drafting posts rather than managing them, see our guide to AI social media content tools for agencies.

What is each tool best and worst at?

The suites lead on inbox and reporting depth, the agency specialists lead on white-label and per-client economics, and the planners lead on approvals. The descriptions below are based on the product information reviewed and should be confirmed in a trial.

Sprout Social: best for large agencies that live in the inbox

Best for: agencies with dedicated community managers and clients who expect polished reports. Skip it if: you're a small team, because per-seat pricing scales with every hire.

Sprout Social combines publishing, a unified inbox, approval workflows and a strong reporting suite. The trade-off is cost: every internal user is a paid seat, so model headcount before comparing it with profile-priced tools.

Hootsuite: best for broad coverage and enterprise controls

Best for: agencies serving larger clients that need permissions, compliance controls and wide network support. Skip it if: you want transparent self-serve pricing for an agency setup.

Hootsuite covers scheduling, inbox, analytics and team approvals, with higher tiers sold through sales. Small agencies often find the pricing steep for what they use; our Hootsuite alternatives for agencies page compares the options.

Agorapulse: best for community management

Best for: agencies whose retainers are mostly about replying, moderating and reporting on engagement. Skip it if: publishing volume matters more than conversations.

Agorapulse is built around an inbox with assignment, labels and saved replies, plus a shared calendar clients can review and reporting by profile. Pricing is per user, so large teams should model seats carefully.

Sendible: best white-label experience for small agencies

Best for: small and mid-sized agencies that want a branded client experience. Skip it if: clients need enterprise-grade governance.

Sendible is designed for agencies, with client-specific dashboards, approvals and report templates, and white-label options on higher plans. Check which plan includes white-labeling and how profiles are counted before you commit.

SocialPilot: best for many clients on a budget

Best for: cost-sensitive agencies scheduling for many small clients. Skip it if: deep inbox workflows or advanced analytics are central to your retainers.

SocialPilot focuses on bulk scheduling, client management and branded reports at a lower entry cost than the suites. The trade-off is depth in community management and analytics.

Vista Social: best newer agency all-in-one

Best for: agencies wanting publishing, inbox, approvals and reports in one budget tool. Skip it if: you need a long-established vendor with a large partner ecosystem.

Vista Social positions itself as an agency-friendly platform with broad network support. It's a younger company than the suites, so check the roadmap, support terms and data export during your trial.

Planable: best when approvals are the bottleneck

Best for: agencies whose delays come from client feedback loops. Skip it if: you need a heavy social inbox or deep analytics in the same tool.

Planable makes review and sign-off the center of the product, with comments on post previews and multi-step approval workflows. Many agencies pair it with a separate analytics or inbox tool, or with dedicated creative proofing software for non-social assets.

Loomly: best for calendar-led planning

Best for: small agencies and in-house teams that plan from a calendar and need simple approvals. Skip it if: inbox volume is high.

Loomly centers on a content calendar, post ideas and approval flows, with plans tiered by accounts and users. It's easy to hand to clients, but heavy community managers will want more.

Which network and API limits affect every tool?

Every third-party tool can only do what each network's API allows, so some posting formats, inbox features and metrics are unavailable or delayed regardless of vendor. Check each vendor's network support page for the exact formats you sell to clients.

  • Account types: Instagram publishing generally requires a professional account, and LinkedIn treats personal profiles and company pages differently.
  • Formats: stories, carousels, reels, polls and collaborative posts vary in support and may need mobile reminders instead of direct publishing.
  • Inbox coverage: not every network exposes messages, mentions or ad comments to third parties.
  • Groups and communities: posting to Facebook Groups through third-party tools has been restricted by Meta, so confirm current support.
  • Reconnection: access tokens expire, and clients changing passwords or admin roles will break connections.

Build these limits into your statement of work, so clients know which formats are scheduled and which are posted by hand.

What changes between 5 and 20 client accounts?

At 5 clients the pricing unit barely matters, but at 20 it decides your margin: per-seat tools punish headcount, while profile-based tools punish clients with many channels. Model both before you sign an annual plan.

As a hypothetical model, assume each client has four profiles (for example Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok) and one external approver.

  • 5 clients: 20 profiles, about 2–3 internal users, 5 approvers and 5 monthly reports. Most mid-tier plans fit, so choose on workflow fit and onboarding speed.
  • 20 clients: 80 profiles, perhaps 6–8 internal users, 20 approvers and 20 reports. Profile caps, seat costs and whether approvers need paid seats now drive the bill, and per-client reporting time becomes a real cost.

For each shortlisted tool, ask: how are profiles counted, do client approvers need paid seats, how many workspaces are included, can reports be scheduled and branded, and in which formats can they be exported (PDF, CSV, presentation)? Will clients actually use the approval portal?

Vendors design approval flows to be simple, but adoption depends on the client, and some will still reply by email or chat. Plan for a fallback, and judge each tool on how easily a reluctant client can approve from a phone.

How should you onboard a new client into the tool?

Onboard in a fixed order: access first, then structure, then workflow, then reporting. A repeatable checklist prevents the wrong-account posts and broken connections that damage client trust.

  1. Confirm the client owns every account, and get admin access through the network's business tools rather than shared passwords.
  2. Create a separate workspace or brand group, and name profiles consistently.
  3. Connect profiles and test-publish a draft to each network, then delete it.
  4. Set roles: who drafts, who approves internally, who approves for the client.
  5. Load brand assets, hashtags, saved replies and posting windows.
  6. Agree response times and escalation rules for the inbox.
  7. Set up UTM conventions for links, using your campaign naming standards.
  8. Build and schedule the monthly report template, and confirm the export format with the client.
  9. Document which formats are posted manually because of network limits.

What else do agencies ask about social media tools?

Agencies usually ask about client seats, white-labeling and switching costs. The answers below depend on plan terms, so verify them with each vendor.

Do clients need a paid seat to approve posts?

It depends on the vendor and plan. Some tools let external approvers review through a link or limited role, while others count every login as a seat, so confirm this before quoting a client.

Is white-label reporting worth paying for?

It is worth it if reports are a visible part of your retainer and clients forward them internally. If clients only want a short monthly summary, a standard export with your own cover page is usually enough.

How hard is it to switch social media tools?

Scheduled posts, saved replies and historical analytics rarely migrate cleanly. Export reports and content libraries first, run both tools in parallel for a month, and reconnect profiles client by client.

Should an agency use one tool for every client?

Usually yes, because one tool keeps training, templates and reporting consistent. Make an exception only when a client's contract or compliance rules require their own platform.

What's the verdict?

Our editorial assessment: pick the tool that matches your heaviest weekly job, then check that its pricing unit still works at 20 clients. There is no single winner for every agency.

Sprout Social and Hootsuite suit larger agencies with inbox and reporting depth, Agorapulse suits community-led retainers, Sendible and SocialPilot suit cost-conscious multi-client work, Vista Social is a credible newer all-rounder, and Planable or Loomly suit approval-led teams.
