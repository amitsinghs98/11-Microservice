Which AI email copywriting tool should your team choose?

Start with the AI built into your email platform, because it writes next to your customer data and sends from the same place. Add a standalone tool only for sequence planning, variant scoring or one-to-one sales email. Our picks follow the criteria below, not payment.

  • Ecommerce campaigns and flows: Klaviyo.
  • Small-business newsletters: Mailchimp.
  • B2B lifecycle email tied to a CRM: HubSpot Marketing Hub.
  • Automation-heavy small teams: ActiveCampaign.
  • Planning whole sequences with voice rules: Claude.
  • Scoring subject-line variants before you test: Anyword.
  • Individual sales emails: Lavender.

Should you use a one-off writing assistant or your campaign platform's AI?

Use your campaign platform's AI for day-to-day sends, because it can personalize with real fields and test inside the platform. A standalone assistant is better for planning a full sequence, enforcing a detailed voice guide or writing outside the ESP.

The trade-off is control versus context. Standalone tools give finer control over tone and structure but know nothing about who opened last week. Built-in tools know your segments but offer lighter brand controls. What three tests should you run before choosing?

Run a subject-line test, a welcome email and a reactivation brief through each shortlisted tool, using your real voice guide. Together they test short copy, brand voice and sensitive, offer-driven messaging.

  1. Subject line: ask for ten subject lines and preview texts for a product launch, under your character limit, with no false urgency. Check variety and honesty.
  2. Welcome email: supply your brand story, the signup promise and one approved offer, then ask for a first welcome email. Check voice fit and whether it invents any benefit.
  3. Reactivation brief: describe a segment inactive for six months and ask for a two-email win-back sequence. Check tone, offer accuracy and a clear way to opt out.

Score each for accuracy, voice, compliance fixes needed and edit minutes. The same method works for other short copy, as set out in our AI copywriting software guide.

What does each AI email tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Each block covers the buyer it suits and who should skip it. Platform tools appear first because most teams should start there.

Klaviyo: best for ecommerce campaigns written where they're sent

Best for: ecommerce teams whose flows and campaigns already run in Klaviyo.

Klaviyo builds generative AI into its email editor for subject lines and body copy, alongside the customer data that drives segmentation and personalization.

  • Key capabilities: AI subject lines and content inside the editor, segmentation from store data, flows and A/B testing.
  • Pricing model: priced by contact count, with AI features inside the platform; the vendor's page wasn't reachable for this draft, so see Klaviyo pricing.
  • Strengths: copy, data and sending live in one place, so personalization uses real customer fields.
  • Limitations: brand voice controls are thinner than a dedicated writing platform's, and your bill grows with the list, not the copy.
  • Skip it if: you aren't selling through an online store.

Official site: Klaviyo.

Mailchimp: best for small businesses that want AI drafting built in

Best for: small businesses and newsletters that want suggestions inside a familiar campaign builder.

Mailchimp includes generative AI tools for drafting email content and subject lines within its campaign builder, tied to its templates and audience tools.

  • Key capabilities: AI content and subject-line suggestions, templates, segmentation and automated journeys.
  • Pricing model: tiered plans priced by contacts, with feature differences between tiers; see Mailchimp pricing.
  • Strengths: low friction for teams already sending from it.
  • Limitations: AI and automation features vary by plan, so the plan you need may not be the one you're on.
  • Skip it if: you need deep brand governance or complex B2B lifecycle logic.

Official site: Mailchimp.

HubSpot Marketing Hub: best for B2B emails grounded in CRM data

Best for: B2B teams whose contacts, deals and lifecycle stages already live in HubSpot.

HubSpot's Breeze AI tools draft marketing emails and subject lines inside Marketing Hub, where personalization tokens draw on CRM properties.

  • Key capabilities: AI email drafting, CRM-based personalization, workflows, and reporting tied to contacts and deals.
  • Pricing model: seat- and contact-based tiers; see HubSpot Marketing Hub pricing.
  • Strengths: email copy and the CRM record share one system, which helps lifecycle and sales alignment.
  • Limitations: marketing contact tiers can make it expensive as the database grows.
  • Skip it if: you only need an occasional newsletter.

Official site: HubSpot Marketing Hub. For the platform decision itself, see our lifecycle email automation software shortlist.

ActiveCampaign: best for automation-heavy small teams

Best for: small businesses running multi-step automations who want AI help writing each step.

ActiveCampaign adds generative AI for email content and subject lines to its automation builder and campaign editor.

  • Key capabilities: AI content generation, automation builder, segmentation and split testing.
  • Pricing model: tiered plans priced by contacts; see ActiveCampaign pricing.
  • Strengths: sequences and their copy are built in the same flow view.
  • Limitations: AI-written steps still need human review for tone across the whole sequence.
  • Skip it if: you mainly send one-off newsletters.

Official site: ActiveCampaign.

Lavender: best for one-to-one sales emails, not campaigns

Best for: SDRs and founders writing individual prospecting emails in Gmail or Outlook.

Lavender is an AI email coach that scores drafts and suggests edits for clarity, length and personalization as you write, rather than generating bulk campaigns.

  • Key capabilities: draft scoring, rewrite suggestions, personalization research and inbox extensions.
  • Pricing model: individual and team plans; see Lavender pricing.
  • Strengths: coaches the writer instead of replacing them, which suits personal outreach.
  • Limitations: not a campaign tool, and outbound volume brings its own compliance duties.
  • Skip it if: you write marketing campaigns to opted-in lists.

Official site: Lavender. Outbound sending itself is covered in our cold email software comparison.

Anyword: best for scoring subject-line and copy variants

Best for: teams that generate many subject lines and want a pre-send signal to choose which to test.

Anyword generates email subject lines and body variants with a predictive performance score, and can work from your own channel data on higher tiers.

  • Key capabilities: subject-line and email variants, predictive scoring and brand rules.
  • Pricing model: tiered plans; see Anyword pricing.
  • Strengths: narrows a long list of subject lines to the few worth testing.
  • Limitations: sits outside your email platform, so copy has to move across by hand or integration.
  • Skip it if: your ESP's built-in testing already covers what you need.

Official site: Anyword.

Claude: best general assistant for full sequences and voice rules

Best for: lifecycle marketers who plan whole sequences and want voice, compliance and offer rules applied every time.

A general assistant rather than an email tool. A Claude Project can hold uploaded reference files and standing instructions, such as your voice guide, approved offers and legal footer rules (checked 4 November 2026).

  • Key capabilities: sequence planning and drafting, Projects with instructions and files, shared projects on Team plans.
  • Pricing model: the pricing page lists Pro at $17 a month billed annually or $20 monthly, and Team standard seats from $20 per seat a month annually (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Strengths: good at keeping a five-email sequence consistent in tone and logic.
  • Limitations: no ESP integration, personalization data or send-time testing; you paste copy into your platform.
  • Skip it if: you want copy generated from live customer data inside the ESP.

Official site: Claude.

Do AI-written subject lines actually lift open rates?

There's no general answer, and open rates are a weak measure anyway, because privacy features in some mail clients inflate opens. Judge AI subject lines by clicks and conversions in your own split tests, not by vendor claims or predicted scores.

What review and compliance checks should every AI-written email pass?

Every AI-drafted email needs a human check for accuracy of offers, consent and unsubscribe handling, honest subject lines, and brand voice before it sends. These checks protect both deliverability and legal standing.

  • Offer accuracy: discounts, dates and terms match what's actually approved; AI tools invent offers easily.
  • Honest subject lines: no misleading urgency or claims the body doesn't support.
  • Consent and opt-out: sent only to contacts with a lawful basis, with a working unsubscribe and sender details.
  • Personalization fallbacks: merge tags have defaults so nobody gets "Hi {first_name}".
  • Rendering and links: checked across clients with tools from our email testing tools guide.

Where are the pricing boundaries for AI email copy tools?

Campaign platforms price mainly by contact count and plan tier, so AI features cost nothing extra on some plans but sit behind upgrades on others. Standalone assistants price per seat or plan, independent of list size.

That makes the boundary practical: if your list is large and your writers are few, a seat-priced assistant is cheap relative to the platform. For example, Anthropic's pricing page lists Team standard seats at $20 per seat a month billed annually or $25 monthly (checked 4 November 2026). If your list is small, the platform's built-in AI may be enough. Confirm which AI features sit on which tier before upgrading a plan for them.

What else do campaign teams ask about AI email copy?

These answers cover the practical edge cases: privacy, personalization, deliverability and sales email. Each one assumes a person approves the send.

Is it safe to put customer data into an AI writing tool?

Keep personal data inside your email platform where possible, and check any standalone tool's data-use terms first. Merge tags let you write personalized copy without pasting real customer records into a chatbot.

Can AI-written emails hurt deliverability?

The copy itself is rarely the main cause, but misleading subject lines, spammy phrasing and sending to unengaged contacts all raise complaint rates. Keep list hygiene and authentication in order whichever tool writes the words.

Should we use the same tool for sales emails and marketing campaigns?

Usually not. Marketing campaigns go to opted-in lists from an ESP, while one-to-one sales email is written in the inbox and carries different compliance duties, so the tools and rules differ.

How many subject lines should we test?

Test as many as your list size can support with a clear result, which for most small lists means two at a time. Generating forty options is easy; proving one is better needs volume.

What's the verdict on AI email copywriting tools?

Use the AI inside your email platform first, and add one standalone tool only where it fixes a gap: Claude for sequence planning and voice rules, Anyword for variant scoring, Lavender for sales emails. Klaviyo suits ecommerce, Mailchimp suits small senders, HubSpot suits CRM-led B2B, and ActiveCampaign suits automation-heavy small teams.
