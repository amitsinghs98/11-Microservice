Lemlist vs Reply.io: Multichannel Outreach for Sales Teams

Should your team choose lemlist or Reply.io?

Pick lemlist if several people send mostly email from one account, because its Email plan is priced on volume with unlimited users. Pick Reply.io if your SDRs run long multichannel sequences or you're an agency that wants a published multi-client plan.

  • Email-only team of several reps: lemlist's Email plan covers the whole team on one volume-based price. Reply.io's Email Volume plan is per user until its higher contact tiers.
  • One SDR working email, LinkedIn and calls: seat prices are within $10 a month of each other. Data needs and extra mailboxes will decide the real total.
  • Agency with several clients: Reply.io publishes an Agency Core plan. Lemlist's pricing page shows no agency tier.
  • Team that needs SSO and audit logs: on lemlist these sit in Enterprise, so budget for a sales conversation either way.

How are lemlist and Reply.io positioned?

Lemlist grew out of personalized cold email and deliverability tooling, then added LinkedIn, calling, a lead database and AI agents. Reply.io sells itself as a multichannel sales engagement platform, with its Jason AI SDR product and dedicated agency plans alongside the per-seat tiers.

Lemlist's pricing page groups the product into Email, Multichannel and Enterprise plans, with data sold as add-ons (checked 4 November 2026). Reply.io's pricing page lists an Email Volume plan, a per-user Multichannel plan, AI SDR plans and agency plans (checked 4 November 2026).

In practice, Reply.io competes most with the tools in our sales engagement platform guide for SDR teams. Lemlist is just as often weighed against the sending-first tools in our cold email software roundup.

Which billing unit suits your sequences: emails sent or active contacts?

Reply.io's active-contact unit is kinder to long sequences, because follow-ups to the same person don't count again. Lemlist's emails-sent unit is kinder to short sequences sent to many people.

A four-step sequence to 2,500 new contacts uses 10,000 emails on lemlist but only 2,500 active contacts on Reply.io. Reply.io describes an active contact as a unique person who gets a first-step email in the month. Before you compare prices, write down your average steps per sequence and new contacts per month, because those two numbers decide which unit stretches further.

Deliverability tooling is closer than the units. Lemlist includes its lemwarm warm-up and a deliverability hub, while Reply.io includes warm-up with its mailboxes and sells domains and mailboxes in-app. Reply.io lists domains from $6 a year plus $5 per mailbox per month, and pre-warmed packages from $25 a year plus $15 per mailbox per month (checked 4 November 2026). What do lemlist and Reply.io really cost for one SDR and a five-seat team?

On monthly billing, one multichannel SDR costs $109 on lemlist and $99 on Reply.io, and five seats cost $545 and $495. On yearly billing the two are nearly level, at $435 and $445 a month for five seats.

These are hypothetical scenarios using list prices checked 4 November 2026. Mailbox provider fees (Google Workspace or Microsoft 365) are excluded because they're the same either way. Telephony minutes and phone numbers weren't itemized on either pricing page, so ask both vendors.

One SDR on a multichannel plan

  • lemlist: $109 monthly, or $129 with one enrichment pack; $87 yearly, or $103 with the pack.
  • Reply.io: $99 monthly or $89 annual, including 50 live-data credits a month.

Five SDRs on a multichannel plan

  • lemlist: $545 monthly, or $565 with one shared enrichment pack; $435 yearly, or $451 with the pack.
  • Reply.io: $495 monthly or $445 annual.

Two lines can flip that result. Reply.io charges $29 a month per extra mailbox, so a rep who needs more than the included senders adds cost quickly. Data is the other: neither tool's included credits support a full prospecting quota, so most teams buy data on top.

Five reps sending email only

The picture changes for email-only work. Five reps sharing lemlist's Email plan at 50,000 emails a month pay $69 on monthly billing, because users are unlimited. On Reply.io, unlimited users start at the 10,000-active-contact tier, listed at $179 a month. With four-step sequences, 50,000 emails reach about 12,500 contacts, which is more than that Reply.io tier covers. How do team controls, AI add-ons and CRM sync differ?

Lemlist keeps custom roles, activity logs, SSO and its "use lemlist from your CRM" feature for Enterprise. Reply.io's Agency Core and multichannel reporting give managers more structure on published plans, and its AI SDR is a separate purchase.

Both vendors sell AI agents: lemAgent at lemlist and Jason AI SDR at Reply.io, which starts at $500 a month billed annually (checked 4 November 2026). Judge these as separate products with their own business case, not as part of the seat price.

For CRM sync, decide which system owns the contact record before you connect anything. Then test that replies, meetings and opt-outs sync back without creating duplicates. Where do lemlist and Reply.io fall short?

Lemlist charges extra for WhatsApp, data and enterprise controls, and has no published agency plan. Reply.io's active-contact unit takes effort to forecast, its included data credits are small, and its mailbox count for Multichannel was inconsistent across the views reviewed.

  • lemlist: Multichannel is priced per user, so occasional users still need a seat. Whether data add-ons are per user or per workspace isn't stated on the pricing page.
  • Reply.io: LinkedIn and calling cost extra on Email Volume, and prices for additional live-data credits weren't shown in the content reviewed.
  • Both: telephony usage costs aren't clearly published, and neither vendor's AI agent claims were independently checked for this page.

Compliance sits with the sender in both tools. Outreach has to respect opt-outs across every channel, identify the sender accurately and have a lawful basis where the GDPR applies. Our walkthrough for a compliant outreach stack covers suppression and data sourcing.

Is LinkedIn automation in either tool worth the account risk?

Both tools automate LinkedIn steps, and LinkedIn's User Agreement restricts bots and other automated activity, so accounts can be limited regardless of vendor. Treat LinkedIn steps as a policy risk you accept knowingly, keep volumes modest and never put a rep's only account on the line.

How hard is it to switch between lemlist and Reply.io?

Plan on rebuilding sequences by hand, because step types, conditions and personalization variables don't map one-to-one. Move your do-not-contact data first and pause the old tool's live sequences so nobody hears from both.

  1. Export unsubscribe and do-not-contact lists and import them before any sequence launches.
  2. Rebuild sequences and test personalization variables on a small segment.
  3. Reconnect mailboxes and LinkedIn accounts, then restart at reduced volume.
  4. Recreate CRM field mappings and confirm activity logging doesn't duplicate records.
  5. Finish or pause live sequences in the old tool before the new ones go out.

What else do sales teams ask about lemlist vs Reply.io?

The common follow-ups cover single-seat cost, the active-contact unit and CRM sync. Answers use pricing pages checked 4 November 2026.

Is lemlist or Reply.io cheaper for a single SDR?

On monthly billing, Reply.io's Multichannel seat at $99 is below lemlist's $109. On yearly billing they're $89 and $87, so data and extra mailboxes usually decide the total.

What counts as an active contact on Reply.io?

Reply.io counts a unique contact who receives a first-step email in the month. Follow-ups to that same contact don't count again.

Can a whole team share lemlist's Email plan?

Yes. The pricing page lists unlimited users and senders on the Email plan, with the price set by monthly email volume.

Do both tools sync with HubSpot and Salesforce?

Lemlist lists HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive integrations on all plans, and Reply.io offers CRM integrations too. Check field-level sync and activity logging during the trial.

What's the verdict on lemlist vs Reply.io?

Our editorial assessment: lemlist is the better value for email-heavy teams that want many users on one volume-priced plan with deliverability tooling built in. Reply.io is the more economical choice for genuinely multichannel SDRs running long sequences, and the only one of the two with a published agency structure.

Run your own sequence length, seat count and mailbox needs through both vendors' trials before you commit.
