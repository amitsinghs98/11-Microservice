Best Agency Profitability Reporting Tools for Owners

Agency profitability reporting software tells you which clients, projects and services actually make money, by joining revenue and budgets to the labour cost of the hours spent. Productive, Scoro and Kantata do this inside an agency management platform; Accelo and Function Point suit service firms wanting one system; Teamwork.com and Harvest give lighter views for smaller teams.

Which profitability tool should an agency owner choose?

If your team already tracks time and you want margin by client without a spreadsheet, choose an agency platform such as Productive or Scoro. If you have more than a few dozen people, several offices or complex resourcing, Kantata is built for that scale, while Teamwork.com or Harvest are enough for a small agency's first real margin view.

  • Small and mid-sized agencies wanting budgets, time and margin together: Productive.
  • Agencies wanting quotes, work, billing and reporting in one system: Scoro.
  • Larger professional services firms with complex resourcing: Kantata.
  • Service firms that also run retainers and support tickets: Accelo.
  • Creative agencies that want estimates, jobs and financials joined: Function Point.
  • Agencies already managing delivery in a PM tool: Teamwork.com.
  • Small teams that want budget and cost views from timesheets: Harvest.

Why isn't a finance dashboard enough?

A finance dashboard reports revenue, costs and cash for the business as a whole, but it can't tell you that one client takes twice the hours its fee covers. Agency profitability needs labour cost allocated to clients and projects, which means time data joined to cost rates and to what you billed.

Accounting tools see salaries as one overhead line. Profitability tools turn each hour into a cost using a cost rate per person or role, then compare it with the revenue recognised on that client. Generic dashboards that read only from your ledger can't do that allocation. They're still useful for cash and overhead trends, just not for deciding which clients to reprice.

Three measures matter most. Utilization is the share of available hours spent on billable work. Realization is how much of the value of those hours you actually bill and collect. Client margin is revenue minus the labour and direct costs of serving that client. If time isn't tracked consistently, none of the three is reliable, so start with our guide to time tracking for marketing agencies.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Productive, Scoro and Kantata give the fullest margin picture because they own the budget, the time and the billing. Accelo and Function Point suit particular kinds of service firm, while Teamwork.com and Harvest trade depth for simplicity.

Productive: best for small and mid-sized agencies that want margin by client

Productive is an agency management platform with budgets, time tracking, resource planning, invoicing and profitability reports, including forecasts based on scheduled work. Its strength is showing a retainer's or project's projected margin before the month ends, not just afterwards. It needs consistent time tracking and cost rates to be accurate. Skip it if you don't want to change how the team logs time. See the Productive pricing page.

Scoro: best for one system from quote to report

Scoro combines CRM, quotes, projects, time, billing and reporting, with dashboards for utilization and profitability. Owning the quote and the invoice means reports reflect what was sold and billed. The trade-off is setup effort and change management across the team. Consider another option if you only want reporting on top of tools you already like. Plans are on the Scoro pricing page.

Kantata: best for larger professional services firms

Kantata, formed from Mavenlink and Kimble, is a professional services automation platform with resource management, project accounting and financial forecasting. It suits agencies with many people, multiple offices and complex staffing. For a small agency, it's likely more system than you need. Skip it if you're under a few dozen people. See the Kantata pricing page.

Accelo: best for service firms mixing projects, retainers and tickets

Accelo brings sales, projects, retainers, tickets, time and billing into one platform with reporting across them. That's useful for firms whose revenue comes from several engagement types at once. Its modular structure means reports depend on which modules you buy. Consider another option if you only run projects. See the Accelo pricing page.

Function Point: best for creative agencies running jobs

Function Point is built for creative and marketing agencies, with estimates, job tracking, time, resourcing, invoicing and financial reporting. Its job-based model matches how many creative shops already think about work. It's less of a fit for agencies whose work is mostly ongoing SEO or paid media retainers. Skip it if you want a general project tool. See the Function Point pricing page.

Teamwork.com: best for margin views inside agency project management

Teamwork.com is a project management platform aimed at client-service teams, with time tracking, budgets and profitability reporting. If delivery already runs there, its reports may be enough before you buy anything else. It's lighter on financial forecasting than Productive or Kantata. Consider another option if finance needs revenue recognition by period. Plans are on the Teamwork.com pricing page.

Harvest: best for a first margin view from timesheets

Harvest tracks time and expenses against project budgets and can apply cost rates to show project costs. For a small agency, that's often the first time anyone sees which projects run over. It doesn't do full resource forecasting or client-level margin across many projects. Skip it once you need forward-looking capacity and margin. See the Harvest pricing page.

How do retainer and fixed-fee economics differ?

A retainer's margin is decided every month by how many hours the team spends against a steady fee, so overservicing erodes it slowly. A fixed-fee project's margin is decided by the estimate, so one bad estimate can wipe out a whole project's profit at once.

Hypothetical retainer, using stated assumptions: a client pays 6,000 per month. The team spends 50 hours at a blended cost rate of 60 per hour, so labour cost is 3,000 and gross margin is 3,000, or 50%. If the same client starts taking 70 hours, cost rises to 4,200 and margin falls to 1,800, or 30%, with no change in fee. These figures are illustrative assumptions, not benchmarks or vendor data.

Hypothetical fixed-fee project: a website is sold for 20,000 and estimated at 200 hours at the same cost rate, giving 12,000 in labour cost and 8,000 margin. If it takes 280 hours, cost is 16,800 and margin drops to 3,200. A profitability tool that compares actual hours with budgeted hours mid-project gives you the chance to change course before that happens.

Forecasting depends on knowing who's booked on what, so the reports above work best alongside resource planning software. Budgets are only as good as task coding, which is easier when your PM software is built around client projects.

Can you trust margin reports built on timesheets?

Margin reports are only as accurate as the time logged and the cost rates behind it, and both tend to be optimistic: people under-log time on troubled clients and cost rates often ignore overhead. Treat the first months of reports as a test of data quality, not as a verdict on clients.

What mistakes do owners make with profitability reporting?

The most common mistake is buying the reporting tool before fixing time tracking, which produces confident-looking charts built on incomplete data. The second is using salary alone as the cost rate, which overstates margin on every client.

  • Cost rates without overhead: include benefits, software and a share of non-billable time, or every client looks more profitable than it is.
  • Revenue in the wrong month: align revenue to when work was delivered, not only when it was invoiced.
  • Utilization targets as a stick: pushing utilization too hard encourages padded timesheets.
  • Ignoring pass-through costs: ad spend or software billed to clients should be separated from fee revenue.
  • No action loop: review margin by client monthly and decide on scope, price or staffing changes; see how to build an operating stack that protects margins.

What else do owners ask about agency profitability?

Owners usually ask about targets, data and timing. These answers are general; your own numbers and accountant should guide decisions.

What is the difference between utilization and realization?

Utilization measures how much of the team's available time goes to billable work. Realization measures how much of that billable work's value you actually invoice and collect.

Do we need time tracking to measure client profitability?

For service work, yes, because labour is usually the largest cost and it must be allocated to clients. Without time data you can estimate, but you can't measure.

How should cost rates be calculated?

A common approach is to take each person's fully loaded annual cost, including benefits and a share of overhead, and divide it by realistic available hours. Ask your accountant to agree the method so reports match the books.

Can we get profitability reports from accounting software alone?

Accounting software reports overall profit and can track by class or tracking category, but it rarely allocates labour hours to clients. For client margin, you need time data joined to revenue.

How often should owners review client margin?

Monthly is a sensible cadence for retainers, with a mid-project check for fixed-fee work. Quarterly reviews are useful for deciding on repricing or ending unprofitable relationships.
