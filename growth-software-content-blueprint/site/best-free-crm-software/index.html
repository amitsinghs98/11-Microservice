Which free CRM should a startup choose?

Choose HubSpot's free tools if you're one or two founders who want forms, email and meetings on the same contact record, and Zoho CRM's free edition or Attio's free plan if the whole founding team needs a login. Choose self-hosted open source only if someone technical will own the server.

For narrower cases, Capsule Free suits a consultancy with a short client list, and Bitrix24 Free suits a team that wants CRM, tasks and chat in one workspace.

If you already have budget and more than a couple of sellers, skip the free tier and start with our main shortlist of small-business CRMs, which compares paid plans.

Who should use a free CRM, and who should pay from day one?

A free CRM suits pre-seed and seed founders, and small service firms, that have outgrown a spreadsheet but can't justify per-seat software yet. Teams with a hired sales function, outbound targets or permission requirements should pay from the start, because those are exactly the features free plans hold back.

The free CRM also becomes the system your email, scheduling and enrichment tools plug into. Treat the choice as the first decision in your stack, not a throwaway, and read it alongside our plan for a full-funnel growth stack for a small B2B team.

What does each free CRM offer, and where does it stop?

Each hosted free plan is generous in one direction and tight in another, and the tight spot is your upgrade trigger. The blocks below name the buyer each plan suits, the limit that usually ends the free period, and who should skip it.

HubSpot free CRM tools: best for founders who want marketing on the same record

HubSpot's free tier bundles contact, company and deal records with basic forms, email, live chat and a meetings link, which makes it the most connected free starting point for a founder who'll add inbound marketing later. Check the user cap, the email allowance and where HubSpot branding appears on the HubSpot pricing page.

Limitations: sales automation, extra pipelines and custom reporting live in paid tiers, and paid hubs are priced separately, so the jump off the free plan can be larger than one seat. Our HubSpot and Salesforce comparison for growing businesses follows that path further. Skip it if your founding team already exceeds the free user cap.

Zoho CRM free edition: best for small teams wanting automation without a bill

Zoho's free edition is aimed at very small teams and, based on the product information reviewed, includes some workflow automation, which most free plans keep behind a paywall.

Limitations: multiple pipelines and mailbox integration have historically sat in paid editions, and the interface is configuration-heavy for a team that wants something minimal. Confirm the user cap and any limit on data exports on the Zoho CRM pricing page. Skip it if nobody on the team wants to configure modules and layouts.

Attio Free: best for relationship-led founding teams

Attio uses a flexible data model built around people, companies and custom lists, which suits founders tracking investors, partners and early customers in one place. Its free plan is a real workspace rather than a demo; check seats, object and record allowances, and report limits on the Attio pricing page.

Limitations: report counts on the free plan are tight once investors ask for pipeline views, and paid plans add usage-based credits for AI and workflow features, which makes later cost harder to forecast. Skip it if you need role-based permissions from day one.

Capsule Free: best for consultancies with a short client list

Capsule is a clean contact-and-pipeline tool; its free plan covers a single pipeline and a small contact base; confirm both limits on the Capsule pricing page.

Limitations: the contact cap is the constraint, and every prospect, referrer and investor counts against it. Workflow automation sits in paid plans. Skip it if you plan to import a purchased or scraped prospect list.

Bitrix24 Free: best for teams that want CRM, tasks and chat together

Bitrix24 puts a basic CRM inside a workspace with task boards, chat and video calls, and its paid plans use a flat rate per organization with a set number of users included. That flat-rate model can work out cheaper than per-seat pricing as headcount grows; check tiers on the Bitrix24 prices page.

Limitations: the free CRM is genuinely basic, and the wider workspace can feel heavy for teams that already live in Slack and Google Workspace. Skip it if you want a focused sales tool rather than an all-in-one intranet.

Twenty: best for technical founders who want an open-source, modern CRM

Twenty is an open-source CRM you can host yourself, so you avoid license fees and keep control of the data. You pay instead in hosting, patching and backups, and the integration catalogue is smaller than the large vendors'. Skip it if nobody wants on-call responsibility for a production database.

SuiteCRM: best for teams wanting a traditional, configurable open-source CRM

SuiteCRM is a long-established open-source CRM with sales, marketing and service modules, but setup is heavier than any hosted option here. Skip it if you need the team productive this week.

Which plan fits a solo founder or a three-person team?

A solo founder can use almost any hosted free plan, so pick the one whose connected tools you'll use most. A three-person team should eliminate every plan whose user cap is below three before comparing anything else.

Scenario A: solo founder doing founder-led sales (hypothetical)

Assumptions: one user, about 400 contacts, one pipeline and a demo-request form on the website. User caps don't bite; a small contact allowance might. HubSpot's free tools fit best here because the form, meetings link and contact record live together; accept that free-tier assets may carry vendor branding.

Scenario B: three-person founding team (hypothetical)

Assumptions: two founders and a first seller, about 3,000 contacts, a sales pipeline and a partnerships pipeline. First, confirm each vendor's current free user cap and drop any plan below three seats. Second, check whether the survivors allow two pipelines on the free tier; if not, the second pipeline is your paying trigger. Of the plans here, Zoho CRM and Attio are the ones we'd check first for this team, depending on whether automation or relationship context matters more.

When the upgrade comes, model the whole bill rather than the per-seat headline. Our guide to CRM pricing and total cost works through 5-seat and 15-seat scenarios, including setup and admin time.

Is it a free plan or only a free trial?

A free plan has no end date but carries caps, while a free trial unlocks a paid tier for a fixed window and then stops. Pipedrive, for example, offers a trial rather than a permanent free plan.

The Pipedrive pricing page lists a 14-day free trial alongside paid plans starting at US$14 per seat per month billed annually for Lite (checked 4 November 2026). That's useful for testing a pipeline-first workflow, but it isn't a free CRM. If that style appeals, our roundup of Pipedrive alternatives for small sales teams covers similar tools.

What happens to your data when you outgrow the plan?

Contacts, companies and deals usually export cleanly, but email threads, notes, activity history and stage-change dates often don't. Check the export before you import, because startups put their earliest and messiest data into free plans.

  • Export allowances. Some free plans limit how often or how much you can export. Confirm it on the pricing page or help center.
  • History. Ask whether activities and emails export with timestamps and owner, or only as flat notes.
  • Custom objects. A bespoke data model may not map onto a conventional CRM's contacts, companies and deals.

Keep a field dictionary from day one (name, purpose, allowed values, owner). It makes a later move to a CRM built for B2B SaaS sales teams far less painful.

What mistakes do startups make with free CRMs?

The costliest mistake is choosing a plan whose user cap is smaller than your hiring plan, which forces a migration or upgrade within months. The second is importing a large cold list into a contact-capped plan.

  • Assuming a first-year or promotional price is also the renewal price.
  • Letting each founder add fields before agreeing pipeline stages.
  • Paying for a separate email tool when the CRM's allowance would do; compare what free email marketing plans actually allow first.

Ask each vendor four questions: what triggers the first paid tier, whether activities and email history export, what happens to data on downgrade, and whether any discount also applies at renewal.

What else do founders ask about free CRMs?

Most questions come down to user caps and when to start paying. The answers below cover the common ones.

Which free CRM allows the most users?

Free user caps change often, so confirm them on each vendor's pricing page before you decide. Self-hosted open-source CRMs such as Twenty and SuiteCRM have no vendor seat license, but you take on hosting and admin.

Is HubSpot's CRM really free?

HubSpot offers a permanent free tier of CRM tools with no fixed end date. It has user and feature limits, and sales automation and additional pipelines sit in paid tiers.

Can a free CRM handle outbound prospecting?

Rarely well, because contact caps, email limits and missing sequences push outbound teams to paid tiers or separate tools. If nurture is part of the plan, our guide to free marketing automation tools and their real limits picks up where free CRMs stop.

When should a startup start paying for a CRM?

Usually when a new hire exceeds the user cap, you need a second pipeline, or a new seller needs permissions. Those are operational triggers, so write yours down before you import any data.

What's the verdict?

Our editorial assessment: for a solo founder, HubSpot's free tools are the most connected starting point; for a three-person team, check Zoho CRM's and Attio's current seat caps first, because seating everyone matters more than any feature. Capsule and Bitrix24 suit narrower cases, and self-hosted tools suit teams that would rather spend engineering time than money.
