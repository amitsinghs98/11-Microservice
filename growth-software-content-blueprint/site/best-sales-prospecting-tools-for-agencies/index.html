Which prospecting tool should your agency choose?

If you're prospecting for your own agency's new business, start with Apollo.io or Hunter plus one account-signal source that matches what you sell. If you're running outbound for clients, put contract terms and client separation ahead of database size, and look at Clay for the research layer.

  • All-in-one database and sequencing for your own pipeline: Apollo.io.
  • Simple, domain-first email finding: Hunter.
  • Budget email finding with built-in drip campaigns: Snov.io.
  • Technology-based account lists (SEO, web and ecommerce agencies): BuiltWith.
  • Relationship and account research on LinkedIn: LinkedIn Sales Navigator.
  • Quick phone and email lookups for account managers: Lusha.
  • Automating the research step across many sources: Clay.

What makes prospecting different for agencies?

Agencies prospect on two fronts: for their own new business, and often for clients as a service. The second raises questions most tools aren't designed around, such as whether you're allowed to use your licence for someone else, how to keep one client's lists away from another's, and who owns the data when the engagement ends.

The other difference is what counts as a good prospect. An SEO agency's best signal is a website with a visible problem; a paid media agency's is a company already spending on ads; an outbound agency's is whatever the client's ideal customer profile says. Contact data is the commodity part; the account research behind it is where agencies win or waste time. Our B2B contact database comparison covers the commodity layer in depth.

What does each type of agency need?

SEO and web agencies need technology and site signals, paid media agencies need evidence of ad spend and growth, and outbound agencies need volume, verification and strict client separation. Buy for the signal first and the contacts second.

SEO and web agencies

Your strongest lists come from site characteristics: a CMS or ecommerce platform you specialise in, a recent replatform, or a site you can audit quickly to open a conversation. A technographic source such as BuiltWith narrows the account list, and an email finder such as Hunter fills in the marketing lead at each domain. Your own SEO platform supplies the audit you'll use as the opener.

Paid media agencies

You're looking for companies that already buy media or have just raised money and are hiring marketers. Sales Navigator's account filters and hiring signals help here, and Apollo's company filters can build a first list. Keep lists small and researched; a generic pitch to every advertiser burns your domain reputation fast.

Outbound and lead generation agencies

You run prospecting as a deliverable, so credits, exports and client separation decide the tool. Clay suits building a research process you repeat per client, and a dedicated sending tool handles delivery; our cold email software guide covers that layer.

Which tools are worth shortlisting?

Pick one contact source and one signal source, then add Clay only when the research step is the bottleneck. The blocks below flag the agency-specific catch for each.

Apollo.io: best for an agency's own new business

Apollo combines a contact database, filters, enrichment and sequencing, so a founder or business-development lead can go from list to first email in one tool. Agency catch: read the terms on the Apollo pricing page about internal versus client use before you run client campaigns from your account. Skip it if you already pay for a sequencer and only need contacts.

Hunter: best for domain-first email finding

Hunter finds and verifies email addresses for a given domain or name, which fits agencies that start from a list of websites. It also offers a lightweight campaigns feature. Tiers are on the Hunter pricing page. Agency catch: it tells you who to email, not which accounts to pick. Skip it if you need phone numbers or org charts.

Snov.io: best for small agencies on a budget

Snov.io bundles an email finder, verifier and drip campaigns, useful for a two-person agency that wants one cheap tool. Credit tiers are on the Snov.io pricing page. Agency catch: serious outbound for clients needs proper mailbox and deliverability management elsewhere. Skip it if you run high-volume client programmes.

BuiltWith: best for SEO, web and ecommerce agencies

BuiltWith tracks the technologies websites use and builds lists of sites running a given platform, so you can find every store on a cart you specialise in. Plans are on the BuiltWith plans page. Agency catch: it gives you domains, so pair it with an email finder. Skip it if your service isn't tied to a technology choice.

LinkedIn Sales Navigator: best for account research and warm paths

Sales Navigator adds advanced lead and account filters, saved lists and alerts on top of LinkedIn, and shows shared connections that can turn a cold approach into an introduction. Compare tiers on LinkedIn's plan comparison page. Agency catch: seats are personal, and scraping or automating it breaks LinkedIn's rules; our guide to LinkedIn sales prospecting tools covers compliant use. Skip it if you need exportable lists.

Lusha: best for account managers spotting expansion

Lusha's extension and web app return emails and phone numbers quickly, which suits account managers chasing a new contact at an existing client. Tiers are on Lusha pricing. Agency catch: few controls for separating data by client. Skip it if you need bulk research workflows.

Clay: best for outbound agencies running a research process

Clay chains data providers, web research and AI steps in a table, so a research recipe you build for one client can be copied for the next. See the Clay pricing page for plan limits. Agency catch: usage-based billing needs tracking per client so you can pass costs on. Skip it if nobody will own the tables. For enrichment depth, see our lead enrichment software comparison.

What does a repeatable prospecting workflow look like?

Run the same six steps for every list, whether it's for you or a client: define the profile, research accounts, find contacts, verify, sync to the CRM with an owner, then hand to outreach with suppression applied. Writing the steps down is what makes the process billable and auditable.

  1. Profile. Agree industries, size bands, regions, technologies and exclusions (current clients, competitors, past opt-outs) in writing.
  2. Account research. Build the account list from the signal source and record why each account qualified.
  3. Contact discovery. Find two or three roles per account, not everyone with a marketing title.
  4. Verification. Verify emails close to send time; our email verification tool guide compares options.
  5. CRM sync. Push accounts and contacts to the CRM with source, list name, client tag and owner, and deduplicate on domain.
  6. Handoff. Load only approved contacts into outreach, with a suppression list shared across every campaign.

The CRM step is where agencies most often cut corners. If yours can't tag records by client and source, our CRM guide for marketing agencies covers options built for client accounts.

Can one tool safely serve several clients?

Sometimes, but only if the vendor's terms allow prospecting on a client's behalf and you can keep each client's lists, suppressions and exports separate. Many tools are licensed for the buyer's internal use, so confirm this in writing before you sell prospecting as a service.

What else do agencies ask about prospecting tools?

Agencies mostly ask about seats, exports and whether to buy one tool or two. Short answers follow.

Should each salesperson have their own seat?

Usually, yes. Most prospecting tools license per user, and sharing a login breaks the terms of many vendors and removes any audit trail of who exported what.

Do agencies need a contact database and an enrichment tool?

Not at first. A single tool that finds and verifies contacts covers most new-business needs, and a separate enrichment layer becomes worth it when you're filling CRM fields at volume.

How should we pass data costs on to clients?

Track credits used per client list and either bill them as a disclosed pass-through or have the client hold the licence. Bundling unknown data costs into a flat retainer tends to erode margin.

Is it better to prospect from a client's own CRM?

When you're running outreach for a client, often yes. Working inside their CRM keeps ownership clear and avoids duplicating contacts they already know, though it needs agreed access rules.

What's the verdict?

In our editorial assessment, most agencies should pair one contact tool (Apollo.io for an all-in-one, Hunter for simplicity) with one signal source that matches their service, such as BuiltWith for web and SEO work or Sales Navigator for account research. Clay earns its place when an outbound team needs a repeatable research recipe per client.
