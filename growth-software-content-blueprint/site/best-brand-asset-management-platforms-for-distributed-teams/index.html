Best Brand Asset Management Platforms for Distributed Teams

For distributed teams, brand asset management software has one job: make the approved logo, template or message the easiest thing to find, and make the outdated one impossible to use. Frontify and Bynder lead on brand portals and guidelines, Marq and Canva on locked templates for non-designers, Templafy on Office documents, and Brandfolder, Lytho and Papirfly on specific mixes of library, workflow and local marketing.

Which brand asset platform fits your team?

Start from what people outside the central brand team need to produce. If they mostly download finished assets, choose a brand portal; if they mostly create local versions, choose a template platform that locks the brand elements.

  • Living brand guidelines plus an asset library for many markets: Frontify.
  • Enterprise DAM with brand portals and templating: Bynder.
  • Local teams creating flyers, social posts and sales collateral from locked templates: Marq or Canva.
  • On-brand Word, PowerPoint and email templates across a workforce: Templafy.
  • Brand portals with simple sharing and usage insight: Brandfolder.
  • Brand plus creative workflow in one place: Lytho.
  • Large brands with franchise or partner marketing and print: Papirfly.

What problem does brand asset management solve that a DAM doesn't?

A DAM manages every asset for the people who produce and govern them; brand asset management is the outward-facing layer that helps everyone else use the brand correctly. It adds guidelines, usage instructions, templates and controlled external access on top of a library.

The audience is the difference. A distributed company has sales teams in several countries, franchisees, channel partners, PR agencies and event contractors, and most of them aren't designers. They need the current logo in the right format, a short note on how to use it, and a template that won't let them stretch it. If your bigger need is the central library itself, with metadata, renditions and rights, start with our guide to DAM software for marketing teams.

Where is each platform strong and weak?

Portal-first platforms are strongest at explaining the brand and controlling downloads, while template-first platforms are strongest at stopping local teams from improvising designs. Few do both equally well, so weigh which failure costs you more.

Frontify: best for living brand guidelines

Frontify combines web-based brand guidelines with an asset library and templates, so usage rules sit beside the files they govern. Our editorial assessment is that it's a good fit for brand teams who want guidelines to be a working tool rather than a PDF. Limitation: deep rights management and rendition automation are stronger in dedicated DAMs. Skip it if your problem is a large production library rather than brand usage. See Frontify's official site.

Bynder: best for enterprise libraries with brand portals

Bynder pairs an enterprise DAM with brand guidelines and templating, which suits companies that want production assets and brand distribution in one vendor. Limitation: you'll pay for and configure more than a portal-only buyer needs. Skip it if you only need a guidelines site and a few hundred files. See Bynder's official site.

Marq: best for locked templates in sales and local marketing

Marq focuses on brand templating: designers build templates and decide which elements local users can edit. It suits franchise, real-estate and sales organizations producing local collateral. Limitation: it isn't designed as the master library for originals and licensing. Skip it if your users only download finished assets. See Marq's official site.

Canva: best when non-designers create most content

Canva's team and enterprise offerings add brand kits, template controls and approvals to an editor most people already know, which lowers training effort. Limitation: brand controls depend on plan, and ease of editing cuts both ways if templates aren't locked. Skip it if you need strict asset rights and expiry governance. See Canva's enterprise page.

Templafy: best for on-brand documents across a workforce

Templafy manages templates and approved content inside the tools employees use every day, such as Word, PowerPoint and email. In our assessment, it solves the "old deck template" problem better than campaign-focused tools. Limitation: it isn't a campaign asset library. Skip it if your brand risk is in social and ads rather than documents. See Templafy's official site.

Brandfolder: best for portals with usage insight

Brandfolder offers brand-focused collections, sharing and reporting on how assets are used. That helps brand teams see which assets partners download. Limitation: confirm which template and analytics features need higher tiers. Skip it if local editing of templates is your main need. See Brandfolder's official site.

Lytho: best for brand assets plus creative requests

Lytho combines a brand asset library with creative workflow, such as intake and review, which suits in-house creative teams serving many internal clients. Limitation: confirm its integrations cover the tools your team already uses. Skip it if you already have a mature work management tool you won't replace. See Lytho's official site.

Papirfly: best for enterprise local and partner marketing

Papirfly positions itself as an all-in-one brand management platform covering guidelines, assets, templating and local marketing, including print. It's built for large brands with many markets or partners. Limitation: it's priced and scoped for enterprises. Skip it if you're a single-market team. See Papirfly's official site.

How do you stop people using outdated assets?

Combine expiry dates, versioning that replaces files behind the same link, and templates that pull the logo from the library rather than embedding a copy. Then make the approved asset faster to get than the old one sitting in someone's downloads folder.

  • Expiry and archiving: set expiry dates on campaign and licensed assets so they disappear from search automatically.
  • Version replacement: update an asset in place so shared links and embeds serve the new version.
  • Linked templates: templates that reference library assets update when the logo changes.
  • Permissions by audience: partners see only what they're cleared to use, in the markets they serve.
  • Usage guidance next to the file: a one-line "use this for…" note beats a long guideline PDF.
  • Download and share analytics: spot which teams still pull retired assets.
  • A retirement routine: when the brand changes, announce, replace, expire and then audit.

These safeguards are part of wider governance; our list of content governance tools for distributed teams covers approval rules and audits beyond assets, and the rights and usage management roundup covers licence expiry.

Will regional teams and partners actually use the portal?

Only if it's quicker than asking the brand team, which means single sign-on or simple guest access, search that speaks their language, and assets in ready-to-use formats. Adoption is a design and communication problem as much as a software one.

Which platform fits your distribution model?

Map the platform to how far assets travel. The further they go from the central team, the more you need locked templates and audience-specific permissions.

Regional marketing teams in several countries

Prioritize localization: language variants, market-specific collections and regional admins. Frontify or Bynder suit this, often with a template tool alongside. Our guide to localization and translation management software covers the translation side.

Agencies and freelancers

Prioritize time-limited guest access, upload rules for returned work, and a clear split between drafts and approved assets. Pair the portal with a review tool from our content approval roundup.

Franchisees, dealers and channel partners

Prioritize locked templates and simple self-service: Marq, Canva or Papirfly fit here. If partners also need a broader client-facing space, see our list of client portal software.

What mistakes do buyers make?

The most common mistake is buying a library when the real problem is local creation, or buying a template tool when the real problem is a messy library. The second is launching without retiring the old shared folders.

Others to avoid: treating guidelines as a one-off project instead of a maintained page; giving every partner the same permissions; ignoring Office templates, which often carry the oldest logos; and judging platforms by the admin interface rather than by what a busy salesperson sees. What else do teams ask about brand asset management?

The usual follow-ups concern overlap with DAM, cost and measurement. Here are short answers.

Is brand asset management the same as DAM?

They overlap, but brand asset management focuses on helping people outside the brand team use approved assets correctly, while a DAM manages the full library and its metadata. Many vendors sell both, sometimes as separate modules.

Do we need a separate template tool?

You need one if local teams create their own materials. Without locked templates, a portal can only distribute files, and people will rebuild designs in whatever tool they have.

How do you measure brand asset adoption?

Track searches, downloads and shares by team or partner, failed searches, and how often retired assets are requested. Pair those with spot checks of live materials in each market.

Should partners get their own logins?

Usually yes, or at least named guest access, so you can control what each partner sees and revoke access when a relationship ends. Shared passwords make both impossible.

What's the verdict?

Choose Frontify or Bynder when guidelines and a governed library are the core need, Marq, Canva or Papirfly when local creation is, and Templafy when the risk lives in documents. Brandfolder and Lytho suit teams wanting portals with usage insight or creative workflow attached.
