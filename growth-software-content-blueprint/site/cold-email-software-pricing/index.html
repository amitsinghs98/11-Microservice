What does cold email really cost per month?

A small, careful program with one platform plan, two mailboxes and a few hundred verified prospects a month comes to roughly $60 to $70 in software before labor and domains. Add reps, clients or a data provider and the mailboxes, data and staff time quickly outweigh the sequencer.

Budget outbound as a stack of seven lines: the sending platform, mailboxes, domains, verification, inbox monitoring, prospect data and human operations. The first four are easy to price from official pages. The last three vary with how you work, and labor is the one most budgets leave out.

This guide doesn't recommend high volumes. A smaller, well-targeted program costs less, and it carries less risk to your reputation and less legal exposure.

How do sending platforms charge?

Most cold email platforms charge by monthly sends and stored or active contacts, not by mailbox. Some multichannel tools charge per user instead, which changes the math for teams.

  • Volume and contact tiers: you pay for emails sent and prospects stored or contacted each month, with unlimited connected mailboxes. Instantly, Smartlead and Saleshandy work this way.
  • Per user: each seat pays, with a set number of sender mailboxes per seat. lemlist's Multichannel plan is an example.
  • Contacted prospects: Woodpecker meters pricing on prospects contacted per month and describes mailboxes and team members as unlimited; check its pricing page for current tiers, because its published entry prices varied across its own pages when we checked on 4 November 2026.
  • Credits: data lookups, enrichment and AI features are often sold as a separate credit balance that resets or expires.

"Unlimited mailboxes" doesn't mean free mailboxes. Each sending address is still a paid account with your email provider, and each one is another reputation to look after.

What do mailboxes, domains and verification add?

Each sending mailbox on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 costs about $7 to $8.40 a month, and verification runs about $8 per 1,000 addresses on a pay-as-you-go plan. Domains are an annual registrar fee that varies by registrar and extension.

  • Google Workspace: Business Starter lists $8.40 per user a month on the Flexible Plan, or $7 a month with a one-year commitment (checked 4 November 2026). Each mailbox is a user.
  • Microsoft 365: Business Basic lists $7 per user a month paid yearly, with a higher monthly-commitment price (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Platform-sold mailboxes: several sequencers resell pre-configured domains and mailboxes. Reply.io, for example, lists a new domain and mailbox option at $6 per domain a year plus $5 per mailbox a month (checked 4 November 2026). Check who owns those domains if you leave.
  • Verification: NeverBounce lists pay-as-you-go verification at $8 for 1,000 credits, which expire 12 months after purchase (checked 4 November 2026). Our comparison of email verification and list cleaning tools covers the alternatives.
  • Monitoring and data: inbox placement tests, blocklist checks and DMARC reporting are sometimes bundled and sometimes sold separately; the deliverability tools roundup explains the options. Contact data is priced by credits or seats, as our B2B contact database guide shows.

How do you calculate your own monthly cost?

Add the platform fee, mailboxes, a twelfth of your annual domain fees, verification for every new contact, monitoring, data and labor. Then divide by qualified conversations, not by emails sent.

Monthly cost = P + (M × m) + (D × d ÷ 12) + (N × v) + I + R + (H × h), where:

  • P is the platform plan that covers your sends, stored contacts and required features.
  • M × m is the number of mailboxes times the monthly price per mailbox.
  • D × d is the number of sending domains times the annual registration fee.
  • N × v is new contacts per month times the cost per verification credit.
  • I is any monitoring tool that isn't bundled, and R is data or enrichment spend.
  • H × h is hours a month times the loaded hourly cost of whoever researches, writes and replies.

Cost per prospect is useful for comparing tools. Cost per qualified conversation is the number that tells you whether the program is worth running.

What do three realistic programs cost?

Using the figures above, a founder-led program costs about $57 a month in software, a three-rep team about $120 to $145, and a five-client agency about $275, all before domains, data, monitoring and labor. These are hypothetical planning models with stated assumptions, not recommended volumes.

Scenario A: founder-led outreach

Assumptions: about 200 new, researched prospects a month, a three-step sequence (about 600 emails), one secondary domain and two Google Workspace mailboxes on the Flexible Plan. Smartlead Base at $39 covers the volume, two mailboxes add $16.80, and 200 verifications cost about $1.60 a month from a $8 pack of 1,000 credits. That's about $57.40 a month before the domain fee and the founder's time, which will almost certainly be the largest cost.

Scenario B: three-rep sales team

Assumptions: about 1,000 new prospects a month across three reps, four-step sequences (about 4,000 emails) and six mailboxes on annual Google Workspace. Six mailboxes cost $42 and 1,000 verifications cost $8. On lemlist's Email plan at $69, the total is about $119. On Smartlead, stored contacts pass the Base plan's 2,000 within two months unless you delete old ones, so Pro at $94 is the realistic plan, giving about $144. Add the data provider and CRM. If reps also make calls and LinkedIn touches, a sales engagement platform may replace the sequencer.

Scenario C: agency running five clients

Assumptions: five clients, each with two domains and four mailboxes (20 mailboxes and 10 domains in total), and about 300 new prospects per client a month. Twenty mailboxes on the Flexible Plan cost $168, Smartlead Pro is $94 for its unlimited email accounts and 30,000 contacts, and 1,500 verifications cost $12. That's about $274 a month before domains, client workspace add-ons and reply handling across 20 inboxes, which should be priced into the retainer. Decide early whether the client or the agency owns and pays for the domains.

What do the estimates leave out?

They leave out labor, tax, data, monitoring, domain fees and anything the platform charges for client separation, and they assume your list is relevant and your program legal. They also can't tell you what reply rate or inbox placement you'll get, and no tool can promise either.

Buyers are right to doubt neat totals like these. The real budget depends on how many hours research and reply handling take, and that differs a lot between teams.

Should you pay monthly or annually?

Pay monthly until a pilot shows reply quality you're happy with, then consider annual billing for the savings. A deliverability problem or a change of strategy can stall a program partway through a contract.

  • Annual discounts: Saleshandy, Reply.io and lemlist all list lower per-month prices for annual billing in their current pricing (checked 4 November 2026).
  • Credit expiry: data and verification credits can expire, as Instantly's two-month rule and NeverBounce's 12-month rule show. Buy credits in amounts you'll use.
  • Next-tier triggers: stored contacts, monthly sends, users, client workspaces and API access. Ask whether the vendor upgrades you automatically or pauses sending.
  • Renewal: introductory or first-month promotions don't carry into year two. Get renewal terms in writing.

What should you ask before you buy?

Ask how contacts are counted, what's included at your tier and how opt-outs are shared across workspaces. The answers change the real price more than the headline rate does.

  • What counts as a stored or active contact, and when does a contact stop counting?
  • Are warm-up, inbox rotation and placement tests included, or extra?
  • How are client workspaces and white-label reports priced?
  • Do credits roll over, and what happens to them if you downgrade?
  • How are unsubscribes and suppression handled across workspaces and mailboxes?
  • What does the acceptable-use policy prohibit, and what triggers suspension?

What else do buyers ask about cold email costs?

Most follow-up questions are about domains, warm-up and when to add a CRM. The short answers follow.

Why not send cold email from my main domain?

Complaints from outreach can affect the reputation of the domain that also carries invoices, support and marketing email. A secondary domain adds cost and limits that exposure, but it doesn't change your legal duties.

Is warm-up a substitute for list quality?

No. Warm-up may help a new mailbox build sending history, but relevant, verified recipients and low complaint rates matter far more.

What is a sensible first budget?

Price Scenario A with current figures and run it for a month. Measure qualified replies before you add mailboxes, domains or clients.

When do I need a separate CRM?

Usually as soon as more than one person handles replies, or when deals run longer than a single conversation. Built-in inboxes rarely replace pipeline tracking.

How often should you re-check these prices?

Re-check every 60 to 90 days, and straight away when a vendor announces new plans, because cold email pricing changes often. Several vendors' own pages disagreed with each other on 4 November 2026, so always confirm on the live pricing page on the day you buy.
