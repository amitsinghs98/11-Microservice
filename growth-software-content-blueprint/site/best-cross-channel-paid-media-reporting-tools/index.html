Which paid media reporting tool should you choose?

Teams with analysts should pair Looker Studio with Supermetrics, or use Funnel when many channels need normalizing into one governed data set. Agencies that mainly need branded client reports should compare AgencyAnalytics, Whatagraph, Swydo and DashThis, which trade modelling depth for speed.

  • Free, flexible dashboards for analyst-led teams: Looker Studio.
  • Moving ad data into sheets, Looker Studio or a warehouse: Supermetrics.
  • Normalizing many channels, currencies and naming schemes: Funnel.
  • Agencies wanting an all-in-one client reporting platform: AgencyAnalytics.
  • Polished multi-channel client reports with little setup: Whatagraph.
  • PPC-focused agencies that want templated reports: Swydo.
  • Simple dashboards for clients who want one page: DashThis.

Why do metric normalization and data freshness matter most?

Every ad platform counts conversions with its own attribution window, time zone and currency, so adding their numbers together without normalizing them double-counts sales and misstates cost. Freshness matters because a report built on yesterday's partial data can trigger a budget move that the full data wouldn't support.

Normalization means agreeing a reporting currency and exchange-rate source, a single time zone, consistent campaign names and channel groupings, and clear labels for each platform's attribution setting. Some tools handle currency conversion and field mapping for you; others leave it to formulas in the dashboard. That difference is the main reason a connector-plus-dashboard stack and an all-in-one reporting platform feel so different once they are in daily use.

What is each tool good at, and when should you skip it?

Data-layer tools reward teams that will maintain a model, and presentation tools reward teams that need reports out the door. Read each block with that split in mind.

Looker Studio: best free dashboard layer

Good at: shareable dashboards on Google Ads, GA4, Sheets and BigQuery, plus partner connectors for other sources. Weak at: blending many sources gets slow and fragile, and non-Google connectors are often a paid add-on. Skip it if: nobody will own report maintenance. See Looker Studio.

Supermetrics: best for moving ad data where you already report

Good at: pulling ad, social and analytics data into spreadsheets, Looker Studio or a warehouse on a schedule. Weak at: check how cost changes as you add clients and ad accounts, and normalization is largely your job. Skip it if: you report on one or two Google sources only. Check limits on Supermetrics' pricing page.

Funnel: best for normalizing many channels

Good at: collecting channel data and mapping it into common fields, so spend and conversions are comparable before they reach a dashboard or warehouse. Weak at: quick wins; the mapping work is the product. Skip it if: you run a handful of channels in one currency. See Funnel's pricing page.

AgencyAnalytics: best all-in-one agency reporting platform

Good at: client dashboards, scheduled reports and white-labelling across paid, SEO and social channels; check current plans on AgencyAnalytics' pricing page. Weak at: custom data modelling compared with a warehouse stack. Skip it if: an analyst needs raw, joinable data.

Whatagraph: best for polished multi-channel client reports

Good at: turning connected channel data into presentable, branded reports with little setup. Weak at: deep ad hoc analysis. Skip it if: your audience is an internal analyst. See Whatagraph pricing.

Swydo: best for PPC-led agencies that want templates

Good at: templated reporting and monitoring for paid search and social accounts. Weak at: breadth beyond marketing channels. Skip it if: you need warehouse-grade data. See Swydo pricing.

DashThis: best for simple one-page client dashboards

Good at: quick dashboards from common marketing sources for clients who want one page. Weak at: complex blending and custom metrics. Skip it if: you need normalization across currencies and many accounts. See DashThis pricing.

Which questions should you ask before buying?

Ask how the tool counts accounts and sources, how often each connector refreshes, how it handles currency and attribution differences, and what you can export if you leave. Those four answers predict both your bill and your trust in the numbers.

  • Account limits: does an ad account, a client, a source or a dashboard count toward the plan?
  • Refresh and backfill: how often does each connector update, and are recent days re-pulled as conversions arrive?
  • Currency and time zone: can you set one reporting currency and time zone, and which exchange rate is used?
  • Blended metrics: can you define blended CPA and ROAS once and reuse them across clients?
  • Exports and white-label: can raw data leave the tool, and can reports carry your or your client's branding?

What should a weekly cross-channel review cover?

A weekly review should check data health first, then spend against plan, then blended and channel efficiency, and end with three decisions and their owners. Keep it to one page so it actually gets read.

  1. Data health: any broken connectors, missing days, renamed campaigns or tracking changes since last week.
  2. Spend versus plan: month-to-date spend by channel against budget, and projected month-end.
  3. Blended efficiency: total spend, total conversions and revenue from your source of truth, with blended CPA or ROAS.
  4. Channel efficiency: each channel's own CPA or ROAS, labelled with its attribution setting.
  5. Movers: the campaigns with the largest change in spend or efficiency, and a one-line reason.
  6. Decisions: three actions for the coming week, each with an owner.

Can a reporting tool give you one trustworthy number for every channel?

No tool can make platform-reported conversions agree, because each platform credits sales by its own rules; what a good tool can do is make the differences visible and consistent. Treat any "single source of truth" claim as a configuration goal you have to set up, not a feature you receive.

What else do buyers ask about paid media reporting software?

Most follow-up questions concern accuracy, refresh timing and client-facing reports. Here are short answers.

Why don't the numbers in my reporting tool match the ad platform?

Usually because of attribution windows, time zones, currency conversion, refresh timing or late-arriving conversions. Reconcile one week per channel, document the expected differences and show them on the report.

How fresh is cross-channel reporting data?

It depends on the tool's refresh schedule and each platform's own reporting delays, and conversions can keep updating for days after a click. Ask each vendor how often each connector refreshes and whether recent days are re-pulled.

Should blended ROAS replace channel ROAS in client reports?

Show both. Blended figures stop channels claiming the same sale twice, while channel figures guide day-to-day optimization, and the report should say which attribution settings produced each.

Is Looker Studio enough for an agency's paid media reporting?

It can be, especially for Google sources. Non-Google connectors, governance and white-labelling are where agencies usually add a connector tool or a dedicated reporting platform.

What's the verdict?

Choose Looker Studio with Supermetrics if you have analyst time, Funnel if normalization across many channels is the hard part, and AgencyAnalytics, Whatagraph, Swydo or DashThis if client-ready reports are the main output. Decide how you'll normalize currency, time zones and attribution before you choose.

If you trial a tool, use two real client accounts, reconcile a week against each platform, and confirm pricing on the vendor's site.
