Which account-based advertising platform should you choose?

If your named accounts are mostly reachable on LinkedIn and your list is a few hundred companies, start with LinkedIn's own company-list targeting before buying anything. Add a dedicated platform such as Demandbase, 6sense or RollWorks only when you need account-level frequency control, off-LinkedIn reach and reporting that ties impressions to account progression.

Our editorial picks, based on the product information reviewed:

  • Lowest commitment, highest B2B relevance: LinkedIn Ads with an uploaded company list.
  • Buying-group targeting across the open web: Demandbase.
  • Intent-led audiences pushed into LinkedIn and display: 6sense.
  • Smaller teams that want packaged account-based ads: RollWorks.
  • Managed multi-channel programs, including CTV and content syndication: Madison Logic.
  • Teams that want campaign execution automated across paid channels: Metadata.io.

What makes advertising account-based rather than ordinary paid social?

Account-based advertising starts from a named list of companies and measures success by what happens inside those accounts, not by clicks or cost per lead. Generic paid social starts from an audience definition and optimizes toward whoever converts cheapest, which often means people outside your target list.

The difference shows up in four places. Targeting is anchored to an account list, usually synced from the CRM. Frequency is managed per account, so one enthusiastic intern doesn't soak up the budget meant for a buying group. Reporting rolls impressions and engagement up to the account. And the outcome you care about is account progression: more people engaged, a meeting booked, an opportunity created.

If your goal is form fills from anyone in a broad industry, a paid social advertising management platform is the better fit. Account-based ads earn their keep when sales already has a target list and needs marketing to warm specific companies. What does each platform do well, and where does it fall short?

Each platform below gets a best-for line, a pricing model and an honest limitation. None of them is right for every team, and the cheapest option is often the one you already have access to.

LinkedIn Ads: the starting point for most B2B account lists

LinkedIn's help center describes how to upload a company list as a Matched Audience in Campaign Manager and match it to LinkedIn Pages using fields such as company name, website or Page URL, with formatting requirements for company targeting lists. You can then layer job function, seniority and other targeting attributes on top to reach the buying group rather than everyone at the company.

Pricing model: self-serve auction; you control budget and bids, with no platform contract.

Strengths: Role-level targeting on a network many B2B buyers already use, and no new vendor to onboard.

Limitations: It only reaches people on LinkedIn, list matching is never complete, and reporting is campaign-shaped rather than account-shaped unless you export and join it to CRM data.

Skip it if: your buyers aren't active on LinkedIn, or you need frequency control across channels. Our roundup of LinkedIn Ads management and reporting tools covers the reporting gap.

Demandbase: buying-group advertising across the open web

Demandbase positions its own B2B demand-side platform as built to serve impressions to buying committees inside target accounts, with account-level frequency caps. Its buying groups product maps roles within accounts so ads can be aimed at the people who influence a deal.

Pricing model: quote-based, usually as part of a wider platform contract.

Strengths: Account-level frequency and buying-group targeting are the core design, not an add-on, and reporting is positioned at account level rather than per click.

Limitations: It's a suite. You're buying data, intent and orchestration alongside the ads, and the onboarding effort reflects that. The conversion claims on its own pages are vendor research, not independent evidence.

Skip it if: you only need to reach a few hundred accounts on LinkedIn, or you have no one to own the platform day to day.

6sense: intent-led audiences for LinkedIn and display

6sense builds account segments from intent and buying-stage signals, then pushes them into display and LinkedIn campaigns. Its LinkedIn advertising FAQ describes two commercial routes: campaigns configured in 6sense with a fee based on campaign spend, or a segment-sync subscription. The campaign setup documentation also sets a minimum matched audience before a campaign can launch.

Pricing model: quote-based platform, plus a choice of spend-based or segment-sync fees for LinkedIn.

Strengths: Designed to decide which accounts deserve impressions this week, so budget follows buying signals rather than a static list.

Limitations: Results depend on how well the intent model fits your market, and the fee options need careful modeling against your expected LinkedIn spend.

Skip it if: your account list is small, hand-picked and already well understood by sales; prediction adds less there.

RollWorks: packaged account-based ads for smaller teams

RollWorks, part of AdRoll, sells account-based advertising through tiered packages described on its pricing page, with media billed alongside the package. Its free-tier onboarding guide notes that without a paid package you don't get playbooks, LinkedIn campaigns or the account-based features.

Pricing model: subscription packages plus media spend; a free self-serve tier exists but excludes the ABM features.

Strengths: A lighter commercial entry into account-based display than the enterprise suites.

Limitations: Tier contents vary, so confirm in writing which account-based features, playbooks and LinkedIn campaigns your package includes.

Skip it if: you need deep orchestration or website personalization in the same contract.

Madison Logic: managed multi-channel ABM media

Madison Logic's company facts page says it doesn't publish a price list and quotes programs by channel and media investment, covering display, connected TV, audio, content syndication and LinkedIn ads.

Pricing model: quoted per program, based on channels and media budget.

Strengths: Reach beyond LinkedIn and display, including channels most teams can't buy account-targeted on their own.

Limitations: A managed model gives you less hands-on control, and you rely on the vendor's reporting to judge account impact.

Skip it if: you want to run and tune campaigns yourself every week.

Metadata.io: automated B2B paid campaign execution

Metadata.io automates campaign building and budget shifting across paid channels. Its pricing page says pricing is scoped per team, based on channels under management, managed spend and delegated scope.

Pricing model: scoped per team on a demo; no public list price.

Strengths: Takes repetitive campaign operations off a small team, with account and audience targeting built in.

Limitations: Its own buyer's guide frames it for teams with substantial monthly paid budgets, so it's a poor fit for small tests.

Skip it if: your paid budget is small or LinkedIn is your only channel.

Do account-based ads actually move named accounts, or just impressions?

The vendors' published evidence is mostly their own research, so treat any conversion multiple as a hypothesis to test rather than a forecast. The honest test is a holdout: advertise to half of a matched account list and compare meetings and opportunities with the other half.

How should you connect ad engagement to account progression?

Report at the account level: how many target accounts were reached, how many engaged beyond a single click, and how many moved stage in the CRM within a set window. Impressions and click-through rate are diagnostic numbers, not outcomes.

A workable scorecard has five rows: accounts matched, accounts reached at a sensible frequency, accounts with multiple engaged contacts, meetings or opportunities created, and pipeline value. Compare each row against a holdout group so you aren't crediting ads for deals sales would have closed anyway. Our guide to revenue operations analytics tools covers the reporting layer, and cross-channel paid media reporting tools help when spend sits across several ad accounts.

Feed the list from somewhere reliable. Intent and fit signals from B2B intent data platforms can reorder the list each month, but sales should agree to the accounts before any money is spent.

Which platform fits your situation?

  • Under 500 target accounts, LinkedIn-heavy buyers: LinkedIn company lists plus CRM reporting. Revisit a platform once you need off-LinkedIn reach.
  • Large buying committees and long cycles: Demandbase, for buying-group targeting and account-level frequency.
  • You already trust an intent model: 6sense, and model both LinkedIn fee routes before signing.
  • Small team, want packaged display: RollWorks, after checking which account features your package includes.
  • Need CTV, audio or syndication against named accounts: Madison Logic.
  • Campaign operations are the bottleneck: Metadata.io, if your budget matches its target customer.

What mistakes do B2B marketers make with account-based ads?

The most expensive mistake is buying a platform before sales has agreed to the account list. The second is judging the program on click-through rate, which rewards the wrong audience.

  • Lists that are too small to match: platforms and LinkedIn need a minimum matched audience, so tiny lists fail to launch or burn budget on a handful of people.
  • No frequency discipline: without account-level caps, a few accounts absorb most impressions.
  • Ignoring minimum commitments: ask for minimum spend, managed-service fees and contract length in writing.
  • No holdout: without a comparison group you can't tell whether ads changed anything.
  • Treating it as a marketing-only project: sales follow-up on engaged accounts is where the value lands.

What else do buyers ask about account-based advertising platforms?

Most questions come down to minimum spend, list size and how long to wait for results. The short answers are below; confirm commercial terms with each vendor.

Is LinkedIn enough for account-based advertising?

For many B2B teams with a few hundred target accounts, yes. LinkedIn company lists and role targeting cover the core; you add a platform when you need off-LinkedIn reach, account-level frequency or unified reporting.

Do account-based advertising platforms require a minimum ad spend?

Some do and some don't, and terms change, so ask each vendor in writing. Managed and automated services usually scope their fee around your channels and media budget.

How large should a target account list be?

Large enough to match a usable audience on each channel and small enough that sales will follow up on engaged accounts. Check each platform's minimum matched audience before you build the list.

How long before account-based ads show results?

Expect to judge early engagement within weeks but pipeline effects over a full sales cycle. Set the measurement window to your typical cycle length before launch.

What is the difference between account-based advertising and retargeting?

Retargeting follows people who already visited your site. Account-based advertising reaches people at named companies whether or not they've visited, and measures results by account.

What's the verdict?

Start with LinkedIn company-list targeting and account-level reporting from your CRM, because it answers whether account-based ads work for your market at the lowest commitment. Move to Demandbase, 6sense or RollWorks when you need cross-channel reach and frequency control, and to Madison Logic or Metadata.io when execution capacity is the constraint.
