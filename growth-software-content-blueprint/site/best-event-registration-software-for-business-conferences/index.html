Best Event Registration Software for Business Conferences

Event registration software for a business conference has to do more than sell tickets: it sets up the attendee record that check-in, badges, sessions and sales follow-up all depend on. Cvent and Bizzabo suit large, complex conferences; Whova suits mid-sized events; Eventbrite and Tito suit simpler paid workshops and meetups.

Which event registration software should you choose for a business conference?

Choose based on how complicated your attendee record is. If you need multiple ticket types, approval rules, session sign-ups, onsite badge printing and CRM sync, look at Cvent, Bizzabo or Whova; if you are selling tickets to a workshop or customer meetup, Eventbrite or Tito will be faster to launch and easier to run.

  • Enterprise conferences with complex registration paths: Cvent.
  • Marketing-led conferences that want onsite check-in and badges in the same platform: Bizzabo.
  • Mid-sized conferences and associations that want registration, an event app and kiosk check-in together: Whova.
  • Public or semi-public events that benefit from a ticketing marketplace: Eventbrite.
  • Developer, community and workshop events that want lightweight, flexible ticketing: Tito.
  • Trade-show-style events where registration feeds matchmaking and exhibitor leads: Swapcard.

What should conference registration software handle?

At minimum, it should handle ticket types, custom fields, payments and refunds, confirmation emails, waitlists, check-in and a clean export. For B2B conferences, add session registration, group or company bookings, badge printing and a CRM or marketing automation sync, because registration data is lead data.

The fields you collect at registration decide what you can do later. Role and company let you segment follow-up; dietary and accessibility needs drive logistics; session choices drive room capacity. Collect what you will use, and nothing else, because every extra field lowers completion and raises privacy obligations.

What does each registration tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Cvent, Bizzabo and Whova connect registration to check-in, badges and the event app, which is what multi-day business conferences need. Eventbrite and Tito are quicker and cheaper to start but leave more of the conference operation to other tools.

Cvent: best for complex enterprise conferences

Cvent is a full event-management suite. Its documentation covers attendee check-in with OnArrival and QR-code check-in from the event app, with the registration confirmation number embedded in each attendee's code.

Strengths: handles complex registration paths and many events. Limitations: quote-based pricing and a long learning curve. Skip it if you run a single annual event with simple tickets.

Bizzabo: best for marketing-led conferences with onsite operations

Bizzabo's onsite event management page describes check-in and on-demand badge printing tied to registration data, and its registration page targets larger conferences.

Strengths: registration, onsite and analytics in one place. Limitations: priced and scoped for event teams, not occasional organizers. Skip it if your events are small and infrequent.

Whova: best for mid-sized conferences and associations

Whova combines registration software with an event app, and its kiosk app supports self check-in with on-demand badge printing. Pricing is not published as a price list.

Strengths: registration, badges and attendee app in one workflow. Limitations: you need a quote to budget. Skip it if you only need a ticket checkout.

Eventbrite: best for paid public workshops and meetups

Eventbrite's help center explains its ticketing fees: free tickets carry no ticketing fee, and on paid tickets buyers pay the fees by default unless you choose to absorb them. Its organizer check-in app handles ticket scanning at the door.

Strengths: fast setup and marketplace discovery. Limitations: limited B2B data handling and attendee-facing fees on paid tickets. Skip it if registration must sync richly to your CRM.

Tito: best for developer and community events

Tito's pricing page describes a per-ticket fee model with no monthly subscription and no charge for free events, with payment processing billed separately by your gateway. That page may not have been updated recently, so confirm current terms with Tito. It suits organizers who want flexible ticketing without an enterprise contract.

Strengths: simple economics and flexible ticket setup. Limitations: fewer conference features such as agenda apps and sponsor tools. Skip it if you need onsite badge printing and session management in one tool.

Swapcard: best when registration feeds matchmaking

Swapcard offers advanced event registration as part of a platform built around matchmaking and exhibitor leads.

Strengths: registration data powers meetings and exhibitor lead capture. Limitations: most valuable for events with exhibitors. Skip it if you are running a workshop with no sponsors.

What does a conference registration runbook look like?

A runbook turns registration from a form into an operation, with owners and deadlines for each stage. Use the sequence below as a starting template and adapt the timing to your event's size.

  1. Define ticket types and rules: early, standard, group, speaker, sponsor and comp tickets, each with eligibility and price rules.
  2. Design fields: required fields for routing and logistics, conditional fields for sessions and accessibility needs, and consent checkboxes.
  3. Set payment and refund policy: payment methods, invoices for company purchases, refund cut-off and transfer rules.
  4. Configure capacity and waitlists: overall and per-session caps, with automatic or manual waitlist release.
  5. Connect integrations: sync registrants to the CRM or marketing automation with source and ticket type; test with a tagged registration.
  6. Plan communications: confirmation, reminders, know-before-you-go and post-event emails.
  7. Prepare check-in: QR codes, badge templates by ticket type, kiosks or staffed desks, printers and an offline fallback.
  8. Run event day: walk-in registration, badge reprints, session scanning and an escalation contact for the platform.
  9. Close out: export attendance, reconcile payments and refunds, and hand segmented lists to sales within an agreed window.

From the documentation reviewed, most failures cluster at check-in: mismatched records, missing badges and attendees registered under the wrong ticket type. What mistakes do teams make with event registration software?

The costly mistakes are collecting fields nobody uses, launching without a tested CRM sync, and leaving refund and transfer rules undefined until someone asks. Each one is cheap to fix before registration opens and expensive afterwards.

  • Using a consumer ticketing tool for a conference that needs session registration and badges.
  • Buying an enterprise suite for a single workshop.
  • Not planning walk-ins and name changes, which on-demand badge printing handles better than pre-printed badges.
  • Exporting attendee lists by hand instead of syncing them; our guide to event marketing automation integrations covers the options.

What else do buyers ask about event registration software?

The common follow-ups cover fees, invoices, waitlists and data ownership. Answers are general; check each vendor's terms.

Who pays the ticketing fees, the organizer or the attendee?

It depends on the platform and your settings. Some tools pass fees to buyers by default and let you absorb them, while others bill the organizer through a contract.

Can attendees pay by invoice instead of card?

Many business-focused platforms support invoices or offline payments, but consumer ticketing tools may not. Confirm invoice support if companies buy tickets for their staff.

Do I need a waitlist?

If any session or the event itself can sell out, yes. A waitlist captures demand you would otherwise lose and lets you release seats from cancellations.

Who owns the attendee data?

You should, but read the vendor's terms on data use and marketplace marketing. Confirm you can export full attendee records at any time.

Is on-demand badge printing worth it?

For conferences with walk-ins, name changes or late registrations, usually yes. Pre-printed badges are cheaper for small, fixed guest lists.
