Which ecommerce affiliate tracking tool should you choose?

Match the tool to your partner mix: creator and coupon-heavy programs on Shopify are well served by store apps, while programs that depend on content publishers, cashback sites and comparison sites need publisher-grade tracking or a network. The real test is order sync, because a tool that can't see refunds, cancellations and edits will overpay partners.

  • Small Shopify store launching its first program: UpPromote or GoAffPro.
  • Shopify brand recruiting creators who already shop with it: Shopify Collabs.
  • Growing DTC brand mixing creators, coupons and affiliates: Refersion.
  • Large brand with publishers, creators and strategic partners: impact.com.
  • Teams that want server-side tracking and strong fraud tooling: Everflow.
  • Brands that need access to a large publisher base: Awin.

Who is this list for, and what makes ecommerce tracking different?

This list is for ecommerce and DTC growth teams that pay partners per sale and need those payments to be right. Ecommerce tracking differs from SaaS tracking because orders change after checkout: items are returned, orders are cancelled, discounts stack and the same customer may arrive through a creator's link and a coupon site's code.

What are the strengths and limits of each tool?

Store apps win on speed and price but stop short on publisher tracking and fraud depth, while impact.com, Everflow and Awin cover those gaps at a higher cost and admin load. Refersion sits in between for brands that have outgrown a basic app.

UpPromote: best for small Shopify stores

As publicly positioned (still to be confirmed on its official site), UpPromote is a Shopify affiliate and influencer app with link and coupon tracking, an affiliate portal and recruitment features. It's quick to install and reads Shopify orders directly. Plans are tiered; check which tier unlocks automatic payouts and advanced commission rules. Skip it if you sell mainly off Shopify.

GoAffPro: best for cost-conscious stores

GoAffPro is a budget-positioned affiliate app with coupon and link tracking; confirm which commission structures and store platforms it currently supports. It's a reasonable way to prove the channel before paying more. Skip it if you need detailed fraud review or hands-on support.

Shopify Collabs: best for Shopify brands recruiting creators

Shopify Collabs is Shopify's own app for creator affiliate programs, letting brands find and invite creators, send products and track sales with links and codes. It's native to Shopify orders, which keeps sync simple. Skip it if your partners are mostly publishers, cashback or comparison sites.

Refersion: best for growing DTC brands

Refersion tracks affiliates and creators through links and coupon codes, integrates with major ecommerce platforms and offers commission rules by product and partner group. It suits brands whose partner base has outgrown a basic app. Confirm how it adjusts commissions on refunds and order edits for your platform, and what each tier caps. Skip it if you only have a handful of partners.

impact.com: best for large multi-partner programs

impact.com manages publishers, creators, affiliates and strategic partners in one platform, with contracting, payments, partner discovery and fraud review. It's built for brands where partnerships are a major revenue line. Skip it if nobody owns the program full time.

Everflow: best for server-side tracking and fraud review

Everflow is a partner marketing platform used by performance teams, with server-to-server tracking, flexible payout rules and fraud tooling. Server-side tracking helps when browser cookies are unreliable. It has a steeper setup than a store app. Skip it if you want plug-and-play.

Awin: best for reaching established publishers

Awin is an affiliate network, so you gain access to its publishers and its tracking in exchange for network fees on top of partner commissions. It suits brands whose growth depends on content, deal and cashback publishers they can't recruit directly. Skip it if your program is creator-led and you want full control of terms.

How should attribution, coupons and refunds be handled?

Decide one attribution rule for links and coupons, then make sure commissions are only approved after your return window closes. Without those two rules, coupon sites and late refunds will quietly inflate your affiliate cost.

  • Link versus coupon conflicts: decide whether a coupon code overrides a link click from a different partner. Many brands credit the coupon owner, because the customer chose it, but write the rule down.
  • Coupon leakage: creator codes end up on coupon aggregator sites. Track which partner's code is used and from where. Our list of coupon and discount attribution tools covers this problem.
  • Order status: commission should follow the final order value after cancellations, partial refunds and exchanges.
  • Approval lock: hold commissions until the return window ends, then approve in a batch.
  • Segmented rates: set different commissions for creators, content publishers and deal sites, and exclude low-margin products if needed.

Do coupon and cashback partners add new sales, or just take credit for them?

It varies by partner, and no vendor page can tell you the answer for your store. The usual warning sign is a partner whose orders arrive at the last step of checkout from customers who already had items in their cart.

How does the choice change for DTC and marketplace-heavy stores?

A DTC brand selling mainly on its own store can track nearly every affiliate order, so a store app or Refersion usually covers it. A brand selling heavily through marketplaces can only track orders on its own store, so affiliate spend should be judged on site sales, not total revenue.

Scenario A, DTC brand (hypothetical): a Shopify store with around 40 creators and a few content sites. A Shopify-native app or Refersion fits, with coupon codes for creators, a return-window hold and a lower rate for deal sites. Revisit the choice when publishers become a larger share of partner revenue.

Scenario B, marketplace-heavy brand (hypothetical): a brand where most revenue comes from third-party marketplaces and its own store is smaller. Affiliate software only sees store orders, so creators who send fans to a marketplace won't be credited. Keep the program focused on store-exclusive products or bundles, and judge partners only on store sales.

What mistakes do brands make when buying tracking software?

The most common mistake is approving commissions on gross orders before returns are known. The second is paying a network premium before testing whether your own creators and customers can carry the program.

  • Not testing refund and cancellation sync before launch.
  • Letting creator codes run without monitoring where they're shared.
  • Ignoring payout operations such as tax forms, currencies and thresholds.
  • Adding another app without checking overlap with your existing stack. Our guide to building an ecommerce growth stack without app overload helps with that review.

What else do ecommerce teams ask about affiliate tracking?

These answers cover the operational questions that follow a tool choice. Check plan specifics on each vendor's official pages, because tiers and fees change.

Should I join an affiliate network or run my own program?

Run your own program if your partners are creators and customers you can recruit directly. Join a network when you need established publishers and can absorb the network's fees.

How long should I hold commissions before paying?

Hold them at least until your return window closes. That way refunded orders never reach a payout and you avoid clawing money back from partners.

Who gets credit when a link click and a coupon code conflict?

There's no universal rule, so pick one and publish it in your partner terms. Many brands credit the coupon owner, but consistency matters more than the choice.

Can affiliate software track sales on marketplaces?

Usually not, because the order happens outside your store. Judge partners on your own store sales and use marketplace-specific programs where they exist.

What's the verdict?

Start with a Shopify-native app if your program is small and creator-led, move to Refersion as partners multiply, and pay for impact.com, Everflow or Awin only when publishers, fraud review or recruitment reach justify the cost. Whatever you choose, test refunds and coupon conflicts before your first payout.
