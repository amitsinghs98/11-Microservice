What can marketing attribution actually tell a small team?

Attribution tells you which recorded touchpoints preceded a conversion, and how much credit a chosen rule gives each one. It cannot tell you what caused the sale, and for small teams with small samples, its numbers deserve wide error bars.

That is the frame for choosing marketing attribution tools for small business: compare them by the question each can answer honestly, not by the dashboard. A web analytics tool answers "which channel brought the session?", a CRM answers "which campaigns touched this deal?", and only an experiment answers "would this sale have happened anyway?"

How do the main attribution models differ?

First-touch gives all credit to the first recorded interaction, last-touch to the final one, and multi-touch spreads credit across the path by a rule or an algorithm. Self-reported attribution asks the buyer, and incrementality testing measures lift against a control group.

  • First-touch: good for judging what creates awareness, but it ignores everything that closed the deal.
  • Last-touch: good for judging what converts, but it over-credits branded search and direct visits that were caused by earlier work.
  • Multi-touch (linear, time-decay, position-based, data-driven): more balanced, but only across touchpoints the tool can see. Algorithmic models need conversion volume that many small teams don't have.
  • Self-reported: a required "How did you hear about us?" field on the demo or signup form. It captures podcasts, word of mouth and private shares that no tracking tool sees, but buyers misremember and pick the first plausible answer.
  • Incrementality: holdout or geo tests that pause a channel for part of the audience and compare results. It is the closest thing to causal evidence, but needs enough volume and patience to run cleanly.

How does one buyer journey look under each model?

The same deal gets credited to a different channel under almost every model, which is why no single report should set your budget. Here is a hypothetical journey for a B2B software purchase.

  1. The buyer sees a colleague share your post in a private Slack channel. Nothing is tracked.
  2. Weeks later they search your brand name and read two blog posts (organic search).
  3. They subscribe to the newsletter and click two issues (email).
  4. They click a retargeting ad but don't convert (paid social).
  5. They type your URL directly and book a demo (direct).

First-touch credits organic search; last-touch credits direct; a linear model splits credit four ways; and the self-reported field says "a colleague recommended you". Each answer is partly right. Only the last one names the touch that started the journey, and no tool recorded it.

Why is so much of the journey missing from the data?

Tracking only sees clicks on links it can tag, on devices and browsers that allow it. Private shares, podcasts, events, word of mouth and multi-device research, often called dark social, sit outside that view.

Consent requirements and browser privacy features remove more: visitors who decline cookies, block scripts or switch devices appear as new, direct or unattributed. For B2B, the buying group adds another gap: the person who booked the demo is often not the person who first found you.

Which tools answer which attribution questions?

Web analytics answers channel questions, CRM attribution answers campaign-to-revenue questions, dedicated B2B platforms answer account-level path questions, and a form field answers the "where did you really hear about us" question. Pick by the question you need answered most.

Web analytics: GA4 and alternatives

GA4 is where most small teams start. Google's Analytics help documentation describes its reporting attribution settings; at the time of writing GA4 offers data-driven and last-click options, after Google retired several rule-based models, so check the current list before you build reports on one. If GA4 doesn't fit, our GA4 alternatives guide covers complementary tools, and the marketing analytics software shortlist covers cross-channel reporting.

CRM attribution: HubSpot and Salesforce

CRM attribution ties campaigns to contacts and deals, which is closer to revenue than web analytics. HubSpot places multi-touch revenue attribution reports in its higher Marketing Hub editions, and Salesforce offers campaign influence reporting; confirm which edition includes what before you buy for this feature. Both see only touches recorded against a known contact.

B2B attribution platforms

Dreamdata and HockeyStack position themselves as B2B revenue attribution platforms that join web, ad and CRM data at the account level. They suit teams with long, multi-person buying journeys and enough deal volume to make the paths meaningful. Skip them if you close a handful of deals a quarter; a CRM report and a form field will tell you nearly as much. Ruler Analytics positions itself around linking form fills and calls to CRM revenue, which suits lead-generation businesses where many conversions happen by phone. Dedicated call tracking software for paid acquisition covers the phone side in more depth.

How should a small team combine these methods?

Triangulate: read a channel report, a CRM source report and the self-reported field side by side, and act only where at least two agree. Reserve incrementality tests for your largest spend line.

  • Monthly: compare first-touch and last-touch pipeline by channel in the CRM, alongside self-reported answers grouped into the same channels.
  • Quarterly: run one holdout test on your biggest paid channel or email programme.
  • Always: report attributed numbers as ranges or shares, not exact revenue per channel.

What else do small teams ask about attribution?

These are the questions that follow the first attribution report. The short answers are below.

Which attribution model is best for a small business?

No single model is best. Use first-touch and last-touch side by side in the CRM, add a self-reported source field, and trust a conclusion only where two of those views agree.

Do small teams need a dedicated attribution platform?

Usually not at first. A CRM that stores original and latest source, plus web analytics and a self-reported form field, answers most questions until deal volume and journey length justify a dedicated tool.

What is dark social in B2B marketing?

It is sharing and discovery that tracking tools cannot see, such as private messages, community channels, podcasts and word of mouth. It usually shows up in analytics as direct or unattributed traffic.

How is incrementality different from attribution?

Attribution assigns credit for conversions that happened. Incrementality compares a group that saw the marketing with one that did not, to estimate how many conversions the marketing actually caused.

What should you set up first?

Add a required self-reported source field to your highest-intent form this week, and make sure your CRM stores original and latest source for every contact. Those two steps cost nothing and make every later tool more useful.
