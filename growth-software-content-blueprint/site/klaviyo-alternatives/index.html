Which Klaviyo alternative fits your reason for switching?

For lower cost with ready-made ecommerce flows, start with Omnisend, Drip or Brevo. For SMS as the main channel, compare Attentive and Postscript; for more data and channel depth than Klaviyo offers, look at Bloomreach.

  • Bill is rising faster than revenue: Omnisend for email and SMS in one simpler tool, or Brevo if you'd rather pay for sends than stored profiles.
  • You use a fraction of Klaviyo's features: Drip or Omnisend cover the core ecommerce flows with a lighter interface.
  • SMS is where the revenue is: Attentive or Postscript, often run alongside a separate email platform.
  • You need a CDP-grade data layer: Bloomreach, priced by quote.
  • Small catalog, familiar tool wanted: Mailchimp still covers basic store email for teams that don't need predictive segments.

What does Klaviyo do well that a replacement must match?

Klaviyo's strength is store event data: purchases, browsing and catalog activity flow into profiles and drive segments, flows and predictive analytics. Any alternative has to reproduce that data feed reliably before its lower price means anything.

That matters because ecommerce email revenue usually comes from flows such as welcome, cart abandonment, browse abandonment, post-purchase and winback, and every one depends on the right event arriving on time. A cheaper platform with a weaker store sync can cost more in missed flow revenue than it saves in fees. Klaviyo also combines email and SMS in one profile, so consent and suppression are shared across channels.

Why do ecommerce brands switch away from Klaviyo?

The legitimate triggers are cost that rises with profiles, SMS spend that's hard to forecast, and complexity a small team doesn't use. Larger brands sometimes leave for the opposite reason: they want a deeper data layer or more channels than Klaviyo provides.

  • Profile growth. As your list grows, so does the bill, even if many profiles rarely buy.
  • SMS budgeting. Message costs vary by country, length and media, so the SMS line can move more than the email line.
  • Unused depth. Teams of one or two often run five flows and a weekly campaign, and pay for capability they never touch.
  • Outgrowing it. Brands with many data sources and channels may want a full customer data platform.

How do email, SMS and profile billing differ between Klaviyo and its alternatives?

Klaviyo bills email on active profiles, with a send allowance tied to each tier, and bills SMS separately as you send. Alternatives use contact tiers, send volume or per-message SMS pricing, so the cheapest option depends on whether your cost driver is list size, send frequency or text volume.

Klaviyo explains its model in how Klaviyo pricing works, including which profiles count toward the active total. Read that page with your own numbers: how many profiles are reachable, how many you mail monthly and how many SMS subscribers you have.

  • Profile or contact billing (Klaviyo, Omnisend, Mailchimp, Drip): you pay for list size, so suppressing and sunsetting unengaged profiles directly lowers cost.
  • Send-volume billing (Brevo's plans are built around this): you pay for emails sent, so a large list mailed rarely costs less.
  • SMS billing (all of them): usually per message or per credit, varying by destination, message length and MMS. Ask whether carrier fees are included or passed through.

Which Klaviyo alternatives cost less at higher volumes?

Omnisend and Drip usually suit stores that want similar ecommerce flows for less complexity, while Brevo suits large lists that are mailed rarely. Whether any of them is actually cheaper depends on your profile count, send frequency and SMS volume, so price all three against your own numbers.

Omnisend: best for smaller stores adding SMS

Omnisend bundles email, SMS and prebuilt automations such as cart, browse and post-purchase flows, with native Shopify and WooCommerce integrations. A good fit for a small team that wants working flows in a day. Skip it if you rely on predictive segments like expected next order date, or on custom event modeling.

Drip: best for email-led DTC brands

Drip focuses on ecommerce email with visual workflows and store-based segmentation. It suits brands whose revenue comes mainly from email flows rather than SMS. Consider another option if SMS is a growing share of revenue.

Brevo: best for large, quiet lists

Brevo bundles marketing and transactional email, SMS and a basic CRM, with plans built around how much you send. It suits brands with big lists they email a few times a month. Skip it if you rely on deep product-level personalization or daily campaigns to the whole list.

Mailchimp: best for simple stores that want a familiar tool

Mailchimp connects to common store platforms and covers basic abandonment and product emails. Consider another option if flows drive most of your revenue. Our Mailchimp alternatives guide covers when brands outgrow it.

Which alternatives are better if SMS drives your revenue?

Attentive and Postscript specialize in SMS, with deeper list-growth, compliance and conversational tooling than most email-first platforms. Many brands run one of them for texting and keep a separate email platform.

Attentive: best for larger SMS programs

Attentive sells SMS and email to larger brands, with pricing by quote. It's a good fit when texting is a primary revenue channel and you want a managed program. Skip it if your SMS list is small or you can't justify a sales-led contract.

Postscript: best for Shopify brands building SMS

Postscript is built for Shopify SMS marketing, with plan pricing and usage-based messaging. A good fit for Shopify stores that want a specialist texting tool. Consider another option if you aren't on Shopify or want one platform for email and SMS. Our SMS marketing software guide for ecommerce brands covers the field.

Splitting email and SMS across two vendors means two consent records and two suppression lists. Decide which system is the source of truth for opt-outs and test the sync before launch.

Which alternatives go further than Klaviyo on data and channels?

Bloomreach is the most common step up, combining a customer data platform with email, SMS and on-site personalization. It suits brands with several data sources and an analyst or data team, at a quote-based price and a heavier implementation.

What changes in cost at 5,000, 25,000 and 100,000 profiles?

At 5,000 profiles, platform fees are close and your time and SMS spend matter most. At 25,000 the gap between billing models widens, and at 100,000 list hygiene and negotiated pricing usually outweigh any list-price difference.

Vendor tiers change often, so instead of quoting prices that would date, here is what to model at each size. Run each scenario through every shortlisted vendor's calculator, and price both today's list and the list you expect in 12 months.

About 5,000 profiles

Price differences between email platforms are usually small in absolute terms. The decision should rest on how quickly you can launch working flows and how much SMS you'll send. A free or entry tier may be enough to test fit before committing.

About 25,000 profiles

This is where profile billing and send billing diverge. Compare the profile-based quote with a send-based one using your real campaign frequency, and calculate how many profiles a sunset policy would suppress. SMS often becomes the fastest-growing line here.

About 100,000 profiles

Ask every vendor for an annual quote, because negotiated terms and contract length often matter more than list price. Model SMS separately by destination country and message type, and ask whether onboarding or migration help is included.

What should you check before switching from Klaviyo?

Check that the new platform's store integration syncs the events your flows depend on, that historical orders and catalog data backfill, and that SMS consent moves with its proof. Then rebuild flows by hand, because they don't export between vendors.

  1. Integration reliability. Confirm which store events sync natively, how quickly they arrive and whether historical orders backfill. Test with real orders in a trial.
  2. Event mapping. List every custom event and property your flows use and map each to the new platform's names.
  3. Predictive fields. Predicted values computed by Klaviyo won't transfer, so plan how segments will be rebuilt once the new tool has enough history.
  4. Flow rebuild. Recreate the highest-revenue flows first, test every branch and run them in parallel briefly before switching off the old ones.
  5. SMS consent and numbers. Export SMS consent with timestamps and source. Ask whether your sending number can move or must be re-registered, and pause texting until consent records are in place.
  6. Suppressions. Import email unsubscribes, bounces and SMS opt-outs before the first send.
  7. Deliverability. Authenticate your sending domain and ramp volume from engaged segments outward.

What else do ecommerce teams ask about Klaviyo alternatives?

Follow-up questions usually cover whether a switch is cheaper, whether flows can be moved and how SMS should be handled. Brief answers follow.

Is switching from Klaviyo always cheaper?

No. A lower subscription can be offset by weaker store data, lost flow revenue and migration time. Compare total cost and expected flow performance, not list price alone.

Can I export my Klaviyo flows to another platform?

Not directly. Profiles, consent and many properties can be exported, but flows have to be rebuilt by hand in the new platform.

Should email and SMS stay in one platform?

One platform keeps consent and suppression in one place, which is simpler. Splitting makes sense when an SMS specialist adds enough revenue to justify syncing two systems.

Which alternative suits a Shopify store under 10,000 profiles?

Omnisend and Drip are reasonable starting points for email-led stores, and Postscript is worth a look if SMS matters. Trial each with your real store connected.

What's the verdict on Klaviyo alternatives?

In our editorial assessment, Omnisend is the most practical swap for smaller stores, Drip for email-led brands, Brevo for large and quiet lists, Attentive and Postscript for SMS-first programs, and Bloomreach for brands that have outgrown Klaviyo's data layer. Stay on Klaviyo if your flows perform and the bill tracks revenue.
