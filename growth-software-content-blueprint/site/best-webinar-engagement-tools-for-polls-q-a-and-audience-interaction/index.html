Which webinar engagement tool should you choose?

If your webinar platform's built-in polls and Q&A already export per attendee, use them and skip an add-on; if they don't, Slido is a strong add-on candidate for moderated Q&A and polls, while Mentimeter suits presenter-led interactive content. Pigeonhole Live and Vevox fit moderated, governance-heavy sessions, and Poll Everywhere suits simple audience-response polling across many presenters.

The useful distinction is between add-ons, which sit on top of whatever hosts your video, and hosting platforms with native interaction. Webinar engagement tools are worth paying for only when the signals they collect, such as questions, poll answers and quiz results, reach your follow-up process tied to a named attendee.

  • Add-on, moderated Q&A at scale: Slido
  • Add-on, presenter-led interactive slides: Mentimeter
  • Add-on, governance and moderation: Vevox and Pigeonhole Live
  • Add-on, simple audience response: Poll Everywhere
  • Native interaction in the host: Livestorm and ON24

Who needs a separate webinar engagement tool?

You need a separate tool when your hosting platform's interaction features are too basic, don't export per attendee, or differ across the several platforms your team uses. Teams that already get attendee-level poll and Q&A exports from their webinar platform rarely need an add-on.

The strongest case for an add-on is consistency. Agencies running sessions on clients' platforms, or companies using Zoom for some events and a virtual-event platform for others, get one interaction layer and one export format. The weakest case is novelty: word clouds and reactions look good in a demo but produce little a salesperson can use.

Do polls and Q&A actually produce better follow-up?

Only if the answers are designed to qualify and are routed to someone who acts on them; a poll asking how attendees feel produces a nice chart and no follow-up. The product information we reviewed describes collection and export, not results, so the follow-up gain is something you'll need to measure yourself.

What does each engagement tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Slido and Pigeonhole Live are strongest on moderated Q&A, Mentimeter on interactive presentation content, and Vevox and Poll Everywhere on broad audience-response needs. Livestorm and ON24 matter when you'd rather not add a tool at all.

Slido: best for moderated Q&A across platforms

Slido offers live polls, quizzes and audience Q&A with upvoting and moderation, and it integrates with several video platforms. Strengths: familiar to audiences and flexible across hosts. Limitations: lead-follow-up value depends on whether participants are identified, so check how names and emails are captured. Skip it if your host's native Q&A already exports per attendee.

Mentimeter: best for presenter-led interactive slides

Mentimeter builds polls, word clouds, quizzes and Q&A into a presentation deck. Strengths: it makes a training-style session genuinely interactive. Limitations: it's designed around presenting, not lead qualification, so you'll design exports around your follow-up yourself. Skip it if your goal is pipeline data rather than audience participation.

Vevox: best for governed, often anonymous Q&A

Vevox covers polling, surveys and moderated Q&A, and is commonly positioned for internal communications and education as well as events. Strengths: moderation controls suit sensitive audiences. Limitations: anonymous participation is good for candour and bad for attribution. Skip it if every interaction must map to a lead.

Pigeonhole Live: best for conference-style moderation

Pigeonhole Live focuses on moderated Q&A, polls and surveys for live and virtual events. Strengths: moderation workflows suit panels with many questions. Limitations: its event orientation may be more than a short weekly webinar needs. Skip it if you run simple single-speaker sessions.

Poll Everywhere: best for simple audience response

Poll Everywhere offers audience polls and Q&A that work across presentation software. Strengths: easy for many presenters to adopt. Limitations: check whether its integrations reach your webinar host and CRM. Skip it if interaction data must reach marketing automation automatically.

Livestorm and ON24: when native features are enough

Livestorm includes polls, Q&A, chat and reactions inside its browser-based rooms, and ON24 builds interaction into its engagement scoring. Strengths: interaction lands on the same attendee record as attendance. Limitations: you're tied to that host for every event. Skip them if you present on many different platforms.

What should you check for accessibility and chat control?

Check that participants can vote and ask questions with a keyboard and a screen reader, that captions are available in the host, and that moderators can hold, approve and hide messages before they appear. Ask each vendor for its accessibility conformance documentation rather than relying on a marketing claim.

Chat controls matter more than they sound in B2B. A public chat with competitor questions or off-topic promotion distracts your panel, and an unmoderated Q&A wall can surface a complaint in front of prospects. Pre-moderation, private questions and the option to turn chat off are basic requirements.

How do you connect interactions to follow-up?

Map every poll and every question category to a follow-up action before the session, then confirm the tool exports answers per attendee into your CRM or marketing-automation platform. Without that mapping, interaction data stays in a report nobody opens.

  1. Write one qualifying poll per session with fixed answer options that match CRM fields.
  2. Tag questions by theme during moderation, such as pricing, integration or competitor.
  3. Sync answers to contact fields or campaign-member records, not just an event summary.
  4. Route high-intent answers to the account owner within a day, and send everyone else a replay with answers to the top questions.

What else do buyers ask about webinar engagement tools?

Buyers mostly ask whether native features are enough and how to handle anonymity and replays. The answers below assume interaction data should support sales follow-up.

Should we use our webinar platform's polls or an add-on?

Use the native polls if they export per attendee and integrate with your CRM. Add a separate tool when you need consistency across several hosts or deeper moderation than the platform offers.

Are anonymous questions useful for B2B webinars?

They can increase candour in sensitive sessions, but you lose the ability to follow up with the person who asked. Offer anonymity for specific sessions rather than by default.

How many polls should a webinar include?

Fewer than most presenters plan. One qualifying poll and one or two content polls keep attention without turning the session into a survey, and each should map to a follow-up action.

Do engagement tools work with on-demand replays?

Usually not in the same way, since live polls close when the session ends. If replays matter, check whether the host supports on-demand interaction or add a short form to the replay page.

What's the verdict on webinar engagement tools?

Start by checking what your webinar host already exports; for many teams that removes the need for an add-on. If you do need one, Slido is a sensible cross-platform starting point, Mentimeter suits interactive teaching, and Vevox or Pigeonhole Live suit heavily moderated events.
