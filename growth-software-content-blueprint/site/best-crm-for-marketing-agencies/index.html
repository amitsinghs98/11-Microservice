Which CRM should a marketing agency choose?

Pick HubSpot if your agency wins work through its own content and email, Pipedrive if two or three people run new business from referrals, and monday CRM or Productive if you want a won deal to become a delivery project in the same tool. Insightly and Zoho CRM are credible second looks for smaller budgets.

  • Inbound-led agencies: HubSpot, for one timeline from form fill to signed retainer.
  • Founder-led new business: Pipedrive, for a fast, clear deal board.
  • Delivery already on monday.com: monday CRM.
  • Margin and utilization matter as much as pipeline: Productive.
  • CRM with simple built-in projects: Insightly.
  • Configurable and budget-conscious: Zoho CRM.

What does an agency's sales pipeline need to track?

An agency pipeline needs five stages (lead, discovery, proposal, negotiation and onboarding handoff), a required lead-source field, and the ability to hold several open deals against one client account. Without those three things, you can't see which channels produce retainers or which clients are ready for an upsell.

Here's the hypothetical model we judged each CRM against: a 5 to 10 person agency selling SEO, paid media and content retainers.

  1. Lead: a referral, an inbound form or an outbound reply, with source and referrer recorded.
  2. Discovery: a booked call, notes and a qualification checklist covering budget, scope and timing.
  3. Proposal: scope, pricing and a proposal document sent and tracked.
  4. Negotiation and signature: revisions, contract and start date.
  5. Onboarding handoff: the won deal creates a client record and kickoff task in the delivery tool.

Two agency quirks shape the choice. One client often has a retainer renewal and a separate website project open at once, so you need multiple pipelines and clean account-to-contact links. And partners refer a lot of agency work, so the CRM should let you report on partner-sourced revenue.

Where does each CRM fit, and where does it fall short?

Each tool below suits a different agency shape, and none is the right answer for every one. Pricing facts come from vendor pages; fit judgments are our editorial assessment.

HubSpot: best for agencies that win work through their own marketing

HubSpot connects form submissions, email engagement and deal records on one timeline, so you can trace which webinar or article produced a signed retainer. Many agency staff already know it from client work, which shortens training. Seats are priced by hub and tier on the HubSpot Sales Hub pricing page.

Limitation: full campaign attribution relies on Marketing Hub, and the Professional tiers are a big step up. Skip it if nearly all your work comes from referrals and you won't use the marketing side.

Pipedrive: best for founder-led new business

Pipedrive's deal board maps neatly onto lead, discovery, proposal and signature, which suits an agency where the founder and one or two leads run sales. Its pricing page lists a Projects add-on for post-sale work and a Smart Docs add-on for proposals (checked 4 November 2026).

Limitation: marketing attribution is light, and the document and project add-ons raise the per-seat cost. Skip it if you need resourcing and margin tracking in the same tool.

monday CRM: best when delivery already runs on monday.com

Because monday CRM sits on the same work-management platform as monday.com projects, a won deal can trigger an automation that creates a client delivery board. If your team already lives there, that's the cleanest handoff on this list. Check seat options on the monday CRM pricing page.

Limitation: you build your own pipeline structure, and attribution reporting needs extra setup. Skip it if delivery runs in Asana, ClickUp or another tool.

Productive: best for agencies that care about profit per client

Productive is agency management software rather than a pure CRM. Its sales pipeline sits beside budgeting, time tracking, resource planning and invoicing, so a proposal can become a budget and a project in one system. Tiers are per user on the Productive pricing page.

Limitation: email sequencing and marketing attribution aren't its focus. Skip it if outbound prospecting is central to your growth plan.

Insightly: best for small agencies wanting CRM and projects together

Insightly can convert a won opportunity into a project, keeping contact, account and delivery history linked. See per-user tiers on the Insightly pricing page.

Limitation: project features are simpler than dedicated delivery tools, and the integration ecosystem is smaller than HubSpot's. Skip it if you already have a delivery platform you like.

Zoho CRM: best for cost-conscious agencies with an ops lead

Zoho CRM handles multiple pipelines, custom modules for partners and referrers, and workflow rules, while Zoho's separate project and email apps can cover delivery and newsletters from one vendor. Editions are per user on the Zoho CRM pricing page.

Limitation: the experience across Zoho apps is less unified than a single product. Skip it if nobody has time to own configuration.

Does the sales-to-delivery handoff hold up in practice?

Vendor documentation shows that a won deal can trigger a project or record in a delivery tool, but it can't show whether scope, budget and contacts arrive intact when an account manager is under deadline pressure. That gap between "integration exists" and "handoff works" is where agency CRM projects usually disappoint.

Should the sales CRM and the delivery system be separate?

Yes, for most agencies: let the CRM own leads, deals, proposals, renewal dates and lead source, and let the project tool own tasks, time, budgets and deliverables. Join them with one automated "deal won" event rather than asking one tool to do both jobs badly.

  • The CRM owns contacts, accounts, deals, proposal status, lead source, referrer and renewal date.
  • The delivery tool owns tasks, time, budgets, deliverables and day-to-day client communication.
  • The handoff carries scope, value, start date and the client contact list into a kickoff project.
  • Upsells flow back: account managers log expansion opportunities in the CRM, not in a project comment.

Client performance reporting belongs in neither system. Keep it in a dedicated tool from our client reporting software for agencies list, so CRM seats aren't bought just to show dashboards.

What do 5 and 10 seats cost?

On Pipedrive's checked annual-billing prices, five Growth seats cost US$2,340 a year and ten cost US$4,680, while five Premium seats cost US$3,540 and ten cost US$7,080, before add-ons and tax. Count the account managers who log upsells, not just the people who sell new business.

Those figures are seats multiplied by the published per-seat price and by 12 (checked 4 November 2026). An agency adding Projects and Smart Docs should price those separately. For the other vendors, apply the same arithmetic to their official pages and check three things:

  • HubSpot: whether only new-business staff need paid sales seats while others use free or view-only access.
  • monday CRM: whether 5 or 10 users match a purchasable seat option exactly.
  • Productive: whether it replaces a separate time-tracking or resourcing subscription, which changes the real comparison.

What else do agencies ask about CRM software?

The common follow-ups are about per-client CRMs, project tools as substitutes, and tracking referral partners. Here are direct answers.

Do agencies need a separate CRM for each client?

No. Your CRM is for your own new business and account growth. If you manage a pipeline on a client's behalf, that work belongs in the client's own CRM.

Can a project management tool replace an agency CRM?

For a very small agency with few leads, sometimes. Once you need lead source, proposal tracking and renewal dates, a dedicated CRM or an agency platform with a pipeline is much easier to report from.

How should an agency track referral partners in its CRM?

Create partners as companies or a custom record type and link every referred deal to the partner. That lets you report partner-sourced revenue and thank the right people.

Should proposals live inside the CRM?

Proposal status should, because it's a pipeline stage. The document itself can come from the CRM or a dedicated proposal tool, as long as the sent, viewed and signed events update the deal.
