Which LinkedIn Ads tool should you choose?

Teams already on HubSpot should start with its ads tool, because lead sync and CRM reporting live in the same place. Teams that need to prove pipeline from LinkedIn across a long buying cycle should compare Dreamdata, HockeyStack and Factors.ai, and teams that want campaign execution handled with pipeline targets should look at Metadata.io.

  • Building and running campaigns, with native reporting: LinkedIn Campaign Manager.
  • HubSpot CRM users wanting lead sync and ad-to-deal reporting: HubSpot ads tool.
  • Demand teams wanting campaign execution tied to pipeline: Metadata.io.
  • B2B attribution across long, multi-contact journeys: Dreamdata.
  • Revenue reporting that blends ads, web and CRM activity: HockeyStack.
  • Account-level engagement and LinkedIn-focused attribution: Factors.ai.
  • Analysts who want LinkedIn data in a sheet or warehouse: Supermetrics.

Why is cost per lead a weak way to judge LinkedIn Ads?

Cost per lead treats a student downloading an ebook the same as a VP of Finance at a target account, and LinkedIn's targeting makes that mix easy to get wrong. Judge LinkedIn by cost per qualified opportunity and by pipeline from target accounts, measured in the CRM.

A campaign tuned for the lowest cost per lead often drifts toward cheap audiences and gated content that sales won't touch. The opposite also happens: a campaign with an ugly cost per lead may be the one producing meetings with named accounts. You only see that once lead records carry campaign data into the CRM and the CRM records what became of them.

A practical scorecard runs down the funnel: spend, leads, leads matching your ideal customer profile, sales-accepted leads, opportunities, pipeline value and closed revenue, each divided by spend. Expect the lower rows to lag by weeks or months. That lag is why B2B teams need reporting that keeps campaign history attached to contacts.

What does each LinkedIn Ads tool do well, and when should you skip it?

Each block gives the best fit, the main strength and the reason to look elsewhere. Check current plan features and CRM support on the official pages before you shortlist.

LinkedIn Campaign Manager: best for running the campaigns themselves

Campaign Manager is LinkedIn's native ad manager, and every other tool here reads from or writes to it. It's where you set professional targeting, lead forms, website conversion tracking and budgets, and its native reporting includes audience breakdowns; confirm the current targeting and reporting options on LinkedIn's site. Skip it as your only tool once you need to see what happened after the lead reached sales.

HubSpot ads tool: best for teams whose CRM is HubSpot

HubSpot's ads tool is designed to connect ad accounts such as LinkedIn to HubSpot, so leads and ad interactions can be reported against contacts and deals; confirm current features on HubSpot's site. That shortens the path from campaign to closed revenue without a separate attribution product. The limit is obvious: if Salesforce is your system of record, much of the value disappears. Check which reporting features sit on which HubSpot tier, since our HubSpot Marketing Hub comparison shows how tiering shapes the cost.

Metadata.io: best for demand teams wanting execution tied to pipeline

Metadata.io positions itself as a B2B paid advertising platform that runs campaigns with pipeline-oriented reporting; check its current channel coverage and CRM requirements on the vendor's site. It suits teams without a dedicated paid specialist that still run meaningful LinkedIn budgets. Consider another option if you want full manual control in Campaign Manager or your spend is small.

Dreamdata: best for multi-touch B2B attribution

Dreamdata joins ad, website and CRM data into account-level customer journeys, then attributes pipeline and revenue across them. That's the clearest route from LinkedIn impressions and clicks to opportunities when buying committees involve several people over months. It's only as good as your CRM hygiene, so fix duplicate contacts and missing opportunity sources first; our guide to CRM data quality tools covers that step.

HockeyStack: best for blended revenue reporting

HockeyStack combines ad, web and CRM activity into revenue reporting and attribution for B2B teams. It's a fit when leadership wants one answer to "what drove pipeline this quarter" across LinkedIn, search, events and outbound. It doesn't manage campaigns, so you still run LinkedIn in Campaign Manager.

Factors.ai: best for account-level views of LinkedIn engagement

Factors.ai focuses on account identification, account journeys and attribution, with LinkedIn among its main ad sources. That suits ABM-minded teams who want to know which target accounts are engaging before anyone fills a form. Treat account identification from website traffic as directional, because it's inferred rather than confirmed.

Supermetrics: best for analysts building their own model

Supermetrics pulls LinkedIn Ads data into destinations such as spreadsheets or a warehouse (confirm current destinations on the vendor's site), where you can join it with CRM exports yourself. It's flexible and avoids another analytics platform, but someone has to own the joins and campaign mapping.

How should LinkedIn leads hand off to the CRM?

Every lead should arrive in the CRM within minutes with campaign, ad and form identifiers attached, and land in a routing rule that a named person owns. If campaign data is lost at handoff, no reporting tool can recover cost per qualified opportunity later.

  1. Connect Lead Gen Forms to the CRM natively or through an integration tool, and test with a real submission.
  2. Map hidden fields for campaign, campaign group and form, and keep UTM values on website conversions consistent with them.
  3. Set lead source and original campaign once and protect them from being overwritten.
  4. Route by ICP fit and account ownership, so leads from target accounts reach the right rep.
  5. Feed qualified-lead and opportunity stages back as offline conversions where your tools support it, so optimization learns from quality, not volume.

What mistakes do B2B teams make with LinkedIn Ads tools?

The biggest mistake is buying attribution before fixing lead handoff and CRM fields. The next is optimizing to platform-reported leads while sales quietly disqualifies most of them.

  • Leaving audience expansion on by default and drifting away from the target account list.
  • Reporting cost per lead to leadership without the qualified-opportunity row next to it.
  • Judging a campaign after two weeks when the sales cycle is two quarters.
  • Running spend with no account-level or campaign-group caps, so one test absorbs the monthly budget.

What else do B2B buyers ask about LinkedIn Ads tools?

The common questions are about lead sync, attribution and budget size. Here are short answers.

Do I need a third-party tool to sync LinkedIn Lead Gen Form leads to my CRM?

Not always. Some CRMs offer a direct Lead Gen Forms connection and integration tools may cover others, so check your CRM's native connector before paying for a separate sync product.

What is a good cost per qualified opportunity on LinkedIn?

There's no universal benchmark, because it depends on deal size, sales cycle and how you define qualified. Set your own baseline from the last two quarters of CRM data and judge changes against it.

Is LinkedIn worth it for small B2B budgets?

It can be if your target accounts are well defined and your offer suits a senior audience. With a small budget, narrow the audience, run fewer campaigns and judge results on qualified opportunities rather than lead volume.

Can LinkedIn's Insight Tag replace a B2B attribution tool?

No. The Insight Tag tracks website conversions and builds retargeting audiences, but it doesn't follow contacts into the CRM or connect ads to opportunities and revenue.

What's the verdict on LinkedIn Ads management tools?

Run campaigns in Campaign Manager, fix lead handoff into the CRM, then add the measurement layer that matches your CRM and budget: HubSpot's ads tool for HubSpot users, Dreamdata, HockeyStack or Factors.ai for pipeline attribution, Metadata.io if you want execution handled. Retire CPL as the headline number.
