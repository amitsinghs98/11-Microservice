How are AI content tools priced?

Most charge per person, then meter usage through fair-use limits, credits or media minutes, so two tools with the same headline price can cost very different amounts for your workload.

  • Per user with usage limits. General assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT charge per person and cap usage over time windows rather than by word.
  • Per seat with feature tiers. Marketing platforms such as Jasper reserve brand and collaboration features for higher tiers.
  • Flat plans plus credits. Copy.ai and Writesonic bundle seats or allowances into a plan, with automation or tracking metered separately.
  • Media meters. Video and design tools add minutes, exports or AI credits.

What do the main AI writing plans cost?

Entry paid plans run from $8 to $79 a month on the figures we could confirm, and team pricing ranges from published per-seat rates to sales quotes. Each block covers entry price, team pricing, billing, free plan or trial, and who should skip it.

One difference in evidence: Claude's page was fetched directly, while the other vendors' figures were confirmed through a search index of their official pages, so re-check every figure on the linked page.

Claude: published prices from free to Enterprise

Claude's pricing page lists Free at $0, Pro at $20 billed monthly or $17 a month on an annual subscription ($200 up front), Max at $100 and Ultra at $200 a month (checked 5 November 2026). Team runs from 2 to 150 seats: standard seats are $25 monthly or $20 annually, premium seats $125 or $100, and Enterprise is US$20 per seat billed annually with a 20-seat minimum plus usage at API rates.

Best for long-form drafting where you want every price published. Skip it if you need image generation in the same seat, because the plan comparison lists Design, Slides and Docs but no image generator.

ChatGPT: low entry price, monthly-only individual tiers

OpenAI's help center lists ChatGPT Go at $8 a month in the US, with local pricing in some markets, Plus at $20 a month, and Pro tiers at $100, $200 and $500 a month, billed monthly only (checked 5 November 2026). ChatGPT Business standard seats are $25 a user monthly and premium seats $125 monthly or $100 annually, with a two-seat minimum; Enterprise is quoted by sales (checked 5 November 2026).

Best for teams that want writing and image creation, which is available on all tiers, in one subscription. Skip it if your workflow depends on custom GPTs, which OpenAI plans to retire in favor of plugins, with a standard retirement date of 11 December 2026 (checked 5 November 2026). OpenAI's sources give conflicting annual rates for Business standard seats, so confirm that figure before you budget.

Jasper: single-seat Pro, quoted Business

Jasper's pricing page lists Pro at $69 per seat a month billed monthly or $59 billed yearly, with one seat and a 7-day free trial; Business is custom-priced and starts with a 12-month commitment (checked 5 November 2026). The Annual Plans help article confirms the $59 yearly Pro rate (checked 5 November 2026).

Best for marketing teams that will pay for brand-voice controls, with SSO and API access on Business. Skip it if you're a team of two to five that wants a published team price, because anything beyond one Pro seat means a sales conversation. Jasper's sources disagree on how credits apply to Business, so ask.

Copy.ai: one flat price for five chat seats

Copy.ai's pricing page lists the Chat plan at $29 a month billed monthly or $24 a month billed annually ($288 a year), with five seats, unlimited chat words and access to OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini models (checked 5 November 2026). Workflow automation sits on much larger plans metered in workflow credits, which the page says scale with the steps and content a workflow generates.

Best for small teams that mainly need shared chat drafting at the lowest per-seat cost here. Skip it if you want workflow automation on a small budget, and don't count on a free plan: official sources conflict on whether one exists and what it allows.

Writesonic: annual prices public, allowances unclear

Writesonic's pricing page and its plan documentation list Basic at $199 and Growth at $399 a month billed annually, with the pricing page adding Starter at $79 (checked 5 November 2026). Its signup page offers a 7-day free trial with no credit card (checked 5 November 2026).

Best for teams that want article generation alongside AI search visibility tracking. Skip it if you only need a writer, and confirm monthly prices, included users and article allowances on the live page, because the official sources we found disagree on all three.

Why isn't cost per word a fair comparison?

Because the words you pay for aren't the words you publish. A tool that produces 3,000 words you rewrite costs more in total than one that produces 1,200 words you lightly edit, even if its per-word rate is lower.

What workloads and assumptions do the scenarios use?

We use two units: an article is a researched 1,500 to 2,000-word post with sources, and an asset is a short piece such as a social post, ad variant, email or product description. All scenarios are hypothetical and assume a blended internal editing cost of $50 an hour, which you should replace with your own rate.

Editing estimates are 2.5 hours per article, 10 minutes per short asset, 1 hour per short video and 5 minutes per image. These are editorial assumptions, not measurements.

What do the three workloads cost?

On these assumptions, editing costs about $1,250 for 10 articles, $833 for 100 assets and $2,650 for a mixed five-person month, while the writing invoice ranges from $20 to $225 where prices are published. Editing time is over 90% of every total we could calculate, so it's the number to manage.

Does the subscription matter next to editing time?

Barely, on these assumptions, but the assumption most likely to be wrong is the editing estimate. If your real editing time per article is double ours, the tool you choose matters more, because a better draft is the only lever on the larger cost.

Should you pay monthly or annually?

Pay monthly until you know which roles use the tool every day, then move only those seats to annual. Listed annual discounts are about 20% on Claude Team standard seats and premium seats on both Claude and ChatGPT Business, about 17% on Claude Pro and Copy.ai Chat, and $59 instead of $69 a month on Jasper Pro.

ChatGPT's Pro tiers have no annual option, and Jasper's Business plan starts with a 12-month commitment. Ask whether annual seats can be reassigned when people leave, because an idle annual seat erases the discount.

What pushes you into the next tier?

Usage limits, a second user and security requirements, in roughly that order. Each has a cheaper response than upgrading everyone.

  • One person hits limits weekly: upgrade that person only, for example to a premium seat on Claude Team or ChatGPT Business.
  • A second writer joins: Claude and ChatGPT have published team seats, Copy.ai's Chat plan already includes five, and Jasper moves you to a quoted Business plan.
  • Clients need SSO or audit logs: Claude's pricing page lists SSO from Team and audit logs on Enterprise (checked 5 November 2026), which brings a 20-seat minimum.

At renewal, audit real logins. Our marketing stack audit guide shows how to find unused seats and overlapping tools.

What isn't public, and what should you ask sales?

Jasper Business and ChatGPT Enterprise are quoted, Claude Enterprise adds usage at API rates to its seat price, and renewal discounts are rarely public anywhere. Get them in writing before you sign.

  • How are usage limits or credits defined, and what happens when we hit them mid-month?
  • Which brand, collaboration and security features sit on which tier?
  • Can annual seats be reassigned or reduced at renewal?
  • Is our content used for model training, and can that be switched off on our plan?

What else do buyers ask about AI content costs?

The recurring questions are about fair per-article costs, platforms versus assistants, and free plans. Here are short answers.

What is a fair cost per AI-assisted article?

There is no universal figure. In hypothetical Workload A it is about $127 to $133 including editing, almost all of it human time, so your own editing hours and internal rate decide the answer.

Is a general assistant cheaper than a marketing platform?

On the invoice, usually yes for one writer, though Copy.ai's five-seat Chat plan undercuts per-seat assistants for small teams. Overall, it depends on whether a platform's brand and workflow features save enough editing time to cover the difference.

Are free plans enough for a content team?

For trying prompts, yes; for deadline work, rarely. Claude and ChatGPT both have free tiers with tighter limits, Jasper and Writesonic offer 7-day trials, and Copy.ai's free option is unclear from official sources.

How often should you re-check these numbers?

Every 60 to 90 days, re-dating every figure, because plan names and limits in this category change often. Writesonic's allowances, ChatGPT's annual Business rate and Copy.ai's free option are the figures to re-confirm first.
