Which retainer billing tool should you choose?

Choose a tool that tracks usage against the retainer, such as Accelo or Productive, if your retainers include hour allowances, rollover or overages. If your retainers are fixed fees with no allowance, a recurring invoice in your accounting software or an automated engagement in Ignition is simpler and cheaper to run.

  • Service businesses with hour-based retainers and rollover rules: Accelo.
  • Agencies that want retainer budgets tied to projects, time and profitability: Productive.
  • Firms that want the signed engagement to bill automatically: Ignition.
  • Agencies that want quoting, work and billing in one system: Scoro.
  • Teams that track time against monthly budgets and invoice from it: Harvest.
  • Fixed-fee retainers with no allowance to track: Xero repeating invoices.
  • Payment-first schedules with automatic card or bank retries: Stripe Billing.

How is a service retainer different from a SaaS subscription?

A SaaS subscription bills for access to a product at a set price, while a service retainer bills for people's time or deliverables, which vary month to month and need tracking, approval and sometimes carry-over. That's why subscription billing engines are good at charging cards but weak at answering "how many hours are left this month?"

Retainers also involve human judgement: an account manager decides whether a request is in scope, whether extra work was approved and whether unused hours roll forward. Subscription tools assume the price is the product. If you sell software or productized plans, our guides to subscription commerce software and subscription analytics are better starting points.

What does each tool do well, and where does it fall short?

Accelo and Productive are the strongest fits when the retainer is an allowance to track; Ignition and Stripe Billing are strongest at getting paid on schedule. Scoro, Harvest and Xero sit in between, each with a different centre of gravity.

Accelo: best for hour-based retainers with rollover and overage rules

Accelo is a professional services platform whose retainers feature is built around recurring service agreements, with allowances, periods, usage tracking and billing. It's a good fit for agencies and IT or consulting firms that sell blocks of hours each month and need to know what's left. The trade-off is weight: Accelo covers sales, projects, tickets and billing, so implementation takes time. Skip it if you only bill flat monthly fees. Check which plan includes retainers on the Accelo pricing page.

Productive: best for retainer budgets tied to margin

Productive is an agency management platform that combines budgets, time tracking, resourcing, invoicing and profitability reporting, with support for recurring retainer budgets. Its advantage is that you see a retainer's revenue and the cost of the hours spent on it side by side. It's less suited to firms that only want billing. Consider another option if you don't track time. See the Productive pricing page, and our guide to agency profitability reporting for the margin side.

Ignition: best when the signed engagement should start billing

Ignition links proposals, engagement letters and payment collection, so a client who signs a monthly retainer is billed on schedule without re-entering anything. That's ideal for fixed-fee retainers and for accounting-style firms. It's lighter on tracking hours against an allowance, so pair it with a time tool if you sell hour blocks. Skip it if your retainers change every month. See the Ignition pricing page.

Scoro: best for quote-to-bill in one system

Scoro covers quotes, projects, time, resourcing, billing and reporting, including recurring and retainer-style invoicing. One system means fewer syncs between a signed quote and the monthly invoice. The cost is setup effort, which suits mid-sized agencies more than freelancers. Consider another option if you're a very small team. Plans are on the Scoro pricing page.

Harvest: best for tracking time against monthly budgets

Harvest tracks time against project budgets, alerts you as budgets run down and invoices from tracked hours. For a simple retainer, a monthly project with an hours budget works well. Rollover isn't part of its public positioning, so expect carry-over to be a manual adjustment unless its official documentation says otherwise. Skip it if rollover and overage rules are central to your contracts. See the Harvest pricing page and our review of time tracking for marketing agencies.

Xero repeating invoices: best for fixed-fee retainers

Xero's repeating invoices let you schedule the same invoice each month, saved as a draft for review or sent automatically. If your retainer has no allowance to track, that may be all the retainer billing you need. There's no usage tracking, so extras must be added by hand. Consider another option once overages become regular. Plans are on the Xero pricing page.

Stripe Billing: best for payment-first schedules with retries

Stripe Billing manages recurring charges, invoices and automatic retries for failed payments, and can add usage-based line items. It's a good fit for productized services billed by card or direct debit. It's built for subscriptions, so hour allowances and rollover need custom handling. Skip it if most clients pay by manual bank transfer against purchase orders. Fees are on the Stripe Billing pricing page.

How should SEO and PPC retainers handle usage, overages and rollover?

Write the rules into the SOW first: the monthly allowance, whether unused time rolls over and for how long, the overage rate and who approves extra work. Then configure the billing tool to follow those rules, never the other way round.

Hypothetical SEO retainer, using stated assumptions: the client buys 30 hours a month. Unused hours roll over for one month only, capped at 10. Overage beyond allowance plus rollover is billed in arrears at an agreed hourly rate, only when the client approved it in writing. In month one the team uses 24 hours, so 6 roll forward. In month two it uses 38, against an available 36 (30 + 6), so 2 approved overage hours appear on the next invoice. These numbers are illustrative assumptions, not vendor data.

Hypothetical PPC retainer: a fixed management fee plus a variable component tied to ad spend bands, recalculated each month from the platform's reported spend. Here the tool must take an input figure each month, apply a band and add the line before the invoice is sent, which is closer to usage billing than to hours. Spend pacing itself belongs in PPC budget pacing tools.

Whatever the model, clients should see usage before the invoice arrives. A monthly usage summary in a portal or report avoids most disputes, and our guide to client portals for agencies covers that view.

Will clients accept overage invoices without a fight?

Overage invoices hold up when the client approved the extra work in writing and saw usage climbing before the month ended. Surprise overages, even fair ones, damage trust faster than almost any other billing issue.

What mistakes do agencies make with retainer billing?

The most expensive mistake is selling an hour allowance and then not tracking against it, which turns the retainer into unlimited work at a fixed price. The second is unlimited rollover, which builds a bank of owed hours that can come due all at once.

  • Vague scope: without a list of included work, every request becomes an argument about overages.
  • Rollover with no expiry: cap and expire carried hours, or the liability grows quietly.
  • Overages without approval: require written approval before work beyond the allowance starts.
  • No renewal trigger: set a reminder well before the term ends to review scope and price.
  • Billing in one tool, time in another, with no sync: reconcile hours to invoices monthly, or connect them through your project management software.

What else do agencies ask about retainer billing?

Questions usually cover rollover, payment timing and when to upgrade from accounting software. Answers here are general; your contracts set the actual terms.

Should unused retainer hours roll over?

Limited rollover, such as one month with a cap, is a common compromise that feels fair without building a large backlog. Unlimited rollover is risky because the owed hours can arrive all at once.

Should retainers be billed in advance?

Billing the fixed fee in advance protects cash flow and is common for agency retainers. Overages and pass-through costs are usually billed in arrears once they're known and approved.

Can accounting software handle retainer billing on its own?

Yes, for fixed fees with no allowance, using repeating invoices. Once you track hours, rollover or overages, you need time tracking or an agency platform feeding the invoice.

What happens when a retainer payment fails?

Payment-first tools can retry failed card or direct debit payments automatically and notify the client. For bank-transfer clients, your reminder schedule and contract terms do that job.

How often should retainer pricing be reviewed?

Review at each renewal, or sooner if usage is consistently above or below the allowance. Persistent overages usually mean the retainer should be re-scoped.

What's the verdict?

Choose Accelo or Productive if your retainers are allowances you must track, Ignition or Xero if they're fixed fees, and Stripe Billing if payment collection is the main job. Scoro and Harvest are sensible middle grounds depending on whether you want one system or a lighter time tool.
